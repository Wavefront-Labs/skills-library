# Bovine dystocia assessment and calving assistance

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** decide quickly whether a calving can be delivered per vaginam, correct the
problem with minimal trauma, and get a live calf and a cow that rebreeds.

## 1. When to examine (intervention guidelines)
- Stage 2 (amnion/feet visible or straining) **> 60–70 min in heifers / ~ 30–60 min in
  cows** without progress (Schuenemann data; farm protocols vary).
- No progress for 15–20 min after previous progress, or straining for > 30 min with
  nothing visible.
- Abnormal: only one leg, head without legs, tail first, bloody discharge, exhausted cow,
  stage 1 > 6 h (possible torsion).

## 2. Restraint and hygiene
- Cow restrained (calving gate/crush) with room; standing preferred. Clean the
  perineum, lubricate generously, clean gloves/arm. **Caudal epidural** (e.g. lidocaine
  per local dosing) reduces straining for manipulation — it doesn't stop uterine
  contractions.

## 3. Vaginal exam — describe three things
- **Presentation:** anterior (front feet + head), posterior (hind feet — hocks bend
  opposite to fetlocks; soles up), transverse.
- **Position:** dorsal (normal), lateral, ventral relative to the cow's spine.
- **Posture:** head and limbs extended vs flexed (carpal, shoulder, head-back, hock,
  breech).
- Assess: is the cervix fully dilated? Calf alive (pedal withdrawal, suck reflex,
  anal tone in posterior; heartbeat)? Size vs pelvis? Uterine wall twisted (spiral
  vaginal folds → torsion)?

## 4. Corrections
- Repel the calf (between contractions, cranially, under epidural) to make space; then
  correct posture: **cup the hoof** in your hand when bringing a limb up to avoid
  tearing the uterus.
- Carpal flexion → repel, bring the foot up in an arc. Head back → repel, grasp
  mandible/orbit (eye-hooks only by experienced), bring head up.
- **Breech** (both hindlegs forward): repel, convert to hock flexion, then to extended
  hind limbs, deliver posterior **quickly** (umbilical cord compression).
- **Uterine torsion:** confirm direction; roll the cow (plank-in-flank) or rotate the
  calf manually with a live calf; surgery if fails.

## 5. Traction rules
- Chains/ropes: **double loop on each limb** (one above fetlock, one below) — single
  loops break legs.
- **"Two-person test":** if two people pulling moderately cannot advance the calf
  (head through vulva, or anterior calf's elbows into pelvis), reassess for
  caesarean — a calving jack multiplies force and causes injuries; use it gently and
  in time with contractions.
- Anterior: pull up-and-out initially, then downward toward the hocks once the head is
  out; rotate the calf ~ 45–90° to bring the hips through the widest pelvic diameter
  ("hip lock").
- Posterior: deliver promptly once committed.

## 6. Caesarean or fetotomy
- Caesarean: foeto-maternal disproportion, irreducible torsion, incompletely dilated
  cervix with live calf, emphysematous calf where fetotomy unsafe. Left flank standing
  approach typically.
- Fetotomy: dead calf, competent operator, appropriate equipment — can avoid surgery.

## 7. Afterwards
- **Calf:** clear airways, stimulate (straw in nostril, cold water on head), sternal
  position; avoid hanging upside down for prolonged periods. **Colostrum** 10–12% of
  body weight within 6 h, first feed asap (e.g. 3–4 L for a dairy calf). Dip navel.
- **Cow:** check for a second calf, tears (vagina, cervix, rectum), haemorrhage, NSAID
  for pain; monitor for retained foetal membranes, metritis, milk fever, nerve paralysis.
- Record the event and calving-ease score for herd-health (sire selection, heifer
  growth targets).

## Gotchas
- Pulling before the cervix is fully dilated.
- Single-loop chains or jack used with excessive force.
- Missing a second calf or a vaginal tear.
- Forgetting colostrum after a hard calving (weak calf, poor suck).

## Verify
- [ ] Presentation, position, posture, cervix and calf viability recorded.
- [ ] Two-person test applied before mechanical traction.
- [ ] Post-delivery check for second calf and tears.
- [ ] Calf breathing, colostrum fed within 6 h.
- [ ] Event and calving-ease logged for herd records.
