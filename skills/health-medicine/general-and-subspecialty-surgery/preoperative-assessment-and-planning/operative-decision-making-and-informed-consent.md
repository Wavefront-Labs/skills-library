# Operative decision-making and informed consent

> General professional reference, not legal advice. Consent law and documentation rules differ by jurisdiction; follow your institution's policy. Medical optimisation (cardiac, respiratory, anticoagulants) is covered in `health-medicine.internal-medicine.consultative-and-perioperative-medicine.preoperative-risk-assessment-and-optimisation`.

**Goal:** a decision to operate (or not) that matches the patient's goals and a
realistic picture of risk, and consent that would stand up to the question "would a
reasonable patient in this position have wanted to know this?"

## 1. Frame the decision before the operation
Write down, in one line each:
- **Problem** and natural history without surgery (what happens if we do nothing?).
- **Goal** of surgery: cure, symptom control, prevention, diagnosis, palliation.
- **Options:** the proposed operation, alternative operations (open vs minimally
  invasive, staged, stoma vs anastomosis), non-operative management (antibiotics,
  endoscopic/radiological intervention), and **no intervention**.
- **Urgency:** immediate, urgent (hours), expedited (days), elective — this governs how
  much optimisation is possible.

## 2. Quantify risk — numbers, not adjectives
- **ASA grade** is a crude descriptor, not a risk model.
- Use a validated calculator suited to the setting: **ACS NSQIP** (procedure-specific),
  **P-POSSUM** (general/vascular; overestimates in low-risk patients), **NELA** score
  for emergency laparotomy (≥ 5% predicted mortality = "high risk": consultant surgeon
  and anaesthetist present, planned critical-care admission).
- **Frailty** (e.g. Clinical Frailty Scale ≥ 5) predicts complications, loss of
  independence and death beyond what ASA captures; involve geriatric medicine where
  available.
- Functional capacity: METs/stairs history; **CPET** for major elective surgery where
  available.
- Translate to what matters to the patient: chance of going home, of a stoma, of
  needing a care home, of being alive at 1 year.

## 3. Shared decision for high-risk surgery
Use a structured conversation such as **Best Case/Worst Case**: describe the realistic
best outcome, worst outcome and most likely course **with surgery and without**. Ask
about the patient's priorities and acceptable trade-offs. Agree a **treatment
escalation plan** (ICU, CPR status, re-operation limits) before theatre — not at 3 a.m.
when things go wrong.

## 4. Consent that meets the standard
- **Capacity:** the patient understands, retains, weighs and communicates. If capacity
  is lacking, follow the legal framework (best interests, advance decisions, legal
  proxies) — never consent "on behalf" without that authority.
- **Material risks:** since Montgomery (UK; similar patient-centred standards elsewhere),
  disclose risks a reasonable person in the patient's position would consider
  significant, **and** risks this particular patient would value (a musician and a
  hand nerve injury). Common-and-minor and rare-but-serious both count.
- Include: what the operation involves, expected benefits, common and serious risks with
  approximate numbers, alternatives including doing nothing, what recovery looks like,
  who will operate (trainee involvement), possible additional procedures (e.g. stoma,
  conversion to open), blood products, tissue use.
- **Timing:** for elective surgery, consent is a process — discuss in clinic, give
  written information and time, confirm on the day. Signing in the anaesthetic room is
  a confirmation, not the conversation.
- Document the discussion, numbers quoted, written information given and questions asked.

## 5. Operative plan
Approach, positioning, equipment (e.g. robot, specific implants, intraoperative imaging),
blood availability, antibiotic and VTE prophylaxis, need for other specialties (urology
stents, plastics), critical-care bed, and booking urgency category. Share it at the
**team brief**.

## Gotchas
- "Because it's operable" is not an indication; ask whether it is *worthwhile* for this patient.
- Calculators are population averages; state uncertainty honestly.
- Interpreters must be professional, not family, for consent discussions.
- Withdrawal of consent can occur at any time — respect it and document.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication, goal, urgency and all options (including none) documented.
- [ ] Risk quantified with an appropriate validated tool plus frailty.
- [ ] High-risk patients: shared decision and escalation plan recorded before theatre.
- [ ] Capacity assessed; material and patient-specific risks disclosed with numbers.
- [ ] Operative plan shared at team brief.
