# Laparoscopic cholecystectomy: the critical view of safety

> General surgical reference. Use of routine cholangiography, ICG and drains differs between units; follow local practice and supervision requirements.

**Goal:** remove the gallbladder without injuring the common bile duct. Bile duct
injury (roughly 0.3–0.5% of laparoscopic cholecystectomies) is mostly a **visual
misperception** error — the surgeon is certain the CBD is the cystic duct. The method
below exists to force proof before clipping.

## 1. Set-up and orientation
- Fundus retracted cephalad over the liver, Hartmann's pouch retracted **laterally and
  inferiorly** to open the hepatocystic triangle and separate the cystic duct from the CBD.
- Identify landmarks before dissecting:
  - **Rouvière's sulcus** (fissure in the right liver): stay **above** the line through
    it (the "R4U" line: Rouvière, segment 4, umbilical fissure) — dissection below it risks the CBD.
  - The common bile duct's general course, duodenum, and segment 4.
- Start dissection **high on the gallbladder** (posterior peritoneum on both sides of
  Hartmann's pouch), staying on the gallbladder wall.

## 2. The critical view of safety (CVS) — three criteria
1. The **hepatocystic triangle cleared** of fat and fibrous tissue (the CBD and common
   hepatic duct need not be exposed).
2. The **lower third of the gallbladder separated from the liver bed** (cystic plate
   visible).
3. **Two and only two structures** enter the gallbladder: cystic duct and cystic artery.
Take a **time-out** before clipping: confirm all three, ideally with a second observer,
and **photograph** (anterior and posterior "doublet" views) for the record.

## 3. When CVS can't be achieved — bail out
Severe inflammation, contracted gallbladder, or Mirizzi syndrome make anatomy unsafe.
Options, in a rough order of preference by situation:
- **Subtotal cholecystectomy:**
  - **Fenestrating** — leave the back wall/Hartmann's open, close the cystic orifice from
    inside if possible; more bile leaks, fewer remnant gallbladders.
  - **Reconstituting** — close the remnant to form a small pouch; fewer leaks, but the
    remnant can form stones and symptoms later.
  Laparoscopic subtotal is often safer than conversion to open in hostile anatomy.
- **Fundus-first (top-down)** dissection — only if it can be done safely; it can lead
  straight onto the hilum if you get into the wrong plane.
- **Cholecystostomy** tube (operative or percutaneous) and abort.
- Call a second surgeon early. Asking for help is a safety behaviour.

## 4. Cholangiography
- **Intraoperative cholangiogram (IOC)** or **ICG fluorescence cholangiography**
  clarifies anatomy and detects CBD stones. Routine vs selective use is debated;
  use it whenever there is doubt.
- An IOC that shows no flow into the hepatic ducts may mean you've cannulated the CBD.

## 5. Acute cholecystitis
Tokyo Guidelines 2018 grade severity. Early cholecystectomy (ideally within ~72 h of
symptoms, extended to ~7–10 days in practice) is generally preferred over delayed
surgery for fit patients; high-risk Grade III patients may need drainage first.

## 6. After the operation
- Bile leak (from cystic stump or ducts of Luschka): pain, fever, rising bilirubin,
  bilious drain output → ultrasound/CT, percutaneous drainage, **ERCP with stent**.
- Suspected major duct injury (jaundice, high-output leak, clips across the CBD on
  imaging) → **early referral to a hepatobiliary centre**; do not attempt repair without
  HPB expertise — outcomes are best when the first repair is specialist.

## Gotchas
- "Infundibular technique" (calling the duct cystic because it seems to flare into the
  gallbladder) is the classic misidentification trap — use CVS, not this sign.
- Bleeding near the hilum: pressure, not blind clips or diathermy.
- A short, wide "cystic duct" may be the CBD tented up.

## Verify
- [ ] Landmarks identified; dissection above R4U line.
- [ ] All three CVS criteria met, time-out held, photo-documented.
- [ ] Bail-out used rather than clipping without CVS.
- [ ] Cholangiography used where anatomy was uncertain.
- [ ] Post-op leak/injury pathway known; HPB referral for suspected major injury.
