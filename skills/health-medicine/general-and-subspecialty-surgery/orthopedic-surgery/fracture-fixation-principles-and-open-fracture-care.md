# Fracture fixation principles and open fracture care

> General orthopaedic reference, not patient-specific advice. Antibiotic agents, durations and timing targets must follow current national standards (e.g. BOAST) and local microbiology policy.

**Goal:** match the fixation method to the fracture's biology and mechanics, treat
open fractures as a time-critical soft-tissue injury, and never miss a compartment
syndrome.

## 1. Assessment
- Mechanism, energy, and the **soft tissues** (swelling, blisters, wounds) — the
  envelope often dictates timing more than the bone.
- **Neurovascular examination** distal to the injury, documented before and after any
  manipulation or splinting.
- Imaging: two orthogonal views including the **joint above and below**; CT for
  intra-articular fractures and planning.
- Classify (AO/OTA) to communicate and plan.

## 2. Stability concepts (AO)
- **Absolute stability** (no motion at the fracture) → primary bone healing without
  callus. Use for **articular** fractures (anatomical reduction is mandatory to avoid
  post-traumatic arthritis) and simple diaphyseal patterns where perfect reduction is
  achievable: **lag screw + neutralisation plate**, compression plating.
- **Relative stability** (controlled micromotion) → secondary healing with callus. Use
  for **multifragmentary diaphyseal** fractures: **intramedullary nail**, **bridge
  plating**, external fixation. Restore length, alignment and rotation; don't strip
  each fragment.
- Mixing the two on one fracture line (gaps under a rigid construct) is a classic
  cause of non-union.

## 3. Choosing the implant (rules of thumb)
- Femoral and tibial shaft → **IM nail** in most adults.
- Periarticular/intra-articular → plates, screws (locking plates in osteoporotic bone
  or short metaphyseal segments).
- Severe soft-tissue injury or physiologically unstable patient → **spanning external
  fixator** as damage-control orthopaedics, then definitive fixation when the soft
  tissues ("wrinkle sign") and patient allow.
- Hip fracture in older adults: surgery within ~36 h (UK standard) — intracapsular
  displaced → arthroplasty; extracapsular → sliding hip screw or nail. Orthogeriatric
  co-management.

## 4. Open fractures
- **IV antibiotics as soon as possible** — ideally within **1 hour** of injury
  (agent per local policy); tetanus status.
- Photograph the wound, remove gross contamination, cover with saline-soaked dressing,
  splint. Avoid repeated wound inspection in ED.
- **Gustilo-Anderson** grade at surgery (I < 1 cm clean; II 1–10 cm; III high-energy,
  extensive soft tissue damage; IIIB needs flap cover; IIIC has arterial injury requiring repair).
- **Debridement** by senior orthopaedic and plastic surgeons together (orthoplastic
  approach): typically within 12 h for high-energy, 24 h for others; **immediately** for
  gross contamination (agricultural, marine, sewage), vascular injury or compartment syndrome.
- **Definitive soft-tissue cover within 72 h** where possible (BOAST 4: ideally at the
  same time as definitive fixation).

## 5. Compartment syndrome
- Highest risk: tibial fractures, forearm fractures, crush injury, young men, after
  reperfusion.
- Earliest and most reliable sign: **pain out of proportion**, escalating analgesia
  requirement, pain on **passive stretch**. Pallor, pulselessness and paralysis are
  **late** — a palpable pulse does not exclude it.
- Remove circumferential dressings/casts to skin, limb at heart level (not elevated).
- Compartment pressure monitoring in obtunded patients: **ΔP (diastolic − compartment)
  < 30 mmHg** supports diagnosis.
- **Emergency fasciotomy** (all four leg compartments via two incisions) — it is a
  clinical diagnosis; don't wait for numbers in an awake patient.

## Gotchas
- Rotational malalignment after nailing: check clinically and radiologically before
  leaving theatre.
- Smokers, diabetics and NSAID use → higher non-union risk; counsel and follow up.
- Fat embolism: hypoxia, confusion, petechiae 24–72 h after long-bone fractures.

## Verify
- [ ] Neurovascular status documented pre- and post-manipulation.
- [ ] Fixation chosen to match fracture pattern (absolute vs relative stability).
- [ ] Open fracture: antibiotics ≤ 1 h, photo, combined debridement, cover plan ≤ 72 h.
- [ ] Compartment syndrome assessed serially in high-risk limbs.
- [ ] Rotation/length checked before leaving theatre.
