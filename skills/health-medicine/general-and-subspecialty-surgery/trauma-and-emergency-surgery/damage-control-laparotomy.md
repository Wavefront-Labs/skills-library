# Damage-control laparotomy

> General surgical reference, not patient-specific advice. Transfusion ratios, haemostatic agents and REBOA use follow local major-haemorrhage and trauma protocols. Primary survey: `health-medicine.emergency-medicine.trauma-management.major-trauma-primary-survey`.

**Goal:** keep a physiologically exhausted patient alive by doing **only** what stops
bleeding and contamination, then hand them to the ICU to be warmed, resuscitated and
de-coagulopathied — and come back for definitive repair once they can survive it.

## 1. Who needs damage control (decide early — ideally before or at incision)
- The **lethal triad**: hypothermia (< 35 °C), acidosis (pH < 7.2, base deficit worse than
  ~-8, rising lactate) and coagulopathy (clinical oozing, INR/viscoelastic tests); some
  add hypocalcaemia ("lethal diamond").
- Massive transfusion requirement (e.g. > 10 units red cells), persistent shock.
- Injury pattern: major vascular injury plus hollow-viscus injury, high-grade liver
  injury, pelvic plus abdominal bleeding, multiple cavities.
- Inability to close the abdomen without tension, or a need to reach another cavity/IR.
The trap is a "heroic" definitive operation that ends with a technically perfect repair
in a dead patient.

## 2. Damage-control resuscitation (in parallel)
Permissive hypotension until bleeding is controlled (not in head injury), early
**balanced blood products** (ratio-based, e.g. ~1:1:1 plasma:platelets:red cells, or whole
blood), **tranexamic acid** within 3 h of injury, calcium replacement, active warming,
limited crystalloid. Viscoelastic testing guides later correction where available.

## 3. The abbreviated operation
1. **Midline xiphoid-to-pubis** incision; prep chest to knees.
2. **Evacuate blood and clot** and **pack all four quadrants** firmly; let the
   anaesthetist catch up.
3. Remove packs quadrant by quadrant, starting away from the most likely source, and
   **control haemorrhage**: ligation, shunts for major vessels, splenectomy rather than
   salvage, perihepatic packing for liver injury (Pringle manoeuvre to test inflow),
   Pringle/aortic control at the hiatus for torrential bleeding.
4. **Control contamination:** staple off injured bowel, leaving it in discontinuity —
   **no anastomoses, no stomas** in the first operation as a rule.
5. **Temporary abdominal closure:** negative-pressure laparostomy (commercial system or
   improvised "vac pack"); do not close the fascia.
6. Aim for **~60–90 minutes**; communicate time and physiology with anaesthesia
   throughout.
Consider **hybrid theatre/interventional radiology** for pelvic or solid-organ bleeding,
and **REBOA** as a bridge only where the team is trained (evidence is mixed).

## 4. ICU phase (typically 24–48 h)
Rewarm, correct acidosis and coagulopathy, normalise calcium, optimise ventilation.
Watch for **abdominal compartment syndrome** (bladder pressure > 20 mmHg with new organ
dysfunction) even with an open abdomen — the dressing can be too tight.
**Return to theatre early** for ongoing bleeding (high drain output, falling Hb despite
correction): unplanned re-look beats waiting.

## 5. Planned re-look
When physiology has normalised: remove packs, full inspection for missed injuries,
definitive repair (anastomosis or stoma), and **early fascial closure** (aim within
~7 days; delayed closure increases fistula and frozen-abdomen risk). Sequential fascial
traction systems help when primary closure isn't possible.

## Gotchas
- Missed injuries are common in damage-control patients — do a full tertiary survey
  once stable.
- Packs left behind: count them in and out, document the number in the op note.
- Hypothermia worsens in theatre: warm room, warmed fluids, forced-air warming from
  the start.
- Bowel left stapled must be reassessed; ischaemia evolves.

## Verify
- [ ] Damage-control decision made early on physiology and injury pattern.
- [ ] Balanced resuscitation, TXA, calcium and warming running.
- [ ] Bleeding and contamination controlled; no definitive reconstruction.
- [ ] Temporary closure applied; packs counted and recorded.
- [ ] ICU targets and re-look timing documented; compartment pressure monitored.
