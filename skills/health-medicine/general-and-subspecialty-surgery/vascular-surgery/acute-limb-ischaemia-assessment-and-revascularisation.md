# Acute limb ischaemia: assessment and revascularisation

> General vascular reference, not patient-specific advice. Heparin and thrombolytic dosing must follow current local protocols; all suspected cases need immediate vascular surgical input.

**Goal:** recognise a threatened limb within minutes, anticoagulate, decide how
viable it is, and get blood flow back by the right route before muscle dies — muscle
tolerates roughly **4–6 hours** of complete ischaemia.

## 1. Recognise it
Sudden decrease in limb perfusion (< 2 weeks) threatening viability. The **6 Ps**:
pain, pallor, pulselessness, perishingly cold, **paraesthesia** and **paralysis**
(the last two mean the limb is threatened). Pain on passive movement or calf
tenderness suggests muscle necrosis.

## 2. Embolus or thrombosis? (changes the operation)
- **Embolic:** abrupt onset, identifiable source (**AF**, recent MI with mural thrombus,
  aneurysm), normal pulses in the other leg, no claudication history → embolectomy works well.
- **Thrombotic (in situ):** known peripheral arterial disease/claudication, prior
  bypass or stent, absent pulses in the contralateral leg, collateral-limited onset
  → often needs thrombolysis or bypass/endovascular treatment of the underlying lesion.
- Also: popliteal aneurysm thrombosis, dissection, trauma, iatrogenic (catheter access sites).

## 3. Grade viability (Rutherford)
| Class | Sensory loss | Muscle weakness | Arterial Doppler | Venous Doppler |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| I viable | none | none | audible | audible |
| IIa marginally threatened | minimal (toes) | none | often inaudible | audible |
| IIb immediately threatened | more than toes, rest pain | mild–moderate | inaudible | audible |
| III irreversible | profound, anaesthetic | paralysis, rigor | inaudible | inaudible |
Use a **handheld Doppler** — don't rely on palpated pulses.

## 4. Immediate management
- **Unfractionated heparin** IV bolus then infusion (unless contraindicated) as soon
  as the diagnosis is made — stops clot propagation.
- Analgesia, oxygen if hypoxic, fluids, ECG (AF?), bloods incl. CK, lactate, U&E, group and save.
- Imaging: **CT angiography** for class I–IIa if it doesn't delay; **IIb → straight to
  theatre** (on-table angiography in a hybrid theatre).

## 5. Revascularisation options
- **Surgical embolectomy** (Fogarty balloon catheter via femoral or popliteal
  arteriotomy) — first line for embolism, especially IIb.
- **Catheter-directed thrombolysis** — for thrombotic occlusion and graft thrombosis in
  **class I–IIa** (time for lysis exists), then treat the unmasked lesion; contraindicated
  with recent surgery, stroke, active bleeding. Percutaneous mechanical thrombectomy devices
  shorten time.
- **Bypass** or endovascular angioplasty/stent for underlying atherosclerotic disease.
- **Class III → primary amputation** (revascularising dead muscle causes fatal
  reperfusion syndrome); decide with senior input and the patient/family.

## 6. After revascularisation
- **Compartment syndrome** from reperfusion oedema: low threshold for **four-compartment
  fasciotomy**, prophylactically when ischaemia was prolonged (> ~6 h) or in IIb limbs.
- **Reperfusion syndrome:** hyperkalaemia, acidosis, myoglobinuria/AKI — monitor K+, CK,
  urine output, aggressive fluids.
- Long-term **anticoagulation** for embolic source (AF), investigate source (echo);
  antiplatelet and statin for atherosclerotic disease.

## Gotchas
- Diabetic neuropathy blunts pain — rely on Doppler and motor/sensory examination.
- A cold, pale leg in a shocked patient may be low flow, not occlusion — re-examine after
  resuscitation, compare sides.
- Aortic dissection can present as a cold leg — check the other limbs and chest.
- Mortality after acute limb ischaemia is high (~10–20% at 30 days); involve the
  patient in decisions about futility.

## Verify
- [ ] Doppler signals and Rutherford class documented with time of onset.
- [ ] Heparin started unless contraindicated.
- [ ] Embolic vs thrombotic cause considered; imaging chosen by class.
- [ ] Revascularisation route matches class and aetiology; class III discussed for amputation.
- [ ] Fasciotomy considered; K+, CK and urine output monitored after reperfusion.
