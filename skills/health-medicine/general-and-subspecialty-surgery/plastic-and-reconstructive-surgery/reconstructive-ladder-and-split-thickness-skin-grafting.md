# Reconstructive ladder and split-thickness skin grafting

> General surgical reference. Technique details (dermatome settings, dressing protocols) vary by unit and device; follow local plastic surgery guidance. Facial reconstruction specifics: `health-medicine.otolaryngology.facial-plastic-and-reconstructive-surgery.facial-fractures-rhinoplasty-and-local-flaps`.

**Goal:** close a wound with the simplest method that gives **reliable function and
acceptable appearance**, and when that method is a skin graft, get it to take.

## 1. The reconstructive ladder (and elevator)
From simple to complex:
1. Healing by **secondary intention** (± negative-pressure wound therapy)
2. **Direct (primary) closure**
3. **Split-thickness skin graft (STSG)**
4. **Full-thickness skin graft (FTSG)**
5. **Local flap** (advancement, rotation, transposition)
6. **Regional/pedicled flap**
7. **Free flap** (microvascular transfer)
The **reconstructive elevator** concept: go straight to the rung that gives the best
result, not the lowest one that technically closes the hole — e.g. exposed tendon
without paratenon, bone without periosteum, joints, implants or major vessels need a
**flap**, not a graft.

## 2. What a graft needs
A graft has no blood supply of its own: it survives by **plasmatic imbibition**
(first ~48 h), then **inosculation** and revascularisation (~days 3–7).
A graftable bed is vascular: granulation tissue, muscle, fascia, paratenon,
periosteum, perichondrium. **Not graftable:** bare bone/cartilage/tendon, irradiated
or infected beds, heavy slough.

## 3. STSG vs FTSG
- **STSG** (epidermis + part of dermis, typically ~0.2–0.4 mm / 8–16 thousandths of an
  inch): takes on less ideal beds, covers large areas, donor site heals in ~10–14 days;
  but **contracts more** (secondary contraction) and looks worse (shiny, pigment change).
- **FTSG** (whole dermis): better colour/texture match and less contraction — face,
  hands, flexural creases; donor site closed directly (post-auricular, supraclavicular,
  groin); needs an excellent bed.

## 4. Doing an STSG
1. **Prepare the bed:** debride to bleeding tissue, achieve meticulous haemostasis
   (haematoma is the commonest cause of graft loss), consider quantitative culture in
   doubtful wounds (beta-haemolytic streptococci destroy grafts at any count).
2. **Donor site:** usually anterolateral thigh (also buttock, scalp in children —
   hidden scar, quick healing). Lubricate skin, apply tension with boards.
3. **Harvest** with a powered dermatome set to the chosen thickness and width; even
   pressure, steady speed, ~30–45° angle. Translucent graft with fine punctate
   bleeding at the donor = appropriately thin; larger, sparse bleeding points and
   whiter dermis = thicker.
4. **Meshing** (e.g. 1:1.5) lets exudate escape and expands coverage; avoid on the face
   and hands where appearance matters (use sheet grafts and roll out seromas).
5. **Orientation:** dermal side (shiny) down onto the bed.
6. **Fixation:** staples, sutures or glue; immobilise with a **tie-over bolster** or
   **NPWT** (good for irregular or mobile beds). Shear is the second enemy of take.
7. **Donor dressing:** moist or alginate/film dressings per protocol; donor sites are
   painful — plan analgesia.

## 5. After the graft
- First dressing check commonly at **day 5** (earlier if infection suspected).
- "Take" = pink, adherent, blanching. Loss from haematoma, seroma, shear or infection.
- Elevate limbs; splint across joints. Moisturise and protect from sun after healing.

## 6. Flaps in brief
Choose by defect needs (bulk, pliability, bone), donor morbidity and vessel
availability. **Free-flap monitoring** (colour, capillary refill, temperature, Doppler
or implantable probe) every hour early on; venous congestion (purple, brisk refill)
or arterial insufficiency (pale, cold, no refill) → **urgent return to theatre**:
salvage rates fall with every hour.

## Gotchas
- Grafting over exposed tendon or bone "because it's simpler" fails and costs weeks.
- Smoking and uncontrolled diabetes reduce take and flap survival.
- Wrong-side-down grafts never take — check before fixing.

- [ ] Bed debrided and haemostasis meticulous; graft dermis down; fixed against shear.
- [ ] Dressing check date set; take documented.
