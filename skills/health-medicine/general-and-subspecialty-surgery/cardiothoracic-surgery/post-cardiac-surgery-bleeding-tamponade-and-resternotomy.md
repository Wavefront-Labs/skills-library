# Post-cardiac surgery bleeding, tamponade and resternotomy

> General cardiothoracic reference, not patient-specific advice. Re-exploration thresholds, protamine and haemostatic product dosing follow unit protocol and must be verified against current guidelines and local formulary.

**Goal:** in the first 24 hours after cardiac surgery, distinguish medical (coagulopathic)
from surgical bleeding, recognise tamponade even when the drains look quiet, and open
the chest early when that is the answer.

## 1. Quantify bleeding
- Record **chest drain output hourly** (and strip/milk drains per unit policy to keep them patent).
- Typical re-exploration triggers (vary by unit): > ~400 mL in the first hour,
  > ~200 mL/h for 2–4 h, or > ~1 L total in 4 h after correction of coagulopathy —
  or **sudden** increase in a previously settling patient.
- Falling haemoglobin, rising lactate, increasing inotrope/vasopressor need confirm significance.

## 2. Correct the medical causes in parallel
- **Normothermia** (> 36 °C), **ionised calcium**, normal pH.
- **Residual heparin / heparin rebound:** check ACT; extra protamine if prolonged.
- **Viscoelastic testing (ROTEM/TEG)** plus platelet count and fibrinogen to guide
  targeted products: fibrinogen concentrate/cryoprecipitate, platelets (dysfunction after
  bypass and antiplatelets even with a normal count), PCC or plasma.
- Control hypertension (it drives bleeding from suture lines), ensure adequate PEEP per
  unit practice.
- Tranexamic acid is usually given intraoperatively already.
If bleeding persists despite correction → **surgical cause** until proven otherwise.

## 3. Tamponade after cardiac surgery
Can occur with **low or stopped drain output** (clot blocking drains, localised clot
compressing the right atrium or ventricle).
- Signs: rising CVP, falling BP and cardiac output, tachycardia, rising inotrope needs,
  **falling urine output**, pulsus paradoxus; equalisation of filling pressures.
- Echo: **transthoracic often inadequate** post-op; **TOE** shows localised clot.
- Treatment is surgical: **re-exploration** (in theatre if stable enough; at the bedside if not).

## 4. Cardiac arrest after cardiac surgery (CALS approach)
Differs from standard ALS because reversible causes are common and the chest can be
reopened:
- **VF/pulseless VT:** up to **3 stacked shocks** before compressions (within the first
  minute), then CPR.
- **Asystole/severe bradycardia:** **epicardial pacing** (DDD, max output) if wires present.
- **PEA:** turn off pacing to exclude underlying VF, then CPR.
- **Emergency resternotomy within ~5 minutes** of arrest if no return of circulation —
  team roles pre-assigned, resternotomy kit ready by every bed.
- Adrenaline **held or given in small doses** — full-dose boluses risk severe
  hypertension and suture-line disruption after ROSC (follow unit protocol).

## 5. Other early problems to recognise
- **Low cardiac output:** check preload, rhythm/pacing, contractility (echo), afterload,
  tamponade; inotropes, mechanical support as needed.
- **Postoperative AF** (very common, day 2–4): rate/rhythm control, electrolytes (K, Mg),
  anticoagulation decision if persistent.
- Vasoplegia after bypass: vasopressors (noradrenaline, vasopressin).
- Graft ischaemia after CABG: new ECG changes, arrhythmia — discuss angiography.

## Gotchas
- A quiet drain is not reassurance in a deteriorating patient.
- Massive drain output can be blood that pooled earlier — the trend matters.
- Hypocalcaemia from transfused citrate quietly worsens bleeding and contractility.
- Delayed re-exploration (after > 12 h, massive transfusion) has much worse outcomes than
  early re-exploration.

## Verify
- [ ] Hourly drain output charted; re-exploration triggers written on the chart.
- [ ] Temperature, calcium, ACT/protamine and viscoelastic-guided correction done.
- [ ] Tamponade considered with haemodynamic decline even with low drain output; TOE used.
- [ ] CALS roles assigned; resternotomy kit and pacing box at the bedside.
- [ ] Decision to re-explore documented with times.
