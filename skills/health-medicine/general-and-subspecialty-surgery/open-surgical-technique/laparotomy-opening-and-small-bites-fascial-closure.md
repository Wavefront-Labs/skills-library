# Laparotomy: opening and small-bites fascial closure

> General surgical reference. Suture choice and mesh use depend on local evidence review and product availability; follow your unit's standard.

**Goal:** enter the abdomen without injuring bowel, get the exposure the operation
needs, and close the fascia so the patient doesn't come back with a burst abdomen or
an incisional hernia (historically 10–20%+ after midline laparotomy).

## 1. Opening
- **Plan the incision for the operation**, not habit: midline gives the most flexible
  access; transverse/subcostal incisions may have lower hernia rates for targeted access.
- Skin with the scalpel, then diathermy through fat to the **linea alba** — find it
  where it is widest, usually just above the umbilicus; decussating fibres confirm the midline.
- Enter the peritoneum **away from previous scars** (adhesions are worst under old
  incisions): lift the peritoneum between two clips, check nothing is caught by rolling
  it between fingers, nick, then open under vision with a finger or instrument protecting
  the viscera.
- Extend in the line of the incision; deviate around the umbilicus.
- **Wound protector/retractor** — reduces SSI in some trials (evidence mixed); a
  self-retaining retractor saves an assistant but watch nerve compression (femoral
  nerve with deep lateral blades).

## 2. During the operation
- Gentle tissue handling: hold bowel with the hand or a moist swab, not toothed forceps.
- Keep exposed bowel moist and warm; pack to define the operative field and count packs.
- Hemostasis by layer as you go; a dry wound is cleaner and heals better.

## 3. Closure — small bites (the high-yield part)
Evidence (STITCH trial and EHS guideline) supports:
- **Continuous** running suture, **slowly absorbable** monofilament (e.g. polydioxanone),
  **USP 2-0** on a small needle (the STITCH trial used 2-0 on a 31 mm needle).
- **Bites 5–8 mm** from the fascial edge (aponeurosis only, not muscle or fat) and
  **stitches ~5 mm apart**.
- **Suture length : wound length ≥ 4:1.** Measure the incision and the remaining suture
  length at the end — it is the one number that checks your technique.
- Tension: approximate, don't strangle; tissue necrosis inside a tight loop causes
  hernias and dehiscence.
- Mass closure vs layered: include the posterior sheath where present; don't take
  peritoneum separately.
- **Prophylactic mesh** (onlay or retromuscular) for high-risk patients (abdominal
  aortic aneurysm, BMI ≥ 27 in PRIMA) reduces incisional hernia; uptake and guidance
  vary — decide with your unit.

## 4. Emergency and contaminated cases
- Small-bites remains reasonable in emergencies, but evidence is weaker; consider
  reinforced closure or delayed closure in gross contamination.
- If the fascia can't be closed without tension (bowel oedema, intra-abdominal
  hypertension), **do not force it**: temporary abdominal closure (negative-pressure
  system) and planned return — see
  `health-medicine.general-and-subspecialty-surgery.trauma-and-emergency-surgery.damage-control-laparotomy`.

## 5. Skin
Subcuticular absorbable suture or staples for clean wounds; leave contaminated skin
open or loosely closed (delayed primary closure) to reduce SSI. Negative-pressure
incisional dressings in selected high-risk wounds.

## Gotchas
- Large bites (1 cm × 1 cm) feel "secure" but produce more hernias.
- Fat necrosis and seroma follow over-zealous diathermy of subcutaneous fat.
- Burst abdomen typically presents ~day 5–10 with serosanguinous ("salmon-pink")
  discharge — treat as fascial dehiscence until proven otherwise.
- Count swabs, needles and instruments before closure (see
  `health-medicine.nursing.perioperative-nursing.surgical-safety-checklist-and-counts`).

## Verify
- [ ] Peritoneal entry under vision, away from old scars.
- [ ] Closure: continuous slowly-absorbable monofilament, 5–8 mm bites, ~5 mm apart.
- [ ] SL:WL ratio measured and ≥ 4:1, recorded in the op note.
- [ ] Mesh or temporary closure considered for high-risk/tense abdomens.
- [ ] Counts correct before fascial closure.
