# Postoperative complication recognition and return to theatre

> General surgical reference, not patient-specific advice. CRP cut-offs and early-warning thresholds are indicative and differ by operation and unit; follow local escalation policy. Sepsis bundle: `health-medicine.critical-care-medicine.sepsis-management.sepsis-hour-one-bundle-and-source-control`.

**Goal:** catch the complication early. Hospitals differ less in how many complications
they have than in how many patients **die from** them — "failure to rescue". The
difference is recognition and timely escalation.

## 1. Think by timing
- **Day 0–1:** bleeding, anaesthetic complications, airway, atelectasis, missed
  intraoperative injury, fluid problems, MI.
- **Day 2–5:** **anastomotic leak**, unrecognised bowel injury, pneumonia, urinary
  retention/UTI, ileus, delirium, AKI.
- **Day 5–10:** surgical site infection, collection/abscess, **fascial dehiscence**,
  line infection, C. difficile.
- **Day 7–21+:** **VTE** (PE can occur after discharge), late collections.
"Fever" on day 1 is usually inflammatory/atelectasis; fever on day 5 is infection until proven otherwise.

## 2. The deteriorating patient — what to look at
- **Early warning score (e.g. NEWS2)** trend, not just the value. Tachycardia is often
  the first and only sign of a leak or bleed; new **AF** after abdominal surgery is a
  leak clue.
- **"Not right" day 3:** reduced mobility, failure to progress on ERAS milestones
  (eating, bowels, mobilising, oral analgesia), new confusion, oliguria.
- Abdomen: pain beyond expected, peritonism, distension; drains (when present) — but
  **a quiet drain doesn't exclude a leak** (drains block and drain the wrong place).
- Wound: erythema, discharge, salmon-pink fluid (dehiscence).

## 3. Biomarkers
- **CRP** rises after every operation and peaks ~day 2–3, then should fall. A CRP on
  **day 3–4 above ~150 mg/L** after colorectal surgery raises suspicion of
  leak/infection and supports CT; a falling CRP has strong negative predictive value
  (rules out more than it rules in).
- Lactate, WCC, creatinine, bilirubin trends.

## 4. Imaging and decisions
- **CT abdomen/pelvis with IV contrast** (± rectal contrast for low pelvic anastomoses)
  for suspected leak, collection or bleeding. Don't let imaging delay surgery in a
  peritonitic, shocked patient.
- **Collection without generalised peritonitis** → antibiotics + percutaneous drainage.
- **Leak with peritonitis or sepsis** → **relaparotomy** (or laparoscopy): washout and
  usually take down the anastomosis/defunction; damage-control principles if unstable.
- **Bleeding:** correct coagulopathy; CT angiography and IR embolisation where suitable;
  return to theatre for haemodynamic instability.

## 5. The return-to-theatre call
Go back if: peritonitis, sepsis with a likely surgical source not drainable
percutaneously, haemodynamic instability from bleeding, fascial dehiscence,
ischaemic stoma below the fascia, or **unexplained deterioration with a negative
search** in a patient who "isn't right" — a negative relaparotomy is less harmful than
a late positive one. Senior review must happen in person, early.

## 6. Prevention (ERAS elements)
Pre-op carbohydrate loading, avoid prolonged fasting, goal-directed fluids, multimodal
opioid-sparing analgesia, early feeding and mobilisation, early catheter and drain
removal, VTE prophylaxis (extended for cancer pelvic/abdominal surgery), chest physio.

## 7. Classify and learn
Grade with **Clavien-Dindo** (I deviation → V death); discuss at M&M.

## Gotchas
- Opioids and epidurals mask abdominal signs; steroids and elderly blunt fever and peritonism.
- Attributing tachycardia to "pain" or "dehydration" without reassessment is the common
  failure pattern.
- PE presents as tachycardia/hypoxia; check prophylaxis was actually given.

## Verify
- [ ] Differential considered by post-op day.
- [ ] NEWS2 trend and ERAS milestones reviewed every day.
- [ ] CRP trajectory checked (day 3–4) after major abdominal surgery.
- [ ] CT obtained or theatre booked without delay when suspicion is high.
- [ ] Senior review documented in person; Clavien-Dindo grade recorded.
