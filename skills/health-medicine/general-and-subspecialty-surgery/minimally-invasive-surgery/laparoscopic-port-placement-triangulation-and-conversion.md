# Laparoscopic port placement, triangulation and conversion

> General surgical reference. Entry technique preferences (open Hasson, Veress, optical) vary by specialty and unit; entry is covered in detail in `health-medicine.obstetrics-and-gynecology.gynecologic-surgery.safe-laparoscopic-entry`.

**Goal:** place ports so the instruments, camera and surgeon work in natural geometry;
keep a clear view; and recognise when the operation is safer open.

## 1. Pneumoperitoneum
- Typical working pressure **12–15 mmHg** (lower, e.g. 8–10, often adequate with deep
  neuromuscular block and reduces pain/haemodynamic effects).
- Losing the view? Run a checklist: insufflator tubing kinked or gas cylinder empty, a
  port valve open or a port pulled back into the wall, suction running, inadequate
  muscle relaxation (ask the anaesthetist), smoke (vent with suction/smoke evacuator).
- Haemodynamic collapse after insufflation: think **CO2 embolism, tension
  capnothorax, vagal response, major vessel injury** — desufflate and alert the team.

## 2. Port placement for triangulation
- **Baseball-diamond principle:** camera at home plate, the target at second base, the
  working ports at first and third base. Instruments should converge on the target at
  roughly **60–90°** to each other, with the camera between them.
- Distance to target: ideally about **half the instrument length** inside the abdomen
  (~15–20 cm), so the fulcrum gives useful leverage without exaggerated movement.
- Ports too close together → **"sword fighting"**; ports in line with the camera →
  instruments block the view; too far from the target → loss of fine control.
- Patient positioning (Trendelenburg, reverse, lateral tilt) lets gravity retract bowel —
  secure the patient before tilting.
- Place secondary ports **under direct vision**, transilluminate to avoid abdominal
  wall vessels (inferior epigastrics run lateral to the rectus edge), and inspect for
  entry injury after the first port.

## 3. Camera work (often the assistant's job)
- Keep the **horizon level** and the operating instruments in the centre.
- With a **30° scope**, rotate the light cable to look around structures while keeping
  the camera head upright — the light post points opposite to the direction of view.
- Move slowly; announce zooming in/out; clean the lens with warm saline or anti-fog
  rather than repeatedly wiping on bowel.

## 4. Ergonomics
- Monitor directly in front at or slightly below eye level; table height so elbows are
  ~90° with relaxed shoulders. Foot pedals placed for the operating foot. Poor
  ergonomics is a real career-shortening injury risk for surgeons.

## 5. Suturing and extraction
- Intracorporeal suturing: needle loaded at ~90° to the needle driver, bite with wrist
  rotation, C-loop technique for square knots. Practise on simulators (FLS tasks).
- Close fascia at ports **≥ 10 mm** (and smaller ports in the umbilicus/children per
  local practice) to prevent port-site hernia; use specimen bags for extraction.

## 6. Conversion
Convert (or add a hand port) when:
- Anatomy can't be defined (dense adhesions, inflammation — e.g. no critical view of
  safety in cholecystectomy).
- Bleeding can't be controlled laparoscopically — **apply pressure first**, call for help
  and blood, then open.
- No progress over an agreed time, or the patient can't tolerate pneumoperitoneum.
Conversion is a safety decision; a late, forced conversion after injury carries much
worse outcomes than an early, elective one. Bail-out alternatives (subtotal
procedure, drain) are also options.

## Gotchas
- Thermal injury from monopolar instruments out of view (capacitive coupling, insulation
  failure) — keep active instruments in view; inspect insulation.
- Unrecognised bowel injury presents late (day 2–4) with sepsis.
- Shoulder-tip pain after surgery is residual CO2; desufflate fully at the end.

## Verify
- [ ] Working pressure set and view troubleshooting done systematically.
- [ ] Ports triangulated (~60–90°), at a sensible distance, placed under vision.
- [ ] Camera horizon level; 30° scope used deliberately.
- [ ] Fascia closed at ≥ 10 mm ports.
- [ ] Conversion criteria considered and documented when encountered.
