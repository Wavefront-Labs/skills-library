# Transtibial socket fitting and alignment troubleshooting

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for clinical assessment by a qualified prosthetist/orthotist or component manufacturer instructions. Alignment numbers are starting points, not rules.

**Goal:** a socket that loads pressure-tolerant tissue, spares sensitive areas, stays
on, and is aligned so the person walks efficiently. Fit problems are the main reason
people abandon prostheses.

## 1. Assessment
- Limb: length, shape (conical/cylindrical/bulbous), scar adhesions, skin condition,
  bony prominences, volume stability, sensation, contractures (knee flexion contracture
  limits alignment).
- Person: goals, activity level (K-level classification for funding in some systems),
  vascular status and diabetes, hand function (donning liners, pin locks), cognition.

## 2. Capturing shape
- **Plaster casting** (hands-on, applies modification while setting) or **3D scanning**
  with digital rectification. Either way, mark tolerant areas (patellar tendon,
  medial flare of tibia, popliteal area, gastrocnemius) and sensitive ones (tibial crest,
  distal tibia, fibular head, peroneal nerve).
- Design choice: **PTB** (patellar-tendon-bearing, selective loading) vs **TSB** (total
  surface bearing with gel liner). Suspension: pin-lock, suction/expulsion valve with
  sealing liner, elevated vacuum, sleeve, supracondylar.

## 3. Check socket
Clear thermoplastic test socket lets you see blanching:
- Donned with the liner/socks intended; standing weight-bearing.
- Look and ask: distal end — **light contact**, not pistoning or heavy load; no
  redness lasting > ~20 min after removal; no pain over fibular head or crest.
- Pistoning > ~1 cm → under-filled socket or poor suspension.
Modify, re-check; only then laminate or print the definitive socket.

## 4. Alignment
1. **Bench alignment:** socket set in slight flexion (~5° plus any contracture),
   slight adduction; foot with toe-out ~5–7°; plumb line from socket centre near the
   heel third of the foot per manufacturer guidance.
2. **Static:** standing with even weight, correct height (pelvis level), pylon vertical
   in both planes, comfortable knee position.
3. **Dynamic:** walk on parallel bars then open floor; observe from front and side.
| Deviation | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Knee pushed back (hyperextension) early stance | Foot too anterior, too plantarflexed, or heel too firm |
| Knee buckles / excessive flexion | Foot too posterior or dorsiflexed, heel too soft |
| Lateral thrust of socket in stance | Foot too inset (medial) |
| Medial thrust | Foot too outset |
| Short step, vaulting | Prosthesis too long, poor suspension |
Adjust one variable at a time and re-walk.

## 5. Volume management
Residual limbs shrink, especially in the first 6–18 months. Teach **sock-ply management**
(add ply when the limb sinks or the socket feels loose; remove when tight), daily skin
checks with a mirror, and when to come back (wounds, persistent redness, pain).

## 6. Orthotic fit (AFO example)
Trimlines matched to control needed (posterior leaf spring vs solid AFO), malleoli and
navicular relieved, strap position over the ankle crease, footwear with room. Check
gait and skin after use.

## Gotchas
- Fixing a fit problem with alignment (or vice versa) — sort fit first.
- Ignoring the contralateral limb in diabetic users; overload risk is real.
- New liner thickness changes socket fit as much as volume change.

## Verify
- [ ] Sensitive and tolerant areas mapped and addressed in the design.
- [ ] Check socket: no persistent redness, controlled pistoning.
- [ ] Bench, static and dynamic alignment documented with final settings.
- [ ] User can don/doff, manage socks and do skin checks independently.
- [ ] Follow-up booked for volume change.
