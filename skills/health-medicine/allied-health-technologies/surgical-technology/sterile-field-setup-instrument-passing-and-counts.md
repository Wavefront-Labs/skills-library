# Sterile field setup, instrument passing and counts

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for facility policy, surgeon preference cards or manufacturer instructions for use.

**Goal:** keep the field sterile, hand the surgeon the right thing before they ask, and
make sure nothing is left inside the patient.

## 1. Before opening
- Room cleaned, equipment checked, **preference card** reviewed, implants/specials
  confirmed available.
- Inspect every sterile package: integrity, dryness, expiry/event-related sterility,
  **external chemical indicator** changed. Inside trays, check the **internal indicator**
  before using contents. Any doubt → item is not sterile.
- Open as close to the case start as practical; sterile fields are not left unattended.

## 2. Scrub, gown, glove
Surgical hand antisepsis per facility protocol (timed brush-less rub or scrub), gown
and **closed gloving**; double gloving reduces perforation exposure. Sterile areas of a
gown: front from chest to table level and sleeves to 5 cm above the elbow. Hands stay
above waist, below shoulders, in sight.

## 3. Back table and Mayo
- Arrange back table in a consistent layout (by procedure stage) so anyone can relieve you.
- Mayo stand holds instruments for the immediate phase: knife, scissors, forceps,
  haemostats, retractors, suture — refresh as the case progresses.
- Medications and solutions on the field: **label every container and syringe**
  immediately (name, strength), confirm verbally with the circulator when received.
- Load blades and needles with instruments, never fingers.

## 4. Instrument passing
- Anticipate: follow the procedure steps and the surgeon's hands; have the next
  instrument and suture ready.
- Pass firmly into the palm in the position of use (ring-handled instruments with the
  curve matching the surgeon's hand), so they don't need to look away.
- **Neutral zone** for sharps (magnetic mat/tray): place and pick up, never hand-to-hand.
  Announce "sharp" when moving one.
- Keep instruments clean of blood/tissue during the case (sterile water, not saline,
  for wiping — saline corrodes) to aid function and reprocessing.

## 5. Counts
- **Initial count** before incision with the circulator: sponges, sharps, instruments,
  miscellaneous items — counted aloud, visually separated (sponges counted individually,
  in standard bundle numbers), recorded on the count board.
- Add items to the count as they're opened; nothing leaves the room during the case.
- **Closing counts:** before closure of a cavity within a cavity, before wound closure,
  at skin closure, and at permanent change of scrub or circulator.
- Discrepancy → stop, tell the surgeon, search field, floor, linen and waste; if not
  found, **intraoperative imaging** per policy before the patient leaves. Document.
- Radiopaque sponges only in the wound; some facilities add RF/barcode sponge systems.

## 6. Specimens
Pass specimens in correct container, confirm name, site/side with the surgeon,
handle per instructions (fresh, formalin, frozen section), hand off to circulator with
labelling verified immediately — never at the end of the case.

## Gotchas
- Unlabelled syringes on the field cause wrong-drug events.
- Sponge "towels" or non-counted items used inside the wound.
- Rushing the final count at handover or when the surgeon is closing.

## Verify
- [ ] Indicators and packaging checked; field never left unattended.
- [ ] Every medication on the field labelled and verbally confirmed.
- [ ] Sharps via neutral zone; blade/needle loaded with instruments.
- [ ] Initial, interim and closing counts correct and documented; discrepancies resolved.
- [ ] Specimens labelled and handed off with verbal confirmation.
