# Oxygen titration, ventilator checks and weaning trials

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Targets and settings must follow the prescriber's orders and local protocols.

**Goal:** give enough oxygen and ventilatory support to be safe, no more than needed,
and get patients off support as soon as they can manage.

## 1. Oxygen therapy
- Oxygen is a drug: prescribe a **target range**. Common targets: SpO2 **94–98%** for
  most acutely ill adults (some guidelines accept 92–96%), **88–92%** for patients at
  risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure (COPD, obesity hypoventilation, neuromuscular,
  chest wall disease) until blood gases guide otherwise.
- Devices by need: nasal cannula (1–4 L/min), simple face mask (5–10 L/min, avoid
  < 5 L/min — CO2 rebreathing), **Venturi** (fixed FiO2, best for CO2 retainers),
  non-rebreather (15 L/min, short-term), **high-flow nasal cannula** (up to ~60 L/min,
  humidified, set FiO2).
- Titrate down as well as up. Rising O2 need is a warning sign (escalate), not just a
  number to chase.
- SpO2 limitations: poor perfusion, motion, carbon monoxide (falsely normal), and
  **over-estimation in darker skin** — confirm with arterial gas when it matters.

## 2. Ventilator check (each round and after any change)
1. Patient first: chest rise, synchrony, work of breathing, SpO2, haemodynamics.
2. Verify settings match orders: mode, VT, rate, PEEP, FiO2, pressure/flow settings, alarms set.
3. Measure **plateau pressure** (inspiratory hold) — keep ≤ 30 cmH2O; **driving
   pressure** (plateau − PEEP) ideally ≤ ~15.
4. **Auto-PEEP** (expiratory hold) in obstructive patients; fix with longer
   expiratory time (lower rate, shorter Ti) rather than more PEEP blindly.
5. Lung-protective VT 6 (4–8) mL/kg **predicted** body weight (from height, not actual
   weight).
6. Circuit: humidification, water traps, ETT position (cm at lips/teeth), cuff
   pressure ~20–30 cmH2O, head of bed elevated.

## 3. Sudden deterioration: DOPES
**D**isplacement of tube, **O**bstruction (secretions, kinks, biting), **P**neumothorax,
**E**quipment failure, **S**tacking (auto-PEEP). Disconnect and hand-ventilate with
100% O2 while assessing if the cause isn't obvious.

## 4. Suctioning
Only when indicated (audible secretions, rising pressures, sawtooth flow trace,
desaturation). Pre-oxygenate, catheter ≤ 50% of ETT internal diameter (≤ 70% in
some guidance), ≤ 15 s, limit negative pressure (~-100 to -150 mmHg adults). Avoid
routine saline instillation.

## 5. Weaning: daily screen and SBT
- Screen daily: cause improving, FiO2 ≤ ~0.4–0.5 with PEEP ≤ 5–8, haemodynamically stable,
  able to initiate breaths, minimal sedation (paired with sedation interruption).
- **SBT:** 30–120 min on low pressure support (e.g. 5–8 cmH2O, PEEP 5) or T-piece.
- Failure signs: RR > 35, SpO2 < 90%, HR or BP change > ~20%, distress, diaphoresis,
  arrhythmia. RSBI (f/VT) < 105 supports success but is an adjunct.
- Before extubation: cough strength, secretion volume, mental status, cuff-leak in
  high-risk patients. Consider extubation to NIV or HFNC in high-risk patients.

## Gotchas
- High FiO2 hides deterioration in hypoventilating patients — check PaCO2.
- Setting VT on actual body weight in obese patients over-distends lungs.
- Alarms silenced and not re-enabled after procedures.

## Verify
- [ ] Oxygen target range documented and device matches need.
- [ ] Vent settings vs order, plateau/driving pressure and auto-PEEP recorded.
- [ ] ETT position, cuff pressure and alarms checked.
- [ ] Daily SBT screen documented with outcome and failure reason.
