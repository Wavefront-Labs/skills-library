# ECG, Holter and EEG acquisition quality

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for departmental procedures or the interpreting clinician. Escalation criteria for acute findings are set locally.

**Goal:** a clean, correctly labelled recording the first time — misplaced electrodes and
noise create false diagnoses that follow patients for years.

## 1. 12-lead ECG
**Placement (standard)**
- V1: 4th intercostal space (ICS), right sternal edge. V2: 4th ICS, left sternal edge.
- V4: 5th ICS, mid-clavicular line. V3: midway V2–V4.
- V5: anterior axillary line, horizontal with V4. V6: mid-axillary line, same level.
- Count interspaces from the sternal angle (2nd rib) — "eyeballing" puts V1/V2 too high,
  mimicking anterior infarct or Brugada-like patterns.
- Breast tissue: place V4–V6 **under** the breast at the correct anatomical position.
- Limb electrodes on limbs (wrists/ankles or proximal limbs); torso placement (Mason-
  Likar) changes the axis and must be labelled.

**Quality**
- Skin prep (clean, dry, shave if needed, light abrasion), fresh electrodes.
- Patient supine, relaxed, warm, not talking; standard 25 mm/s, 10 mm/mV, filters per
  departmental settings (aggressive low-pass filters distort ST segments).
- Artifact fixes: tremor → support limbs, warm patient; AC interference → unplug nearby
  devices, check leads; wandering baseline → better contact, slow breathing.
- **Lead reversal check:** lead I negative with positive aVR (unexpected) suggests
  arm reversal; compare with prior ECGs.
- Label name, date/time, symptoms at time of recording. Escalate STEMI-pattern, very
  fast/slow rhythms or symptoms immediately per local pathway.

## 2. Holter / ambulatory monitoring
- Choose duration by symptom frequency: 24–48 h for daily symptoms; 7–14 day patches
  or event/loop recorders for less frequent ones.
- Prep skin, place electrodes over bone not muscle, loop and tape cables to reduce
  pull artifact; check the signal on the recorder before the patient leaves.
- Teach the **diary/event button**: time, activity, symptom. A recording without
  symptom correlation answers half the question.
- Advise on bathing restrictions and return time.

## 3. EEG
- **Measure** the head and mark positions with the **10-20 system** (nasion–inion and
  preauricular measurements; 10%/20% intervals) — don't estimate.
- Prep and apply electrodes; impedance < 5–10 kΩ and **balanced** between electrodes.
- Add ECG channel; consider eye and EMG leads per protocol.
- Record with eyes open/closed, document patient state (awake, drowsy, asleep).
- **Activation procedures** per protocol: hyperventilation (3–5 min; contraindicated in
  e.g. recent stroke, significant cardiac or respiratory disease, sickle cell, pregnancy
  — check policy) and photic stimulation.
- Annotate events, behaviours, artifacts (movement, chewing, electrode pop) live.
- Seizure during recording: ensure safety, call for help, observe and annotate, test
  responsiveness per protocol.

## 4. Cath-lab technical support
Zero and level pressure transducers at the phlebostatic axis, record haemodynamic
pressures and calculations, monitor ECG and alert the team to arrhythmia or ST changes,
log times and contrast/radiation figures.

## Gotchas
- V1/V2 placed in the 2nd–3rd ICS: commonest cause of spurious anterior changes.
- Electrode gel bridging on EEG produces falsely flat/identical channels.
- Holter patients told "act normally" but not to push the button → no correlation.

## Verify
- [ ] Electrodes placed by landmark; limb configuration labelled.
- [ ] Recording free of correctable artifact; lead-reversal check done.
- [ ] Holter signal checked and diary taught.
- [ ] EEG 10-20 measured, impedances balanced, activation procedures documented.
- [ ] Urgent findings escalated per pathway.
