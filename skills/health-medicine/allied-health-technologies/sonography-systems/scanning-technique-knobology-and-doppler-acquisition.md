# Scanning technique, knobology and Doppler acquisition

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for departmental protocols, vendor manuals or the interpreting physician. What a sonographer may communicate to patients is set by local policy.

**Goal:** a complete, reproducible study whose images prove both the findings and the
normal structures — an incomplete protocol is the commonest cause of callbacks.

## 1. Set-up
- Confirm patient ID, exam, clinical question, prep (fasting 6–8 h for gallbladder,
  full bladder for some pelvic work), and prior studies to compare.
- Select the **preset** for the exam; enter patient data so measurements and
  calculations populate correctly (e.g. LMP/dates for obstetrics).
- Transducer: curvilinear (abdomen/OB, 2–5 MHz), linear (vascular, MSK, thyroid,
  superficial, 5–15+ MHz), phased array (cardiac, intercostal windows), endocavitary.
  Higher frequency = better resolution, less penetration.
- Ergonomics: adjust bed/chair height, support the scanning arm — work-related
  musculoskeletal injury is endemic in sonography.

## 2. B-mode knobology
1. **Depth:** structure of interest fills ~2/3 of the screen, with a margin beyond it.
2. **Overall gain** then **TGC** for uniform brightness from near to far field (fluid
   should look black, liver uniformly grey).
3. **Focal zone** at or just below the area of interest; fewer zones = higher frame rate.
4. Harmonics and compound imaging reduce clutter but can blur fine detail or erase
   useful artefacts (shadowing behind stones).
5. Zoom/write-zoom for small structures; freeze, label and measure in standard planes.
Image in two orthogonal planes; label every image (organ, side, plane).

## 3. Doppler acquisition
- **Colour box:** small as practical (frame rate), steered toward the flow, scale (PRF)
  set to expected velocities — low for venous/low flow, higher for arteries.
- **Aliasing** (colour wrap / spectral wrap-around): raise PRF/scale, move baseline,
  lower transducer frequency, or use a shallower window.
- **Spectral Doppler:** sample gate inside the vessel (small for velocities, larger to
  detect flow), **angle ≤ 60°**, angle-correct cursor parallel to the vessel wall/jet.
  Velocity error rises steeply above 60°.
- Wall filter low for slow flow; too high hides diastolic flow.
- Missing flow? Check PRF, gain, filter, angle and the box before calling occlusion.

## 4. Protocol completion
Follow the department protocol image list; capture required measurements (e.g. CBD,
spleen length, kidney lengths, CRL/biometry, ICA/CCA velocities). Add cine clips for
dynamic findings. Document limitations (habitus, bowel gas, patient mobility).

## 5. Preliminary worksheet
Record measurements, findings, technical limitations and comparison. Escalate **critical
findings** (e.g. ectopic pregnancy signs, aortic rupture, testicular/ovarian torsion
concern, DVT per policy) immediately per the escalation pathway — not just on the worksheet.

## Gotchas
- Oblique measurements overstate size — measure perpendicular to the long axis.
- Mirror and reverberation artefacts mimic structures; confirm in a second plane.
- Obstetric Doppler and long dwell time increase thermal index; keep TI low (ALARA),
  especially in the first trimester.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct preset, transducer and patient data entered.
- [ ] Depth, gain/TGC and focus optimised; images labelled in two planes.
- [ ] Doppler angle ≤ 60° and corrected; no uncorrected aliasing.
- [ ] Protocol image list complete; limitations documented.
- [ ] Critical findings escalated and logged.
