# Cardiopulmonary bypass conduct and circuit emergencies

> General clinical/professional reference for trained perfusion staff, not a substitute for institutional protocols, device IFUs or the surgical and anaesthetic team's decisions. Numeric targets vary between centres.

**Goal:** maintain adequate oxygen delivery and a still, protected heart for surgery,
then hand back a beating heart — with zero gas or circuit catastrophes.

## 1. Before bypass
- Written **pre-bypass checklist** every case (AmSECT-style): correct circuit and
  oxygenator, connections secure, gas supply and blender, vaporiser (if used) off/on as
  intended, heater-cooler running, **level and bubble detectors armed**, pressure
  monitors zeroed, arterial filter de-aired, emergency hand crank present, backup
  oxygen cylinder, cardioplegia system primed, alarms on.
- Prime: crystalloid/colloid volume calculated for **haemodilution** (estimate on-pump
  haematocrit; keep it above the centre's transfusion trigger).
- Calculate target flow: commonly cardiac index 2.2–2.5 L/min/m² at normothermia.

## 2. Anticoagulation
- Heparin (commonly 300–400 U/kg) before cannulation; **ACT > ~400–480 s** before going
  on bypass (thresholds vary). Check ACT every 20–30 min on bypass.
- Heparin resistance: consider antithrombin deficiency (antithrombin concentrate or
  FFP) after additional heparin.
- HIT history: alternative anticoagulation (e.g. bivalirudin) — changes ACT targets and
  stasis management.

## 3. On bypass
- **Flow and pressure:** maintain flow to target; MAP commonly 50–80 mmHg depending
  on patient (higher for carotid disease, chronic hypertension).
- **Goal-directed perfusion:** monitor oxygen delivery index (**DO2i ≥ ~270–280
  mL/min/m²** in several studies), venous saturation (SvO2 > 65–70%), lactate, NIRS.
- Blood gases every ~30 min (alpha-stat is common in adults; pH-stat in some
  paediatric/deep hypothermic cases); glucose, potassium, haematocrit.
- Temperature: avoid arterial outlet > 37 °C; limit gradients between water and
  blood during cooling/rewarming (≤ ~10 °C) to prevent bubble formation.
- Urine output, venous reservoir level — **never run the reservoir low**.

## 4. Myocardial protection
- Cross-clamp on → cardioplegia (antegrade aortic root, coronary ostia, or
  retrograde via coronary sinus). Watch delivery pressure (root ~60–100 mmHg; coronary
  sinus < ~40 mmHg) and arrest time.
- Redose interval per solution (e.g. blood cardioplegia ~every 15–20 min; del Nido
  single dose often ~60–90 min). Track total crystalloid load.

## 5. Weaning
Rewarm to target, correct K+, Ca2+, Hb, acid–base; ventilate lungs; de-air; reduce venous
drainage gradually while the heart fills; communicate each flow step aloud. After
separation, protamine (with team announcement — **stop pump suckers** to avoid clotting
the circuit) and keep the circuit ready for return to bypass.

## 6. Emergencies (rehearse)
- **Massive arterial air:** stop pump, clamp arterial line, alert surgeon, Trendelenburg,
  de-air circuit, retrograde cerebral perfusion via SVC per protocol, resume, consider
  hypothermia/steroids per protocol.
- **Oxygenator failure** (falling PaO2 with adequate FiO2/sweep, rising pressure drop):
  check gas supply first; change-out procedure with clamps and pre-primed spare.
- **Pump/power failure:** hand crank, battery backup.
- **Reversed cannulation, aortic dissection, high line pressure:** stop and tell the surgeon.

## Gotchas
- Sweep gas disconnected or blender at 21% — always confirm gas flow on initiation.
- Vasodilated patients on pump: chasing MAP with flow alone can over-dilute.
- Communication is closed-loop; ambiguous orders cause most near misses.

## Verify
- [ ] Checklist completed and signed; safety devices armed.
- [ ] ACT above threshold before initiation; monitored throughout.
- [ ] Flow, MAP, DO2i, SvO2, lactate and temperatures logged at set intervals.
- [ ] Cardioplegia doses and times recorded.
- [ ] Emergency drills current; spare oxygenator and crank on hand.
