# Inhaler technique teaching and counselling

> General professional reference. Treatment regimens (e.g. MART dosing, step changes) must follow current GINA/GOLD and local guidance; check the specific device's patient leaflet for its exact steps.

**Goal:** most people with asthma or COPD use their inhaler incorrectly, and poor
technique looks exactly like "the drug isn't working". Watching a patient use their
device and fixing the one critical error is often worth more than a step-up in therapy.

## 1. Open with "show me", not "tell me"
Ask the patient to demonstrate with their own device (or a placebo trainer). Watch
silently, then correct **one or two critical errors** — not a 12-step lecture. Ask them to
demonstrate again (**teach-back**). Recheck at every opportunity; technique decays within weeks.

## 2. Device-specific critical steps
**Pressurised MDI (pMDI)**
- Shake (most suspensions), remove cap, prime if new/unused (see leaflet).
- Breathe out gently away from the device.
- Seal lips, **start a slow, deep breath, then press** once — slow and steady over ~3–5 s.
- Hold breath ~10 s (or as long as comfortable). Wait ~30–60 s before a second puff.
- **Commonest errors:** fast inhalation, poor coordination (firing before or after inhaling), multiple puffs at once.
- **Spacer/valved holding chamber:** one puff into the spacer, then slow deep breath or
  4–5 tidal breaths; a mask for young children. Clean per instructions; air-dry (static).
  Recommend for anyone with coordination problems, children, and all ICS by pMDI where practical.

**Dry powder inhaler (DPI)**
- Load the dose with the device held as instructed (orientation matters for some).
- Breathe out **away** from the mouthpiece (humidity clumps the powder).
- **Quick, forceful, deep inhalation** from the start — the opposite of a pMDI.
- Commonest error: slow inhalation; also exhaling into the device.
- Requires adequate inspiratory flow — frail elderly, severe COPD exacerbation and young
  children may not generate it (an In-Check-type device can measure).

**Soft-mist inhaler (e.g. Respimat):** turn, open, slow deep breath while pressing — slow like a pMDI; cartridge assembly and priming confuse patients.

## 3. Counselling content beyond technique
- **What each inhaler is for:** reliever vs preventer vs combination/MART. A patient
  who uses only the blue reliever and "saves" the brown one for bad days is a safety
  issue — under-use of ICS and **over-use of SABA (≥3 canisters/year) predicts severe attacks**.
- ICS: rinse mouth/gargle after use (oral thrush, dysphonia).
- Dose counter: how to read it, when to reorder; empty canisters still "puff" propellant.
- Personal asthma action plan: what to do as symptoms worsen, when to seek urgent care.
- Adherence: ask non-judgementally ("Many people miss doses — how often does that happen for you?").
- Minimise device types per patient — one technique (all pMDI or all DPI) where possible.

## Red flags / refer
Using reliever daily or waking at night, recent oral steroids or ED visit, SABA over-use,
breathlessness at rest, or inability to use any device effectively → prescriber review promptly.

## Gotchas
- Switching device brands at dispensing without retraining causes errors.
- Children commonly need age-appropriate spacer + mask until ~4–5 years.
- Environmental switch (pMDI → DPI) is reasonable only if the patient can inhale fast enough.

## Verify
- [ ] Patient demonstrated technique; critical error(s) identified and corrected.
- [ ] Teach-back demonstration correct before leaving.
- [ ] Patient can state which inhaler is reliever and which preventer and when to use each.
- [ ] Spacer supplied where indicated; mouth rinse after ICS explained.
- [ ] SABA over-use or poor control flagged to the prescriber.
