# Aseptic technique and USP <797> cleanroom practice

> General professional reference summarising US standards as recalled; figures (BUD days, test frequencies) must be checked against the current USP <797>/<800> text and your state board rules. Outside the US, use the equivalent (e.g. EU GMP Annex 1, PIC/S PE 010).

**Goal:** a compounded sterile preparation (CSP) goes straight into a vein; there is no
second chance to remove contamination. Sterility comes from **environment + technique +
verification**, and technique is the one you control at every manipulation.

## 1. Know the category before you start
- **Category 1:** compounded in a segregated compounding area (SCA) with an ISO 5 PEC;
  short BUD (≈12 h room temp / 24 h refrigerated).
- **Category 2:** compounded in an ISO 5 PEC inside a full cleanroom suite (ISO 7 buffer,
  ISO 8 ante-room or ISO 7 if it opens to a negative-pressure HD room); longer BUDs per the
  USP table, depending on starting components (sterile vs non-sterile), sterility testing and storage.
- **Category 3:** extended BUDs; requires additional environmental monitoring, sterility testing and personnel qualification.
- Immediate-use CSPs (outside these rules) are limited — e.g. administration starting within ~4 h, simple, few components.

## 2. Garb in the right order (dirtiest to cleanest)
Remove jewellery, cosmetics, outer garments → shoe covers → hair/beard covers → face mask
→ **hand hygiene** (nails cleaned, wash hands and forearms ≥30 s) → non-shedding gown →
alcohol-based hand rub → **sterile gloves**; sanitise gloves with **sterile 70% IPA**
frequently and every time you re-enter the PEC.

## 3. Work in the hood — protect first air
- **First air** = HEPA-filtered air before it touches anything. Nothing (hands, vials,
  wrappers) may sit between the filter and a critical site (needle hub, syringe tip, vial septum, port).
- Horizontal laminar flow workbench: work ≥6 inches (15 cm) inside, no items blocking air behind.
  Vertical (BSC/CACI): don't block front/rear grilles.
- Disinfect PEC surfaces with sterile 70% IPA before and during sessions; daily cleaning,
  disinfecting (and sporicidal at the stated frequency) per the SOP.
- Wipe every vial septum and ampoule neck with sterile 70% IPA and let it dry.
- Vials: pressure balance (inject equal air or use a vented spike); bevel up, insert at
  45° then upright to avoid coring. Ampoules: filter needle or straw to withdraw glass.
- Never touch the plunger shaft or syringe tip; recap only with a one-handed/aseptic method.
- Label immediately; verify drug, dose, diluent, volume before release (gravimetric or
  barcode workflow preferred over visual "syringe pull-back" checks).

## 4. Competency and environment proof
- Initial qualification: **gloved fingertip and thumb sampling ×3** after garbing (zero CFU),
  plus a **media-fill test**; then repeat at the USP frequencies (roughly every 6 months for
  Category 1/2, more often for Category 3 — verify).
- Environmental monitoring: viable air and surface sampling, pressure differentials
  logged, certification of PECs and rooms at least every 6 months.

## 5. Hazardous drugs (USP <800>) additions
Chemotherapy in a **containment** PEC (Class II BSC or CACI) in a negative-pressure
room; two pairs of chemo-tested gloves and impermeable gown; closed-system transfer
devices (CSTDs) recommended; spill kit and deactivation/decontamination/cleaning steps.

## Gotchas
- Sanitising gloves with IPA does not make them sterile — just keeps them low-bioburden.
- A "fast" compounder who blocks first air is a hazard; speed is not competence.
- Talking, sneezing, or stepping out of the PEC without re-sanitising breaks the chain.
- BUD is not the same as manufacturer stability; take the more conservative.
- Out-of-range pressure or failed EM = stop, investigate, document; don't compound "just this one".

## Verify
- [ ] CSP category and BUD assigned from the current USP table and SOP.
- [ ] Garbing order followed; gloves sanitised on every PEC re-entry.
- [ ] No obstruction of first air over any critical site during the whole preparation.
- [ ] Final check of drug, dose, diluent, volume and label completed by a second person/system.
- [ ] Personnel GFT/media-fill and room certification current.
