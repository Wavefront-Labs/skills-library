# Oral suspension compounding and beyond-use dating

> General professional reference. Formulas, concentrations and BUDs must come from a validated, referenced source and your SOPs; the default BUDs below are USP <795> values as recalled — verify against the current chapter. Pediatric doses for any compounded liquid must be checked against a current pediatric formulary.

**Goal:** turn a solid dosage form into a uniform, stable, accurately concentrated liquid
— typically for a child or a patient who can't swallow — that delivers the labelled dose
in every 5 mL until its beyond-use date.

## 1. Before compounding: is it necessary, and is there a validated formula?
- Check for a **commercial liquid** first (or an alternative drug that has one); compounding
  a copy of an available product is restricted in many jurisdictions.
- Use a **published, stability-tested formula** for that drug, concentration and vehicle.
  Changing vehicle or concentration invalidates the stability data.
- Pick a **standard concentration** (institution or regional standard) to reduce
  concentration-mix-up errors between hospital and community.
- Prepare/approve a **Master Formulation Record (MFR)**: ingredients, quantities,
  equipment, method, container, BUD with reference, labelling, QC checks.

## 2. Calculate
- Total drug needed = concentration × final volume (e.g. 2 mg/mL × 100 mL = 200 mg).
- Tablets needed = 200 mg ÷ tablet strength; round to whole tablets and adjust final volume
  to match, rather than splitting tablets inaccurately.
- Independent second check of every calculation; mg vs mcg and salt vs base forms
  (e.g. hydrochloride salt) are classic error points.

## 3. Compound (typical tablet-to-suspension method)
1. Hand hygiene, clean area, PPE; hazardous drugs need USP <800> containment.
2. Count tablets; remove film coating effect by thorough **trituration** to a fine,
   uniform powder in a mortar (glass for staining/oily drugs, Wedgwood/porcelain for trituration).
3. **Levigate**: add a small amount of vehicle/wetting agent and work to a smooth,
   lump-free paste — this is where uniformity is made or lost.
4. **Geometric dilution**: add vehicle in portions roughly equal to the volume in the mortar, mixing each time.
5. Transfer to a **calibrated** container/graduate; rinse mortar with vehicle and add rinses.
6. **q.s.** to final volume with vehicle; mix thoroughly; transfer to an amber, tight container.
7. Label: name, concentration (mg/mL), BUD, storage, "**Shake well**", lot/compounding
   record number; provide oral syringes.

## 4. Assign the BUD (USP <795> defaults if no specific data)
| Preparation | Default BUD (verify) |
|---|---|
| Aqueous (water activity ≥0.6), **no preservative** | 14 days, refrigerated |
| Aqueous, **preserved** | 35 days |
| Nonaqueous liquids (aw <0.6) | 90 days |
| Solid dosage forms (capsules, powders) | 180 days |
A longer BUD requires drug-specific stability data for that exact formula, within chapter limits.
BUD may never exceed the earliest expiry of any ingredient.

## 5. Document and check
Compounding record: MFR reference, lot numbers/expiry of every ingredient, quantities
weighed/measured, who compounded and checked, QC (appearance, pH if specified, final
volume), BUD. Counsel caregivers: shake, measure with oral syringe, storage, discard date.

## Gotchas
- Settling and caking → dose variability; a suspension that won't resuspend with gentle shaking is unusable.
- Extended-release or enteric-coated tablets generally **must not be crushed** for a liquid.
- Some vehicles contain sugar, alcohol, or excipients unsuitable for neonates (e.g. propylene glycol, benzyl alcohol) — check.
- Water for compounding should be purified water, not tap water.

## Verify
- [ ] Commercial alternative excluded; validated formula and standard concentration used.
- [ ] Calculations independently double-checked, salt/base accounted for.
- [ ] Uniform suspension produced (no lumps, resuspends readily); final volume accurate.
- [ ] BUD assigned from data or <795> defaults, not beyond any ingredient expiry.
- [ ] Compounding record complete; label says "shake well" with concentration in mg/mL.
