# Tonsillectomy, grommets and paediatric stridor

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or local protocols. Not patient-specific advice; paediatric drug doses must come from a verified paediatric formulary.

**Goal:** operate on children only when the benefit is clear, run safe post-op care
(above all for bleeding), and tell the stridor that can wait from the airway that can't.

## 1. Tonsillectomy indications
- **Recurrent tonsillitis:** Paradise criteria: ≥7 documented episodes in 1 year, ≥5/yr
  for 2 years, or ≥3/yr for 3 years. Each episode should have documented features
  (fever, exudate, cervical nodes or positive strep test). Below that, **watchful waiting**.
- **Obstructive sleep-disordered breathing** with tonsillar hypertrophy is now the
  commonest indication. Ask about snoring, witnessed apnoeas, restless sleep, daytime
  behaviour/attention, enuresis and growth.
- **Polysomnography before surgery** if: age < 2, obesity, Down syndrome, craniofacial
  anomaly, neuromuscular disease, sickle cell, mucopolysaccharidosis, or the history and
  exam don't agree. (Who gets PSG varies a lot with local resources.)
- Other: quinsy (peritonsillar abscess) recurrence, PFAPA, suspicious asymmetry (lymphoma).

## 2. Peri-operative safety
- High-risk OSA children (severe AHI, age < 3, comorbidity) → **overnight monitored stay**.
- Analgesia: regular paracetamol + ibuprofen (ibuprofen does not meaningfully increase
  bleeding in current evidence). **Codeine is contraindicated** in children after
  tonsillectomy (ultra-rapid metaboliser deaths). A single intra-op dexamethasone dose
  is common.
- **Post-tonsillectomy haemorrhage:** primary (< 24 h) or secondary (typically day 5–10).
  Any bleed → assess in hospital. Children swallow blood, so **tachycardia comes before
  hypotension**, and haematemesis may be the first sign. Resuscitate, give IV access and
  tranexamic acid per protocol, keep the child sitting forward, and **return to theatre** if
  the bleeding persists. The anaesthetist should expect a full stomach.

## 3. Grommets (ventilation tubes)
- **Otitis media with effusion (glue ear):** watchful waiting for **3 months** with
  audiometry. Offer grommets if it is bilateral and persists with hearing loss
  (commonly ~25–30 dB HL in the better ear) or affects speech, learning or behaviour.
  Hearing aids are an alternative. **Down syndrome and cleft palate** have a lower threshold
  for hearing aids and specialist pathways.
- **Recurrent acute otitis media:** consider tubes for ≥3 episodes in 6 months or ≥4 in a
  year with **effusion present at assessment**. Don't place them if the ears are clear
  between episodes.
- Adenoidectomy with tubes: mainly for repeat tube insertion or nasal obstruction (age-dependent evidence).
- After tubes: treat otorrhoea with **topical antibiotic drops** (non-ototoxic
  quinolone), not oral antibiotics first. No routine water precautions needed.

## 4. Stridor triage
| Feature | Think |
|---|---|
| Inspiratory, from first weeks, worse feeding/supine, thriving | Laryngomalacia (most self-resolve) |
| Barking cough, acute, 6 mo–3 yr | Croup: steroids, nebulised adrenaline if severe |
| Sudden choking episode, unilateral wheeze | Foreign body → rigid bronchoscopy |
| Biphasic, post-intubation | Subglottic stenosis |
| Drooling, toxic, tripod | Epiglottitis/bacterial tracheitis → **airway team, don't examine the throat** |

Laryngomalacia red flags for supraglottoplasty: failure to thrive, apnoeas, cyanosis,
feeding aspiration.

## 5. Congenital neck lumps
- **Thyroglossal duct cyst:** midline, moves on tongue protrusion → ultrasound to confirm a
  normal thyroid → **Sistrunk** (includes central hyoid) to reduce recurrence.
- **Branchial anomalies:** second arch commonest (anterior SCM border). Excise the full
  tract once infection has settled. A neck lump in a teenager still needs malignancy
  considered (lymphoma).

## Gotchas
- Calling a normal chest X-ray proof of no foreign body. Most inhaled foreign bodies
  don't show on X-ray.
- Discharging a child with a "small" post-tonsillectomy bleed. A small bleed can come
  before a large one (sentinel bleed).
- Placing tubes for OME without a documented 3-month observation and audiogram.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication documented against Paradise or OSA criteria; PSG decision recorded.
- [ ] Post-op plan names analgesia (no codeine), overnight stay criteria, and bleed advice.
- [ ] OME has 3-month watchful waiting + age-appropriate audiometry before tubes.
- [ ] Stridor classified (phase, onset, red flags) with escalation route.
- [ ] Thyroid confirmed on imaging before thyroglossal cyst excision.
