# Facial fractures, rhinoplasty and local flaps

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or local protocols. Not patient-specific advice.

**Goal:** catch the urgent findings in facial trauma, reduce nasal fractures at the right
time, assess a nasal airway before calling it "cosmetic", and reconstruct facial defects
with tissue that matches the subunit.

## 1. Nasal fractures
- **Always look inside the nose:** a **septal haematoma** (boggy, fluctuant septum,
  bilateral) needs drainage the **same day**. If it isn't drained, the cartilage necroses
  (saddle nose) or an abscess forms.
- Swelling hides the deformity, so review at **~5–7 days**. Closed manipulation works best
  within **~2–3 weeks** in adults and sooner in children (they heal faster). After that,
  bones set and correction needs formal septorhinoplasty at ~6 months or more.
- X-rays don't change management for an isolated nasal fracture. Image only if another
  facial fracture is suspected.
- CSF rhinorrhoea (clear, salty, unilateral, positional) → test fluid for **β2-transferrin**.

## 2. Other facial fractures: red flags
- **Orbit:** reduced acuity, relative afferent pupillary defect, proptosis with tense orbit
  → **retrobulbar haemorrhage** → lateral canthotomy/cantholysis immediately. Restricted
  upgaze with pain, nausea or bradycardia in a child = **trapdoor/white-eyed blowout**,
  which needs urgent release of the entrapped muscle.
- **Zygoma:** cheek flattening, infraorbital numbness, trismus (arch impinging coronoid).
- **Mandible:** malocclusion, step, sublingual haematoma, lip numbness. Open fractures
  (into the mouth) get antibiotics.
- **Le Fort:** mobile midface. Look for CSF leak and airway problems.
- Imaging: **CT face with fine cuts** for anything beyond the nasal bones. Get an
  ophthalmology review for orbital injuries.

## 3. Functional rhinoplasty assessment
- History: obstruction side, fixed vs alternating (cycle vs structural), allergic
  rhinitis, decongestant overuse (rhinitis medicamentosa), prior trauma/surgery.
- Exam: external deviation, **internal nasal valve** angle, alar collapse on inspiration,
  septal deviation, turbinate hypertrophy before and after decongestant. A **Cottle
  manoeuvre** (lateral cheek traction) helps localise valve problems. Modified Cottle with
  an ear curette is more specific.
- Score symptoms (NOSE scale) before surgery. Correct the medical rhinitis first.
- Surgical tools: septoplasty, spreader grafts (internal valve), alar batten/lateral crural
  strut grafts (external valve), turbinate reduction. Keep a **≥1 cm L-strut** of septal
  cartilage dorsally and caudally to prevent collapse.
- For cosmetic change, check expectations and screen for body dysmorphic disorder.

## 4. Local flaps for facial defects
- Plan around **aesthetic subunits** and **relaxed skin tension lines**. Hide scars in
  subunit borders. Confirm clear margins (often **Mohs**) before reconstructing.
- Common choices (orientation, not a rule):
  - Nasal tip/ala small defect (< ~1.5 cm): **bilobed flap** or full-thickness skin graft.
  - Ala larger: **nasolabial flap**, staged. Large or full-thickness nasal defect:
    **paramedian forehead flap** (staged, ~3 weeks to division), with cartilage support and lining.
  - Cheek: advancement/rotation flaps. Lip: primary closure up to ~1/3, then Abbe/Karapandzic.
- Avoid distorting free margins (eyelid, alar rim, lip). An ectropion or alar notch is worse
  than a slightly bigger scar.

## 5. Scar revision
- Wait for maturation (~6–12 months) unless the scar is clearly contracted. **Z-plasty**
  lengthens and reorients contractures. W-plasty or geometric broken-line closure helps
  scars that run against the tension lines. Steroid injection for hypertrophic/keloid scars.

## Gotchas
- Sending a "broken nose" home without a speculum look at the septum.
- Reducing a nasal fracture at day 1 under swelling, or at 5 weeks after it has set.
- Rhinoplasty that removes too much (over-resected dorsum, weak L-strut) gives valve
  collapse and saddling.
- Reconstructing before histology confirms clear margins.

## Verify
- [ ] Septum examined and septal haematoma excluded in every nasal injury.
- [ ] Visual acuity/pupils/eye movement documented in any orbital or midface injury.
- [ ] Nasal fracture review booked in the 5–7 day window.
- [ ] Functional rhinoplasty: valve assessment, NOSE score and medical therapy trial documented.
- [ ] Flap choice tied to subunit and margin status; free margins protected.
