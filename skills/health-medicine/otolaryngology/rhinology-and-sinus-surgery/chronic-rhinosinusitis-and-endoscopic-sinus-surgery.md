# Chronic rhinosinusitis and endoscopic sinus surgery

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** confirm CRS objectively, give a proper trial of medical therapy, and when
surgery is indicated, read the CT like a surgeon so the orbit and skull base stay intact.

## 1. Diagnosis (EPOS-style)
- ≥ 12 weeks of two or more symptoms, one being **nasal blockage or discoloured
  discharge**, ± facial pain/pressure, ± reduced smell.
- **Plus objective evidence:** endoscopy (polyps, mucopurulence, oedema in the middle
  meatus) *or* CT mucosal change. Facial pain alone with a normal scope is rarely CRS —
  think midfacial segment pain or migraine.
- Phenotype: CRS with nasal polyps (CRSwNP, often type-2: eosinophilia, asthma,
  N-ERD/aspirin sensitivity) vs without (CRSsNP). Unilateral polyp = biopsy/imaging for
  inverted papilloma or malignancy.
- Record **SNOT-22** at baseline; repeat after treatment.

## 2. Appropriate medical therapy (typically 8–12 weeks)
1. **High-volume saline irrigation** (e.g., 240 mL squeeze bottle) daily; teach
   head-down-forward position.
2. **Intranasal corticosteroid** — spray correctly (opposite hand, aim away from septum),
   or steroid rinses (e.g., budesonide added to irrigation, off-label in some regions).
3. Short oral steroid course for CRSwNP in selected patients (weigh cumulative
   steroid burden — count courses per year).
4. Long-term low-dose macrolide is an option for CRSsNP in some guidelines (check QTc
   and interactions).
5. Treat contributing factors: allergy, smoking, asthma control, dental source in
   unilateral maxillary disease.

## 3. CT review before surgery — "CLOSE"
Read the non-contrast sinus CT (thin axial with coronal/sagittal reformats) for:
- **C**ribriform plate: Keros depth of olfactory fossa (type III = high risk).
- **L**amina papyracea: dehiscence or medial bulging.
- **O**nodi (sphenoethmoidal) cells — optic nerve may run in them.
- **S**phenoid sinus: carotid/optic dehiscence, septation inserting on the carotid.
- **E**thmoidal arteries: anterior ethmoid artery hanging on a mesentery below skull base.
Also score Lund–Mackay and map frontal recess cells for drainage pathway planning.

## 4. FESS principles
- Indications: CRS failing appropriate medical therapy with objective disease.
- Extent tailored to disease: uncinectomy, maxillary antrostomy, ethmoidectomy,
  sphenoidotomy, frontal sinusotomy (Draf I–III) as needed. Full-house surgery in
  CRSwNP reduces recurrence vs limited polypectomy.
- Image guidance: register and check accuracy on fixed landmarks before relying on it;
  it supports, never replaces, anatomical knowledge.
- Keep the middle turbinate stable; avoid stripping mucosa (scarring, osteitis).

## 5. Biologics
For severe uncontrolled CRSwNP despite surgery/steroids — dupilumab, omalizumab,
mepolizumab are licensed in many regions. EPOS-type criteria use eosinophilia/IgE,
steroid need, smell loss, SNOT-22 and asthma; payer rules vary — check locally.

## 6. Complications to recognise immediately
- **Orbital haematoma:** proptosis, pain, tight orbit → remove packing, lateral
  canthotomy/cantholysis without waiting for imaging.
- **CSF leak:** clear rhinorrhoea, salty taste; test fluid for beta-2 transferrin/beta-trace;
  repair endoscopically.
- Post-op: irrigation, crust debridement, continue steroids — surgery opens cavities for
  topical therapy, it doesn't cure type-2 inflammation.

## Gotchas
- Operating on a CT obtained during an acute cold.
- Unilateral disease with bone erosion or a mass: biopsy before definitive surgery.
- Septoplasty for "blockage" when the real cause is turbinate hypertrophy or valve collapse.
