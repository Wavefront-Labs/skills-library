# Voice disorder assessment and phonosurgery

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or local protocols. Not patient-specific advice.

**Goal:** find the laryngeal cancer and the paralysed cord early, label benign lesions
correctly with stroboscopy, and pick voice therapy vs surgery so surgery is kept for
lesions that therapy can't fix.

## 1. History and red flags
- Ask about onset, how the voice is used (singer, teacher, call-centre), smoking/alcohol,
  reflux symptoms, recent intubation or neck/chest surgery, swallowing, breathing.
- **Red flags → urgent laryngoscopy:** smoker with persistent hoarseness, stridor,
  haemoptysis, dysphagia/odynophagia, otalgia with normal ear, neck mass, weight loss,
  hoarseness after thyroid, cardiac or chest surgery.
- Guideline rule of thumb: laryngoscopy if hoarseness doesn't resolve in **~4 weeks**, or
  at once with a red flag. Don't start empirical anti-reflux drugs without looking at the
  larynx. (Exact wait times differ between guidelines and countries. Check your local pathway.)

## 2. Examination
- **Flexible nasendoscopy** for structure and movement: "eee" (adduction), sniff
  (abduction), cough, sustained phonation. Look at both cords, arytenoids, subglottis
  where visible, piriform fossae and pooling of secretions.
- **Stroboscopy** (rigid 70°/90° or distal-chip flexible): judge symmetry, periodicity,
  closure pattern, **mucosal wave** and amplitude. An absent or stiff wave over a white
  lesion suggests invasion (dysplasia or carcinoma), not a nodule.
- Rate the voice with a perceptual scale (GRBAS/CAPE-V) and a patient-reported score
  (VHI-10). Record a voice sample before any treatment.

## 3. Benign lesions: what each needs
| Lesion | Look | First line |
|---|---|---|
| Nodules | Bilateral, symmetric, junction of anterior/middle third | Voice therapy; surgery rarely |
| Polyp | Usually unilateral, may be haemorrhagic | Therapy trial; microsurgical excision if it persists |
| Cyst | Unilateral submucosal, reduced wave | Usually microflap excision + therapy |
| Reinke's oedema | Bilateral floppy swelling, smoker, low pitch | Stop smoking; surgery if airway or voice demands |
| Leukoplakia | White patch | **Biopsy/excise for histology**; it is a description, not a diagnosis |

Phonosurgery principle: work subepithelially with a **microflap**, keep the vocal
ligament and the epithelium, and don't strip the cord. Scarred lamina propria is
permanent dysphonia.

## 4. Vocal-fold paralysis
- Unilateral immobility with no clear cause (such as recent surgery) needs **imaging of
  the whole recurrent laryngeal nerve path**, from skull base to aortic arch (left) or
  subclavian (right). CT neck + chest is common.
- Check for aspiration and a breathy weak cough. Glottic gap drives the symptoms.
- **Injection laryngoplasty** (temporary filler such as hyaluronic acid or CaHA, done
  in clinic or theatre) bridges the months while recovery may happen. **Medialisation
  thyroplasty** ± arytenoid adduction when the paralysis is permanent. Many units wait
  ~6–12 months before permanent surgery. Reinnervation is an option in some centres.
- Bilateral immobility presents with **stridor and a good voice**, so it is an airway
  problem first.

## 5. Airway stenosis (basics)
- Subglottic/tracheal stenosis: history of intubation, granulomatosis with polyangiitis,
  or idiopathic (mostly women). Exertional stridor gets labelled "asthma".
- Grade with Cotton-Myer on endoscopy. Options run from endoscopic dilation ± steroid
  injection up to open resection. Recurrence after dilation is common.

## Gotchas
- Treating "reflux laryngitis" for months without a scope delays a cancer diagnosis.
- Calling bilateral lesions "nodules" in an adult non-voice-user without stroboscopy.
- Operating on nodules before a proper trial of voice therapy.
- Injecting too superficially or too much → stiff cord or airway compromise; inject lateral
  to the vocal process, slight overinjection only with temporary fillers.
- Missing a pulmonary or mediastinal tumour behind a left cord palsy.

## Verify
- [ ] Red flags asked and documented; larynx visualised before empirical treatment.
- [ ] Stroboscopy findings recorded (closure, wave, symmetry), with baseline voice sample and VHI-10.
- [ ] Every leukoplakia or suspicious lesion has a histology plan.
- [ ] Unexplained cord palsy imaged along the full nerve course.
- [ ] Voice therapy offered before surgery for benign phonotraumatic lesions.
