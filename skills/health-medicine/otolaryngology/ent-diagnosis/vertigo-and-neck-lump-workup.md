# Vertigo and neck lump work-up

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** sort dizziness into peripheral vs central with a bedside exam, catch the
ENT emergencies (sudden hearing loss, posterior-circulation stroke), and investigate an
adult neck lump in the right order so a cancer isn't biopsied open or missed.

## 1. Core ENT exam (every patient)
- **Otoscopy:** pull pinna up/back (adults), largest speculum that fits. Note canal,
  drum colour, landmarks, retraction pockets (attic crust = possible cholesteatoma),
  perforation site.
- **Tuning forks (512 Hz):** Weber lateralises to the conductive-loss ear or away from a
  sensorineural-loss ear. Rinne negative (BC > AC) = conductive loss ≥ ~20–30 dB.
  Confirm with formal audiometry.
- **Flexible nasendoscopy:** topical decongestant/anaesthetic, pass along the floor or
  middle meatus, then view nasopharynx, base of tongue, vallecula, piriform fossae, cord
  movement. Ask patient to sniff, say "eee", puff cheeks to open hidden areas.

## 2. Dizziness: timing and triggers first
| Pattern | Think |
|---|---|
| Seconds, triggered by lying/rolling over | BPPV |
| Hours, episodic, with fluctuating hearing loss, tinnitus, fullness | Ménière's |
| Days, continuous, acute onset ("acute vestibular syndrome") | Vestibular neuritis vs stroke |
| Episodic with headache/photophobia | Vestibular migraine |
| Chronic non-spinning unsteadiness > 3 months | PPPD, multifactorial |

**Dix-Hallpike:** head turned 45°, lie back rapidly with neck extended ~20°. Posterior
canal BPPV gives torsional upbeating nystagmus after a few seconds' latency, fatiguing
within ~60 s. Treat with **Epley** on the same side. Horizontal canal → supine roll test.

**HINTS** (only in continuous vertigo *with* nystagmus, ideally by a trained examiner):
a *normal* head impulse, direction-changing nystagmus, or skew deviation = central until
proven otherwise → urgent stroke pathway/MRI with DWI (early MRI misses some posterior
strokes). Add hearing ("HINTS plus"): new unilateral hearing loss can be AICA infarct.

## 3. Sudden sensorineural hearing loss = urgent
≥ 30 dB over 3 contiguous frequencies within 72 h. Confirm SNHL (Weber away, Rinne
positive), audiogram within days, offer oral or intratympanic corticosteroid early per
local protocol, MRI IAMs for retrocochlear cause. Don't dismiss as "wax" without looking.

## 4. Adult neck lump
1. **Red flags:** age > 40, smoker/alcohol, persistent > 3 weeks, hard/fixed, hoarseness,
   dysphagia, otalgia with normal ear, weight loss. HPV-positive oropharyngeal cancer
   often presents as a cystic node in a younger non-smoker — **a "branchial cyst" in an
   adult is cancer until proven otherwise.**
2. **Exam:** all neck levels (I–VI), thyroid, parotid, skin of scalp/face, full scope.
3. **Imaging + cytology:** ultrasound with ultrasound-guided FNA (or core) is first line;
   CT/MRI neck and chest for suspicious lesions.
4. **Avoid open excision** of a suspected metastatic squamous node — it seeds the neck and
   complicates definitive treatment. If FNA non-diagnostic, repeat or core first.
5. Tonsillar asymmetry or unknown primary → panendoscopy / PET-CT pathway.

## Gotchas
- Labelling all dizziness "labyrinthitis" and giving prochlorperazine for weeks —
  suppressants delay compensation; limit to a few days.
- Doing Dix-Hallpike in someone with significant cervical/vertebral disease without
  modification (use side-lying test).
- Unilateral middle-ear effusion in an adult: scope the postnasal space (nasopharyngeal
  carcinoma).
- Unilateral tinnitus/asymmetric SNHL needs MRI for vestibular schwannoma.

## Verify
- [ ] Dizziness classified by timing/trigger before any test.
- [ ] Dix-Hallpike result (side, nystagmus type) documented.
- [ ] Central signs/HINTS considered in continuous vertigo; stroke pathway if positive.
- [ ] Sudden SNHL audiogram and treatment decision within days.
- [ ] Adult neck lump has full scope + ultrasound-FNA before any excision.
