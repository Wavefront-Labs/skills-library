# Tendinopathy: progressive loading programme

> General clinical/professional reference. Related exercise dosing principles: `health-medicine.physical-therapy.therapeutic-exercise-prescription.dosing-and-progressing-rehabilitation-exercise`.

**Goal:** tendons adapt to load slowly (months). Rehab is the progressive restoration of
the tendon's capacity — first to tolerate load, then heavy slow load, then fast
energy-storage load — while the patient manages the pain rather than chasing rest.

## 1. Confirm the diagnosis and screen
- Localised pain at the tendon, **load-dependent** (pain with energy-storage activity —
  hopping, running, jumping — that warms up then worsens later/next day), morning stiffness.
- Differentials: partial/complete rupture (Achilles: Thompson test, palpable gap → urgent
  ortho), bursitis, referred pain, inflammatory enthesitis (spondyloarthropathy, multiple
  sites), fluoroquinolone-associated tendinopathy, stress fracture, nerve entrapment.
- Baseline measures: **VISA-A** (Achilles) / **VISA-P** (patellar), single-leg heel raise count,
  hop tests, pain on a provocative load test (e.g. single-leg decline squat).

## 2. Staged programme (progress by criteria, not weeks)
**Stage 1 — Isometrics (pain-dominant):** e.g. 5 × 30–45 s holds at ~70% MVC, several times a
day; aim for pain relief and tolerance. Analgesic effect is variable between people.
**Stage 2 — Isotonic heavy slow resistance (HSR):** slow tempo (~3 s up / 3 s down),
progressing to ~6–8RM, 3–4 sets, 3×/week; e.g. seated and standing heel raises, leg press,
split squats. Eccentric-only protocols (Alfredson: 3 × 15 twice daily, 12 weeks)
are an alternative for Achilles; HSR has similar outcomes with better adherence.
**Stage 3 — Energy storage:** skipping, hopping, bounding, then sport-specific
plyometrics; build volume then intensity, every second day initially.
**Stage 4 — Return to sport:** graded return of running/jumping volume; full training before competition.
Progress when: pain on loading test stable or improving, 24-h response acceptable, strength
targets met (e.g. single-leg heel-raise endurance comparable to the other side).

## 3. Pain-monitoring rules
Pain during exercise up to ~5/10 allowed if it settles by next morning and morning
stiffness/pain is not trending up. Worse next day → reduce load for that session type.
Complete rest is counterproductive (tendon capacity drops).

## 4. Manage aggravating load and compression
- Reduce (don't stop) high-energy-storage activities; keep activities that load without flare.
- **Compression** aggravates insertional tendinopathies: avoid deep dorsiflexion stretching
  (insertional Achilles) — work from a flat surface, not off a step; for gluteal tendinopathy
  avoid sitting cross-legged, side-lying on the affected side, and hip adduction postures.
- Education on timeframe: **3–6+ months** typical; recovery is not linear.

## 5. Other interventions — adjuncts at most
Corticosteroid injections give short-term relief with worse medium-term outcomes in some
tendinopathies (lateral elbow) and carry rupture concern near the Achilles — refer for
medical discussion rather than recommend. Shockwave, PRP and other injections: mixed
evidence; adjunct to loading, never instead of it.

## Gotchas
- Stretching the painful insertional tendon makes it worse.
- Imaging changes don't track symptoms well; don't let a scan dictate progression.
- Patients stop at the point of pain relief, before capacity is rebuilt → recurrence. Keep a maintenance programme.

## Verify
- [ ] Rupture, enthesitis and other differentials excluded; baseline VISA/strength recorded.
- [ ] Stage chosen on irritability; progression criteria written down.
- [ ] Patient can state the 24-h pain-monitoring rule.
- [ ] Compressive positions identified and modified.
- [ ] Re-measured outcome score and strength at review; maintenance plan in place.
