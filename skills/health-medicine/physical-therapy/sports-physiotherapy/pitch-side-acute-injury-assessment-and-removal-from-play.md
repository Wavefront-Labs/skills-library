# Pitch-side acute injury assessment and removal from play

> General clinical/professional reference; it does not replace a certified pitch-side trauma/immediate-care course or the governing body's concussion protocol for your sport and level.

**Goal:** in the first minutes after an injury, find and manage anything life- or
limb-threatening, decide **play on / remove / hospital** correctly, and never let a
concussed or spinally injured athlete back on because the game is close.

## 1. Before the match (the part that saves lives)
- **Emergency action plan (EAP):** ambulance access route and gate keys, nearest
  appropriate hospital, roles (who calls, who fetches AED, who meets the ambulance),
  communication.
- Equipment checked: **AED** (sudden cardiac arrest is the leading medical cause of
  death in sport — collapse without contact = assume cardiac arrest, start CPR, AED fast),
  airway kit, spinal immobilisation equipment, splints, ice, gloves, concussion tools.
- Know the athletes' relevant medical history (asthma, diabetes, anaphylaxis, prior concussions).

## 2. Approach and primary survey
Scene safe (stop play) → **D R <C> A B C D E**: Danger, Response, catastrophic
haemorrhage, Airway **with C-spine control**, Breathing, Circulation, Disability (AVPU/GCS,
pupils, glucose if diabetic), Exposure.
- Unresponsive or mechanism suggests spinal injury (axial load, head-first tackle, neck pain,
  neurological symptoms) → manual in-line stabilisation, don't move them for convenience;
  helmet removal only per sport-specific protocol by trained staff.

## 3. Head injury: recognise and remove
- **Immediate removal, no return that day** for any observable sign: loss of consciousness,
  tonic posturing/seizure, balance disturbance/ataxia, confusion, dazed/blank look,
  clutching head, or player reports symptoms.
- Red flags → emergency department: neck pain/tenderness, double vision, weakness/tingling
  in limbs, severe or increasing headache, seizure, deteriorating consciousness, vomiting, increasing agitation.
- Off-field: structured assessment with **SCAT6** (medical professionals) or **CRT6** (non-medical).
  A normal sideline test does **not** clear a player — "if in doubt, sit them out".
- Graduated return-to-learn/sport only after symptom resolution and medical clearance; follow sport/governing-body rules.

## 4. Limb injuries on the field
- Obvious deformity, open wound over fracture, neurovascular compromise (pale, pulseless,
  numb distal limb) → splint in position found (unless distal ischaemia needs gentle
  realignment by a trained clinician), ambulance.
- Dislocations: relocation on-field only by appropriately trained practitioners and
  within protocol (e.g. some finger/patellar dislocations); otherwise splint and transfer.
- Ankle: **Ottawa ankle rules** (bone tenderness at the posterior edge/tip of either
  malleolus, base of 5th metatarsal, navicular, or inability to weight-bear 4 steps both
  immediately and in the ED) → X-ray.
- Knee: pop, rapid effusion (<2 h), giving way → suspect ACL/haemarthrosis; no return to play.
- **Can't run, cut, jump or perform sport-specific movement at full speed → off.** Pain
  masked by adrenaline is still injury.

## 5. Acute soft-tissue care and handover
**PEACE** (Protect, Elevate, Avoid anti-inflammatories early — debated, Compress,
Educate) then **LOVE** (Load, Optimism, Vascularisation, Exercise). Ice for analgesia.
Hand over with mechanism, findings, treatment given and times; written concussion
advice to the athlete **and** a responsible adult.

## Gotchas
- Coaches and athletes pressure for a quick return — the clinician's decision is final; agree this before the season.
- Heat illness: collapse with altered mental state in heat = exertional heat stroke until proven otherwise; **cool first, transport second** (cold-water immersion).
- Diabetic athletes: hypoglycaemia can mimic concussion — check glucose.

## Verify
- [ ] EAP written, rehearsed, and AED location known by the whole team.
- [ ] Primary survey with C-spine consideration completed before limb assessment.
- [ ] Every suspected concussion removed and not returned the same day; red flags sent to ED.
- [ ] Limb injury decision documented (play on/remove/hospital) with Ottawa rules where relevant.
- [ ] Handover and written advice given.
