# Musculoskeletal examination to treatment plan

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for clinical judgement or local direct-access/referral rules.

**Goal:** leave the first session with (1) confidence that this is appropriate for
physiotherapy, (2) a working hypothesis of what is driving the problem, (3) a baseline
you can re-measure, and (4) a plan the patient agrees with.

## 1. Subjective examination (most of the diagnosis is here)
- **Body chart:** every area of symptoms, type (ache, sharp, burning, pins and needles),
  depth, constant vs intermittent; relationships between areas.
- **Behaviour:** aggravating and easing factors, 24-hour pattern (morning stiffness >30–60
  min suggests inflammatory; night pain unrelated to position is a flag).
- **History:** mechanism/onset, progression, previous episodes and treatment.
- **SINSS** to set exam intensity: **Severity, Irritability** (how little activity provokes
  symptoms, how long to settle), **Nature** (diagnosis, flags), **Stage** (acute/subacute/chronic), **Stability** (getting better/worse/same).
  Highly irritable → limited, gentle exam; low irritability → load and reproduce symptoms.
- **Patient goals and context:** work, sport, beliefs and fears, expectations.

## 2. Screen for serious pathology and psychosocial risk
- **Red flags** (combine with clinical suspicion, not a checklist alone): history of
  cancer, unexplained weight loss, fever/immunosuppression/IV drug use, significant trauma or
  osteoporosis risk, progressive neurological deficit, **cauda equina symptoms** (saddle
  anaesthesia, new bladder/bowel/sexual dysfunction, bilateral sciatica) → same-day
  emergency referral; cervical arterial dysfunction signs (dizziness, diplopia,
  dysarthria, dysphagia, drop attacks) before neck treatment; non-mechanical, unremitting pain.
- **Yellow flags:** fear-avoidance, catastrophising, low mood, work issues. Use a
  stratification tool where validated (e.g. **STarT Back** for low back pain).

## 3. Objective examination (planned, hypothesis-driven)
Observation/posture and functional demonstration (the movement they struggle with) →
active and passive ROM with overpressure → resisted/strength testing (MMT or dynamometry)
→ neurological screen where indicated (dermatomes, myotomes, reflexes, neurodynamic tests)
→ **special tests** chosen to rule in/out your top hypotheses → palpation → joint
accessory movements.
- Use tests by their statistics: high **sensitivity** tests rule *out* when negative (SnOut),
  high **specificity** tests rule *in* when positive (SpIn). Clusters beat single tests
  (e.g. Laslett's SIJ cluster, Wainner's cervical radiculopathy cluster).
- Identify an **asterisk sign**: a meaningful, reproducible measure to re-test after treatment.

## 4. Outcome measures (baseline now, re-measure later)
Region-specific: ODI (low back), NDI (neck), DASH/QuickDASH (upper limb), LEFS, KOOS/HOOS.
Patient-specific: **PSFS**. Pain: NPRS. Performance: 30-s sit-to-stand, timed up and go, hop tests as appropriate.
Know the MCID for the tool you use.

## 5. Reason to a plan
Write a hypothesis in ICF terms (impairments, activity limitations, participation
restrictions; contributing factors), prognosis, SMART goals, interventions (education,
exercise, manual therapy, load management), frequency, and review date. Explain it to
the patient in plain language and agree it.

## Gotchas
- Special tests in isolation have modest accuracy; history usually carries more weight.
- Over-examining an irritable patient flares them and loses trust.
- Imaging findings (disc bulges, rotator cuff changes) are common in asymptomatic people — don't let a report define the plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Red flags screened and documented; any positive actioned with urgency matched to risk.
- [ ] SINSS judged and exam intensity matched to it.
- [ ] At least one asterisk sign and one validated outcome measure recorded at baseline.
- [ ] Working hypothesis, goals and plan written and agreed with the patient.
