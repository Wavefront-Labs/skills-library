# Dosing and progressing rehabilitation exercise

> General clinical/professional reference. Medical screening (cardiac, post-surgical restrictions) comes first; this covers the dose once exercise is appropriate.

**Goal:** most failed rehab is under-dosed (three sets of ten with a yellow band
forever) or progressed by the calendar instead of by criteria. Prescribe with a target
adaptation, an intensity you can measure, and explicit rules for when to go up or back.

## 1. Match the dose to the adaptation you need
| Goal | Intensity | Reps × sets | Rest |
|---|---|---|---|
| Strength | ~70–85%+ 1RM, RPE 7–9 (1–3 RIR) | 3–8 × 2–5 | 2–3 min |
| Hypertrophy | ~65–80% 1RM, RPE 7–9 | 6–15 × 3–4 | 1–2 min |
| Muscular endurance | <65% 1RM | 15–25+ × 2–3 | <1 min |
| Power | light–moderate load, max intent/velocity | 1–5 × 3–5 | full |
| Motor control/skill | low load, high quality | many short reps, varied practice | as needed |
Frequency: 2–3 sessions/week per muscle group for strength; motor control can be daily.
Strength gains need **effort near failure** — light loads only work when taken close to fatigue.

## 2. Make intensity measurable
- **RPE (0–10) or reps in reserve (RIR):** "this set should finish with ~2 reps left in the tank."
- Estimated 1RM from a submaximal set (e.g. a 5–8RM test) once tissue tolerance allows.
- Isometrics: % of maximal voluntary contraction, duration (e.g. 5 × 30–45 s holds) — used
  early in tendinopathy and painful phases.
- Write it down: exercise, load, sets × reps, tempo, RPE target, rest, frequency.

## 3. Pain-monitoring model (load, don't fear)
Pain during exercise up to **~3–5/10 is acceptable** if it settles to baseline by the next
morning and doesn't trend up week to week; pain above that, or worse next day, = reduce
load (not stop). Explain this explicitly — it is the main tool for patient self-regulation.
Adjust for condition: acute post-op, neuropathic or inflammatory pain need different rules.

## 4. Progression rules
- **Double progression:** hit the top of the rep range at target RPE on all sets → add load
  (~2–10%) and drop back to the bottom of the range.
- Progress one variable at a time: range → load → speed → complexity/instability → sport/task specificity.
- **Criteria-based**, not time-based: e.g. pain response stable, quality maintained,
  strength symmetry targets (limb symmetry index) before plyometrics.
- Regress when: pain response fails, quality degrades, or a flare/illness; plan deload weeks.

## 5. Home programme design for adherence
2–4 exercises, not 12. Link to existing routines, give video or clear images, define
the dose and the pain rule, use a log or app, and ask about barriers at every review.
Agree why each exercise matters to *their* goal.

## Gotchas
- Isolated "core" or "activation" exercises rarely substitute for loading the actual task.
- Older adults and deconditioned patients tolerate — and need — progressive heavy loading;
  under-dosing them is the common error, not over-dosing.
- Specificity: train the speed, range and position the task requires.
- Contraindications to high-intensity effort (uncontrolled hypertension, unstable cardiac
  conditions, early post-op restrictions) override everything above — check with the medical team.

## Verify
- [ ] Each exercise has a stated goal and a full dose (load, sets, reps, RPE/RIR, rest, frequency).
- [ ] Patient can explain the pain-monitoring rule.
- [ ] Progression and regression criteria written down.
- [ ] Home programme ≤4 exercises with a log; adherence reviewed at follow-up.
- [ ] Objective measure (strength, reps, outcome score) re-tested to confirm progress.
