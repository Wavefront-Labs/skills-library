# Six-minute walk test and pulmonary-rehab exercise prescription

> General clinical/professional reference. Exercise testing and training in cardiac and respiratory disease require medical screening and locally agreed stop criteria and oxygen prescribing rules.

**Goal:** get a reproducible measure of functional exercise capacity, then turn it into a
training dose that is hard enough to cause adaptation (breathlessness is expected) and
safe enough to repeat three times a week for 8–12 weeks.

## 1. Before the test
- Screen: recent unstable angina or MI (within ~1 month) is a contraindication; resting
  HR >120, SBP >180 or DBP >100 mmHg are relative contraindications (ATS 2002).
- Patient rested ≥10 min seated beforehand; usual medications taken; usual walking aid.
- Record resting HR, SpO2, BP, modified Borg dyspnoea (0–10) and fatigue.
- Oxygen: if on O2, test with their usual prescription and document flow and how carried.

## 2. Running the test (standardise everything)
- Flat, straight, **30 m corridor** marked every 3 m, turnaround cones. Shorter tracks
  reduce distance — note the track length if non-standard.
- Script: "Walk as far as possible for 6 minutes. You may slow down, stop and rest, but
  resume as soon as you can." Use only the **standard encouragement phrases** each minute
  ("You're doing well, you have 5 minutes to go"). Don't walk alongside (sets the pace);
  continuous oximetry carried by the tester behind is acceptable.
- Stop for: chest pain, intolerable dyspnoea, leg cramps, staggering, diaphoresis, pallor;
  local SpO2 floor (often <80–85% — set locally).
- Record distance, rests, lowest SpO2, peak HR, end Borg scores, recovery.
- **Practice test:** a second test on the first visit typically improves by ~25–30 m
  (learning effect); report the better of two at baseline.

## 3. Interpreting
- Compare with reference equations (age, sex, height, weight) for % predicted.
- **MCID in COPD ≈ 25–33 m**; use condition-specific MCIDs elsewhere.
- **Exertional desaturation** (fall ≥4% or nadir <88–90%) → flag for oxygen assessment
  per local criteria; desaturation is common in ILD and is prognostic.

## 4. Prescribing training (pulmonary rehab)
- **Endurance:** walking or cycling 3×/week, 20–60 min (start 10–20, build), intensity
  ~60–80% of peak work rate, or a **walking speed ≈ 80% of average 6MWT speed**
  (distance ÷ 360 s), or target **Borg dyspnoea 4–6 / RPE 12–14**.
- **Interval training** (e.g. 30 s work : 30 s rest) for severely breathless patients —
  similar gains with less dyspnoea.
- **Resistance training:** major muscle groups, 2–3 sets of 8–12 at ~60–70% 1RM, progressing.
- Breathing strategies: pursed-lip breathing, pacing, recovery positions; inspiratory
  muscle training as an adjunct for weak inspiratory muscles.
- Programme: ≥8 weeks with education and self-management; reassess 6MWT, CAT/CRQ at end.
- Cardiac rehab: similar structure with HR/RPE targets from an exercise test and
  medication considerations (beta-blockers → use RPE).

## 5. Safety while training
Monitor SpO2, HR, symptoms; supplemental O2 during training if desaturating (per
prescription). Stop criteria agreed with the medical team. Red flags — chest pain,
arrhythmia symptoms, new or severe breathlessness at rest, signs of exacerbation (sputum
change, fever) — pause training and refer.

## Gotchas
- Changing corridor, encouragement or O2 method between tests invalidates comparison.
- Finger oximeters lag and misread with poor perfusion — use a good signal, ear probe if needed.
- Breathlessness ≠ danger; explain this, it is what drives fear-avoidance in COPD.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications screened and baseline vitals recorded.
- [ ] Test done on a standard track with scripted encouragement; practice test accounted for.
- [ ] Distance, nadir SpO2 and Borg recorded; desaturation flagged.
- [ ] Training intensity derived from test results and written as speed/work rate + Borg target.
- [ ] Reassessment at programme end compared with MCID.
