# Joint mobilisation: grades and clinical application

> General clinical/professional reference. Manual therapy, especially thrust manipulation, is a hands-on skill learned under supervision; this page cannot teach handling. Scope of practice for manipulation varies by jurisdiction.

**Goal:** use passive joint techniques as a short-term window — reducing pain or
improving movement — that you immediately exploit with active exercise, rather than as a
stand-alone treatment the patient becomes dependent on.

## 1. The Maitland grades (oscillatory, by range and amplitude)
| Grade | Where in range | Amplitude | Typical use |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | Start of range | Small | Pain-dominant, highly irritable |
| II | Within range, short of resistance | Large | Pain-dominant |
| III | Into resistance, up to limit | Large | Stiffness with some pain |
| IV | At the limit, into resistance | Small | Stiffness-dominant |
| V | Beyond the limit (thrust, HVLA) | Small, high velocity | Selected cases, trained clinicians |
Oscillate ~1–2 per second for 30–60 s per set, typically 2–4 sets, re-testing between.

## 2. Pick the technique from the assessment
- **Irritability** (see `health-medicine.physical-therapy.physical-therapy-assessment.musculoskeletal-examination-to-treatment-plan`):
  high → grades I–II, fewer sets, re-assess next day; low → grades III–IV.
- **Pain vs stiffness:** symptoms before resistance (pain-dominant) → I–II; resistance
  before symptoms (stiffness-dominant) → III–IV.
- **Direction:** chosen from accessory movement findings (the stiff/provocative glide),
  and for peripheral joints the **convex-concave rule** (convex surface moving on concave:
  glide opposite to the movement; concave on convex: same direction) — treat this as a
  starting hypothesis; clinical response overrides theory.
- **Mobilisation with movement (Mulligan):** sustained glide while the patient actively
  performs the painful movement; must be **pain-free** to continue.

## 3. Test – treat – retest
Before treatment pick an **asterisk sign** (e.g. painful shoulder elevation range, ODI
item, slump test angle). After each set retest. If no change after 2–3 sets, change
grade, direction or technique — don't keep doing what isn't working. Then **immediately
follow with active exercise** into the gained range to consolidate.

## 4. Thrust manipulation: when and safety
- Evidence supports short-term benefit in selected groups (e.g. thoracic thrust for
  some neck pain; clinical prediction rule for acute low back pain without distal symptoms).
- **Absolute contraindications/red flags:** suspected fracture, malignancy, infection,
  inflammatory instability (e.g. rheumatoid upper cervical), cauda equina, myelopathy,
  **cervical arterial dysfunction** signs, severe osteoporosis, anticoagulation-related
  bleeding risk (relative/precaution — clinician judgement).
- Cervical region: follow the IFOMPT framework — risk assessment of vascular and upper-cervical
  instability before any cervical manual therapy.
- Obtain informed consent describing benefits, common transient soreness, and rare serious risks.

## 5. Explain the mechanism honestly
Effects are largely **neurophysiological** (pain modulation, reduced guarding) and
short-lived; "putting a joint back in" is inaccurate and fosters dependence and fear.
Frame it as a way to make movement easier so exercise can do the lasting work.

## Gotchas
- Patient position and clinician body mechanics (use body weight, not thumbs) determine both comfort and repeatability.
- Post-treatment soreness lasting >24–48 h = dose too high for irritability.
- Hypermobile patients rarely need mobilisation into range; stabilise and strengthen.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications and red flags screened (IFOMPT cervical screen if treating the neck).
- [ ] Grade and direction justified by irritability and assessment findings.
- [ ] Asterisk sign retested after each set; technique changed if no response.
- [ ] Active exercise into the gained range followed in the same session.
- [ ] Consent documented and the mechanism explained without "out of place" language.
