# Cerebral palsy: GMFCS-guided motor intervention

> General clinical/professional reference. Medical management (botulinum toxin, baclofen, orthopaedic surgery) belongs to the multidisciplinary team; dosing is out of scope here and must come from current local protocols.

**Goal:** set realistic, family-chosen goals using what is known about the child's
likely motor trajectory, then deliver enough **active, goal-directed practice** to move
toward them — while protecting the hips and musculoskeletal system over time.

## 1. Classify and predict
- **Early identification:** high-risk infants can be identified before 6 months with
  **General Movements Assessment (absent fidgety movements)**, **HINE**, and MRI — refer
  early; early intervention matters.
- **GMFCS (age-banded, levels I–V)** — stable over time from ~2 years; guides prognosis:
  - I: walks without limitations; II: walks with limitations;
  - III: walks with a hand-held mobility device; IV: limited self-mobility, often powered;
  - V: transported in a manual wheelchair, limited head/trunk control.
- **GMFM-66** scores plotted on **motor growth curves**: most children approach their
  plateau by ~5–7 years (earlier for higher levels); some at levels III–V show decline in
  adolescence. Use the curves to frame goals honestly with families.
- Also classify hand function (MACS) and communication (CFCS); describe tone type and distribution.

## 2. Goal-setting with the family
Use **COPM** or **Goal Attainment Scaling (GAS)**: specific, functional, meaningful goals
("ride a trike to the park with friends", "transfer to the toilet with one helper"),
not impairment goals ("improve hamstring length").

## 3. Intervention — what works (Novak 2020 "green light" evidence)
- **Goal-directed, task-specific training** in the real context — high repetition, child
  active, embedded in **play** and daily routines; therapist coaches parents for home practice.
- Strength training (progressive, functional), treadmill/gait training for ambulant children
  (levels I–III), fitness training, hippotherapy (some evidence for balance), constraint-induced
  movement therapy and bimanual training (with OT) for unilateral CP.
- **Passive stretching alone has weak evidence** for preventing contracture; positioning
  programmes, standing frames (levels III–V: bone density, hip, ROM) and orthoses are used,
  with goals and review dates.
- Equipment: walkers (posterior often favoured), **powered mobility early** for children
  who can't self-propel functionally (independence and development, not "giving up").

## 4. Hip surveillance (don't skip)
Hip displacement risk rises with GMFCS level (very high at IV–V). Follow a surveillance
programme: clinical exam plus **AP pelvis X-ray with migration percentage** at intervals by
GMFCS and age (e.g. every 6–12 months for higher levels). Migration percentage **>30–40%**
or rising quickly → orthopaedic referral. Painful hips in adolescence are a common,
preventable outcome.

## 5. Refer / escalate
Tone interfering with function or care → spasticity team (botulinum toxin with intensive
therapy afterwards, intrathecal baclofen, selective dorsal rhizotomy in selected children);
progressive deformity, scoliosis, hip migration → orthopaedics; loss of skills
(regression) → urgent medical review (CP is non-progressive; regression suggests another diagnosis).

## Gotchas
- Classifying GMFCS by best performance in clinic rather than usual performance at home/school.
- Therapy "dose" is often too low to change anything — prioritise intensive blocks with clear goals.
- Pain is under-recognised, especially in non-verbal children (use validated tools).

## Verify
- [ ] GMFCS (and MACS/CFCS) documented; GMFM-66 baseline plotted on curves.
- [ ] Family-chosen goals recorded with GAS/COPM.
- [ ] Interventions are active and task-specific with a home programme.
- [ ] Hip surveillance up to date with migration percentage; referral thresholds acted on.
- [ ] Equipment and orthoses have goals and review dates.
