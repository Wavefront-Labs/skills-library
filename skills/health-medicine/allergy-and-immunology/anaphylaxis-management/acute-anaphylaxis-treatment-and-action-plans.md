# Acute anaphylaxis treatment and action plans

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** give intramuscular adrenaline early when airway, breathing or circulation is
affected, and send the patient home equipped to treat the next episode. Follows the
Resuscitation Council UK anaphylaxis guidance and its Q&A page; use the full algorithm
for doses by age.

## 1. Act on airway, breathing, circulation
- An allergic rash alone is common and usually responds to an oral antihistamine.
- If the person has, or is developing, airway, breathing or circulation problems, it is
  anaphylaxis: give IM adrenaline per the algorithm. If in doubt, give IM adrenaline and
  monitor the response.
- Adrenaline is the mainstay of treatment. Hydrocortisone and other steroids are no
  longer recommended for routine treatment; chlorphenamine and hydrocortisone are not
  first line and need not be in an anaphylaxis pack.
- The guideline recommends repeating IM adrenaline every 5 minutes if needed. This is a
  pragmatic interval, shorter than the 10 to 15 minutes auto-injector manufacturers
  give, because waiting may be excessive with life-threatening airway, breathing or
  circulation problems.
- The recommended adrenaline dose for teenagers and adults is 500 micrograms. Most adult
  auto-injectors deliver 300 micrograms, and their standard needle may not reach muscle
  in every patient, so ampoule IM dosing is preferred for healthcare providers. Paediatric
  doses are by age band in the algorithm; weight-based dosing by critical care
  specialists is not precluded.

## 2. Autoinjectors and the discharge package
- Prescribe 2 adrenaline auto-injectors, to be carried at all times.
- Train patients and carers on the particular device prescribed: technique varies between
  devices, and training needs hands-on practice with a training device.
- Training should also cover allergen avoidance, early recognition, when to give emergency
  treatment, and care while waiting for emergency services.
- Advise patients to check expiry dates and replace before they expire.
- Under 12 months, an auto-injector containing 150 micrograms is only for paediatric
  allergy specialist prescribing in rare cases.
- In the UK an auto-injector prescribed for a named person may only be given to that
  person under current law.

## Gotchas
- Giving an antihistamine first when airway, breathing or circulation is affected delays
  adrenaline.
- Patients carry expired or missing autoinjectors: check at every visit.
