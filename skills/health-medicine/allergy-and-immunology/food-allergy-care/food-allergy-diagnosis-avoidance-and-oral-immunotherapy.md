# Food allergy: diagnosis, avoidance and oral immunotherapy

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Verify against current local guidelines, product labelling and specialist protocols before acting.

**Goal:** confirm (or remove) a food allergy label accurately, keep the patient safe
with the least restrictive diet that works, and offer treatment options without
inflating fear.

## 1. Allergy versus intolerance
- **IgE-mediated:** onset within minutes to ~2 h, reproducible with each exposure, skin
  (urticaria, angioedema), respiratory, GI or cardiovascular signs. Can be anaphylactic.
- **Non-IgE:** FPIES (repetitive vomiting 1–4 h after the trigger, pallor, lethargy),
  food-protein proctocolitis, eosinophilic oesophagitis. Skin tests and sIgE are usually
  negative; diagnosis is clinical.
- **Intolerance:** lactose malabsorption, FODMAP sensitivity, pharmacological reactions
  — dose-dependent, no anaphylaxis risk, no adrenaline needed.
Labelling an intolerance as allergy drives unnecessary avoidance and nutritional harm.

## 2. Testing well
- Test only foods implicated by history. **Panel testing produces false positives**
  that lead to needless avoidance.
- Sensitisation ≠ allergy. Positive predictive cut-offs (for example skin wheal or sIgE
  levels) vary by food, age and assay; treat published thresholds as guides, not rules.
- Component tests improve specificity: **Ara h 2** (peanut storage protein) predicts
  true peanut allergy far better than whole-peanut sIgE; Ara h 8 / Bet v 1 homologue
  sensitisation suggests pollen-food syndrome (mild oral itch).
- Heated egg/milk tolerance: many children with egg or milk allergy tolerate baked
  forms; ovomucoid and casein components help predict this (uncertain at individual level).

## 3. The oral food challenge (OFC)
- The reference standard. Use when history and tests are discordant, to confirm
  resolution, or to introduce baked forms.
- Graded doses at ~15–30 min intervals to a cumulative serving; stop at objective
  signs; observe ≥ 1–2 h after the last dose (longer after reactions).
- Run only where anaphylaxis can be treated; stop antihistamines beforehand per protocol.

## 4. Avoidance and label counselling
- Teach reading the **ingredients list every time** (recipes change), the regional
  mandatory allergen list (it differs between EU/UK, US and other jurisdictions —
  e.g. sesame was added to the US list in 2023), and that **precautionary "may
  contain" labels are voluntary and unregulated**.
- Cross-contact at restaurants, bulk bins, shared fryers.
- Dietitian review when avoiding milk, multiple foods, or in growing children.
- Every IgE food-allergic patient with anaphylaxis risk: adrenaline autoinjector and
  written action plan (see `health-medicine.allergy-and-immunology.anaphylaxis-management.acute-anaphylaxis-treatment-and-action-plans`).

## 5. Oral immunotherapy (OIT) basics
- Daily ingestion of gradually increasing food protein to raise the reaction threshold
  ("bite-safe"), not cure. Licensed peanut product exists in some regions; others use
  measured food.
- Reactions during OIT are common; exercise, fever, menses and fasting lower the
  threshold — counsel to avoid exercise around dosing.
- Watch for eosinophilic oesophagitis symptoms (dysphagia, food impaction).
- Omalizumab is approved in some regions to reduce reactions to accidental exposure
  in multiple food allergy; check current labelling.

## 6. Anxiety-aware care
Food allergy anxiety is common in patients and parents. Frame risk accurately (fatal
reactions are rare), give practical rules instead of fear, and refer for psychological
support when avoidance generalises (refusing to eat out, school refusal).

## Gotchas
- Removing foods from infants' diets on positive tests alone can *cause* allergy;
  early introduction of peanut/egg is protective in most guidelines.
- Previous tolerance of a food in regular servings argues strongly against allergy.
- Antihistamines mask early challenge signs — reliance on them for "mild" reactions delays adrenaline.

## Verify
- [ ] History classified (IgE / non-IgE / intolerance) before testing.
- [ ] Tests chosen by history; results interpreted with food-specific context.
- [ ] Challenge offered where it would change management.
- [ ] Label/cross-contact teaching done; autoinjector and action plan issued when indicated.
- [ ] Nutrition and psychological impact reviewed at follow-up.
