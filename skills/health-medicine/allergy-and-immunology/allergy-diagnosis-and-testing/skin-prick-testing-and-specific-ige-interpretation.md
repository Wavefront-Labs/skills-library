# Skin-prick testing and specific IgE interpretation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** confirm or refute IgE-mediated allergy to a *suspected* trigger. Tests detect
**sensitisation**; only history (or a challenge) shows **clinical allergy**. Never order
broad "panels" without a story — false positives cause needless avoidance.

## 1. History decides what to test
Capture for each reaction: the trigger and dose, time to onset (IgE reactions usually
within minutes to ~2 h), symptoms by organ (skin, airway, gut, circulation), treatment
needed, co-factors (exercise, alcohol, NSAIDs, illness, menses), and whether the food
has been eaten uneventfully since. Delayed, isolated GI symptoms or chronic symptoms
without a clear trigger point away from IgE allergy.

## 2. Skin-prick test (SPT) technique
1. **Washout:** stop oral H1 antihistamines ~3–7 days before (cetirizine and similar
   typically 3–5 days; verify for the specific drug). Montelukast and inhaled steroids
   don't need stopping; potent topical steroids on the test site, some tricyclic
   antidepressants and omalizumab suppress results.
2. Site: volar forearm or back, skin free of eczema/dermatographism. Mark sites ≥ 2 cm apart.
3. **Controls are mandatory:** histamine (positive, often 10 mg/mL) and saline/glycerol
   (negative).
4. Drop of extract, prick through it with a lancet perpendicular to skin, wipe after.
   Fresh food (prick-to-prick) for fruits/vegetables whose labile allergens degrade.
5. Read at **15–20 min**: measure mean wheal diameter (longest + perpendicular / 2).
6. **Positive:** wheal ≥ 3 mm greater than the negative control. Invalid if histamine
   wheal < 3 mm (suppression) or the saline control raises a wheal (dermatographism).
7. Have adrenaline and a resuscitation plan in the room; systemic reactions are rare
   but real (higher risk with intradermal testing and unstable asthma).

## 3. Specific IgE (blood)
- Use when SPT is impossible: extensive eczema, dermatographism, can't stop
  antihistamines, very high-risk history.
- Higher levels raise the **probability** of clinical reactivity but don't predict
  severity. Published "95% positive predictive" cut-offs are population- and
  assay-specific — use them as guides only (uncertain across labs/countries).
- **Component-resolved diagnostics** sharpen interpretation, e.g. peanut Ara h 2
  (storage protein, systemic risk) versus Ara h 8 (PR-10, birch cross-reactive, usually
  oral symptoms only); hazelnut Cor a 9/14 versus Cor a 1.
- Very high total IgE (atopic dermatitis) produces many low-level positives of no meaning.

## 4. Putting it together
| History | Test | Usual next step |
|---|---|---|
| Convincing, recent | Positive | Diagnose; action plan |
| Convincing | Negative | Re-check technique/extract; consider challenge |
| Low suspicion | Positive | Probably sensitised only — challenge before labelling |
| Tolerates food now | Positive | Keep eating it; don't test |

**Oral food challenge** is the reference standard: graded doses every 15–30 min in a
setting equipped for anaphylaxis, with stopping criteria agreed in advance.

## 5. Adjuncts
- Serum **tryptase** 1–2 h after a suspected anaphylaxis (plus baseline ≥ 24 h later)
  supports mast-cell involvement; elevated baseline suggests mastocytosis or hereditary
  alpha-tryptasemia.
- Basophil activation test: useful where available; limited standardisation.
- Not validated: IgG/IgG4 food panels, kinesiology, hair analysis — say so plainly.

## Gotchas
- Testing "everything" in eczema produces a list of foods to wrongly avoid.
- Old extracts and fruit allergens lose potency → false negatives.
- A negative test in the first weeks after anaphylaxis may be falsely negative
  (refractory period); retest ~4–6 weeks later if doubt remains.

## Verify
- [ ] Every allergen tested was chosen from the history.
- [ ] Both controls valid and wheal sizes recorded in mm.
- [ ] Antihistamine washout documented.
- [ ] Result reported as sensitisation vs clinical allergy, with a challenge plan where unclear.
