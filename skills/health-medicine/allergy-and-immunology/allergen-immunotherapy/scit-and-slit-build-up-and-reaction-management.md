# SCIT and SLIT: build-up and reaction management

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** modify the immune response to a clinically relevant allergen so symptoms and
medication need fall and stay down after stopping. Done safely, it is routine; done
carelessly (skipped checks, no observation), it causes fatal reactions.

## 1. Who is a candidate
- Clear IgE-mediated disease **with matching sensitisation**: allergic rhinitis/
  conjunctivitis (pollen, house-dust mite, animal), **venom allergy** with systemic
  reactions, selected allergic asthma (must be controlled).
- Symptoms despite or intolerant of pharmacotherapy, or patient preference for disease
  modification.
- Commitment to **3–5 years** of therapy.
- Relative/absolute contraindications: uncontrolled or severe asthma (FEV1 < ~70%
  predicted is a common threshold), active autoimmune disease or malignancy, inability
  to use adrenaline; beta-blockers increase reaction severity and blunt rescue — review
  case-by-case, especially for venom. Don't *start* during pregnancy (continuation of
  maintenance is usually acceptable).

## 2. Choosing a route
| | SCIT (injections) | SLIT (tablets/drops) |
|---|---|---|
| Setting | Clinic, every dose | Home after supervised first dose |
| Systemic reaction risk | Low but real | Very low; local oral itch common |
| Evidence base | Broad, incl. venom | Strongest for licensed grass, ragweed, tree, HDM tablets |
| Adherence | Visit-dependent | Daily self-dosing — drops off over time |
Use products with evidence for the specific allergen; mixtures of many unrelated
extracts dilute each one (a US/European practice difference).

## 3. SCIT build-up and maintenance
- **Conventional build-up:** weekly injections through increasing vial strengths
  (e.g. 1:1000 → 1:100 → 1:10 → 1:1 of maintenance concentrate), typically 0.05 →
  0.1 → 0.2 → 0.4 → 0.5 mL steps per vial, per the manufacturer schedule.
- **Cluster** (several injections per visit) and **rush/ultra-rush** (venom) reach
  maintenance faster with higher reaction rates.
- Maintenance: every ~4 weeks (venom often 4–8 weeks once established).

## 4. Pre-injection checks (every dose)
1. Right patient, right vial, right concentration, right volume — two-person check
   when changing vials.
2. Ask: any reaction to the last dose (large local or systemic)? Current asthma
   symptoms, fever, new medicines (beta-blockers)? Recent high pollen exposure?
3. Peak flow or symptom check in asthmatics; **defer if asthma is unstable**.
4. Interval since last dose — late doses need reduction per protocol.
5. Inject subcutaneously, posterior upper arm; aspirate per local practice.
6. **Observe 30 minutes** (longer per local policy) with adrenaline on hand.

## 5. Adjusting after reactions or gaps
- **Large local reaction** (e.g. > ~10 cm, varies by protocol): repeat or step back one dose.
- **Systemic reaction:** treat (IM adrenaline if anaphylaxis); then reduce substantially
  (commonly back 2–3 steps or to a fraction of the dose) and reassess suitability.
- **Missed doses:** reduce according to the time elapsed per the product schedule;
  very long gaps mean restarting build-up.
- Pollen season: many hold the dose constant rather than escalate.

## 6. SLIT practicalities
- First dose in clinic with 30 min observation; then daily at home.
- Start grass tablets ≥ ~2–4 months before the season; continue year-round.
- Oral itch/swelling in the first weeks is expected; stop and seek review for throat
  swelling, breathing difficulty or severe GI symptoms.
- Hold during oral wounds, extractions or eosinophilic oesophagitis symptoms.

## Gotchas
- Wrong-vial errors cause most serious SCIT reactions — colour coding and checks matter.
- Patients leaving early after injections; enforce the observation period.
- Expecting results in weeks: benefit typically builds over the first year.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication matched to history + test result; asthma controlled.
- [ ] Vial, concentration, volume, interval double-checked and recorded.
- [ ] 30-minute observation completed and documented.
- [ ] Reaction-based dose adjustment follows the written protocol.
- [ ] Annual review of benefit, adherence, and planned duration.
