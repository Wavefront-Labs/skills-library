# Asthma and COPD stepwise management

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** pick the right inhaler class for the diagnosis and severity, make sure it is
actually being inhaled, and escalate on evidence (exacerbations, eosinophils) rather
than on habit. GINA and GOLD are revised yearly — confirm the current edition.

## 1. Confirm the diagnosis before escalating
- **Asthma:** variable symptoms + objective variable airflow limitation (BD
  reversibility ≥ 12% and ≥ 200 mL in FEV1, PEF variability, FeNO, or a positive
  challenge test). Testing is easier before ICS is started.
- **COPD:** post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70 (or < LLN) with exposure history.
- **Overlap features** (COPD with high eosinophils, big reversibility, atopy): treat the
  asthma component — ICS is mandatory if asthma is present.

## 2. Asthma (adults/adolescents, GINA "Track 1")
| Step | Preferred controller/reliever |
|---|---|
| 1–2 | As-needed low-dose ICS-formoterol (anti-inflammatory reliever) |
| 3 | Low-dose ICS-formoterol maintenance-and-reliever (MART) |
| 4 | Medium-dose ICS-formoterol MART |
| 5 | Add LAMA; phenotype; refer for biologic; consider high-dose ICS-formoterol |

- **SABA-only treatment is no longer recommended** in adults: SABA overuse (≥ 3
  canisters/year) predicts exacerbations and death.
- MART uses formoterol-containing inhalers only (budesonide-formoterol,
  beclometasone-formoterol); check the product's licensed maximum daily puffs.
- Step down after ~3 months of good control; never stop ICS completely in adults.

## 3. COPD (GOLD ABE groups)
- **Group A** (few symptoms, ≤ 1 moderate exacerbation, no hospitalisation): a
  bronchodilator.
- **Group B** (mMRC ≥ 2 or CAT ≥ 10): LABA + LAMA in one inhaler.
- **Group E** (≥ 2 moderate or ≥ 1 hospitalised exacerbation/year): LABA + LAMA; add
  ICS (triple therapy) if blood eosinophils ≥ 300 cells/µL. Eosinophils < 100 predict
  little ICS benefit and more pneumonia.
- Further options for persistent exacerbations: roflumilast (FEV1 < 50%, chronic
  bronchitis), azithromycin (ex-smokers; check QTc and hearing), dupilumab for
  type-2 COPD with eosinophils ≥ 300 (per current labelling).
- Non-drug core: smoking cessation, pulmonary rehabilitation after any admission,
  vaccines (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID-19, RSV, Tdap per local schedule).

## 4. Before every escalation — the checklist
1. **Watch them use the inhaler.** Most patients have at least one critical error.
   pMDI: shake, exhale away, slow deep breath 3–5 s, hold 10 s; use a spacer. DPI:
   fast, forceful inhalation; never exhale into the device.
2. **Adherence:** dispensing records beat self-report.
3. **Triggers and comorbidities:** smoking/vaping, rhinosinusitis, reflux, obesity,
   OSA, dysfunctional breathing, beta-blockers/NSAIDs in susceptible asthma.
4. **Right diagnosis** (vocal cord dysfunction and cardiac disease mimic both).

## 5. Exacerbations
- Asthma: increase reliever, oral prednisolone ~40–50 mg daily for 5–7 days is a
  typical adult course (verify locally); written action plan for every patient.
- COPD: short-acting bronchodilators, prednisolone 40 mg for 5 days, antibiotics if
  increased sputum purulence; review within 4 weeks of discharge.
- Assess severity objectively: SpO2, PEF/FEV1, ability to speak, blood gas if SpO2 < 92%
  or hypercapnia risk. Use controlled oxygen (target 88–92%) in COPD with CO2 risk.

## 6. Severe asthma and biologics
Refer if uncontrolled at Step 4–5 despite correct technique and adherence. Phenotype:
blood eosinophils, FeNO, total/specific IgE, OCS bursts. Anti-IL5/5R (mepolizumab,
benralizumab), anti-IL4R (dupilumab), anti-IgE (omalizumab), anti-TSLP (tezepelumab)
each have specific eligibility thresholds that vary by country/payer — check them.

## Gotchas
- Switching device types without teaching — a new device means a new technique.
- ICS in COPD with low eosinophils: pneumonia risk without benefit.
- Labelling a patient "controlled" because they don't complain; ask about reliever use
  and night waking.
- Chest X-ray isn't needed for every exacerbation, but is when pneumonia/pneumothorax
  is plausible.
