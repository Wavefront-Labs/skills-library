# Polysomnography interpretation and CPAP management

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** turn a sleep-study report into a correct diagnosis and severity, then get the
patient on PAP that is used and effective — proven from the device download.

## 1. Choose the test
- **Home sleep apnoea test (HSAT):** fine for uncomplicated adults with high pretest
  probability of moderate–severe OSA (snoring, witnessed apnoeas, sleepiness, STOP-BANG
  high). A **negative or technically poor HSAT does not exclude OSA** — proceed to PSG.
- **In-lab PSG:** significant cardiorespiratory disease, neuromuscular disease, opioid
  use, suspected central apnoea or hypoventilation, other sleep disorders (PLMD,
  parasomnia, narcolepsy), children.

## 2. Reading the report
- **AHI** = (apnoeas + hypopnoeas) / hours of sleep (PSG). HSAT uses **REI** per hour of
  *recording* time, which underestimates true AHI.
- Adult severity: **5–14 mild, 15–29 moderate, ≥ 30 severe**.
- **Hypopnoea rule matters:** AASM recommended rule = ≥ 30% flow drop with ≥ 3%
  desaturation **or** arousal; the "4% rule" (used by US Medicare) gives lower AHIs.
  Check which was used before comparing studies.
- Look beyond AHI: supine vs non-supine AHI, REM AHI, ODI, **time with SpO2 < 88–90%**
  (T90), nadir SpO2, central vs obstructive events, Cheyne–Stokes, PLM index, sleep
  efficiency and stage distribution (an AHI from 2 h of mostly non-REM sleep may
  underestimate).
- Sustained hypoxaemia without discrete events → hypoventilation: check bicarbonate,
  blood gas, consider transcutaneous/end-tidal CO2.

## 3. Who to treat
PAP is indicated for moderate–severe OSA, and for mild OSA with symptoms (sleepiness,
insomnia, impaired QoL) or significant comorbidity. Alternatives: mandibular
advancement device (mild–moderate), positional therapy (positional OSA), weight loss
(including pharmacotherapy where licensed for OSA), hypoglossal nerve stimulation
(selected CPAP failures, BMI and DISE criteria), surgery in selected anatomy.

## 4. Starting CPAP — a typical APAP setup
- Auto-titrating PAP is acceptable for uncomplicated OSA. A common start: **min 4–5,
  max 15–20 cmH2O**, ramp auto, humidification on, EPR/flex 1–3 for comfort. Fixed CPAP
  or in-lab titration for heart failure, COPD with hypoxaemia, hypoventilation, opioids.
- **Mask:** nasal or nasal-pillows first for most; full-face if mouth breathing or nasal
  obstruction. Fit with the patient lying down at pressure.

## 5. Reading the download (2–4 weeks, then periodically)
| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Usage | ≥ 4 h/night on ≥ 70% of nights (minimum; more is better) |
| Residual AHI (device) | < 5–10/h |
| Leak | Below the manufacturer's threshold (e.g. ResMed 95th centile < 24 L/min) |
| 95th-centile pressure | If at max pressure → raise max; if far below → narrow range |

- **High residual AHI:** check leak first, then the central-apnoea index (treatment-
  emergent central apnoea, opioids, heart failure) — consider ASV only after
  echo excludes HFrEF with LVEF ≤ 45% (SERVE-HF harm signal).
- **Device AHI is an estimate** — if symptoms persist, repeat a study on PAP.

## 6. Adherence fixes
Dry mouth → chin strap or full-face, more humidity. Nasal congestion → heated tube,
intranasal steroid. Aerophagia → lower max pressure, EPR. Claustrophobia → desensitise
by wearing while awake. Early follow-up in the first week has the biggest effect.

## Gotchas
- Using HSAT in suspected hypoventilation or central apnoea.
- Comparing AHIs scored with different hypopnoea rules.
- Not counselling about **drowsy driving** — check the local licensing rules for
  untreated OSA with excessive sleepiness.
- Persistent sleepiness on adequate PAP: look for insufficient sleep, other disorders,
  depression, medications before prescribing wake-promoting agents.

## Verify
- [ ] Test type appropriate; AHI/REI and scoring rule recorded.
- [ ] Oxygen burden and central events reviewed, not just AHI.
- [ ] Download checked: usage, leak, residual AHI, 95th-centile pressure.
- [ ] Driving advice documented.
- [ ] Symptoms re-assessed (Epworth) at follow-up.
