# Flexible bronchoscopy and EBUS staging

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** get a diagnostic sample and an accurate mediastinal stage in one safe procedure,
so the patient is not re-scoped or wrongly sent to surgery.

## 1. Before the scope
- **Indication fit:** central lesion or adenopathy → bronchoscopy/EBUS; peripheral
  nodule < 2 cm without a bronchus sign → consider navigational/robotic bronchoscopy or
  CT-guided biopsy instead (higher yield for many peripheral lesions).
- **Staging first:** in suspected lung cancer with mediastinal nodes on CT/PET, plan EBUS
  so the *first* biopsy gives both diagnosis and the highest stage.
- **Checks:** platelets and INR when biopsy planned; hold anticoagulants per local
  periprocedural protocol; clopidogrel/ticagrelor usually held before transbronchial
  biopsy (aspirin alone generally acceptable) — confirm with cardiology if recent stent.
  Spirometry/ABG if severe COPD; consent covering pneumothorax, bleeding, hypoxia.
- **Sedation:** midazolam + short-acting opioid (fentanyl/alfentanil) or propofol with
  anaesthesia support; topical lidocaine — track cumulative dose (BTS suggests a ceiling
  around 8.2 mg/kg in adults; verify locally).

## 2. Airway survey (every time, before sampling)
Inspect systematically in the same order — larynx/cords, trachea, carina, right then
left (or normal side first) to every segmental orifice. Note mucosal infiltration,
extrinsic compression, carinal widening. Inspect the **normal side first** to avoid
seeding and to keep the view before bleeding.

## 3. EBUS-TBNA staging order
- Map stations on CT/PET first (IASLC map: 2R/2L, 4R/4L, 7, 10, 11).
- **Sample N3 → N2 → N1** (contralateral first), changing/cleaning the needle between
  stations so a positive N1 cannot contaminate an N3 sample.
- Sample any node ≥ 5 mm in short axis at stations being staged, even if PET-negative —
  a "PET-negative" mediastinum in a central tumour or N1 disease still warrants EBUS.
- Technique: 21G or 22G needle, 10–15 agitations per pass, 3 passes per station is a
  common minimum without ROSE; with **ROSE** (rapid on-site evaluation) stop once
  adequate. Send extra passes for molecular markers (EGFR, ALK, PD-L1).
- Negative EBUS with high suspicion → surgical staging (mediastinoscopy) before
  resection; EBUS NPV is not perfect.

## 4. Other sampling
- **BAL:** wedge in the most affected segment, instil 3–5 × 20–60 mL saline,
  aim for > 30% return; send cell count, cultures, cytology as indicated.
- **Transbronchial biopsy:** fluoroscopy reduces pneumothorax; 4–6 samples for diffuse
  disease. Ask the patient about pleuritic pain during forceps closure.

## 5. Complications
- **Bleeding:** keep the scope wedged, cold saline, dilute adrenaline (e.g. 1:10,000
  topical, small aliquots), tranexamic acid topical; turn bleeding side down; escalate
  early for massive haemoptysis (airway protection, IR).
- **Hypoxia:** supplemental O2 or high-flow nasal; pause procedure.
- **Pneumothorax:** post-TBB chest x-ray (or ultrasound) if symptoms or per local policy.

## Gotchas
- Sampling the obvious tumour first, then the nodes with the same needle.
- Calling an EBUS "negative" on a lymphocyte-poor aspirate — that is non-diagnostic.
- Forgetting molecular material: a diagnosis without enough tissue for biomarkers means
  repeat procedure.
- Lidocaine toxicity in long cases (perioral tingling, seizures).

## Verify
- [ ] Stations mapped pre-procedure and sampled N3 → N2 → N1.
- [ ] Each sample labelled with IASLC station; adequacy (lymphocytes/ROSE) recorded.
- [ ] Molecular/PD-L1 material sent where cancer suspected.
- [ ] Sedation and lidocaine totals documented; post-procedure observation done.
- [ ] Result-driven next step agreed in MDT (resection vs further staging vs systemic).
