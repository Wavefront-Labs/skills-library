# Dyspnea and chronic cough workup

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** turn "short of breath" or "cough for months" into a short differential and a
test sequence that answers it, without missing the dangerous causes.

## 1. Frame the complaint first
- **Tempo:** acute (hours–days) is a different problem (PE, pneumothorax, pneumonia,
  heart failure, anaphylaxis) and belongs in emergency pathways. This skill covers
  **chronic**: dyspnea > 4–8 weeks, cough > 8 weeks in adults (3–8 weeks = subacute,
  often post-infectious).
- **Grade it:** mMRC 0–4 for dyspnea; ask what the patient can no longer do (stairs,
  shopping) — function tracks better than adjectives.
- **Heart vs lung vs other:** roughly two-thirds of chronic dyspnea is cardiac or
  pulmonary; also consider anaemia, deconditioning, obesity, thyroid, neuromuscular
  weakness and anxiety/dysfunctional breathing.

## 2. History pivots that change the plan
| Clue | Points toward |
|---|---|
| Orthopnoea, PND, oedema | Heart failure |
| Episodic wheeze, triggers, atopy, nocturnal cough | Asthma |
| > 10–20 pack-years, sputum, age > 40 | COPD (also lung cancer) |
| Exertional dyspnea + dry crackles, clubbing | ILD |
| Exposures: birds, moulds, asbestos, silica, farming | Hypersensitivity pneumonitis / pneumoconiosis |
| ACE inhibitor (any duration) | Drug cough — stop it; resolution may take up to ~4 weeks |
| Post-nasal drip, throat clearing | Upper-airway cough syndrome |
| Heartburn, cough with meals/talking | Reflux-associated cough |
| Platypnoea, orthodeoxia | Shunt (hepatopulmonary, PFO) |

**Red flags:** haemoptysis, weight loss, fever/night sweats, new cough in a smoker
> 40, hoarseness, dysphagia, stridor, persistent focal signs, recurrent pneumonia in
the same lobe. Any of these moves imaging (usually CT) forward.

## 3. First-line tests (most patients)
1. **Chest radiograph** — baseline for both complaints.
2. **Spirometry with bronchodilator** — see
   `health-medicine.pulmonology.pulmonary-function-systems.spirometry-quality-and-interpretation`.
   Normal spirometry does not exclude asthma; consider FeNO or bronchial challenge.
3. **Bloods:** FBC (anaemia, eosinophils), TSH, renal function, NT-proBNP/BNP for
   suspected heart failure (a normal value makes HF unlikely; elevated values are
   non-specific in renal failure, AF and age).
4. **Resting and ambulatory SpO2** — desaturation on a walk test that the resting value
   hides is common in ILD and pulmonary vascular disease.
5. **ECG ± echocardiogram** when cardiac features or a raised BNP.

## 4. Second-line, guided by the above
- **Full PFTs** (lung volumes, DLCO): restriction or isolated low DLCO → think ILD,
  pulmonary vascular disease, anaemia.
- **HRCT chest** for suspected ILD (prone and expiratory images), bronchiectasis, or
  any red flag. Use contrast CT-PA only for PE questions.
- **CPET** for unexplained dyspnea after normal first-line tests: distinguishes
  cardiac, ventilatory, deconditioning and dysfunctional breathing patterns.
- **Chronic cough specific:** treat the likely trait sequentially (stop ACEi; trial of
  inhaled corticosteroid if eosinophilic/asthma features; intranasal therapy for upper
  airway; reflux therapy only if reflux symptoms). If refractory, think cough
  hypersensitivity syndrome (neuromodulators per specialist guidance) and refer.

## 5. Incidental pulmonary nodules
Use Fleischner (incidental, age ≥ 35, not cancer-screening or known cancer). Example
for a solitary solid nodule in a low-risk patient: < 6 mm → no routine follow-up;
6–8 mm → CT at 6–12 months; > 8 mm → consider CT at 3 months, PET-CT or tissue.
Screening-detected nodules use Lung-RADS instead. **Check the current tables** — the
thresholds differ for subsolid and multiple nodules and for high-risk patients.

## Gotchas
- Labelling COPD without spirometry; up to a third of "COPD" diagnoses don't meet
  FEV1/FVC criteria.
- Mixed causes are the norm: obesity + deconditioning + mild COPD + HF.
- A normal chest X-ray does not exclude ILD, PE, early cancer or bronchiectasis.
- Chronic cough with normal tests in a non-smoker: still examine the ear canal
  (Arnold's reflex) and ask about medications.
- Over-reliance on D-dimer in outpatients: it rules out, never rules in.

## Verify
- [ ] Tempo, severity grade and red flags documented.
- [ ] CXR + spirometry (with bronchodilator) done or justified as not needed.
- [ ] Cardiac causes considered (BNP/ECG/echo) when features present.
- [ ] ACE inhibitor and exposure history explicitly asked.
- [ ] Nodule follow-up interval taken from the current guideline table, not memory.
