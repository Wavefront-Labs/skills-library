# PE risk stratification and pleural effusion workup

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** diagnose or exclude PE without over-scanning, match treatment intensity to
risk, and work up a pleural effusion to a cause and a drainage decision.

## Part A — Suspected pulmonary embolism

### 1. Pretest probability first
- **Haemodynamically unstable** (SBP < 90 mmHg or vasopressors): bedside echo for RV
  dilatation → if CTPA not immediately feasible and RV strain present, treat as
  high-risk PE.
- Stable: Wells (or revised Geneva). **PE unlikely** → apply **PERC** in low-risk ED
  patients (all 8 negative = no test); otherwise **D-dimer**, using an
  **age-adjusted cut-off (age × 10 µg/L FEU over 50)** or YEARS algorithm.
- **PE likely** or positive D-dimer → CTPA. V/Q if contrast contraindicated or in
  pregnancy (with local protocol).

### 2. Risk class once confirmed (ESC)
| Class | Features | Setting / therapy |
|---|---|---|
| High | Shock / hypotension | Systemic thrombolysis unless contraindicated; surgical or catheter therapy if not |
| Intermediate-high | PESI III–V or sPESI ≥ 1 **plus** RV dysfunction on imaging **and** raised troponin | Anticoagulate, monitor closely (ICU/HDU) for decompensation; rescue reperfusion |
| Intermediate-low | PESI III–V/sPESI ≥ 1 with one or neither of RV/troponin | Admit, anticoagulate |
| Low | PESI I–II / sPESI 0 | Consider early discharge / outpatient with DOAC (Hestia also used) |

- DOACs are first-line for most; avoid in antiphospholipid syndrome (triple-positive),
  severe renal impairment, pregnancy. LMWH in cancer is acceptable; some DOACs studied.
- Duration: provoked by major transient factor → 3 months; unprovoked or persistent
  risk → consider extended.
- Follow-up at 3–6 months: persistent dyspnoea → echo, V/Q for **CTEPH** (V/Q is more
  sensitive than CTPA for chronic disease).

## Part B — Pleural effusion

### 1. Is it worth tapping?
Bilateral, small, in clear heart failure → treat and observe; tap if atypical (fever,
pleuritic pain, asymmetric, not resolving). Unilateral new effusion → tap under
**ultrasound guidance**.

### 2. Send
Appearance, protein, LDH (with serum), **pH (blood-gas analyser, anaerobic syringe)**,
glucose, cell count/differential, Gram stain and culture (+ blood-culture bottles),
cytology (≥ 25–50 mL; a second sample increases yield), adenosine deaminase if TB risk.

### 3. Interpret
- **Light's criteria** — exudate if any: fluid/serum protein > 0.5, fluid/serum LDH > 0.6,
  fluid LDH > 2/3 upper limit of serum normal. Diuretic-treated transudates can falsely
  look exudative → check **serum-fluid albumin gradient > 12 g/L** (transudate).
- Parapneumonic: **pH < 7.2** (or glucose < 3.3 mmol/L, pus, positive Gram stain) →
  chest drain. Loculated → consider intrapleural tPA/DNase; surgical opinion if failing.
- Lymphocytic exudate: malignancy, TB, post-CABG. Malignant recurrent → indwelling
  pleural catheter or talc pleurodesis.

## Gotchas
- Ordering D-dimer in high-probability patients (a negative does not exclude).
- Forgetting the age-adjusted cut-off → unnecessary CTPAs in elderly.
- Sending pleural pH through the lab analyser or with air in the syringe (lidocaine
  and air both alter pH).
- Draining > 1.5 L at once → re-expansion pulmonary oedema; stop on chest tightness.

## Verify
- [ ] Pretest probability documented before any test.
- [ ] ESC risk class recorded with sPESI, RV imaging and troponin.
- [ ] Anticoagulant choice checked against renal function, interactions, pregnancy.
- [ ] Pleural sample: pH via blood-gas, paired serum protein/LDH sent.
- [ ] Drainage decision follows pH/glucose/pus criteria, not volume alone.
