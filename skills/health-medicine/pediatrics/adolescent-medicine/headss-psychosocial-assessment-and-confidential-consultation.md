# HEEADSSS psychosocial assessment and the confidential consultation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, local safeguarding procedures, or the law on minor consent in your jurisdiction.

**Goal:** the biggest threats to adolescent health are behavioural (injury, self-harm,
substances, sex, eating, violence). You only learn about them if the young person trusts
the room — so structure the consult to earn that, then screen systematically.

## 1. Set the frame (first 2 minutes)
- From early adolescence (~12), routinely see the young person **alone for part of every
  visit**; tell parents this is standard practice for everyone ("it's how I see all
  teenagers"), not a sign of suspicion.
- State confidentiality **and its limits** to both, in plain words: "What we talk about stays
  between us, unless I'm worried you or someone else is at serious risk of harm — then I'd
  talk to you first about who needs to know."
- Know your local law: which services a minor can consent to (sexual health, contraception,
  mental health, substance treatment); competence frameworks (UK **Gillick/Fraser**; US
  state mature-minor and minor-consent statutes). Note billing/EOB statements and patient
  portals can leak confidential care to parents — check how your system handles it.

## 2. HEEADSSS — start safe, end sensitive
| Domain | Opening questions |
|---|---|
| **H**ome | Who lives with you? How do you get on? Ever felt unsafe at home? |
| **E**ducation/Employment | School going OK? Grades changed? Bullied? |
| **E**ating | Feelings about your body/weight? Dieting, skipping meals, vomiting, laxatives? |
| **A**ctivities | What do you do for fun? Friends? Screen and social-media time, sleep? |
| **D**rugs | Friends using? You? Alcohol, vaping, cannabis, other? (CRAFFT if yes) |
| **S**exuality | Relationships? Attracted to anyone? Sexually active — consensual? Contraception, STI testing? |
| **S**uicide/depression | Mood, sleep, hopelessness; self-harm; thoughts of ending life |
| **S**afety | Seatbelts, helmets, driving after drinking, weapons, online exploitation, violence |
Normalise ("Lots of people your age…"), ask open then specific questions, avoid assuming
gender of partners, and use the young person's terms. Highlight **strengths** — protective factors matter as much as risks.

## 3. Validated tools to plug in
- Depression: **PHQ-A** (annual universal screening from 12 recommended in the US).
- Suicide: **ASQ** (4 items) — any yes → brief safety assessment the same day; acute positive → emergency evaluation, do not leave the young person alone.
- Substance use: **CRAFFT 2.1** (score ≥2 suggests a problem).
- Eating disorders: SCOFF as a prompt; check weight trajectory, HR (bradycardia), orthostatic vitals, temperature.

## 4. When confidentiality must be broken
Serious risk of harm to self (active suicidal intent/plan), to others, or evidence of
**abuse or exploitation** (including sexual activity below the age of consent or with a
large age gap/power imbalance — thresholds are jurisdiction-specific). Tell the young
person first where safe, explain what will happen, involve them in how parents are told,
and follow the safeguarding procedure. Document reasoning.

## 5. Eating-disorder and transition red flags
Admit/escalate for: HR <50 awake, BP <80/50, orthostatic changes, temp <35.5 °C,
electrolyte disturbance, syncope, rapid weight loss or acute food refusal (thresholds
vary by guideline — check local criteria). Start **transition planning** to adult services
from ~14, with a named person and skills training (booking appointments, own medicines).

## Gotchas
- Asking parents to step out *after* a sensitive topic emerges looks like an accusation; build it in from the start.
- "Do you do drugs?" gets "no"; "Which of your friends vape?" opens the topic.
- Document confidentially (some EHRs allow adolescent-confidential notes); avoid putting sensitive disclosures in sections visible to parents' proxy access.

## Verify
- [ ] Confidentiality and its limits explained to adolescent and parent.
- [ ] Time alone given; HEEADSSS completed and documented including strengths.
- [ ] PHQ-A/ASQ/CRAFFT done where indicated and positives acted on the same day.
- [ ] Any confidentiality breach followed procedure and was discussed with the young person.
- [ ] Transition planning started for chronic-condition patients from early teens.
