# Febrile young infant (≤60 days) evaluation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references. Antibiotic/antiviral choice and weight-based doses must come from the current local neonatal/pediatric formulary.

**Goal:** find the ~10% of febrile young infants with a bacterial infection (mostly UTI;
~2–3% bacteraemia or meningitis = invasive bacterial infection, IBI) without lumbar-
puncturing and admitting every one of them.

## 1. Who this framework applies to
- Rectal temperature **≥38.0 °C** at home or in clinic (a documented home fever counts),
  **age 8–60 days, born ≥37 weeks, well-appearing** (AAP 2021 CPG inclusion criteria; age bands 8–21, 22–28 and 29–60 days confirmed via PMC9986852; the other inclusion details are (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- **Out of scope — treat as high risk:** ill-appearing (full sepsis workup, LP, antibiotics,
  admit), age ≤7 days, preterm, focal bacterial infection (cellulitis, omphalitis), known
  immunodeficiency, recent antibiotics, or congenital/surgical complexity.
- Clinical bronchiolitis or a positive viral test lowers but does **not eliminate** UTI risk.

## 2. Core tests for every in-scope infant
- **Urinalysis + urine culture** (catheter or suprapubic sample; bag specimens only to screen, never to culture).
- **Blood culture.**
- **Inflammatory markers (IMs):** procalcitonin (preferred), ANC, CRP, and temperature.
  AAP 2021 abnormal thresholds: **CRP >20 mg/L (2.0 mg/dL), ANC >4,000/mm³, temperature >38.5 °C** (confirmed as the CPG's no-procalcitonin set in Hosp Pediatr 2023;13:e47, PMC9986852); **procalcitonin >0.5 ng/mL** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); the 5,200 ANC variant is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Confirm units in your lab.

## 3. Age-banded pathway (AAP 2021, summarised)
| Age | LP | Empiric antibiotics | Disposition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 8–21 d (the AAP CPG table, (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) | **Yes, all** | Yes, all | Admit |
| 22–28 d | If any IM abnormal: yes. IMs normal: may forgo | IM abnormal or pleocytosis/UA+: yes | IMs abnormal → admit; normal → may observe at home **only** with reliable follow-up in 24 h |
| 29–60 d | IM abnormal: may perform; IMs normal: may forgo | UA+ alone: oral/parenteral UTI treatment; IM abnormal: parenteral | IMs normal → home with follow-up in 24–36 h is acceptable |
If antibiotics are given before an LP is done, do the LP first unless delay harms the
baby — a pretreated non-diagnostic CSF forces a full meningitis course.

## 4. Neonatal HSV — do not forget it
Add HSV PCR (blood, CSF, surface swabs, consider ALT) and **empiric acyclovir** when:
maternal genital HSV/lesions around delivery, vesicles, seizures, hypothermia,
CSF pleocytosis with negative Gram stain, raised transaminases, or ill appearance —
especially in the first 3 weeks. Peak onset is the first month.

## 5. Discharge safety
Home observation needs: well appearance on reassessment, normal IMs, feeding well,
caregivers who understand return signs, **a confirmed follow-up within 24 h**, and a
system to call back on positive cultures. Without all of these, admit.

## Red flags / escalate
Poor perfusion, apnoea, hypothermia (<36 °C is as worrying as fever), lethargy, bulging
fontanelle, seizures, petechiae, persistent tachycardia → treat as ill-appearing and escalate.

## Gotchas
- Pyuria alone with a high-risk picture still needs culture-driven treatment; a negative UA does not rule out bacteraemia.
- PECARN rule (UA, ANC >4,090, PCT >1.71 ng/mL (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) is an alternative for 29–60 days; know which one your unit uses.
- Over-bundling clothes can raise temperature modestly but do not dismiss a recorded fever on that basis.
- Antibiotic regimens differ by age (e.g. coverage for Listeria/GBS in the youngest) — use local pathway; not specified here deliberately.

## Verify
- [ ] Infant confirmed in-scope (age, term, well-appearing) before using the low-risk branches.
- [ ] Catheter urine sent for culture; blood culture and procalcitonin/CRP/ANC obtained.
- [ ] LP decision made by age band and IMs, and documented.
- [ ] HSV risk factors reviewed; acyclovir started if any present.
- [ ] Discharge only with 24 h follow-up arranged and culture callback owner named.
