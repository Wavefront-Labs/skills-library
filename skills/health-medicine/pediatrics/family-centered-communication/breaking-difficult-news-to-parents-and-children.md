# Breaking difficult news to parents and children

> General professional reference for clinicians. Safeguarding conversations follow your local child-protection procedure and are only touched on here.

**Goal:** parents remember for decades *how* they were told. Deliver the news clearly,
make it survivable, and leave the family with a next step — and include the child at a
level they can use rather than leaving them to guess from adult faces.

## 1. Prepare (SPIKES: Setting)
- Read the results and know the next steps before you walk in. Agree with colleagues
  who leads and what will be said (consistent message).
- Private room, phones silenced, enough chairs, tissues. **Both parents/carers** if
  they want; ask who they'd like present. Bring the nurse who will stay with the family.
- **Professional interpreter** if any doubt — never a sibling or the child.
- Decide with parents *beforehand* (briefly, if possible) how and when the child will be told.

## 2. The conversation (SPIKES adapted)
1. **Perception:** "What have you understood so far about why we did the scan?"
2. **Invitation:** "I have the results. Would it be OK to go through them now?"
3. **Knowledge:** warning shot ("I'm afraid the results are not what we hoped"), pause,
   then the headline in plain words — "Ben has leukaemia", not "the film showed blasts".
   Short chunks. Stop and let silence work.
4. **Emotions:** respond before giving more facts — information doesn't land in shock.
   NURSE: Name ("This is a huge shock"), Understand, Respect ("You've been
   incredibly attentive"), Support ("We will go through this with you"), Explore.
5. **Strategy & Summary:** what happens next (today, this week), who their contact is,
   what is uncertain. Check understanding with **teach-back**. Offer to write it down.

## 3. Talking with the child — match development, not age alone
- **Preschool (2–6):** concrete, short, about now; reassure it is not their fault (magical
  thinking — illness as punishment is common). Use dolls, drawings; involve a
  **child life/play specialist**.
- **School age (7–11):** simple mechanism ("the cells that fight germs have gone wrong"),
  what will happen to their body and routine, permission to ask anything.
- **Adolescents:** include them directly, often with parents and sometimes alone first;
  respect their emerging autonomy in decisions; ask what they want to know.
- Honesty with dosing: children cope better with true, age-sized information than with
  secrecy they detect. If parents want to withhold, explore their fear; explain the
  child's likely awareness; agree a plan rather than override (escalate to ethics if a
  competent adolescent's right to know conflicts with parents).

## 4. Specific situations
- **Uncertain prognosis:** "hope for the best, prepare for the rest"; give ranges, avoid
  false precision.
- **Death or life-limiting news:** use the words "died"/"dying"; offer time with the
  child; involve palliative care early; think of siblings and grandparents.
- **Safeguarding concerns:** be factual and non-accusatory, explain your duty and the
  process; do not interrogate — follow local procedure.

## 5. After
Leave a named contact and a booked follow-up (within 24–48 h for major news — families
retain little of the first meeting). Document who was present, what was said in the
family's words, their reaction, questions, and the plan. Debrief the team.

## Gotchas
- Euphemisms ("a growth", "lost") are misheard; jargon is unheard.
- Delivering news to one parent alone and leaving them to tell the other is avoidable harm.
- Standing at the end of the bed, glancing at the clock, or opening with a stack of leaflets all read as "I don't have time for you".
- Cultural and family norms about disclosure vary; ask rather than assume.

## Verify
- [ ] Setting prepared; right people present; interpreter booked if needed.
- [ ] Headline delivered in plain words after a warning shot; pauses left.
- [ ] Emotion acknowledged before further information.
- [ ] Plan for telling the child agreed and carried out at a developmental level.
- [ ] Teach-back done; follow-up and named contact given; conversation documented.
