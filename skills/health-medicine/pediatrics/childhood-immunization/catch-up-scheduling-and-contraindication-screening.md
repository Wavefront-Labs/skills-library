# Catch-up scheduling and contraindication screening

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for the current national schedule, product inserts, or local policy. Schedules change yearly — always build plans from the current official catch-up table.

**Goal:** get a behind or unknown-status child protected as fast as is safely allowed,
without invalid doses and without withholding vaccines for reasons that are not true
contraindications.

## 1. Establish what has actually been given
- Accept only **written, dated** records (registry/IIS, prior clinic, parent-held book).
  Parental recall is not acceptable for most vaccines (influenza and PPSV are exceptions in some guidance).
- Foreign records: accept if dates are plausible and doses match a recognised product;
  if doubtful, serology (e.g. measles, rubella, hepatitis B surface antibody) or revaccination
  are both acceptable — extra doses of most vaccines are safe.
- Unknown status = unvaccinated; start the catch-up schedule for current age.

## 2. Rules that make a dose valid
- **Minimum age and minimum interval** — a dose given too early is invalid and must be
  repeated (a 4-day grace period applies to most, but not to the 4-week (28-day) interval between
  two different live vaccines; CDC General Best Practice Guidelines, confirmed).
- **Never restart a series** because intervals were long: "a dose counts, pick up where you left off."
- **Live injectable vaccines** (MMR, varicella, yellow fever): give same day or ≥28 days apart.
- Age-limited vaccines: **rotavirus** has a maximum age for dose 1 (~14 weeks 6 days) and
  for the series (~8 months) (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); Hib and PCV catch-up needs fall with age and many older
  healthy children need fewer or no doses — read the table, don't extrapolate.
- **Give every due vaccine at the same visit** (coadministration is safe and the single
  biggest catch-up accelerator). Use separate anatomic sites (CDC, confirmed); the ≥2.5 cm same-limb spacing is (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 3. Screening: true contraindications vs precautions vs myths
- **Contraindication (do not give):** severe allergic reaction (anaphylaxis) to a prior dose
  or a vaccine component; encephalopathy within 7 days of pertussis vaccine (for further
  pertussis-containing doses); **live vaccines in severe immunodeficiency or pregnancy** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked);
  SCID or history of intussusception for rotavirus.
- **Precaution (weigh it):** moderate/severe acute illness with or without fever; recent
  antibody-containing blood products (delays MMR/varicella — interval depends on product
  and dose (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)); history of GBS within 6 weeks of a prior dose (CDC lists this as a precaution for influenza and tetanus-toxoid-containing vaccines); progressive neurological
  disorder (defer pertussis until stable).
- **Not contraindications:** mild illness/low fever, on antibiotics, prematurity (vaccinate
  by chronological age), breastfeeding, pregnant household contact, egg allergy for MMR
  or current influenza vaccines, family history of adverse events, local reaction to prior dose.
- Immunocompromised children (chemotherapy, high-dose steroids, biologics, asplenia) have
  separate schedules — liaise with the treating specialist.

## 4. The hesitancy conversation (short version)
Use a **presumptive** opening ("Today Sam is due for MMR and DTaP") rather than "What do you
want to do about vaccines?". If parents hesitate: ask what worries them, acknowledge,
give one clear fact, share your own recommendation, and keep the door open — document
the discussion and offer again at the next visit. Do not dismiss the family from care.

## Gotchas
- Combination vaccines can push a child over a component's maximum dose count — usually acceptable but check.
- DTaP vs Tdap: product depends on age (DTaP <7 y); a Tdap given inadvertently may or may not count — check guidance.
- Record lot, site, route, VIS/information given, and update the registry the same day.
- Observe 15 minutes post-vaccination (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (syncope in adolescents especially); anaphylaxis
  kit and current weight-based adrenaline dosing must be at hand — verify doses against local protocol.

## Verify
- [ ] Every counted dose is documented in writing and met minimum age/interval.
- [ ] Plan built from the current-year official catch-up table, not memory.
- [ ] All due vaccines offered at this visit; next-visit date set to the earliest valid interval.
- [ ] Contraindication screen recorded; any deferral reason is a real contraindication/precaution.
- [ ] Registry updated; hesitancy discussion documented if applicable.
