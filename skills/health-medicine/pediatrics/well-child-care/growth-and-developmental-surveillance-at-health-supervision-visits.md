# Growth and developmental surveillance at well-child visits

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current local guidelines, or the current periodicity schedule.

**Goal:** catch faltering growth, abnormal head growth and developmental delay early —
the conditions where a 6-month delay in recognition costs the child the most.

## 1. Measure properly (garbage in, garbage centile)
- Under 2 years: **recumbent length** on a length board, naked weight, occipitofrontal
  head circumference (OFC, largest of three). Over 2: standing height, stadiometer.
- Correct for prematurity (subtract weeks early from chronological age) until ~2 years.
- Chart choice: in the US, **WHO standards 0–2 y**, CDC charts 2–20 y; UK uses UK-WHO.
  Use condition-specific charts (Down syndrome, Turner) where they exist.

## 2. Interpret the trajectory, not the dot
- **One point means little.** Look for crossing **two major centile lines** (e.g. 50th →
  9th) after the first weeks — a trigger for assessment.
- Weight falling before length before OFC → nutritional (intake, absorption, losses).
  Length and weight down together with normal weight-for-length → consider familial short
  stature, constitutional delay, endocrine (GH, hypothyroid), syndromic causes.
- Mid-parental height target frames whether a small child is "small for the family".
- OFC: rapid upward crossing → hydrocephalus/raised ICP (check fontanelle, sunsetting);
  microcephaly or falling OFC → congenital infection, genetic, brain injury.
- BMI-for-age ≥85th = overweight, ≥95th = obesity (CDC definitions) — raise it
  non-judgementally and plan, not just label.

## 3. Development: surveillance at every visit, screening at set ages
- **Surveillance** (every visit): ask about parental concerns, take a milestone history,
  observe the child, note risk factors.
- **Standardised screening** (AAP schedule): general developmental tool such as **ASQ-3**
  at 9, 18 and 30 months; **autism-specific M-CHAT-R/F** at 18 and 24 months. A
  positive screen means "evaluate", not "diagnose".
- Hard red flags (refer without waiting):
  - **Any loss of previously acquired skills (regression)** at any age.
  - No social smile by ~3 months; not sitting by 9 months; not walking by 18 months.
  - No babbling by 12 months, no single words by 16 months, no 2-word phrases by 24 months.
  - Hand preference before 12–18 months (suggests weakness of the other side).
  - Persistent primitive reflexes, asymmetry, or abnormal tone.
- Refer in parallel: audiology for any speech concern, early-intervention services
  (in the US, Part C — a family can self-refer), and developmental pediatrics.

## 4. Other screening woven into the visit
Hearing (newborn screen result; later risk assessment), instrument-based vision or
red reflex and cover test, haemoglobin/lead risk per local policy, maternal depression
screen in the first months, oral health/fluoride varnish, and age-appropriate anticipatory
guidance (safe sleep, car seats, water safety, screen time, injury prevention).

## Gotchas
- Wrong chart or uncorrected prematurity is the commonest cause of a "faltering" alarm.
- Breastfed infants gain differently from formula-fed after ~3–4 months; WHO standards account for this, older CDC infant charts did not.
- "Wait and see" for speech delay costs months; hearing test first, always.
- Periodicity schedules and screening ages are revised; check the current Bright Futures
  table (or your national programme) rather than relying on the ages above.

## Verify
- [ ] Correct measurement technique and chart; prematurity corrected.
- [ ] Trajectory reviewed across all prior points, not just today's.
- [ ] Standardised developmental/autism screen done at the scheduled ages and scored.
- [ ] Any regression, positive screen or two-centile drop has a documented plan and referral.
- [ ] Anticipatory guidance and immunisation status addressed before the family leaves.
