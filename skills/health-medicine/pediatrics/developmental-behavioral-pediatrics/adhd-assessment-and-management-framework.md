# ADHD assessment and management framework (children and young people, NICE NG87)

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement or current guidelines. Medication choice, doses and titration come from the current formulary and product information, not from this skill.

**Goal:** reach a defensible ADHD diagnosis (or a better alternative), then manage with
parent support, environmental change and, only when criteria are met, specialist-led medication.

## 1. Recognition and referral
- In primary care, when behaviour or attention problems suggest ADHD, establish severity, the
  effect on the child and family, and how far problems pervade different settings.
- If development or family life is being harmed, consider group-based ADHD-focused support
  for parents or carers; this need not wait for a formal diagnosis.
- Persisting problems with at least moderate impairment -> refer to secondary care (child
  psychiatrist, paediatrician or specialist ADHD CAMHS); severe impairment -> refer directly.

## 2. Diagnosis
- Diagnosis is by a specialist and follows a full clinical and psychosocial assessment,
  including behaviour and symptoms in the different domains and settings of everyday life.
- Criteria: those for hyperkinetic disorder in DSM-5 or ICD-11 (NICE does not recommend
  excluding on the basis of a pervasive developmental disorder or uncertain time of onset).
- Assess needs, coexisting conditions, social, family and educational circumstances and
  physical health; assess the parents' or carers' mental health too.
- Consider ADHD at any age, adjusting symptom criteria for age-appropriate behaviour.

## 3. Management by age
- **Under 5 years:** offer an ADHD-focused group parent-training programme as first-line
  treatment. If significant impairment persists across settings after environmental
  modifications are reviewed, seek advice from a specialist ADHD service with expertise in
  young children. Do not offer medication to any child under 5 without a second specialist
  opinion from such a service.
- **5 years and over and young people:** give information and ADHD-focused support to parents
  and carers (can be group based, as few as 1 or 2 sessions). If oppositional defiant or
  conduct disorder symptoms are present, offer a parent-training programme as well.
  Offer medication only if symptoms still cause persistent significant impairment in at least
  one domain after environmental modifications have been implemented and reviewed, the family has
  discussed information about ADHD, and a baseline assessment is done.
- Consider CBT for young people who benefited from medication but remain significantly impaired.
- Diet: ask about foods or drinks that appear to affect behaviour; if a link is clear, keep a
  diary and offer dietitian referral. Do not advise or offer fatty acid supplementation.

## 4. Before medication (baseline)
- Only a healthcare professional trained in ADHD should start medication.
- Confirm the person still meets criteria; review mental health and social circumstances
  (coexisting conditions, education, substance misuse and diversion risk, care needs); review
  physical health: history, current medication, height and weight against age norms, baseline
  pulse and blood pressure with a correct cuff size, and a cardiovascular assessment.
- ECG is not needed before stimulants, atomoxetine or guanfacine unless a cardiac feature
  below is present or a co-existing condition is treated with a medicine posing cardiac risk.
- Cardiology opinion first if: congenital heart disease or cardiac surgery; sudden death in a
  first-degree relative under 40 suggesting cardiac disease; breathlessness on exertion
  compared with peers; fainting on exertion or with fright or noise; rapid regular
  palpitations that start and stop suddenly; cardiac-sounding chest pain; signs of heart
  failure; a murmur; blood pressure hypertensive for adults.
- Blood pressure consistently above the 95th centile for age and height -> paediatric
  hypertension specialist before medication.

## 5. Transition
For young people 16 and over, plan a formal meeting between CAMHS or paediatrics and adult
services, with full information for the young person; the care programme approach can aid transfer.

## Gotchas
- Medication and monitoring schedules are in the full NICE guideline; read the current version.
- Inattention in a quiet, anxious girl is often missed; consider relative age within the school year.

## Verify

- [ ] Parent support or training offered before or alongside any medication.
- [ ] Under 5s: second specialist opinion obtained before medication.
- [ ] Baseline height, weight, pulse, blood pressure and cardiovascular assessment recorded.
