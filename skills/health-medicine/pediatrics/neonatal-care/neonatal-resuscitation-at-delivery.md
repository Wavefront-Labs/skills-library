# Neonatal resuscitation at delivery

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for certified NRP/NLS training, the current algorithm edition, or local protocol. Drug doses below are illustrative and must be checked against the current algorithm and local neonatal formulary.

**Goal:** about 1 in 10 newborns needs some help to breathe and well under 1% needs
compressions. **Ventilation is the intervention**; almost everything else exists to make
ventilation effective.

## 1. Before the birth
- Four pre-birth questions: gestational age? amniotic fluid clear? additional risk factors? umbilical cord management plan?
- Brief the team: leader, airway, monitoring/documentation. Check equipment: warmer on,
  suction, bag/T-piece with PEEP, correct-size masks, laryngeal mask, laryngoscope and
  tubes, pulse oximeter, ECG leads, O2 blender, drugs drawn up per protocol.
- Preterm <32 weeks: room warm, polyethylene wrap/bag without drying, hat, thermal mattress.

## 2. The first 60 seconds (golden minute)
- Term, good tone, breathing or crying → **delayed cord clamping** (at least 60 s in the 2025 AHA/AAP guidelines and NRP 9th edition; the 8th edition said 30-60 s), skin-to-skin, keep warm.
- Otherwise: warm, dry, stimulate, position airway (neutral, "sniffing"), clear secretions
  only if obstructing.
- **Apnoeic or gasping, or HR <100/min → start PPV within 60 s of birth.** Apply pulse
  oximeter to the **right hand** (preductal); consider ECG leads for accurate HR.
- Starting O2 (NRP 9th edition): 21% for ≥35 weeks; 21–30% for 32–34 weeks; ≥30% for <32 weeks
  (8th edition: 21–30% for all <35 weeks). Titrate to preductal targets: the 9th-edition table
  starts at 2 min 65–70%, then 3 min 70–75%, 4 min 75–80%, 5 min 80–85%, 10 min 85–95%
  (the 8th edition also listed 1 min 60–65%).

## 3. Make ventilation effective
- The key sign PPV is working is a **rising heart rate**; chest rise is the second.
- If HR not rising: **MR SOPA** — Mask adjust, Reposition head, Suction mouth/nose, Open
  mouth, Pressure increase, Alternative airway (laryngeal mask or ETT).
- Rate 30–60 breaths/min in the 2025 guidelines and NRP 9th edition (8th edition: 40–60). In the 9th edition the corrective steps start if HR has not risen and the chest is not moving 15–30 s after starting ventilation (8th: 15 s); a laryngeal mask is an acceptable primary interface for babies over 34 weeks. Once an alternative airway is in, use CO2 detection to confirm placement.

## 4. Compressions and drugs
- **HR <60/min after ≥30 s of effective ventilation (ideally through an alternative airway)**
  → chest compressions, two-thumb technique, lower third of sternum, **3:1 ratio**
  (90 compressions + 30 breaths/min), increase O2 to 100%.
- HR still <60 after 60 s of compressions → **adrenaline (epinephrine) IV/IO preferred**
  via emergent umbilical venous catheter; the NRP 8th-edition IV dose was 0.02 mg/kg
  (range 0.01–0.03) of 0.1 mg/mL, repeat every 3–5 min; endotracheal route is less
  effective (higher dose). The 9th edition revises epinephrine dose, route and interval, and
  the compression guidance; I could not retrieve those values, so **take every dose and the
  compression details from the current NRP 9th edition algorithm and local formulary.**
- Suspected hypovolaemia (pallor, poor perfusion, history of blood loss) → volume
  (normal saline or O-negative blood) per protocol.
- Persistent failure: re-check ventilation (most common cause), consider pneumothorax,
  airway malformation, congenital heart disease; call senior/neonatal transport early.

## 5. After the resuscitation
Post-resuscitation care in a neonatal unit: glucose, temperature (target ~36.5–37.5 °C),
blood gas, and screening for **hypoxic–ischaemic encephalopathy** — therapeutic
hypothermia eligibility has a time window (generally within 6 h) so assess and refer early.
Debrief the team and talk to the parents.

## Gotchas
- Routine intubation and tracheal suction for non-vigorous meconium-stained babies is no longer recommended — ventilate.
- Apgar scores describe; they do not drive actions.
- A leaking mask seal is the commonest reason for failed PPV — two-person mask hold fixes many.
- This skill was updated for the NRP 9th edition (released November 2025, in use by instructors from 1 June 2026; the 9th edition also dropped suction from the initial steps) for cord clamping, ventilation rate, oxygen and SpO2 targets, checked against AAP/AHA and CPS summaries. Epinephrine and compression details were not retrievable and still follow the 8th edition; confirm against the current algorithm.
- Decisions about extreme prematurity or non-initiation need senior and parental involvement before birth wherever possible.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-birth questions asked, team briefed, equipment checked before delivery.
- [ ] PPV started by 60 s in an apnoeic/bradycardic baby; HR response assessed.
- [ ] MR SOPA steps used and documented before escalating to compressions.
- [ ] O2 titrated to preductal targets; temperature maintained.
- [ ] Post-resuscitation HIE screen and neonatal team handover completed.
