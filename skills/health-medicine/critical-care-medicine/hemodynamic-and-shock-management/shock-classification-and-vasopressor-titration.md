# Shock classification, fluid responsiveness and vasopressor titration

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** within the first hour, name the shock type, restore perfusion pressure, give fluid only to patients who will respond to it, and titrate vasoactive drugs against perfusion markers rather than a single number.

## 1. Recognise shock
Shock is inadequate tissue oxygen delivery, not just low blood pressure. Look for:
- **Hypotension:** MAP <65 mmHg or SBP <90 (or a fall of >40 from baseline).
- **Hypoperfusion:** lactate >2 mmol/L, capillary refill time >3 s, mottling, oliguria <0.5 mL/kg/h, new confusion.
Normotensive patients with these signs are in (compensated) shock.

## 2. Classify at the bedside (exam + POCUS, e.g. RUSH)
| Type | Clues | Echo pattern |
|---|---|---|
| Distributive (sepsis, anaphylaxis, neurogenic) | Warm peripheries, wide pulse pressure early | Hyperdynamic LV, small/collapsing IVC |
| Hypovolaemic / haemorrhagic | Cold, flat neck veins, bleeding or losses | Small hyperdynamic LV, flat IVC |
| Cardiogenic | Cold, wet, raised JVP, ischaemic ECG | Poor LV/RV function, plethoric IVC |
| Obstructive (PE, tamponade, tension pneumothorax) | Raised JVP, clear lungs | Dilated RV, pericardial effusion with collapse, absent lung sliding |
Mixed shock is common (e.g. septic cardiomyopathy); reassess after each intervention. Obstructive causes need a specific fix (drain, decompress, thrombolyse) more than drugs.

## 3. Fluids: test before you give
1. Initial resuscitation in sepsis or hypovolaemia: crystalloid boluses (balanced solution preferred), 250-500 mL over 10-15 min, reassessing after each.
2. Beyond the first litres, **test fluid responsiveness**: a rise in stroke volume or cardiac output **≥10-15%** predicts response.
   - **Passive leg raise:** semirecumbent to legs at 45°, measure SV/CO (or VTI on echo) within 60-90 s; do not use BP alone.
   - **Pulse pressure variation >12-13%** is valid only in controlled ventilation, VT ≥8 mL/kg, sinus rhythm, closed chest.
   - Mini fluid challenge 100-250 mL with VTI measurement.
3. Stop when not responsive, when oxygenation worsens, or when venous congestion appears (rising CVP, plethoric IVC, abnormal VExUS).

## 4. Vasopressors and inotropes
- **First-line: norepinephrine**, typical start 0.05-0.1 mcg/kg/min, titrated to **MAP ≥65 mmHg** (consider 60-65 in older patients; higher, e.g. 75-80, in chronic hypertension only if perfusion fails to improve). Start early, even before fluid is complete; peripheral administration via a good proximal cannula is acceptable short term with site checks (verify local protocol).
- **Second agent: vasopressin** 0.03 units/min (fixed dose) when norepinephrine reaches roughly 0.25-0.5 mcg/kg/min.
- **Hydrocortisone** 200 mg/day in divided doses or infusion for septic shock needing ongoing vasopressors (SSC 2021: norepinephrine ≥0.25 mcg/kg/min for ≥4 h).
- **Inotrope: dobutamine** 2.5-10 mcg/kg/min when cardiac output is low with adequate filling (cardiogenic or septic myocardial dysfunction). Epinephrine is an alternative or add-on.
- Doses here are common textbook ranges; check current references and local infusion concentrations.

## 5. Monitor and titrate
- Arterial line for any patient on more than low-dose vasopressor.
- Reassess every 30-60 min: MAP, CRT, urine output, mental state; lactate every 2-4 h aiming for clearance (fall ≥10-20% per 2 h). CRT-targeted resuscitation was non-inferior to lactate-targeted in ANDROMEDA-SHOCK.
- Escalate to cardiac-output monitoring (echo, pulse contour, PA catheter) when the phenotype is unclear or not responding.
- Persistent shock on rising doses: re-examine for missed source, bleeding, tamponade, pneumothorax, adrenal insufficiency, abdominal compartment syndrome. Refractory cardiogenic shock: refer early for mechanical circulatory support.

## Gotchas
- Lactate can be raised by epinephrine, liver failure or metformin; do not chase it alone.
- Fluid overload is a harm in its own right; cumulative positive balance is associated with mortality.
- MAP targets above 65 for everyone add arrhythmias without benefit (SEPSISPAM, 65 trial).
- Extravasation of vasopressors: stop, aspirate, consider phentolamine per local policy.

## Verify
- [ ] Shock type named, with exam and echo findings recorded.
- [ ] Each fluid bolus preceded or followed by a responsiveness check.
- [ ] MAP target written and individualised.
- [ ] Vasopressor dose trend, lactate and CRT reviewed at least every few hours.
- [ ] Second agent or steroid added at documented thresholds.
