# Sepsis recognition, the hour-1 bundle and source control

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** identify sepsis early, start the hour-1 bundle within 60 minutes of recognition, find and control the source, and then narrow therapy so the patient gets the shortest effective antimicrobial course.

## 1. Recognise
- **Sepsis (Sepsis-3):** suspected or confirmed infection plus an acute rise in **SOFA ≥2** from baseline (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Septic shock:** sepsis needing vasopressors to keep **MAP ≥65 mmHg** with **lactate >2 mmol/L** despite adequate fluid (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) (SSC 2021 recommends an initial MAP target of 65 mmHg in septic shock on vasopressors).
- **Screening:** qSOFA (RR ≥22, altered mentation, SBP ≤100; ≥2 positive (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) is a prognostic prompt but SSC 2021 Recommendation 2 is *against* using qSOFA instead of SIRS, NEWS or MEWS as a single screening tool; NEWS2 (≥5 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) or SIRS are more sensitive triggers. Use your institution's screening tool.
- Time zero = the moment sepsis is recognised (triage or chart review); record it.

## 2. Hour-1 bundle (SSC 2018, still the working standard)
| # | Action | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Lactate | Repeat within 2-4 h if >2 mmol/L (SSC 2018 bundle (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) |
| 2 | Blood cultures before antibiotics | At least 2 sets (aerobic + anaerobic), one per site; do not delay antibiotics >45 min to get them (SSC 2018 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) |
| 3 | Broad-spectrum antibiotics | Immediately, ideally within **1 h** of recognition for possible septic shock or high likelihood of sepsis (SSC 2021 Rec 12, strong); for possible sepsis without shock, rapid assessment of infectious vs non-infectious causes and a time-limited course of investigation, with antimicrobials within **3 h** if concern persists (Rec 13; the 3 h figure was in the 2021 text I did not locate verbatim (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) |
| 4 | Crystalloid | at least **30 mL/kg** IV crystalloid within the first 3 h for sepsis-induced hypoperfusion or septic shock (SSC 2021 Rec 5, weak, low-quality evidence); balanced crystalloids suggested over saline (Rec 33); reassess with dynamic measures and capillary refill time (Rec 6, 8); the lactate ≥4 trigger is from the 2018 bundle (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |
| 5 | Vasopressor | Norepinephrine first-line (strong recommendation, SSC 2021) to keep MAP ≥65; peripheral start acceptable (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |
UK practice uses the overlapping **Sepsis Six** (oxygen, cultures, antibiotics, fluids, lactate, urine output).

## 3. Choose empirical antimicrobials
Base the regimen on the suspected source, local antibiogram, recent antibiotics and colonisation:
- **MRSA cover** (e.g. vancomycin) only with risk factors (prior MRSA, recent hospitalisation, lines, high local prevalence).
- **Double Gram-negative cover** only for high risk of resistant organisms with shock (SSC 2021 suggests against double cover once the pathogen and susceptibilities are known).
- **Antifungal** only with risk factors (e.g. *Candida* colonisation at multiple sites, TPN, recent abdominal surgery, neutropenia).
- Give the **first dose fully and fast**; use prolonged (extended or continuous) infusion of beta-lactams for maintenance after an initial bolus (SSC 2021 Rec 25, weak). Dose for augmented clearance and large volume of distribution, then adjust to renal function and levels (check current references).

## 4. Source control
- Image early (ultrasound, CT) to find a drainable focus: abscess, obstructed urinary tract, cholangitis, empyema, necrotising soft-tissue infection, perforated viscus, infected device.
- Aim for intervention **within 6-12 h** of diagnosis (SSC 2021: smaller studies suggest source control within 6-12 h is advantageous; no firm recommendation on timing), by the least invasive effective route (percutaneous drain, ERCP, nephrostomy before open surgery).
- **Remove infected intravascular devices** promptly after alternative access is established.
- Necrotising fasciitis and ischaemic bowel: surgery is the treatment; do not wait for "stabilisation".

## 5. Reassess and de-escalate
- At 6 h: MAP, lactate trend, urine output, mental state, need for ICU (septic shock usually needs ICU (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- At 48-72 h: review cultures and clinical course; narrow to targeted therapy, stop agents with no indication.
- SSC 2021 suggests a shorter rather than longer antimicrobial course when source control is adequate (Rec 30, weak); the day count (commonly 5-7 days (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) depends on the source. Procalcitonin plus clinical review can support stopping; SSC suggests against using PCT plus clinical evaluation to decide when to start (Rec 16).

## Gotchas
- Elderly, immunosuppressed and beta-blocked patients may be normothermic and not tachycardic.
- A normal lactate does not exclude sepsis; a high one does not prove it.
- Antibiotics without source control fail; persistent shock means look again for a source.
- Over-resuscitation: stop fluid when the patient is no longer fluid-responsive (see `health-medicine.critical-care-medicine.hemodynamic-and-shock-management.shock-classification-and-vasopressor-titration`).
- Corticosteroids (IV hydrocortisone typically 200 mg/day, 50 mg every 6 h or infusion) are for septic shock with ongoing vasopressor requirement (norepinephrine or epinephrine ≥0.25 mcg/kg/min for ≥4 h), not for sepsis without shock (SSC 2021).
