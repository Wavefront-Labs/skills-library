# VV and VA ECMO: selection, circuit surveillance and weaning

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** match the patient to the right configuration (or to no ECMO), keep the circuit
and patient safe hour by hour, and come off support through a deliberate trial rather
than drift. ECMO is a bridge (to recovery, decision, VAD or transplant), never a therapy
in itself: name the bridge before cannulating.

## 1. Candidacy and configuration
| Question | VV ECMO | VA ECMO |
|---|---|---|
| Failing organ | Lungs only; heart adequate | Heart (± lungs) |
| Typical trigger | Severe ARDS despite optimal care: PaO2/FiO2 < 50 mmHg for > 3 h or < 80 for > 6 h, or pH < 7.25 with PaCO2 ≥ 60 for > 6 h (EOLIA criteria) | Refractory cardiogenic shock (SCAI stage D-E), e.g. post-MI, myocarditis, PE, post-cardiotomy; ECPR in selected arrests |
| Prerequisites tried | Lung-protective ventilation, PEEP titration, neuromuscular blockade, prone positioning | Optimised filling, inotropes/vasopressors, revascularisation, ± IABP/Impella |
| Common cannulation | Femoral drainage + IJ return, or dual-lumen IJ cannula | Femoral vein drainage + femoral artery return (peripheral) |

- Relative contraindications (verify against centre policy): irreversible disease with no exit
  strategy, > 7 days of high-pressure/high-FiO2 ventilation (VV), contraindication to
  anticoagulation, severe aortic regurgitation or aortic dissection (VA), unwitnessed or prolonged no-flow arrest (ECPR).
- Score to inform, not decide: Murray/LIS, RESP (VV), SAVE (VA). Refer early to an ECMO
  centre; transfer on ECMO is safer than late deterioration.

## 2. Hourly circuit and patient surveillance
1. **Flow and RPM:** record both. Typical targets: VV 60-80 mL/kg/min to keep SpO2 ≥ 88%;
   VA 50-70 mL/kg/min (start ~3-4 L/min) to restore MAP ≥ 65 and lactate clearance.
   Rising RPM for the same flow = drainage problem (hypovolaemia, cannula position,
   tamponade, "chattering" lines).
2. **Pressures:** access (pre-pump, negative), pre- and post-oxygenator. A rising
   transmembrane gradient (ΔP) signals oxygenator clot; trend it daily.
3. **Gases:** post-oxygenator PO2 should be high (> 300 mmHg at FDO2 1.0); a falling value
   means oxygenator failure. Sweep gas controls CO2 — reduce PaCO2 slowly (≤ 20 mmHg in the
   first 24 h) to limit neurological injury risk.
4. **Anticoagulation:** commonly unfractionated heparin to anti-Xa ~0.3-0.5 IU/mL or aPTT
   per protocol; daily platelets, fibrinogen, haemoglobin, plasma-free haemoglobin (haemolysis).
   Doses and targets vary widely by centre — check current references.
5. **Circuit check:** clots (torch inspection, especially connectors and oxygenator), air,
   kinks, cannula securement and sites, heater temperature, backup console and clamps at bedside.
6. **Ventilator on VV:** "lung rest", e.g. plateau ≤ 25 cmH2O, PEEP 10-15, RR 10, FiO2 ≤ 0.3-0.5.

## 3. VA-specific hazards
- **Differential hypoxaemia (Harlequin/north-south):** recovering heart ejects deoxygenated
  blood from failing lungs to the coronaries and brain. Monitor **right radial** arterial
  gases and right-hand SpO2; treat with ventilator optimisation or convert to V-AV.
- **LV distension:** retrograde flow raises afterload. Watch for loss of arterial pulsatility,
  closed aortic valve on echo, pulmonary oedema, spontaneous echo contrast. Options: lower
  flow, inotrope, IABP, Impella (ECPELLA) or atrial septostomy.
- **Limb ischaemia:** place a distal perfusion cannula; check the cannulated leg (NIRS, Doppler) hourly.

## 4. Weaning trial
- **VV:** set FiO2 and ventilation to safe settings, then reduce sweep to 0 (flow unchanged)
  for ≥ 2-24 h. Decannulate if SpO2 ≥ 88-90%, PaCO2 acceptable, RR < 30, no distress on settings
  you would accept off ECMO.
- **VA:** reduce flow stepwise (e.g. 0.5 L/min every few minutes to ~1-1.5 L/min) with echo
  assessment: LVEF > 20-25%, LVOT VTI ≥ 10 cm, MAP stable on modest inotropes. Ensure anticoagulation during low-flow (clot risk).

## Gotchas
- Recirculation in VV (cannulas too close) mimics oxygenator failure: pre-oxygenator SO2 rises
  while patient SpO2 falls. Check cannula tip distance on imaging/echo.
- A sudden drop in flow with chattering is usually volume or position, not pump failure; do not just turn up RPM.
- Femoral arterial gases on VA reflect circuit blood, not what the brain receives.
- Bleeding is the commonest complication; intracranial haemorrhage is the most feared. Check pupils and neuro status every shift.

## Verify
- [ ] Bridge goal and exit strategy documented before or at cannulation.
- [ ] Hourly flow, RPM, pressures, sweep, FDO2 charted; daily ΔP and haemolysis trends reviewed.
- [ ] Right radial gas/SpO2 monitored on peripheral VA; LV distension screened by echo daily.
- [ ] Anticoagulation target and bleeding/clot findings reviewed each shift.
- [ ] Weaning trial criteria defined and results recorded before decannulation.
