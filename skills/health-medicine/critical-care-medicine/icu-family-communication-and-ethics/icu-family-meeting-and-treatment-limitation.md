# ICU family meeting and treatment-limitation discussion

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** families leave the meeting understanding where things stand, feeling heard, and
knowing what happens next; the team leaves with a plan grounded in what the patient would
want, documented so every shift acts on it. Law on surrogates, capacity and withdrawal
differs by jurisdiction: verify locally.

## 1. Before the meeting (10-15 min, non-negotiable)
- **Team huddle:** agree the medical facts, the realistic range of outcomes and the
  recommendation. Families notice when doctors disagree in the room.
- Who attends: lead clinician, bedside nurse, ideally social work/chaplain/palliative care;
  interpreter (never a family member) if needed.
- Identify the legal decision-maker or surrogate hierarchy and any advance directive.
- Private room, seats for everyone, phones silenced, 30-60 min protected.

## 2. Structure (SPIKES skeleton with VALUE behaviours)
1. **Setting and introductions:** everyone names their role. State the purpose ("to update
   you and to plan together").
2. **Perception:** "What have you understood from the team so far?" Correct misconceptions later, gently.
3. **Invitation:** "Would it help if I gave you an overview of where things stand?"
4. **Knowledge:** warning shot ("I'm afraid I have serious news"), then 2-3 key messages in
   plain words; no organ-by-organ lab tour. Pause. Silence is useful.
5. **Emotion:** respond before giving more information. VALUE: **V**alue what they say,
   **A**cknowledge emotions, **L**isten, **U**nderstand the patient as a person ("Tell me
   about her"), **E**licit questions. Name the emotion ("This is a shock").
6. **Strategy and summary:** recommend, then summarise and agree the next contact time.

## 3. Talking about uncertainty
- Give ranges, not false precision: "Most people this sick do not survive; some do, and of
  those, many will not get back to living independently."
- **Best case / worst case / most likely**, tied to function, not just survival.
- "Hope and worry" framing: "I hope she improves; I'm worried she won't."
- Ask what the patient would value: "If she could see herself now, what would she say
  matters most?" This is substituted judgement; the surrogate reports the patient's voice,
  not their own wishes.

## 4. Time-limited trials and treatment limitation
- **Time-limited trial:** agree a defined period (often 3-7 days, set by the condition), the
  treatments included, and the markers of improvement or deterioration. Book the follow-up
  meeting before ending this one.
- Make a **recommendation** rather than offering a menu; families should not carry the
  weight of "pulling the plug". Phrase as "we recommend focusing on comfort", not "do you want us to stop?"
- Treatments that would not achieve the patient's goals need not be offered as choices
  (policy on non-beneficial treatment and dispute resolution varies; involve ethics
  committee or second opinion early when conflict persists).
- Never "withdraw care": treatments are stopped, **care continues**. Describe what comfort
  care looks like (symptom control, visiting, music, spiritual support).
- Organ and tissue donation: follow national process; typically the specialist donation
  team raises it, and the decision to limit treatment is made separately from the donation conversation.

## 5. Document and follow through
- Note attendees, information given, family understanding, patient values, decision and
  rationale, resuscitation status, and the next meeting time.
- Brief the nursing team and the night team in person.

## Gotchas
- Doctors talk ~70% of the time in typical meetings; aim for families to speak at least half.
- Jargon ("she's saturating poorly", "multi-organ failure") is not understood; check with teach-back.
- A family's "we want everything done" is often an expression of love or fear; explore it.
- Moral distress in nurses rises when plans drift; inconsistent messages across shifts are the commonest complaint.
- Cultural and religious values shape who decides and how; ask, do not assume.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-meeting huddle held and a single recommendation agreed.
- [ ] Family asked what they understood and what the patient valued.
- [ ] At least one empathic response made to emotion before more facts.
- [ ] Time-limited trial or treatment-limitation plan documented with review date.
- [ ] Resuscitation status updated and bedside/night teams briefed.
