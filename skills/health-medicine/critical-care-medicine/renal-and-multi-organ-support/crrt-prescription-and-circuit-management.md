# CRRT prescription and circuit management

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** start renal replacement for the right reason at the right time, deliver an adequate dose without interruptions, keep the circuit running with safe anticoagulation, and fit RRT into the patient's wider organ-support plan.

## 1. Decide whether and when to start
- **Stage the AKI (KDIGO 2012, creatinine and urine-output criteria confirmed in the guideline PDF):** stage 1 creatinine ×1.5-1.9 or +26.5 µmol/L (0.3 mg/dL) in 48 h, or UO <0.5 mL/kg/h for 6-12 h; stage 2 ×2.0-2.9 or UO <0.5 for ≥12 h; stage 3 ×3, creatinine ≥354 µmol/L (4.0 mg/dL), UO <0.3 for ≥24 h or anuria ≥12 h, or RRT started.
- **Urgent indications (AEIOU), when refractory to medical therapy:** Acidosis (pH <7.15-7.2 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)), Electrolytes (K+ >6-6.5 mmol/L or rising with ECG change), Intoxication (dialysable: lithium, toxic alcohols, salicylate, metformin), Overload (pulmonary oedema despite diuretics), Uraemia (pericarditis, encephalopathy, bleeding).
- **Without urgent indications, waiting is safe:** STARRT-AKI and AKIKI showed no benefit from accelerated starts and more RRT dependence (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); review daily.
- **Modality:** CRRT for haemodynamic instability, raised ICP or acute liver failure (avoids osmotic shifts); intermittent haemodialysis or SLED if stable. Survival is similar between modalities (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 2. Access
Non-tunnelled dialysis catheter, ultrasound-guided: **right internal jugular** first, femoral second, left IJ third, subclavian last (KDIGO 2012 Rec 5.4, confirmed); tip at the cavo-atrial junction, ~15-16 cm for right IJ and ~20-24 cm for femoral (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); subclavian risks stenosis for a future fistula.

## 3. Write the prescription (CVVHDF example)
| Element | Typical setting |
|---|---|
| Blood flow | 150-250 mL/min (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |
| Effluent dose | **20-25 mL/kg/h delivered** (KDIGO 1A; the guideline says this usually needs a higher prescription); prescribe ~25-30 to cover downtime (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) |
| Split | Replacement fluid and dialysate roughly 1:1, part pre-filter to reduce haemoconcentration |
| Net ultrafiltration | 0-150 mL/h initially (often 1-1.5 mL/kg/h max early), set against the daily balance target |
| Fluids | Bicarbonate-buffered; adjust K+ (0-4 mmol/L) and phosphate to labs |
Higher intensity (35-40 mL/kg/h) gave no benefit in ATN and RENAL (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Filtration fraction should stay below ~20-25% to protect filter life (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 4. Anticoagulate the circuit
- **Regional citrate (KDIGO 5.3.2.2, 2B: suggested over heparin when no contraindication; RICH trial: longer filter life (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)):** citrate pre-filter, calcium post-filter or systemic. Target **post-filter iCa 0.25-0.35 mmol/L**, **systemic iCa 1.0-1.2 mmol/L** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); check per protocol (e.g. 6-hourly).
- **Citrate accumulation:** total Ca / iCa ratio **>2.5** (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), rising anion gap, metabolic acidosis, falling iCa needing more calcium. Risk is highest in liver failure and lactic shock: reduce citrate or switch.
- **Unfractionated heparin** if citrate unsuitable: monitor aPTT or anti-Xa per protocol; watch for HIT (platelet fall >50% (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)).
- **No anticoagulation** in active bleeding: use pre-dilution, high blood flow and accept shorter filter life.

## 5. Daily review and troubleshooting
- Record **delivered dose** (actual effluent hours × rate) and downtime; aim >80-85% of prescribed (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Rising TMP and falling filter pressure drop** = clotting; check access position, kinks, flow, citrate dose, and haematocrit.
- **Access pressure alarms:** reposition patient, reverse lines only as a short-term fix, consider catheter change.
- **Electrolytes:** hypophosphataemia and hypokalaemia are common after 24-48 h; replace or add to fluids.
- **Drug dosing:** CRRT clears many antimicrobials; dose to effluent rate, not native GFR (check current references and use levels where available).
- **Hypothermia:** fluid warmers can mask fever.

## 6. Place it in multi-organ support
Review RRT goals alongside ventilation, vasopressor and nutrition plans on the daily round (see `health-medicine.critical-care-medicine.icu-assessment-and-triage.icu-admission-triage-and-daily-systems-review`). In acute liver failure, CRRT also controls ammonia and temperature; in raised ICP, avoid rapid sodium shifts. Revisit whether escalating support still serves the patient's goals.

## Gotchas
- Nominal prescription is not delivered dose; downtime for scans and clotting is often 10-20%.
- A positive fluid balance on CRRT usually means the net UF target was never revised.
- Citrate is safe only with reliable iCa monitoring and staff trained in the protocol.
- Stop criteria: urine output recovering (e.g. >400-500 mL/day spontaneously (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) with falling creatinine prompts a trial off.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication (AEIOU or other) and KDIGO stage documented.
- [ ] Prescribed and delivered dose recorded daily.
- [ ] Anticoagulation targets and iCa (or aPTT) checks charted.
- [ ] Net UF linked to a daily fluid-balance goal.
- [ ] Drug doses adjusted for CRRT clearance; recovery assessed daily.
