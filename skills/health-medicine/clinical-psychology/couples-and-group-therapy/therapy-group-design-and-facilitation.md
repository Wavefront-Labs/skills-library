# Designing and facilitating a therapy group

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** set up a therapy group with a clear purpose and the right members, build
cohesion quickly, steer it through its stages, handle difficult moments without losing
the group, and show measurable benefit.

## 1. Design decisions
| Decision | Typical choice |
|---|---|
| Type | Structured/psychoeducational (e.g. group CBT for depression, anxiety, DBT skills) vs. interpersonal process group |
| Size | 6-10 members (structured groups may run 10-12); start with 1-2 extra to allow for drop-out |
| Duration | Structured: 8-16 weekly sessions of 90-120 min; process: often open-ended |
| Membership | Closed (same members throughout, best for protocols) vs. slow-open |
| Facilitation | Co-facilitation recommended: shared observation, cover, modelling |
Write a one-paragraph group description (purpose, who it is for, what happens) for referrers.

## 2. Screen and prepare members
- Individual pre-group interview (30-60 min): fit with purpose, motivation, ability to
  tolerate a group, risk. Common exclusions (vary by group type): acute psychosis or mania,
  active high suicide risk without individual support, intoxication, severe hostility, or
  being the sole "outlier" (e.g. one man in an otherwise all-female trauma group).
- Pre-group preparation reduces drop-out: explain what sessions look like, likely early
  discomfort, attendance expectations, and confidentiality and its limits.
- Baseline outcome measure (e.g. PHQ-9, GAD-7, CORE-10).

## 3. Opening sessions and cohesion
- Agree ground rules together: confidentiality (and that you cannot guarantee others will
  keep it), attendance and lateness, respect, no contact outside that excludes others,
  substance-free attendance, what to do if absent.
- Cohesion is the group equivalent of the alliance and predicts outcome. Build it early:
  rounds where everyone speaks, link members' experiences ("that sounds close to what
  you described earlier, too"), highlight universality, and keep early disclosure at moderate depth.

## 4. Stage-aware facilitation
- **Forming:** high dependence on leaders; provide structure and explanation.
- **Storming/conflict:** challenges to leaders or each other; treat as normal, model
  non-defensive responses, keep it safe.
- **Working:** members help each other; leaders step back, focus on here-and-now process
  and Yalom's therapeutic factors (instillation of hope, universality, interpersonal learning).
- **Ending:** name the end several sessions ahead; review gains, plan for relapse, allow
  feelings about ending.

## 5. Common difficulties
| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| Monopoliser | Thank, summarise, redirect: "Let's hear how others relate to that" |
| Silent member | Gentle invitation, pairs work; respect pace |
| Conflict | Slow it down, have each speaker reflect the other, link to group goals |
| Risk disclosure | Acknowledge in group, follow up individually the same day |
| Subgrouping / outside contact | Bring it into the room; revisit ground rules |

## 6. Monitor
- Session-by-session or every few sessions: outcome measure plus group climate (GCQ-S) or
  cohesion scale. Contact members after missed sessions; two consecutive absences often
  precede drop-out.
- Co-facilitators debrief for 15-20 min after each session.

## Gotchas
- Inadequate screening causes most group failures — one poorly matched member can sink a group.
- Doing individual therapy in front of an audience wastes the group's power; use member-to-member links.
- Online groups need extra rules (camera, private space, what to do if a connection drops).
- Couples therapy is a different skill set (e.g. Gottman Method, EFT, IBCT) with its own
  assessment of conflict, violence and commitment; do not run it as a two-person group.

## Verify
- [ ] Written group description with purpose, size, length and membership type.
- [ ] Every member screened individually and prepared; baseline measure taken.
- [ ] Ground rules agreed and recorded in session 1.
- [ ] Outcome and cohesion measured during the group; absences followed up.
- [ ] Ending planned in advance; post-group measures collected.
