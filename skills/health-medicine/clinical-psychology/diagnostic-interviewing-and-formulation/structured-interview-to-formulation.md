# Structured diagnostic interview to formulation and treatment match

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** in one intake session (typically 60-90 min, sometimes split over two), arrive at
(a) a defensible working diagnosis with differentials, (b) a risk statement, (c) a shared
formulation the client recognises, and (d) a treatment recommendation tied to both.

## 1. Before the session
- Read the referral and any records; note the referral question in one line.
- Send or administer brief screeners first (e.g. PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, AUDIT) so the
  interview can target positives rather than survey everything.
- Pick the interview tool to fit the question:
  - **SCID-5-CV** (clinician version) — broad DSM-5 coverage, 45-90 min; needs training.
  - **MINI 7.x** — ~15-30 min screening-diagnostic interview; good breadth, less depth.
  - **ADIS-5** — anxiety, mood and related disorders in depth; **K-SADS-PL** for children.
  - **SCID-5-PD** or IPDE if personality pathology is the question.

## 2. Interview sequence
1. **Open (5-10 min):** confidentiality and its limits (risk to self/others, safeguarding,
   court orders), session length, what happens with the information.
2. **Presenting problem in the client's words**, then onset, course, triggers, impact on
   work/relationships/self-care. Ask "why now?" — the precipitant matters for formulation.
3. **Structured modules:** follow skip-outs as written; record the client's examples, not
   just yes/no. Ask about frequency, duration and impairment — criteria hinge on these.
4. **Screen what the referral did not mention:** substance use, trauma history, psychosis,
   mania/hypomania (a missed bipolar history changes treatment), eating, OCD, sleep.
5. **History:** developmental, family psychiatric history, medical conditions and current
   medication (many mimic psychiatric syndromes — thyroid, steroids, stimulants).
6. **Mental status examination:** appearance, behaviour, speech, mood (client's word) and
   affect (your observation), thought form and content, perception, cognition, insight,
   judgement.
7. **Risk:** ask directly about suicidal ideation, intent, plan, means, prior attempts,
   and protective factors; the C-SSRS screener gives structure. Asking does not increase risk.
8. **Close:** summarise back, check accuracy, say what happens next.

## 3. Diagnosis and differential
- Match symptoms to DSM-5-TR (or ICD-11) criteria explicitly: write which criteria are met
  and which are not. Note duration thresholds (e.g. 2 weeks for a major depressive episode,
  6 months for GAD, 1 month of symptoms for PTSD).
- Rule out: substance/medication-induced, due to another medical condition, better
  explained by another mental disorder. Note "provisional" where information is incomplete.

## 4. Formulation (the 5 Ps)
| P | Question | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Presenting | What is the problem now? | Panic attacks, avoidance of trains |
| Predisposing | What made them vulnerable? | Anxious parent, health scare at 12 |
| Precipitating | Why now? | Collapse on a commute after illness |
| Perpetuating | What keeps it going? | Avoidance, safety behaviours, checking pulse |
| Protective | What helps? | Supportive partner, prior coping |

Perpetuating factors are where treatment acts; draw the maintenance cycle with the client.

## 5. Treatment matching
- Link the recommendation to the formulation and evidence base (e.g. NICE/APA guidelines):
  CBT with exposure for panic; trauma-focused therapy for PTSD; refer for medication review
  or psychiatry when severity, psychosis, bipolarity or risk warrants.
- Consider stepped care: guided self-help for mild presentations, high-intensity therapy for
  moderate-severe. State what will be measured to track progress.

## Gotchas
- Diagnostic tools are aids, not oracles: skip-outs miss atypical presentations.
- Comorbidity is the rule — do not stop at the first diagnosis that fits.
- Cultural context: use the DSM-5-TR Cultural Formulation Interview where idioms of distress differ.
- Formulation is a hypothesis; revise it as therapy produces new information.
- Note the limits of a single session; uncertain diagnoses stay "provisional".

## Verify
- [ ] Referral question answered in the report's first paragraph.
- [ ] Criteria met/not met documented; differentials and medical rule-outs listed.
- [ ] Risk assessed directly and documented with a plan.
- [ ] 5 Ps formulation shared and agreed with the client.
- [ ] Recommendation names a specific intervention and an outcome measure.
