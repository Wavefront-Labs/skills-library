# Cognitive domain profile interpretation in neuropsychological assessment

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** after brain injury, neurological disease or suspected dementia, measure each
cognitive domain against a defensible premorbid baseline, confirm the data are valid,
and decide which weaknesses are real, how they fit a neurological pattern, and what to do.

## 1. History and question
- Clarify the question: diagnosis (e.g. MCI vs. depression), capacity, rehabilitation
  planning, return to work, or change over time.
- Gather: education, occupation, first language, handedness, medical and neurological
  history, imaging, medications (anticholinergics, benzodiazepines, opioids), sleep, mood,
  alcohol, sensory status. Collateral history from an informant is essential in dementia.

## 2. Domain-based battery
| Domain | Example measures |
|---|---|
| Premorbid ability | TOPF or NART (word reading), demographic regression |
| General intellect | WAIS-IV indices (or short form) |
| Attention / speed | Digit Span, Coding, Trail Making A |
| Memory (verbal, visual) | WMS-IV, RAVLT, CVLT-3, Rey Complex Figure recall |
| Language | Boston Naming, verbal fluency (category, letter) |
| Visuospatial | Rey Figure copy, Block Design |
| Executive | Trail Making B, Stroop, WCST, fluency |
| Mood / effort | HADS or GDS; standalone and embedded PVTs |
Screens such as the RBANS or MoCA can triage but do not replace a full assessment.

## 3. Establish validity first
- Use at least two performance validity tests (PVTs), standalone (e.g. TOMM, WMT) and
  embedded (e.g. Reliable Digit Span), spread across the session. Consensus guidance treats
  failure of two or more independent PVTs as strong evidence of invalid performance
  (verify current AACN guidance). Interpret failures in context — severe dementia and
  some conditions produce genuine failures.
- If validity fails, say the scores underestimate true ability and limit conclusions.

## 4. Interpret the profile
1. **Premorbid estimate:** reading-based estimates are robust to many acquired injuries
   but fall in aphasia, dyslexia and moderate dementia; combine with education/occupation.
2. **Use demographically corrected norms** (age, education, sometimes ethnicity — know the
   ethical debate around race norms and follow current guidance).
3. **Impairment thresholds:** conventions differ — scores below about the 5th-7th
   percentile (≈1.5 SD below expectation) or ≥1 SD below premorbid estimate are common
   flags; state which convention you use.
4. **Base rates:** healthy adults taking 20+ tests routinely produce several low scores.
   Use multivariate base-rate tables where available rather than counting low scores.
5. **Pattern:** match the profile to known syndromes — e.g. poor encoding and retention
   with rapid forgetting (amnestic, hippocampal); retrieval deficit with intact recognition
   and slowing (subcortical, depression, vascular); executive and speed deficits after
   diffuse TBI. Check consistency with imaging, history and course.
6. **Rule out alternatives:** mood, fatigue, pain, medication, sensory loss, low effort.

## 5. Feedback and recommendations
- Feedback session within a few weeks: explain strengths first, then difficulties in
  everyday terms, and what they mean for daily life.
- Concrete strategies: external memory aids, single-tasking, pacing; driving, work or
  capacity advice where indicated; referral to neurology, rehab or memory services.
- Recommend re-assessment interval (often 12-18 months for suspected neurodegeneration;
  verify against service protocol), and use reliable change indices for retesting.

## Gotchas
- Practice effects inflate retest scores; use alternate forms or RCIs with practice correction.
- "Average" scores can still represent decline in a person with high premorbid ability.
- Low education, bilingualism and cultural unfamiliarity with testing depress scores.
- Never diagnose from a single test or a screening score.

## Verify
- [ ] Premorbid estimate stated with method and its limits.
- [ ] Two or more PVTs given and results reported.
- [ ] Impairment convention and norms named; base rates considered.
- [ ] Profile linked to history, imaging and a plausible neurological pattern.
- [ ] Feedback delivered; practical recommendations and retest plan documented.
