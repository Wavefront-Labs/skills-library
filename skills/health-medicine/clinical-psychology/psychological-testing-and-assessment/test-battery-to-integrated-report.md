# Psychological test battery to integrated assessment report

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** turn a referral question into a focused, multi-method battery, administer it to
standard, check that the data are valid, interpret scores with their error, and write a
report that answers the question in plain language.

## 1. Frame the question and choose the battery
- Rewrite the referral as answerable questions ("Is there evidence of a learning disorder
  in reading?" not "assess the child"). Each test must earn its place against one of them.
- Use **multiple methods**: interview, self-report, informant report, performance tests,
  records and observation. Convergence across methods is what makes a conclusion strong.
- Selection criteria for each instrument: norms that match the person (age, language,
  culture, recency — norms older than ~15 years risk inflated scores), reliability
  (aim r ≥ .90 for individual decisions, ≥ .80 for screening), validity evidence for this
  use, and your competence and licence to use it (test publisher user levels).
- Typical building blocks: WAIS-IV/WISC-V (cognitive), WIAT/WJ (achievement), MMPI-3 or
  PAI (broadband personality/psychopathology), symptom scales (BDI-II, PHQ-9, GAD-7),
  informant scales (BASC-3, CBCL), and validity measures.

## 2. Administer to standard
- Follow the manual verbatim: instructions, timing, discontinue and reverse rules, and
  permitted prompts. Deviations invalidate the norms — record any that occur.
- Control the setting: quiet room, glasses/hearing aids on, breaks, medication and sleep
  noted. Note effort, mood, rapport and behaviour as you go; it informs interpretation.
- Language and interpreters: testing through an interpreter or in a second language
  changes what verbal scores mean; say so in the report.

## 3. Check validity before interpreting
- Personality inventories: inspect non-response, inconsistency (e.g. MMPI-3 VRIN/TRIN,
  PAI ICN/INF), over-reporting and under-reporting scales first. Use the manual's cut-offs
  (verify per edition); if protocol validity fails, do not interpret the substantive scales.
- Performance tests: include embedded or standalone performance validity indicators where
  effort is in question (forensic, disability, compensation contexts).

## 4. Interpret scores with their error
| Metric | Mean (SD) | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Standard score | 100 (15) | ~90-109 often labelled "average" (labels vary by test) |
| T-score | 50 (10) | clinical elevation commonly T ≥ 65 on MMPI-3/PAI scales |
| Scaled score | 10 (3) | subtests |
| Percentile | — | not equal-interval; do not average |

- Report a **95% confidence interval** (±1.96 × SEM) around key scores, not the point alone.
- Treat index/subtest differences as meaningful only if they exceed the manual's critical
  value **and** are uncommon in the normative base-rate tables — many "splits" are normal.
- Interpret from the most reliable level (composite) down; single subtests rarely bear
  diagnostic weight.

## 5. Integrate and write
- Organise by **question or domain, not by test**: state the finding, then the convergent
  evidence across tests, history and observation; address contradictory data explicitly.
- Structure: reason for referral; background; tests used; behavioural observations and
  validity; results by domain; summary and diagnostic impressions; recommendations.
- Recommendations must be specific and actionable (who does what), tied to findings.
- Give **feedback** in person: lead with the answer, use percentiles and everyday language,
  check understanding, and share strengths as well as difficulties.

## Gotchas
- Cut-offs and norms are edition-specific; never mix editions or borrow norms.
- Flynn effect: older norms inflate IQ, which matters near diagnostic thresholds.
- A single elevated scale is a hypothesis, not a diagnosis.
- Do not release raw test materials or protected items; follow test-security and
  jurisdictional rules on releasing test data.
- Computer-generated interpretive reports are aids; never paste them in unedited.

## Verify
- [ ] Every test maps to a referral question; norms fit the examinee.
- [ ] Administration deviations and the validity status are documented.
- [ ] Key scores reported with confidence intervals and descriptive ranges.
- [ ] Conclusions rest on convergent evidence from more than one method.
- [ ] Report answers the referral question in its summary; recommendations are specific.
