# Phase-based PTSD treatment planning

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** assess PTSD reliably, decide how much preparation is needed, pick an evidence-based
trauma-focused therapy, and review progress against a standard measure.

## 1. Assess
- Ask specific questions about re-experiencing, avoidance, hyperarousal, dissociation,
  negative alterations in mood and thinking, and functional impairment (NICE NG116 1.1.3).
- Ask about one or more traumatic events, however long ago, giving specific examples.
- Map current safety, substance use, dissociation, suicide risk, sleep, pain and legal
  processes. Use the structured measure your service has validated (for example PCL-5,
  CAPS-5 or ITQ); look up its current scoring rules rather than relying on remembered cut-offs.

## 2. Decide on preparation
NICE advises helping people manage barriers to engaging with trauma-focused therapy, such
as substance misuse, dissociation, emotional dysregulation, interpersonal difficulties or
negative self-perception, alongside offering treatment. Add a preparation phase when the
person is not currently safe, has unmanaged risk, or dissociates so severely they cannot
stay present. Typical content: psychoeducation, grounding, sleep strategies, safety planning,
building the alliance.

## 3. Choose the therapy (adults, presenting more than 1 month after the event)
- Offer an individual **trauma-focused CBT** intervention (NG116 1.6.16). NICE names
  cognitive processing therapy, cognitive therapy for PTSD, narrative exposure therapy and
  prolonged exposure therapy as examples.
- Trauma-focused CBT for adults should use a validated manual and typically run 8 to 12
  sessions, more if clinically indicated, for example multiple traumas (1.6.17).
- Offer **EMDR** to adults presenting more than 3 months after a non-combat-related
  trauma (1.6.19); EMDR typically also runs 8 to 12 sessions (1.6.20).
- Children and young people: individual trauma-focused CBT, typically 6 to 12 sessions,
  more if clinically indicated. EMDR is only considered for ages 7 to 17 presenting more
  than 3 months after the event, and only if they do not respond to or engage with
  trauma-focused CBT (1.6.13).
- Supported computerised trauma-focused CBT is an option only if there are no severe
  symptoms, in particular dissociative symptoms, and no risk of harm to self or others.
- Practitioners must be trained and supervised.

## 4. Deliver and track
- Monitor arousal during sessions; if the person dissociates, pause, ground and resume in
  smaller segments.
- Track PTSD symptoms with the same measure at intervals and review non-response early:
  avoidance, homework, comorbidity, ongoing threat, alliance.
- Plan booster sessions if needed, particularly around trauma anniversaries.

## Gotchas
- Avoidance is the core symptom; expect it in homework and cancellations.
- Ongoing legal proceedings: agree how the trauma narrative is handled and check local
  legal guidance.
- Vicarious trauma is real: use supervision.

## Verify
- [ ] Assessment covers the symptom clusters, trauma history, risk and dissociation.
- [ ] Preparation need is justified, not routine.
- [ ] A named evidence-based protocol is agreed with the client.
- [ ] Progress is tracked with a standard measure and reviewed.
