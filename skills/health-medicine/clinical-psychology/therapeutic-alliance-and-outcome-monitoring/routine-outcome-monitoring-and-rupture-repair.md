# Routine outcome monitoring and alliance rupture repair

*General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.*

**Goal:** measure outcome and alliance every session, spot clients who are not on track
early, repair alliance ruptures when they happen, and end therapy on a planned,
evidence-based footing.

## 1. Choose the measures
| Measure | Items | What it tracks | Common thresholds (verify per manual) |
|---|---|---|---|
| ORS | 4 (0-10 visual line, 0-40) | Global wellbeing | Adult clinical cut-off ≈ 25; reliable change ≈ 5 points |
| SRS | 4 (0-40) | Session alliance | Below ≈ 36 warrants discussion |
| PHQ-9 | 9 (0-27) | Depression | 10 = moderate; reliable change ≈ 6 |
| GAD-7 | 7 (0-21) | Anxiety | 8-10 = caseness; reliable change ≈ 4 |
| CORE-10 | 10 (0-40) | General distress | Clinical cut-off ≈ 11 |
| WAI-SR | 12 | Goals, tasks, bond | Use trends rather than a cut-off |
Use one general and one problem-specific measure; keep the burden under about 5 minutes.

## 2. Build it into every session
1. Client completes the outcome measure **at the start** (before discussion biases it).
2. Glance at the score and the graph together: "Your score went from 18 to 23 — what
   changed?" Clients disengage from measures that are never discussed.
3. Alliance measure (SRS) **at the end**, with an explicit invitation for criticism:
   "If anything wasn't right today, it really helps me to know."

## 3. Interpret change
- **Reliable change:** the change exceeds measurement error — RCI = (post − pre) /
  (√2 × SEM), significant at |RCI| > 1.96. Use the published reliable-change value for
  the measure rather than recalculating each time.
- **Clinically significant change:** reliable improvement *and* crossing from the clinical
  to the non-clinical range (Jacobson & Truax).
- **Not on track:** no improvement or deterioration by about session 3-4, or below the
  expected recovery curve where software provides one (e.g. OQ-Analyst, PCOMS tools).
  Feedback on not-on-track cases is where ROM most reduces deterioration.

## 4. When a client is not on track
- Discuss openly; ask what is missing. Review the formulation, goals and fit of the method.
- Check for hidden factors: risk, substance use, life events, medication, alliance problems.
- Take it to supervision; consider changing intensity, method or therapist, or referral.

## 5. Repair alliance ruptures (Safran & Muran)
- **Withdrawal ruptures:** vague answers, topic shifts, over-compliance, missed sessions.
  **Confrontation ruptures:** complaints, criticism, challenging the therapist.
- Repair steps: notice and name it non-defensively ("I sensed something shifted when I
  suggested homework"), invite the client's experience, acknowledge your contribution,
  explore what was needed, and renegotiate tasks or goals. Repaired ruptures are associated
  with better outcomes than no rupture at all.

## 6. Ending well
- Plan endings from the outset; review progress against baseline graphs.
- Taper (e.g. fortnightly, then monthly), write a relapse-prevention "blueprint"
  (what helped, warning signs, what to do), and offer a follow-up or booster where possible.
- Collect a final measure, and a follow-up measure if the service allows.

## Gotchas
- High SRS scores are common (ceiling effect); small drops matter more than the absolute value.
- Measures used to judge therapists rather than help clients get gamed; keep the purpose clinical.
- Cut-offs differ across populations, languages and adolescents — check the manual.
- Unplanned endings: send a closing letter, a final measure if possible, and re-access information.

## Verify
- [ ] Outcome measure given every session at the start and discussed.
- [ ] Alliance measured at the end, with criticism invited.
- [ ] Reliable and clinically significant change calculated at review points.
- [ ] Not-on-track cases flagged and taken to supervision.
- [ ] Ending planned with a relapse-prevention blueprint and final measure.
