# Renal colic management and stone intervention choice

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** image promptly, control pain, choose the right procedure by stone size and site,
and reduce recurrence. Follows NICE NG118 (renal and ureteric stones, UK).

## 1. Imaging
- Adults with suspected renal colic: urgent (within 24 hours of presentation) low-dose
  non-contrast CT. If the woman is pregnant, ultrasound instead.
- Children and young people: urgent ultrasound first; consider low-dose non-contrast CT
  only if still uncertain.
- Do not image everyone with abdominal pain this way, only suspected renal colic.

## 2. Analgesia and expulsive therapy
- NSAID by any route is first line (adults, children and young people).
- IV paracetamol if NSAIDs are contraindicated or insufficient.
- Consider opioids only if both NSAIDs and IV paracetamol are contraindicated or insufficient.
- Consider an alpha blocker for distal ureteric stones under 10 mm (off-label use at the
  time of the guideline; check the current prescribing position).
- Watchful waiting is an option for asymptomatic renal stones under 5 mm, or larger if the
  person agrees after an informed discussion of risks and benefits.

## 3. Choosing the procedure (NICE NG118 tables, adults 16 and over)
| Stone | NICE adult recommendation |
|---|---|
| Ureteric < 10 mm | Offer SWL; consider URS if clearance not possible within 4 weeks with SWL, SWL contraindicated or not targetable, or a previous SWL course failed |
| Ureteric 10 to 20 mm | Offer URS; consider SWL if local facilities allow clearance within 4 weeks; consider PCNL for impacted proximal stones when URS has failed |
| Renal < 10 mm | Offer SWL; consider URS if SWL contraindicated, failed or anatomically unsuitable; PCNL if SWL and URS have failed or are not an option |
| Renal 10 to 20 mm | Consider URS or SWL; consider PCNL if those have failed |
| Renal > 20 mm incl. staghorn | Offer PCNL; consider URS if PCNL is not an option |
- Do not offer pre-treatment stenting to adults having SWL.
- Do not routinely offer a stent after URS for ureteric stones under 20 mm. The guideline
  notes stents may still be considered when more treatment is anticipated, with infection
  or obstruction, or in a solitary kidney.
- Children and young people have their own column in the NICE tables: read it, do not
  extrapolate from the adult row.

## 4. Metabolic testing and prevention
- Adults: measure serum calcium; consider stone analysis.
- Children and young people: consider referral to a paediatric nephrologist or urologist
  for metabolic assessment.
- Advise adults to drink 2.5 to 3 litres of water per day (children 1 to 2 litres by age),
  add fresh lemon juice to drinking water, avoid carbonated drinks, and keep daily salt
  to no more than 6 g.

## Gotchas
- Infection with an obstructing stone, a solitary kidney or bilateral obstruction is an
  urgent-specialist situation: follow your local urology pathway (not covered by the
  recommendations used here).
- Forgetting a stent is in situ: keep a stent register with removal date.

## Verify
- [ ] Imaging done within 24 hours and size, site, hydronephrosis recorded.
- [ ] Analgesia followed the NSAID, then IV paracetamol, then opioid order.
- [ ] Procedure matches the NICE table row for size, site and age group.
- [ ] Stone analysis considered, serum calcium measured, diet and fluid advice given.
