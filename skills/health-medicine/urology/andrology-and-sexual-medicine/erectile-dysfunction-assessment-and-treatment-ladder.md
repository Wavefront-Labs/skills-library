# Erectile dysfunction assessment and treatment ladder

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** treat ED effectively while catching the cardiovascular, endocrine and
psychological conditions it often signals.

## 1. Assessment
- **History:** onset (sudden → psychogenic or situational more likely; gradual →
  organic), morning/nocturnal erections, libido, ejaculation, relationship, mood,
  alcohol, smoking, drugs (antihypertensives, SSRIs, antiandrogens, opioids), pelvic
  surgery/radiotherapy, Peyronie's curvature.
- **IIEF-5 / SHIM** for severity and to track response.
- **ED is a cardiovascular risk marker** — it often precedes coronary events by
  ~ 3–5 years. Check BP, BMI, **fasting glucose/HbA1c, lipids**, cardiovascular risk score.
- **Morning total testosterone** (7–11 am; repeat if low with LH/FSH, prolactin) —
  especially if low libido.
- Exam: genitalia (testes, plaques), secondary sexual characteristics, pulses.

## 2. Cardiac fitness for sexual activity (Princeton)
Low risk → treat. Intermediate/uncertain → evaluate (e.g. exercise test: ~ 4 METs
without symptoms ≈ climbing two flights of stairs briskly). High risk (unstable angina,
recent MI, uncontrolled arrhythmia, severe valve disease) → defer sexual activity and
ED treatment until stabilised.

## 3. First line: lifestyle + PDE5 inhibitors
- Lifestyle: weight loss, exercise, smoking cessation, alcohol reduction, optimise
  diabetes/BP; review culprit drugs.
- **PDE5i:** sildenafil 50 mg (25–100) ~ 1 h before sex on an empty stomach; tadalafil
  10–20 mg on demand (up to 36 h) or 5 mg daily; vardenafil, avanafil.
- **Absolute contraindication: nitrates** (any form, incl. amyl nitrite "poppers") and
  riociguat — severe hypotension. Caution with alpha-blockers (separate dosing/start
  low).
- **"Failed PDE5i" checklist before switching:** correct dose titrated to max, ≥ 6–8
  attempts, sexual stimulation needed, food timing (sildenafil/vardenafil), testosterone
  replaced if hypogonadal, realistic expectations.

## 4. Second line
- **Vacuum erection device** — constriction ring max 30 min.
- **Intracavernosal alprostadil** (e.g. start 2.5–5 µg, titrate in clinic) or combination
  vasoactive mixes; first dose supervised. Intraurethral/topical alprostadil (less
  effective).
- **Priapism counselling:** erection > 4 h → emergency department (ischaemic priapism
  needs aspiration ± phenylephrine within hours to save erectile tissue).
- Psychosexual therapy for psychogenic components, often combined with medication.
- Li-ESWT: evidence mixed; mainly mild vasculogenic ED in some guidelines.

## 5. Third line
**Penile prosthesis** (inflatable 3-piece, or malleable) — high satisfaction; counsel on
infection risk (higher in diabetes), device failure over years, shortened perceived
length, irreversibility.

## 6. Post-prostatectomy
Early penile rehabilitation strategies are common though evidence is mixed; set
expectations (recovery over 1–2+ years).

## Gotchas
- Prescribing PDE5i without asking about nitrates/poppers.
- Missing undiagnosed diabetes or CV disease.
- Declaring PDE5i failure after one low-dose attempt.
- Testosterone checked in the afternoon or once only.

## Verify
- [ ] IIEF-5 baseline; CV risk, glucose, lipids, testosterone checked.
- [ ] Cardiac risk category determined.
- [ ] Nitrate/riociguat use excluded and documented.
- [ ] Failure checklist completed before escalation.
- [ ] Priapism advice given with any injection therapy.
