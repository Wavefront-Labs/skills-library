# Localised prostate cancer: risk stratification and active surveillance

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** match treatment intensity to the cancer's risk and the man's life expectancy
and priorities — avoiding both overtreatment of indolent disease and undertreatment of
aggressive disease.

## 1. Grade and risk group
- **ISUP grade groups** from Gleason: GG1 = 3+3; GG2 = 3+4; GG3 = 4+3; GG4 = 8;
  GG5 = 9–10. Note % pattern 4 and cribriform/intraductal histology (adverse).
- Risk group (EAU/NCCN-style, simplified):
  - **Low:** PSA < 10, GG1, cT1–T2a.
  - **Intermediate:** PSA 10–20 or GG2–3 or cT2b(–c). NCCN splits favourable vs
    unfavourable (GG3, > 50% cores positive, or ≥ 2 intermediate factors).
  - **High:** PSA > 20 or GG4–5 or cT2c–T3.
  - UK NICE uses the 5-tier **CPG** (Cambridge Prognostic Groups) — use your system.
- Integrate MRI (extracapsular extension, seminal vesicle invasion, volume).

## 2. Staging
- Low risk: no staging imaging beyond prostate MRI.
- Intermediate unfavourable/high: staging with **PSMA PET-CT** (increasingly preferred)
  or CT + bone scan.
- Estimate **life expectancy** (comorbidity, age) — it drives the choice as much as the
  tumour.

## 3. Options
- **Active surveillance (AS):** for low risk (standard) and selected favourable
  intermediate (GG2 with low % pattern 4, no cribriform, low volume). Aim: curative
  treatment only if progression, without losing the cure window.
- **Watchful waiting:** limited life expectancy; treat symptoms (often hormonal) if
  they arise — not curative.
- **Radical prostatectomy** or **radiotherapy** (external beam ± short/long-course ADT
  for intermediate/high risk; brachytherapy in selected).
- ProtecT 15-year: no meaningful difference in prostate-cancer mortality between
  monitoring, surgery and radiotherapy for mostly low/intermediate disease, but more
  metastasis in monitoring — and different side-effect profiles.

## 4. Active surveillance schedule (typical; protocols vary)
- **PSA every 3–6 months** (PSA density and kinetics; don't trigger on one value).
- **DRE** annually.
- **Repeat MRI at 12 months**, then every 1–3 years or on PSA change.
- **Confirmatory biopsy** within ~ 12 months if diagnosis was not MRI-targeted; then
  MRI-guided biopsy on MRI progression.
- **Triggers to discuss treatment:** grade upgrade (to GG2 with significant pattern 4,
  or ≥ GG3), increasing volume on MRI/biopsy, T-stage progression, patient preference.
  PSA doubling time < 3 years is a prompt to re-image, not an automatic trigger.

## 5. Counselling on side effects (be concrete)
- Surgery: urinary incontinence (most recover over months; some persist), erectile
  dysfunction (depends on nerve sparing and baseline).
- Radiotherapy: bowel urgency/bleeding, urinary irritation, erectile dysfunction later;
  ADT effects (hot flushes, fatigue, bone loss, metabolic, cognitive).
- AS: anxiety, repeated biopsies, risk of missed progression.
- Use a decision aid and document preferences; offer a clinical nurse specialist.

## Gotchas
- Labelling GG2 with high % pattern 4 or cribriform "favourable" for AS.
- Starting AS without good MRI and a confirmatory biopsy plan.
- Letting surveillance lapse (missed PSA tests) — use a tracked recall system.
- Over-staging with bone scans in low risk.

- [ ] Grade group, risk group (system named), MRI stage recorded.
- [ ] Staging appropriate to risk; life expectancy estimated.
- [ ] Options incl. AS/watchful waiting discussed with a decision aid.
- [ ] AS schedule with dates and triggers in the record and recall system.
- [ ] Baseline urinary/sexual function recorded before any treatment.
