# Urodynamic study interpretation and overactive bladder pathway

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** treat most patients conservatively without urodynamics, and when the test is
needed, ask it a clear question and read the trace correctly.

## 1. Initial assessment (no urodynamics yet)
- History: storage (urgency, frequency, nocturia, urge incontinence) vs **stress**
  (cough/sneeze/exercise) vs voiding (hesitancy, poor stream, straining).
- **3-day bladder diary** (frequency-volume chart): voided volumes, nocturnal
  polyuria (> 33% of 24-h output at night in older adults), fluid intake.
- Urinalysis/culture, **post-void residual** (bladder scan), exam (pelvic organ
  prolapse, atrophy, neuro screen), meds (diuretics, anticholinergic burden).
- Red flags → urology: haematuria, recurrent UTI, pain, palpable bladder, neuro deficit.

## 2. OAB treatment ladder
1. **Behavioural:** fluid timing (not restricting below ~1.5 L), caffeine reduction,
   **bladder training** (scheduled voiding with extending intervals, ≥ 6 weeks),
   pelvic floor muscle training (≥ 3 months, supervised) for mixed/stress.
2. **Medication:** antimuscarinic (consider anticholinergic burden and cognition in older
   people — prefer **mirabegron** or vibegron (β3-agonists) where cognitive risk; check
   BP). Vaginal oestrogen for post-menopausal atrophy.
3. **Third-line** after failed meds (urodynamics usually first): intravesical
   **botulinum toxin A** (counsel on clean intermittent self-catheterisation risk),
   **sacral neuromodulation**, **PTNS**.

## 3. When urodynamics helps
Before invasive treatment (botox, SUI surgery when mixed/unclear symptoms or prior
surgery), voiding dysfunction in men before outlet surgery when diagnosis unclear,
neurogenic bladder (safety — storage pressures and compliance). Not needed before
conservative OAB care or straightforward pure stress incontinence surgery in women
(NICE).

## 4. Reading the study (ICS good practice)
- **Quality first:** zero to atmospheric pressure, transducers at the upper symphysis
  level; resting Pves typically 5–20 and Pabd 0–20 cmH2O lying/sitting; **cough tests**
  at start, regularly during filling and at the end — equal Pves and Pabd responses.
- **Pdet = Pves − Pabd.** Rectal contractions cause *negative* Pdet dips — artefact.
- **Filling phase:** first sensation, strong desire, capacity; **detrusor overactivity**
  = involuntary Pdet rise during filling (note provocation); **compliance** =
  ΔV/ΔPdet (low compliance < ~ 20 mL/cmH2O is concerning, especially in neurogenic
  bladder — upper-tract risk if storage Pdet > 40).
- **Stress test:** urodynamic SUI = leakage with raised abdominal pressure without a
  detrusor contraction; record ALPP/VLPP if used.
- **Voiding phase (men):** **BOOI = PdetQmax − 2Qmax** (> 40 obstructed, 20–40
  equivocal, < 20 unobstructed); **BCI = PdetQmax + 5Qmax** (< 100 weak, > 150 strong).
  Women: obstruction criteria differ (no single standard).
- Free uroflow before catheter study: voided volume ≥ 150 mL for reliable Qmax.

## Gotchas
- Interpreting a trace without cough checks → pressure artefacts called pathology.
- Missing detrusor overactivity because filling was too fast (use physiological rates
  in neurogenic patients).
- Starting antimuscarinics in a frail older adult with cognitive impairment.
- Offering botox without assessing ability/willingness to self-catheterise.

## Verify
- [ ] Diary, PVR, urinalysis done before any escalation.
- [ ] Conservative therapy duration documented before drugs/third-line.
- [ ] Urodynamic question stated; quality checks (cough, resting pressures) recorded.
- [ ] Findings reported in ICS terminology with BOOI/BCI where relevant.
- [ ] Treatment matched to the urodynamic diagnosis.
