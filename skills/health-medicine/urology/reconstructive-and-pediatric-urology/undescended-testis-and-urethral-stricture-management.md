# Undescended testis and urethral stricture management

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** operate on undescended testes at the right age without unnecessary imaging,
and treat urethral strictures with the definitive operation instead of repeated
failing endoscopic procedures.

## Part A — Undescended testis (cryptorchidism)

### 1. Examination
- Warm room, relaxed child, supine and frog-leg (or squatting/cross-legged for older
  children). Sweep from the anterior superior iliac spine along the inguinal canal
  toward the scrotum with one hand, palpate with the other.
- Classify: **palpable** (inguinal, high scrotal, ectopic) vs **non-palpable**;
  **retractile** (can be brought down and stays without tension) — needs annual review
  as some ascend.
- Look for hypospadias + undescended testis (especially non-palpable) → evaluate for
  disorders of sex development urgently (karyotype, endocrinology).
- Bilateral non-palpable testes in a newborn → urgent endocrine/DSD assessment
  (exclude CAH in a virilised 46,XX infant).

### 2. Timing and referral
- Spontaneous descent is uncommon after **6 months** (corrected age) — refer by then.
- **Orchidopexy ideally between 6 and 18 months** (fertility and cancer-risk rationale).
- **Ultrasound/MRI are not recommended** to locate a non-palpable testis (AUA/EAU) —
  **examination under anaesthesia then diagnostic laparoscopy** is the standard.

### 3. Surgery outline
- Palpable: inguinal (or scrotal for low testes) orchidopexy, close patent processus,
  dartos pouch fixation.
- Non-palpable: laparoscopy → vas/vessels entering deep ring (explore groin), blind-
  ending vessels (vanished testis, stop), intra-abdominal testis → one- or two-stage
  (Fowler–Stephens) orchidopexy.
- Counsel: increased lifetime risk of testicular cancer persists (teach self-exam at
  puberty), fertility implications especially in bilateral.

## Part B — Urethral stricture (male)

### 1. Evaluation
- Symptoms: poor stream, straining, recurrent UTI, retention. **Uroflowmetry** (plateau
  curve) and PVR.
- **Retrograde urethrogram + voiding cystourethrogram** (or cystoscopy) to define site,
  length and number. Aetiology: idiopathic, iatrogenic (catheter, TURP), trauma
  (straddle → bulbar; pelvic fracture → posterior), lichen sclerosus (penile).

### 2. Choosing treatment
- **Urethrotomy or dilatation:** reasonable **once** for a short (< 2 cm) bulbar
  stricture not previously treated. Success falls sharply with each repeat.
- **Urethroplasty** for recurrence, long, penile or lichen sclerosus strictures:
  - Short bulbar: excision and primary anastomosis (EPA) or non-transecting technique.
  - Longer bulbar: **buccal mucosa graft** (dorsal or ventral onlay).
  - Penile/lichen sclerosus: staged repair with buccal graft (avoid genital skin in LS).
  - Pelvic-fracture urethral injury: delayed posterior anastomotic urethroplasty
    (after ~3 months with suprapubic catheter).
- Repeated urethrotomies make later urethroplasty harder — refer early.

### 3. Follow-up
Uroflow and symptom score at 3, 6, 12 months; cystoscopy/urethrogram if flow falls.

## Gotchas
- Ordering ultrasound for a non-palpable testis and delaying surgery.
- Labelling a gliding/high-scrotal testis retractile.
- Missing a DSD in hypospadias + cryptorchidism.
- Third or fourth urethrotomy instead of urethroplasty referral.

## Verify
- [ ] Testis position documented with exam technique; referral by 6 months.
- [ ] Orchidopexy planned before 18 months; laparoscopy for non-palpable.
- [ ] DSD considered when hypospadias or bilateral non-palpable.
- [ ] Stricture length, site, aetiology defined by imaging/cystoscopy.
- [ ] Number of prior endoscopic treatments recorded; urethroplasty offered when indicated.
