# Holmium laser settings and flexible scope care

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** fragment stones efficiently without damaging the ureter or an expensive
flexible scope, and run the kit safely.

Status: draft — numbers are typical starting points from the literature and vary by
laser generator and fibre; always follow the device IFU and local protocol.

## 1. Laser basics
- **Holmium:YAG (2120 nm)** — workhorse; absorbed in water, so it works in contact
  (~0.5–1 mm). Settings: **pulse energy (J) × frequency (Hz) = power (W)**; pulse width
  (short/long; "Moses"/pulse-modulation modes on some units).
- **Thulium fibre laser (TFL, 1940 nm)** — very low energies at very high frequencies,
  finer dust, less retropulsion; watch for heat (lower total power, irrigate well).

## 2. Typical starting settings
| Goal | Holmium (example) |
|---|---|
| **Dusting** (no basket) | Low energy 0.2–0.5 J, high frequency 40–80 Hz, long pulse |
| **Fragmentation** (then basket) | Higher energy 0.6–1.5 J, low frequency 5–15 Hz, short pulse |
| **Popcorning** (calyx) | ~1 J × 20–40 Hz, fibre near (not on) fragments |
| Soft tissue (e.g. stricture incision) | Lower power, per protocol |
- Keep total power modest in the ureter and **irrigation flowing**: high power with poor
  outflow causes thermal injury (fluid temperature can exceed 43 °C quickly).
- Retropulsion: lower energy, long pulse, or Moses mode; stone trap if needed.

## 3. Fibre handling
- **200–272 µm** fibres in flexible URS (preserve deflection); 365–550 µm in rigid/
  PCNL.
- **Never fire inside the scope** — tip must be visible ≥ ~2–3 mm beyond the scope tip
  (and not touching the scope).
- **Straighten the scope before advancing the fibre**; advancing a fibre through a
  deflected flexible scope perforates the working channel.
- Strip/cleave fibre tips per IFU when burn-back reduces efficiency (or use single-use
  pre-cleaved fibres). Test fire into fluid aimed away from tissue.

## 4. Irrigation and access
- Gravity or pressurised irrigation; keep **intrarenal pressure low** (high pressure →
  pyelovenous backflow, sepsis). A **ureteral access sheath** improves outflow and
  scope life; size to the ureter (e.g. 10/12 Fr) and don't force it.
- Pre-stenting if the ureter won't accept the scope — come back in 1–2 weeks.

## 5. Flexible scope care
- Most damage: fibre through deflected scope, over-torque, scope crushed by bed rails
  or kinked, biting by patient (flexible cystoscopy, rarely).
- **Leak test** before reprocessing; fail → quarantine and send for repair, don't
  reprocess.
- Reprocess per manufacturer IFU (manual clean + high-level disinfection or sterilisation;
  traceability log per scope and patient).
- **Single-use flexible ureteroscopes** for high-damage-risk cases (lower pole,
  large burden) or where reprocessing capacity is limited — weigh cost per case.

## 6. Laser safety
- Designated **laser protection supervisor**, warning signs, door interlock/locked,
  **wavelength-specific eye protection** for everyone in the room (including patient's
  eyes if awake), key control, standby mode when not firing, fire risk with drapes
  and oxygen.

## Gotchas
- Laser fired with fibre tip inside the scope.
- High power with no outflow → thermal ureteric injury.
- Using a 365 µm fibre in a flexible scope → lost deflection to lower pole.
- Skipping the leak test → fluid invasion destroys the scope.

## Verify
- [ ] Settings chosen for dusting vs fragmenting and recorded.
- [ ] Fibre size appropriate; tip visible beyond scope before firing.
- [ ] Irrigation outflow adequate; access sheath use recorded.
- [ ] Leak test passed and reprocessing logged against patient.
- [ ] Laser safety checklist completed (signage, eyewear, key).
