# Haematuria and raised PSA workup

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** find the urothelial, renal and prostate cancers that matter without
over-investigating low-risk patients.

## Part A — Haematuria

### 1. Confirm and classify
- **Visible (gross) haematuria:** treat as significant; investigate even if a UTI or
  anticoagulation is present (anticoagulants unmask, they don't cause, most bleeding).
- **Non-visible (microscopic):** dipstick positive must be **confirmed by microscopy**
  (≥ 3 RBC/HPF per AUA) on a properly collected sample; exclude UTI, menstruation,
  recent exercise or instrumentation and repeat after treating infection.
- Check renal function, BP, urine albumin:creatinine. Dysmorphic RBCs, casts,
  proteinuria, rising creatinine → **nephrology** route (glomerular).

### 2. Risk-stratify (AUA 2020 example)
- **Low risk** (e.g. women < 50 / men < 40, never-smokers, 3–10 RBC/HPF, no risk
  factors) → shared decision: repeat urinalysis or cystoscopy + ultrasound.
- **Intermediate** → cystoscopy + renal ultrasound.
- **High risk** (age ≥ 60 men, heavy smoking > 30 pack-years, > 25 RBC/HPF, visible
  haematuria) → **cystoscopy + CT urogram** (multiphase).
- UK NICE NG12 two-week-wait criteria for adults (e.g. ≥ 45 with unexplained visible
  haematuria) — follow your local system.

### 3. Tests
- **Flexible cystoscopy:** whole bladder, both ureteric orifices, urethra; biopsy or
  arrange TURBT for lesions.
- **CT urogram** for upper tracts and kidneys; MR urogram or retrograde studies if
  contrast contraindicated.
- Urine cytology mainly for suspected high-grade/CIS or upper-tract disease, not as a
  screening test.

## Part B — Raised PSA

### 1. Before acting on a number
- Counsel **before** the test (shared decision: overdiagnosis vs benefit).
- Avoid testing within ~6 weeks of UTI/prostatitis, recent ejaculation (48 h),
  vigorous cycling, catheterisation or biopsy.
- **Repeat** a mildly raised PSA before referral (variation is common).
- **5-alpha-reductase inhibitors** (finasteride, dutasteride) roughly **halve PSA** after
  ~6–12 months — double the value for interpretation.
- Age-specific thresholds are used in some systems (e.g. NICE: ≥ 3.0 at 50–69 in UK);
  DRE: a hard, irregular prostate needs urgent referral regardless of PSA.

### 2. MRI-first pathway
- **Pre-biopsy multiparametric/biparametric MRI**, reported with **PI-RADS v2.1**.
- **PI-RADS 4–5** → targeted (± systematic) biopsy. **PI-RADS 3** → biopsy guided by
  **PSA density** (commonly ≥ 0.15–0.20 ng/mL/cc favours biopsy) and risk calculators.
  **PI-RADS 1–2** with low PSA density → may avoid biopsy with PSA follow-up.
- **Transperineal biopsy** preferred increasingly (lower sepsis risk than transrectal).

## Gotchas
- Attributing visible haematuria to warfarin/DOAC and not investigating.
- Diagnosing microscopic haematuria from dipstick alone.
- Missing upper-tract tumours by doing ultrasound only in high-risk patients.
- Forgetting 5-ARI adjustment → false reassurance.
- Biopsying everyone with a raised PSA without MRI triage.

## Verify
- [ ] Haematuria confirmed and classified; nephrological features checked.
- [ ] Risk category assigned; cystoscopy and correct upper-tract imaging requested.
- [ ] PSA repeated, confounders and 5-ARI accounted for, counselling documented.
- [ ] MRI PI-RADS and PSA density recorded before biopsy decision.
- [ ] Results and next step communicated with a named follow-up.
