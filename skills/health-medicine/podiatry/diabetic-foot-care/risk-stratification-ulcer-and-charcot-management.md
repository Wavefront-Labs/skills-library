# Diabetic foot: risk stratification, ulcer and Charcot management

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Antibiotic
> choice and duration, and any drug dosing, must be checked against the current NICE
> tables, BNF and local infection guideline.

**Goal:** stratify risk, refer at the right speed, offload and document ulcers, and never
miss the infected, ischaemic or Charcot foot. Follows NICE NG19 (UK).

## 1. Assess and stratify
- Assess adults at diagnosis and at least annually, when any foot problem arises, and on
  hospital admission or change in status.
- Remove shoes, socks and dressings; examine both feet for neuropathy (10 g monofilament),
  limb ischaemia, ulceration, callus, infection or inflammation, deformity, gangrene
  and Charcot arthropathy.
- Use ABPI per the NICE peripheral arterial disease guideline, and interpret carefully:
  calcified arteries may falsely elevate results.

NICE risk categories:
| Category | Findings |
|---|---|
| Low | no risk factors except callus alone |
| Moderate | deformity, or neuropathy, or peripheral arterial disease |
| High | previous ulcer or amputation, renal replacement therapy, neuropathy plus PAD, or neuropathy or PAD with callus and/or deformity |
| Active problem | ulceration, infection, chronic limb-threatening ischaemia, gangrene, or suspected acute Charcot (unexplained hot, swollen foot with colour change, with or without pain) |

## 2. Review and referral
- Reassess: low annually with the diabetes review; moderate for example every 3 to 6
  months; high for example every 1 to 2 months, or every 1 to 2 weeks if there is
  immediate concern.
- Refer moderate and high risk to the foot protection service (assessed within 2 to 4
  weeks if high, 6 to 8 weeks if moderate).
- Limb- or life-threatening problem (ulcer with fever or sepsis signs, ulcer with limb
  ischaemia, suspected deep soft tissue or bone infection, gangrene): refer immediately to
  acute services and inform the multidisciplinary foot care service.
- Any other active problem: refer within 1 working day to the multidisciplinary foot care
  or foot protection service, for triage within 1 further working day.
- In hospital, refer to the multidisciplinary foot service within 24 hours of the first
  foot examination. Moderate- or high-risk inpatients need a pressure redistribution
  device to offload the heel.

## 3. Ulcer care
- Record size, depth and position; use a standardised severity system such as SINBAD or
  University of Texas. Do not use the Wagner system.
- Standard care offers one or more of: offloading, infection control, ischaemia control,
  debridement, dressings.
- Offer non-removable casting for plantar neuropathic, non-ischaemic, uninfected forefoot
  and midfoot ulcers, with an alternative offloading device until casting is available.
- Hospital debridement only by the multidisciplinary foot care service; community
  debridement only by suitably trained staff.
- Do not offer electrical stimulation, platelet-rich plasma gel, regenerative matrices,
  dalteparin, growth factors or hyperbaric oxygen outside a clinical trial.

## 4. Infection and osteomyelitis
- Suspected infection with a wound: send a soft tissue or bone sample from the base of
  the debrided wound; a deep swab if that is not possible. Sample before, or as close as
  possible to, starting antibiotics, and start them as soon as possible.
- Consider an X-ray; think about osteomyelitis with local infection or a deep or chronic
  wound; it may be present despite normal inflammatory markers, X-ray or probe-to-bone
  testing. Consider MRI if suspected and the X-ray is unconfirmed.
- Antibiotic choice follows NICE tables by severity; seek specialist advice for under 18s.
  Oral first line if suitable; review IV antibiotics by 48 hours.

## Gotchas
- Neuropathic patients feel no pain: ulcers deteriorate silently between visits.
- Treat an unexplained hot, swollen neuropathic foot as an active problem and refer within
  the timeframes above; common misdiagnoses are cellulitis, gout and DVT.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk category and review interval recorded and booked.
- [ ] Ulcer measured and classified with SINBAD or Texas, same system each visit.
- [ ] Referral timing matches the problem type.
- [ ] Offloading chosen and adherence checked.
- [ ] Microbiology sampled from the debrided base before antibiotics where possible.
