# Debridement, dressing selection and pressure redistribution

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Product
> availability, formulary dressings and scope-of-practice rules for sharp debridement
> differ by service and jurisdiction.

**Goal:** heal lower-limb wounds by treating the cause (pressure, ischaemia, venous
hypertension, infection), keeping the wound bed healthy, and matching dressings to what
the wound is actually doing this week.

## 1. Before you touch it: cause and perfusion
- Classify aetiology: **neuropathic** (plantar, callus rim, painless), **ischaemic**
  (toe tips, heel margins, pale base, painful), **neuro-ischaemic**, **venous**
  (gaiter area, oedema, haemosiderin), **pressure** (heel in bed-bound), **traumatic**.
- Document perfusion (pulses, Doppler, ABPI/toe pressure) before debriding or applying
  compression. See `health-medicine.podiatry.podiatric-assessment-and-diagnosis.foot-and-lower-limb-workup-and-differential`.

## 2. Wound bed preparation (TIME / TIMERS)
- **T**issue: remove non-viable tissue. **I**nfection/inflammation. **M**oisture balance.
  **E**dge: undermined or rolled edges, callus rim. (TIMERS adds **R**egeneration and
  **S**ocial/patient factors.)
- Measure (L × W × D), photograph with a scale, record the same way each visit. A wound
  not reducing ~40–50% in area by **4 weeks** is unlikely to heal on the current plan —
  reassess cause.

## 3. Debridement
- **Sharp (conservative) debridement** with scalpel/forceps/curette removes callus,
  slough and necrotic tissue to bleeding/healthy margins and saucerises undermined edges.
  Within podiatric scope in most services; surgical debridement of deep tissue is not.
- **Do not sharp-debride dry, stable ischaemic necrosis** without vascular review —
  keep it dry and refer.
- Alternatives: autolytic (hydrogel/hydrocolloid), larval therapy, mechanical
  (monofilament pads), ultrasonic.
- Watch for bleeding risk (anticoagulants), pain in non-neuropathic patients (topical
  or local anaesthesia), and probe to bone after debridement.

## 4. Dressings by function, not brand
| Wound state | Typical choice |
|---|---|
| Dry/sloughy, needs moisture | hydrogel, hydrocolloid (avoid occlusion on plantar diabetic ulcers — maceration risk) |
| Moderate–heavy exudate | alginate, hydrofibre, foam |
| Clinically infected/high bioburden | short course antimicrobial (silver, iodine, PHMB, honey), reviewed at ~2 weeks |
| Fragile periwound | silicone contact layer, barrier film |
Evidence for one dressing class over another is weak (IWGDF): choose on exudate,
comfort, cost and change frequency. Dressings do not heal a pressure-driven wound.

## 5. Offloading and pressure redistribution
- **Plantar diabetic forefoot ulcer:** non-removable knee-high device (TCC or
  irremovable walker) first line where safe.
- **Felt/foam deflective padding** (semi-compressed felt with aperture) as an adjunct or
  when casting is unsuitable; change at each visit.
- Post-op/wound shoes, removable walkers, and later custom insoles.
- **In-shoe pressure mapping** (where available) to confirm relief — clinicians
  routinely overestimate how much an insole offloads.
- Heel wounds: heel floats/off-loading boots in bed; casts contraindicated for some
  ischaemic heels.
- Venous ulcers: compression (once ABPI permits) is the offloading equivalent.

## 6. When to escalate
- Spreading infection, systemic signs, crepitus, abscess → emergency.
- Toe pressure low / no healing at 4–6 weeks → vascular.
- Exposed tendon, joint or bone; deep space infection → surgical team.
- Atypical wound (unusual site, rolled edge, exuberant tissue) → biopsy to exclude
  malignancy (Marjolin's) or pyoderma gangrenosum.

## Gotchas
- Callus left in place raises plantar pressure substantially; debride every visit.
- Bulky dressings in normal shoes add pressure — the patient needs footwear change too.
- Casts can cause new ulcers: check fit, skin and pulses each change.

## Verify
- [ ] Aetiology and perfusion recorded before debridement or compression.
- [ ] Wound measured and photographed the same way every visit; 4-week area trend checked.
- [ ] Debridement depth and tissue removed documented; ischaemic necrosis left for review.
- [ ] Dressing choice justified by exudate/bed; antimicrobials reviewed at 2 weeks.
- [ ] Offloading method named and its use verified, not assumed.
