# Overuse injury diagnosis, load management and return to run

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Grading
> systems and timelines below are typical figures from the literature; individual
> progression should be symptom- and criteria-led.

**Goal:** get the tissue diagnosis right (tendon, bone, joint, nerve), catch the
high-risk bone stress injuries, and bring the athlete back through measurable
criteria rather than a calendar.

## 1. Training-load history (most injuries start here)
- Recent change in **volume, intensity, surface, footwear, terrain** (hills, track
  bends), cross-training, competition schedule.
- Sleep, illness, menstrual history, diet/weight changes → **RED-S/REDs** screen
  (low energy availability raises bone stress injury risk in all sexes).
- Previous injuries, especially prior bone stress injury.

## 2. Pattern recognition
| Presentation | Likely | Key test |
|---|---|---|
| Morning/first-step Achilles stiffness, pain warms up then returns | mid-portion Achilles tendinopathy | palpation 2–6 cm above insertion, single-leg heel raise count |
| Pain at Achilles insertion, worse uphill/in shoes with heel counter | insertional tendinopathy ± Haglund | avoid dorsiflexion loading early |
| First-step plantar heel pain | plantar heel pain | medial calcaneal tubercle tenderness, windlass |
| Focal bony tenderness, pain on hopping, worsening with each run | **bone stress injury** | hop test, focal palpation, MRI |
| Diffuse posteromedial shin pain > 5 cm | MTSS | differentiate from tibial BSI (focal) |
| Lateral ankle pain after sprains | peroneal tendinopathy, sinus tarsi, OCD of talus | instability testing |

## 3. High-risk bone stress injury sites — don't manage like low-risk
- **Navicular**, **base of 5th metatarsal (Jones zone)**, **anterior tibial cortex**,
  medial malleolus, sesamoids, femoral neck (tension side).
- These need **MRI**, non-weight-bearing or boot immobilisation, and often orthopaedic
  referral. X-rays are frequently normal early.
- Low-risk sites (metatarsal shafts 2–4, posteromedial tibia, calcaneus) typically
  resolve with relative rest and graded reloading.

## 4. Load management
- Reduce load to a **tolerable** level rather than total rest (tendons need load;
  bones need protection from the provoking load).
- Tendon: pain-monitoring model — pain during/after loading up to ~3–5/10 that settles
  by next morning is acceptable (commonly used, not universal). Progress isometric →
  heavy slow resistance → plyometric → running.
- Maintain fitness with cross-training (cycling, pool running) within symptom limits.
- Modify contributing factors: cadence increase (~5–10%) can reduce loading at the
  knee/hip and tibia; footwear; surface; orthoses where a mechanism is clear (see
  `health-medicine.podiatry.biomechanics-and-orthotic-therapy.biomechanical-assessment-and-orthotic-prescription`).

## 5. Criteria-based return to run
Before running: pain-free walking for ~30 min, pain-free single-leg hop (e.g. 10–20
reps for bone injuries), single-leg heel raise near the uninjured side (often ~25+
reps), no focal tenderness for BSI.
- Walk–run intervals (e.g. 1 min run / 1 min walk), alternate days, progress if
  symptoms stay ≤ mild next day.
- Increase one variable at a time; add speed and hills last.

## Red flags / refer
- High-risk stress fracture, night or rest pain, systemic features, suspected
  osteoid osteoma/tumour, compartment syndrome symptoms (exertional, resolving with
  rest — consider pressure testing), recurrent BSI (bone health workup), suspected
  Achilles rupture (Simmonds-Thompson test) → orthopaedics/sports physician.

## Gotchas
- "Shin splints" that localise to a point are a stress fracture until shown otherwise.
- Athletes under-report symptoms near competition; ask about painkiller use.
- Fluoroquinolones and corticosteroid injections increase tendon rupture risk.

## Verify
- [ ] Training-load change and RED-S screen documented.
- [ ] Bone stress injury actively considered (hop test, palpation) and site risk graded.
- [ ] Load plan names what the athlete *can* do, not just what to stop.
- [ ] Return-to-run criteria written and tested before running resumes.
- [ ] High-risk sites imaged and referred.
