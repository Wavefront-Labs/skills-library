# Foot and lower-limb workup and differential

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Thresholds and
> referral pathways vary by region and service; follow local protocols.

**Goal:** turn "my foot hurts" into a ranked differential, with vascular and neurological
status documented every time, and catch the few presentations that need same-day
escalation.

## 1. History that narrows it fast
- **Site** (heel, arch, forefoot, toes, ankle), **onset** (sudden vs insidious),
  **pattern** (first-step pain, end-of-day ache, night/rest pain), **load link**
  (new shoes, training change, occupation, weight change).
- Systemic: diabetes (duration, HbA1c), smoking, PAD symptoms (claudication distance),
  inflammatory arthritis, gout, renal disease, anticoagulants, steroids, previous ulcer
  or amputation.
- Neuropathic descriptors: burning, tingling, "walking on cotton wool", numbness.

## 2. Vascular screen (every new patient)
- Palpate **dorsalis pedis** and **posterior tibial** pulses; note capillary refill,
  temperature gradient, hair/skin trophic change, dependent rubor, oedema.
- **Hand-held Doppler** waveform: triphasic/biphasic reassuring; monophasic suggests
  disease.
- **ABPI**: roughly 0.9–1.3 normal; < 0.9 suggests PAD; < 0.5 severe. **> 1.3 or
  incompressible** (diabetes, CKD) is non-diagnostic — use **toe pressure / TBPI**
  (TBPI < ~0.7 abnormal; toe pressure < ~30 mmHg implies poor healing potential).
  *Exact cut-offs differ between guidelines — check local protocol.*
- Palpable pulses do **not** exclude PAD, especially in diabetes.

## 3. Neurological screen
- **10 g monofilament** at standard plantar sites (commonly 3–10 sites depending on
  protocol); **128 Hz tuning fork** at the hallux IPJ; **Ipswich Touch Test** where no
  equipment. Add ankle reflexes, proprioception, pinprick.
- Distinguish **length-dependent polyneuropathy** (symmetric, stocking) from focal
  entrapment (tarsal tunnel, Morton's) and radiculopathy (dermatomal, back pain, L5/S1).

## 4. Gait and structural observation
- Barefoot and shod; walking and, if relevant, running. Note foot posture (e.g. Foot
  Posture Index), rearfoot alignment, hallux dorsiflexion (functional hallux limitus),
  ankle dorsiflexion (lunge test), single-leg heel raise, footwear wear pattern.
- See `health-medicine.podiatry.biomechanics-and-orthotic-therapy.biomechanical-assessment-and-orthotic-prescription`.

## 5. Regional differential (common first, dangerous flagged)
| Region | Common | Don't miss |
|---|---|---|
| Heel | plantar heel pain/fasciopathy, fat-pad atrophy, Sever's (children) | calcaneal stress fracture, Baxter's/tarsal tunnel neuropathy, spondyloarthropathy enthesitis |
| Midfoot | midfoot OA, tibialis posterior dysfunction | navicular stress fracture, **Charcot neuroarthropathy** (warm, swollen, neuropathic) |
| Forefoot | metatarsalgia, Morton's neuroma, hallux valgus/rigidus | metatarsal stress fracture, plantar plate tear, gout, Freiberg's |
| Toes/nails | onychocryptosis, onychomycosis, corns | subungual melanoma (Hutchinson's sign), osteomyelitis under ulcer |
| Whole foot | neuropathy, chilblains | acute limb ischaemia, cellulitis, septic arthritis |

## 6. Red flags → same-day escalation
- **Acute limb ischaemia** (pain, pallor, pulselessness, paraesthesia, paralysis, cold)
  → emergency vascular.
- **Hot swollen foot in a neuropathic patient** → treat as Charcot or infection until
  proven otherwise; offload and refer to the MDT foot service urgently.
- Spreading cellulitis, systemic signs, crepitus, or **wet gangrene** → emergency.
- Rest pain, tissue loss, or toe pressure markedly reduced → urgent vascular referral.
- Pigmented nail streak widening or with nail-fold pigment → urgent dermatology referral.
- Unexplained night pain, mass, weight loss → consider tumour; image and refer.

## Gotchas
- Calcified vessels give falsely reassuring ABPI.
- Neuropathic patients under-report pain: the absence of pain is not reassurance.
- Bilateral "plantar fasciitis" in a young patient with back stiffness: think
  inflammatory enthesitis.
- Imaging: plain X-ray early can be normal in stress fracture and Charcot; MRI if
  suspicion persists.

## Verify
- [ ] Pulses, Doppler waveform and ABPI or toe pressure recorded with values.
- [ ] Monofilament and vibration results documented per site.
- [ ] Gait and footwear observed, not just the static foot.
- [ ] Differential lists at least one "don't miss" diagnosis and how it was excluded.
- [ ] Any red flag triggered a documented, timed referral.
