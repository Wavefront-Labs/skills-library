# Forefoot surgery: selection and perioperative care

> General clinical/professional reference for trained surgical practitioners, not a
> how-to for operating. Scope of practice for podiatric surgeons differs widely by
> country (e.g. UK podiatric surgeons, US DPMs). VTE prophylaxis, antibiotic and
> analgesic regimens must follow the current local formulary and guidelines.

**Goal:** operate on the right patient for the right problem with a procedure matched
to the deformity, and run the perioperative pathway so complications are prevented or
caught early.

## 1. Selection — most of the outcome is decided here
- **Indication** is pain and function, not appearance; conservative care (footwear,
  orthoses, padding) tried and documented.
- **Expectations**: swelling for months, shoe restrictions, time off work/driving,
  final result often at 6–12 months.
- Risk screen: vascular status (pulses/ABPI/toe pressure), neuropathy, diabetes
  control, smoking (non-union, wound), inflammatory arthritis and its drugs,
  osteoporosis, previous VTE, BMI, psychosocial factors.
- Baseline **PROM** (e.g. MOXFQ, EQ-5D) so outcome can be audited.

## 2. Imaging
- **Weight-bearing** dorsoplantar and lateral radiographs. Standard angles:
  hallux valgus angle (HVA; > ~15° abnormal), intermetatarsal angle (IMA; > ~9°),
  DMAA, sesamoid position, first MTPJ congruency and arthritis.
- Approximate grading used for planning (systems vary): mild HVA < 20°/IMA < 13°,
  moderate HVA 20–40°/IMA 13–20°, severe HVA > 40°/IMA > 20°.

## 3. Matching procedure to deformity (frameworks, not rules)
| Problem | Typical options |
|---|---|
| Mild–moderate hallux valgus, congruent joint | distal osteotomy (chevron), scarf ± Akin; MIS techniques |
| Severe HV or 1st TMTJ hypermobility | proximal osteotomy, Lapidus (1st TMTJ arthrodesis) |
| HV with arthritic 1st MTPJ | 1st MTPJ arthrodesis |
| Hallux rigidus early | cheilectomy ± proximal phalanx osteotomy |
| Hallux rigidus advanced | 1st MTPJ arthrodesis (implants: mixed evidence) |
| Flexible lesser toe deformity | flexor tenotomy/transfer |
| Rigid hammertoe | PIPJ arthrodesis ± extensor release, plantar plate repair if MTPJ unstable |
| Morton's neuroma failing conservative care | excision (dorsal approach) or other options per surgeon |
Choice also depends on surgeon training, evidence (MIS long-term data still maturing)
and patient factors.

## 4. Perioperative pathway
- Anaesthesia: often ankle block ± sedation or GA; day-case for most forefoot work.
- **VTE risk assessment** using the local tool; mechanical and/or pharmacological
  prophylaxis for immobilised or higher-risk patients per guideline (NICE NG89 covers
  foot and ankle surgery — **check current local protocol**).
- Antibiotic prophylaxis per local policy (usually single dose when metalwork is used).
- Post-op: elevation, ice, protected weight-bearing in a post-op shoe (or NWB per
  procedure), wound review ~2 weeks, X-ray at ~6 weeks for osteotomy/arthrodesis union,
  footwear transition thereafter.

## 5. Complications to watch for
- Wound infection/dehiscence, **DVT/PE**, delayed/non-union (smokers, diabetes),
  malunion, recurrence, **transfer metatarsalgia**, hallux varus (overcorrection),
  avascular necrosis, nerve injury/neuroma, stiffness, CRPS.
- Escalate urgently: calf pain/swelling or breathlessness, spreading erythema with
  systemic upset, dusky or white toe (vascular compromise), uncontrolled pain out of
  proportion (consider compartment syndrome after midfoot/rearfoot work).

## Gotchas
- Non-weight-bearing films underestimate deformity.
- Correcting the hallux alone when lesser toes or metatarsalgia drive symptoms
  disappoints patients — address the whole forefoot plan.
- MIS procedures shorten wounds but not bone healing.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication, failed conservative care and expectations documented.
- [ ] Weight-bearing X-ray angles measured and recorded.
- [ ] Procedure justified against deformity severity and joint status.
- [ ] VTE assessment completed and prophylaxis decision recorded.
- [ ] Follow-up schedule, union check and PROM at 6–12 months booked.
