# Nail surgery, callus/corn, verruca and digital blocks

> General clinical/professional reference, not patient-specific advice. Local
> anaesthetic agents, maximum doses and phenol protocols must be verified against the
> current product information, local formulary and your service's procedure policy.
> Scope of practice (e.g. LA administration rights) differs by jurisdiction.

**Goal:** perform the everyday foot procedures safely and with low recurrence, and
recognise the lesion that is not what it looks like.

## 1. Pre-procedure screen (every time)
- **Vascular status**: palpable pulses/Doppler; poor perfusion is a relative or absolute
  contraindication to nail surgery and phenol (delayed healing, necrosis).
- Diabetes, immunosuppression, anticoagulation, pregnancy (phenol often avoided),
  bleeding disorders, previous LA reactions, cardiac history.
- Consent covering regrowth risk, infection, post-op care and time off normal footwear.

## 2. Digital (toe) block
- Common approach: dorsal two-injection or ring block at the toe base, plain local
  anaesthetic (e.g. lidocaine/lignocaine or a longer-acting agent such as
  bupivacaine/levobupivacaine per protocol).
- **Adrenaline-containing LA in digits**: older teaching forbade it; current evidence
  suggests it is safe in healthy digits, but many podiatry services still exclude it
  (and it is contraindicated with vascular compromise). Follow local policy.
- Aspirate before injecting; stay well under the agent's maximum dose (calculate per kg
  — **verify current product information**); have resuscitation equipment and
  anaphylaxis/LAST protocol available.
- Tourniquet: record application time; remove before patient leaves — a retained
  tourniquet can cause digit loss.

## 3. Nail surgery
- **Partial nail avulsion (PNA)** for ingrown edge (onychocryptosis); **total (TNA)** for
  involuted, thickened or dystrophic nails.
- **Chemical matrixectomy with phenol** (commonly ~80–89% liquefied phenol, applied in
  timed applications to the matrix in a dry, exsanguinated field) gives the lowest
  regrowth rates in most series. Neutralisation/irrigation practice varies; protect
  surrounding skin.
- Alternatives: sodium hydroxide, surgical excision of the matrix (e.g. Winograd,
  Zadik), or simple avulsion without matrixectomy when regrowth is wanted.
- Infected ingrown toenail: surgery can usually proceed with phenol after drainage;
  routine pre-op antibiotics are not required in most cases (check local guidance).
- Post-op: dressing review within a few days, signs of infection, expected drainage for
  several weeks with phenol.

## 4. Callus and corns
- Scalpel debridement (No. 10/15 blade) of hyperkeratosis; **enucleate** the nucleus of
  a heloma durum; interdigital heloma molle needs separation (silicone wedge) and
  footwear review.
- Then fix the cause: pressure (deformity, footwear, gait). Without that, recurrence is
  near-certain.
- In diabetes/neuropathy: haemorrhage under callus is a pre-ulcer — treat as urgent.

## 5. Verrucae (plantar warts)
- Many resolve spontaneously (a large share within ~2 years in children); "do nothing"
  is legitimate.
- Options: salicylic acid with debridement, **cryotherapy**, needling (dry needling under
  LA), and others with variable evidence. No single option is clearly superior.
- Differentiate from corns: warts interrupt skin lines and show capillary dots; corns
  are painful on direct pressure, warts on squeezing (helpful, not definitive).

## 6. Do not treat as routine
- Pigmented nail streak, Hutchinson's sign, non-healing "granulation" or ulcer under a
  nail → **subungual melanoma/SCC**; refer urgently.
- Atypical verruca not responding in an adult → consider SCC/amelanotic melanoma and
  biopsy.
- Painful nail with fluctuance or bony change → subungual exostosis or glomus tumour;
  image first.

## Verify
- [ ] Vascular and medical screen documented before LA or phenol.
- [ ] LA agent, volume and total dose recorded against maximum; tourniquet off time logged.
- [ ] Phenol application method and timing recorded per protocol.
- [ ] Underlying cause of callus/corn addressed, not just debrided.
- [ ] Any atypical lesion referred or biopsied, not repeatedly pared.
