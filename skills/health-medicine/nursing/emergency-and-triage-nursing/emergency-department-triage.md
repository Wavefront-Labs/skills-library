# Emergency department triage

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** in a few minutes per patient, decide who must be seen now, who can safely
wait, and how long — without undertriaging the quietly dangerous patient.

## 1. Rapid "doorway" look (seconds)
Airway, breathing effort, colour, level of consciousness, obvious bleeding, distress.
If something is immediately life-threatening, stop triaging — take them straight to
resus and call for help.

## 2. Use your system's decision points
Common systems: **ESI** (US, 5 levels), **Manchester Triage System** (flowcharts and
discriminators, UK/Europe), **CTAS** (Canada), **ATS** (Australasia). The logic is
similar; ESI as an example:
1. **Needs immediate life-saving intervention?** (airway, emergency drugs, defib,
   unresponsive) → ESI 1.
2. **High-risk situation, new confusion/lethargy/disorientation, or severe
   pain/distress?** → ESI 2. Ask: "Would I give my last bed to this patient?"
3. **How many resources** will they need (labs, imaging, IV fluids/drugs, specialist
   consult, procedure)? ≥2 → ESI 3; 1 → ESI 4; none → ESI 5.
4. ESI 3 candidates with **danger-zone vitals** (e.g. adult HR >100, RR >20, SpO₂ <92%;
   paediatric thresholds age-specific) → consider up-triage to ESI 2.

## 3. High-risk presentations — triage up
- Chest pain possibly cardiac → ECG within **10 minutes** of arrival.
- Stroke symptoms with onset in the treatment window → stroke pathway.
- Suspected sepsis, especially elderly, immunosuppressed, neutropenic (chemotherapy).
- Abdominal pain in older adults; possible ectopic in anyone who could be pregnant.
- Headache "worst ever" or sudden onset.
- Anaphylaxis, airway swelling, stridor.
- Suicidal ideation or acute risk to self/others — safe space, observation.
- Children: lethargy, non-blanching rash, poor feeding, grunting.
- Mechanism-based trauma (high-speed, falls from height, anticoagulated head injury).

## 4. Document concisely
Time, chief complaint in the patient's words, onset, pertinent history and
medications (anticoagulants!), allergies, full vital set, pain score, triage
category with the **discriminator** that set it, initial interventions (ECG,
analgesia, glucose, pregnancy test, splint, isolation).

## 5. The waiting room is part of triage
- Reassess at the interval your system sets for that category, and at any change.
- Tell patients what to report and when to come back to the desk.
- Repeat vital signs; up-triage when they change. Patients who leave before being
  seen should be logged and followed up per policy.

## Gotchas
- **Undertriage risk groups:** older adults (atypical MI, blunted fever), people with
  communication barriers, intoxicated patients, frequent attenders, and those who
  "don't look sick".
- Normal vitals do not exclude serious illness; abnormal vitals rarely mean nothing.
- Pain is a valid triage criterion — and treating it early is part of triage.
- Infection control (isolation for rash/fever, respiratory symptoms) starts at triage.

Uncertainty note: ESI steps per ESI implementation handbook; danger-zone numbers
differ by version and age band — use your system's current card.

## Verify
- [ ] Immediate threats excluded at the doorway.
- [ ] Category assigned with the deciding discriminator documented.
- [ ] Time-critical tests (ECG, glucose, pregnancy test) done at triage.
- [ ] Anticoagulants and allergies recorded.
- [ ] Reassessment time set and met.
