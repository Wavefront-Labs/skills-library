# Caring for the ventilated patient

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** keep the intubated patient safe hour to hour, prevent ventilator-associated
complications, and move them towards extubation as early as is safe.

## 1. Start-of-shift safety check
- **Airway:** ETT size, position at the teeth/lips (cm mark) matches the documented
  value, secure ties/holder, pressure areas at lips. Check the last chest x-ray tip position.
- **Cuff pressure:** commonly kept **20–30 cmH₂O** by manometer (high pressure damages
  trachea; low pressure increases aspiration).
- **Ventilator:** mode and settings match the prescription; alarms set to sensible
  limits and audible; capnography trace present.
- **Emergency kit at the bed:** bag-valve-mask with PEEP valve connected to oxygen,
  working suction, spare ETT, difficult-airway plan known.
- Lines, infusions and pumps traced from bag to patient.

## 2. Hourly monitoring
SpO₂, EtCO₂, RR (set vs total), tidal volume (aim ~6–8 mL/kg **predicted** body
weight in most lung-protective strategies), peak and plateau pressure (plateau
commonly ≤30 cmH₂O), PEEP, FiO₂, haemodynamics, sedation score, urine output.

## 3. VAP prevention bundle
- Head of bed **30–45°** unless contraindicated.
- Daily sedation interruption and readiness-to-wean assessment (below).
- Oral care per protocol (toothbrushing; chlorhexidine use now varies — follow local policy).
- Subglottic secretion drainage if the tube has it.
- Avoid unnecessary circuit changes; drain condensate away from the patient.
- Peptic ulcer and VTE prophylaxis as prescribed.

## 4. ABCDEF / sedation and delirium
- **RASS** target set by the team (often 0 to −2 — lightly sedated).
- **Pain first:** CPOT or BPS score; analgesia-first sedation.
- **CAM-ICU** each shift for delirium.
- **Spontaneous awakening trial (SAT)** paired with a **spontaneous breathing trial
  (SBT)** daily when safety screens pass.
- Early mobility and family engagement.

## 5. Suctioning
Only when indicated (visible/audible secretions, rising peak pressures, sawtooth flow
trace, desaturation). Pre-oxygenate, closed in-line system where used, catheter less
than half the ETT internal diameter, ≤10–15 s per pass, shallow technique preferred.
Routine saline instillation is not recommended.

## 6. Sudden desaturation — DOPE
- **D**isplacement (tube out/endobronchial — check EtCO₂ trace and tube mark)
- **O**bstruction (secretions, kinked tube, biting — pass a suction catheter)
- **P**neumothorax (unequal chest rise, high pressures, hypotension)
- **E**quipment failure (disconnect, ventilator, oxygen supply)
Plus **S**tacking breaths (auto-PEEP, e.g. in asthma/COPD). **Disconnect from the
ventilator and hand-ventilate with 100% O₂** — if the patient improves, the problem
is the ventilator; if not, it's the patient or the tube. Call for help early.

## Gotchas
- **Loss of EtCO₂ waveform = displaced or blocked tube until proven otherwise**, not a sensor fault.
- Sedation holds in patients on neuromuscular blockers or with raised ICP need
  medical sign-off.
- Pressure injuries from ETT holders, NG tubes and prone positioning are common —
  reposition the tube at the lips per policy.
- Ventilator alarms silenced repeatedly mean the limits are wrong; fix the settings.

Uncertainty note: targets reflect common lung-protective and SCCM ABCDEF guidance;
units set their own ranges.

## Verify
- [ ] Tube position and cuff pressure checked and documented.
- [ ] Bag-valve-mask, suction and airway plan at bedside.
- [ ] Settings match prescription; alarms appropriate.
- [ ] RASS, pain score and CAM-ICU recorded.
- [ ] SAT/SBT screen done today.
