# Peripheral IV cannulation and line care

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** get reliable venous access first time with minimal infection and vein damage,
then keep the line safe — or remove it — every day it stays in.

## 1. Choose device and site
- **Indication check:** is IV access actually needed? Could the drug go orally? Is a
  midline/PICC more appropriate (e.g. >~5–7 days, vesicants, poor veins)?
- **Gauge:** smallest that does the job — 20–22G for most fluids and drugs; 18G for
  rapid fluids, blood and contrast (CT protocols often require 18–20G); 14–16G for
  major haemorrhage.
- **Vein:** forearm first (dorsal metacarpal and forearm veins); avoid the antecubital
  fossa for routine use (flexion → occlusion), joints, the arm with an AV fistula or
  after axillary node dissection, and areas with infection, oedema or bruising.
- Two failed attempts → ask someone more experienced, or use ultrasound guidance.

## 2. Insertion with aseptic non-touch technique (ANTT)
1. Hand hygiene, gloves, prepare a clean field; identify **key parts** (needle,
   hub, bung) that must not be touched.
2. Tourniquet ~7–10 cm above site; let the vein fill (hang the arm, warm compress,
   gentle tapping).
3. Clean with **2% chlorhexidine in 70% alcohol** for ~30 s; **let it dry fully**. Do
   not re-palpate the site after cleaning.
4. Anchor the vein with skin traction; insert bevel up at 10–30°.
5. Flashback → lower angle, advance a few mm more, then advance the cannula off the needle.
6. Release tourniquet **before** removing the needle; occlude vein above the tip.
7. Attach primed extension, flush with 0.9% saline (e.g. 5–10 mL), watching for
   swelling or pain.
8. Secure with a sterile semi-permeable transparent dressing; label date and time.

## 3. Ongoing care
- Check the site at least once per shift (and before each use) with a **VIP
  (visual infusion phlebitis) score**: 0 = healthy; 1 = possible early phlebitis — observe;
  ≥2 (pain + erythema or swelling) = remove and resite.
- Flush before and after each use and per local policy if idle.
- Review **daily** whether the line is still needed. Remove promptly; don't keep
  "just in case" cannulas.
- Routine replacement at fixed intervals vs "clinically indicated" replacement varies
  by policy — follow yours.

## 4. Infiltration vs extravasation
- **Infiltration:** non-vesicant fluid in tissue — stop, remove, elevate, monitor.
- **Extravasation:** vesicant (e.g. some chemotherapy, vasopressors, calcium,
  concentrated potassium, contrast, hypertonic glucose) — **stop infusion, leave the
  cannula in**, aspirate what you can, then remove, mark the area, and follow the
  extravasation protocol (specific antidotes/warm or cold compresses depend on the drug). Escalate.

## Central lines (brief)
Maintenance bundles cut CLABSI: hand hygiene, chlorhexidine skin antisepsis,
scrub-the-hub before every access (≥5–15 s per policy), dressing changes on schedule
or when soiled, and daily review of need.

## Gotchas
- Flashback without the cannula advancing = you've passed through or only the needle tip is in.
- Dehydrated or cold patients: warm first; don't chase tiny veins with large gauges.
- Never reinsert the needle into a cannula — it can shear the catheter.

Uncertainty note: thresholds and flush volumes vary by organisation; UK epic3/RCN
standards and INS standards used as background.

- [ ] Flushed without pain or swelling; dressed and dated.
