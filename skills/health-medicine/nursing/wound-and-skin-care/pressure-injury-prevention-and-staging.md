# Pressure injury prevention

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement or current guidelines. Based on NICE CG179 (UK); local policy and tools vary.

**Goal:** stop pressure injuries forming and catch early skin change. Stage and record any
injury using the classification your organisation uses, and refer to tissue viability per local policy.

## 1. Risk assessment
- All patients are potentially at risk.
- Assess and document risk for adults admitted to secondary care or care homes, and for
  others with a risk factor: significantly limited mobility (for example spinal cord injury),
  significant loss of sensation, a previous or current pressure ulcer, nutritional deficiency,
  inability to reposition, or significant cognitive impairment.
- A validated scale (for example Braden, Waterlow or Norton) can support clinical judgement; it does not replace it.
- Reassess when clinical status changes (after surgery, worsening illness, change in mobility).
- For children, use a scale validated for that population (for example Braden Q).

## 2. Skin assessment
- For adults at high risk offer a skin assessment by a trained professional: skin integrity
  in pressure areas, colour changes, heat, firmness and moisture variation, and any pain.
- Non-blanchable erythema may show as colour change or discolouration, particularly in
  darker skin tones.
- Check blanching by finger palpation or diascopy.
- If non-blanching erythema is found, start preventative action and consider repeating the
  skin assessment at least every 2 hours until resolved.

## 3. Prevention
- **Repositioning (adults):** encourage frequent position change, at least every 6 hours if at
  risk and at least every 4 hours if at high risk; help with equipment if they cannot move
  themselves; document the frequency required.
- **Children:** at least every 4 hours if at risk, more often for high-risk neonates and infants.
- **Do not** offer skin massage or rubbing to prevent pressure ulcers.
- **Surfaces:** high-specification foam mattress for adults admitted to secondary care or at
  high risk in primary and community care; consider one for theatre; a high-specification foam
  or equivalent cushion for wheelchair users or prolonged sitters.
- **Heels:** for high-risk adults agree a heel-offloading strategy in the care plan.
- **Moisture:** consider a barrier preparation for people at high risk of moisture lesions or
  incontinence-associated dermatitis.
- **Nutrition:** do not give supplements or subcutaneous or intravenous fluids specifically to
  prevent pressure ulcers when intake and hydration are adequate.

## 4. If a pressure ulcer is present
Offer adults a nutritional assessment by a dietitian or suitably skilled professional, and
nutritional supplements if there is a nutritional deficiency.
