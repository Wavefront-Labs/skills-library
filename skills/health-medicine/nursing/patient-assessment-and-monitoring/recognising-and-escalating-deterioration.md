# Recognising and escalating deterioration

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** catch the patient who is getting sicker hours before arrest or ICU transfer,
and escalate in a way that gets the right response. Most in-hospital arrests are
preceded by abnormal observations that were recorded but not acted on.

## 1. Take a complete set — every time
Respiratory rate (counted for a full 30–60 s, not estimated), SpO₂ and oxygen
delivered, blood pressure, heart rate, level of consciousness (ACVPU — new
**C**onfusion counts), temperature. Add urine output, pain, and capillary glucose where relevant.
**Respiratory rate is the most sensitive early sign** and the most often skipped.

## 2. Score and trend
- Use your organisation's early warning score (e.g. **NEWS2** in adults; PEWS in
  children; MEOWS/MEWS in maternity).
- NEWS2 typical response bands: 0 = routine (≥12-hourly); 1–4 = ward assessment, ≥4–6-hourly;
  **any single parameter scoring 3, or total 5–6** = urgent review by a clinician
  competent in acute illness; **≥7** = emergency response / critical-care outreach,
  continuous monitoring.
- Read the **trend**, not one number: a rising heart rate and respiratory rate over
  three sets with a "normal" BP is a warning (BP falls late, especially in the young).
- SpO₂ scale 2 (target 88–92%) only for documented hypercapnic respiratory failure.

## 3. ABCDE look (one minute)
- **A:** talking? stridor, gurgling?
- **B:** rate, effort, accessory muscles, SpO₂, can they complete a sentence?
- **C:** colour, capillary refill (>2 s), cool peripheries, HR, BP, urine output
  (<0.5 mL/kg/h), new bleeding.
- **D:** ACVPU, new confusion, glucose, pupils, pain.
- **E:** temperature, rash, wounds, drains, calves, lines.

## 4. Screen for sepsis
Suspected infection + any red flag (new confusion, RR ≥25, systolic ≤90 or drop >40,
HR >130, no urine in 18 h, needing O₂ for SpO₂ ≥92%, mottled/non-blanching rash) =
sepsis pathway: senior review, cultures, lactate, antibiotics within the target window.

## 5. Escalate with SBAR
- **S**ituation: "I'm calling about Mr X, bed 12; NEWS has risen from 2 to 7 in four hours."
- **B**ackground: admission reason, relevant history, DNACPR/treatment escalation status.
- **A**ssessment: the ABCDE findings and your concern ("I think he is septic").
- **R**ecommendation: the ask and a time frame ("I need you to review within 15
  minutes; shall I take cultures and a lactate?"). Read back any verbal order.
If the response doesn't match the concern, **escalate again** up the chain or call
the rapid response team. "Worried" is a valid escalation trigger by itself.

## Gotchas
- Beta-blockers, pacemakers and athletic baselines mask tachycardia.
- Elderly and immunosuppressed patients may not mount a fever.
- Opioids and sedation lower RR and consciousness — check sedation scores.
- Observations "rounded" to normal or taken from a monitor that isn't attached
  properly give false reassurance.
- Increase observation frequency when you escalate; don't wait for the doctor.

Uncertainty note: NEWS2 thresholds per Royal College of Physicians (UK) guidance;
other systems use different bands — follow local policy.

## Verify
- [ ] Full set recorded including a counted respiratory rate.
- [ ] Score calculated and trend reviewed against the last three sets.
- [ ] Escalation made at the threshold and documented with time and responder.
- [ ] SBAR includes a specific ask and time frame.
- [ ] Observation frequency increased; re-escalation plan agreed.
