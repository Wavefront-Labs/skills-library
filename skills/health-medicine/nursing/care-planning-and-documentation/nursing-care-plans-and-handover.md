# Nursing care plans and handover

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** turn assessment into a plan any colleague can follow, record care so it is
clear and defensible, and hand over so that nothing is lost between shifts.

## 1. The nursing process (ADPIE)
1. **Assess:** systematic data (head-to-toe, risk tools, the patient's own priorities).
2. **Diagnose:** a nursing problem statement. NANDA-I style: *problem* related to
   *cause* as evidenced by *signs*. E.g. "Impaired gas exchange related to
   pneumonia, as evidenced by SpO₂ 89% on air and RR 26."
3. **Plan:** SMART outcomes — Specific, Measurable, Achievable, Relevant, Time-bound.
   "SpO₂ ≥94% on ≤2 L/min nasal O₂ by 08:00 tomorrow" — not "improve breathing".
4. **Implement:** specific interventions with frequency and who does them —
   "Sit upright ≥45°; deep breathing and incentive spirometer 10 breaths hourly while
   awake; obs 2-hourly; titrate O₂ to target 94–98%."
5. **Evaluate:** against the outcome at the stated time; continue, modify or close.

Prioritise problems with ABC and Maslow (physiological and safety first) — three
well-maintained problems beat ten stale ones.

## 2. Documentation that stands up
- **Contemporaneous:** as soon as possible after the event; record the actual time of
  care and time of entry if different.
- **Objective and specific:** "Ate 2 tablespoons of porridge, refused rest", not
  "poor appetite". Quote the patient where it matters.
- **Include what you did and the outcome:** escalation (who, when, what was said,
  response), patient refusal and the information given, safeguarding concerns.
- **Never** alter or delete an entry; correct by single line/strikethrough (paper) or
  the system's amend function with reason. Label late entries as "late entry" with
  the current time.
- Use only approved abbreviations. Sign, print name, role, date.
- "If it isn't documented, it wasn't done" is how documentation is judged in complaints and court.

## 3. Handover with ISBAR
- **I**dentify: yourself, the patient (two identifiers).
- **S**ituation: why they're here today, current condition, resuscitation/escalation status.
- **B**ackground: relevant history, allergies, infection status.
- **A**ssessment: latest obs and early-warning score trend, lines, drains, wounds,
  pain, mobility, nutrition, mental state, risks (falls, pressure, VTE).
- **R**ecommendation: outstanding tasks with times (bloods due 14:00, IV antibiotic
  at 18:00, chase CT report), concerns, discharge plan.

## 4. Bedside handover
Where possible, hand over at the bedside: check identity, lines, pumps (rate vs
prescription), wound/drain sites and safety equipment together, and involve the
patient. Discuss sensitive information away from others.

## Gotchas
- Copy-forward in electronic records propagates outdated information — read before you carry forward.
- Generic care plans signed without individualisation offer little legal or clinical protection.
- Handovers interrupted mid-patient drop information; use a structured sheet.
- Document the time of escalations precisely; vague times weaken the record.

Uncertainty note: NANDA-I terminology use varies; many organisations use their own
problem lists. Legal standards described are general (e.g. NMC/ANA principles).

## Verify
- [ ] Each active problem has a measurable, time-bound outcome.
- [ ] Interventions specify what, how often and by whom.
- [ ] Entries timed, signed, objective and contemporaneous.
- [ ] Handover follows ISBAR with outstanding tasks timed.
- [ ] Bedside safety check done with the incoming nurse.
