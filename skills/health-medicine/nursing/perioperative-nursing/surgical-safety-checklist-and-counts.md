# Surgical safety checklist and counts

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** prevent wrong-patient/wrong-site surgery and retained surgical items — classic
"never events" — by making the checklist and counts real conversations, not box-ticking.

## 1. Before the list: team brief
Whole team, before the first patient: order of the list, equipment and implants
needed, anticipated critical events, allergies, staffing, any concerns. Name and role
introductions.

## 2. WHO checklist — three pauses
**Sign in** (before induction; nurse + anaesthetist, patient participating):
- Identity, procedure, site and **consent** confirmed with the patient.
- **Site marked** (an indelible arrow/initials at or near the site, visible after
  draping) when laterality or level applies.
- Anaesthesia machine and medication check; pulse oximeter on and working.
- Allergies; difficult airway/aspiration risk; risk of >500 mL blood loss (>7 mL/kg
  in children) with blood availability.

**Time out** (before skin incision; everyone stops):
- All team members state name and role.
- Surgeon, anaesthetist and nurse confirm patient, site, procedure verbally.
- Anticipated critical events: surgeon (critical steps, duration, blood loss),
  anaesthetist (patient-specific concerns), nursing (sterility confirmed including
  indicator results, equipment issues).
- **Antibiotic prophylaxis** given within the last 60 minutes (or not applicable).
- Essential imaging displayed; implants available.

**Sign out** (before the surgeon leaves / before closure is complete):
- Procedure name as recorded.
- **Counts complete and correct.**
- **Specimens labelled** — read the label aloud, including patient name.
- Equipment problems to address; key concerns for recovery.

## 3. Counts — swabs, sharps, instruments
- **When:** before the procedure starts (baseline), when items are added, before
  closure of a cavity within a cavity (e.g. uterus), before wound closure begins, at
  skin closure, and at permanent change of scrub or circulating staff.
- **How:** scrub and circulating practitioner count **together, aloud**, in a
  consistent order (field → trolley → off-field), items separated individually, record
  immediately on the count board.
- Swabs used in cavities should be radiopaque and accounted for in packets of the
  same number; don't cut swabs.

## 4. Count discrepancy
1. Tell the surgeon immediately; stop closure where safe.
2. Search the field, the wound, the floor, bins and linen systematically.
3. If not found: **intraoperative x-ray** before the patient leaves theatre (per
   policy), document, and report as an incident.

## Gotchas
- A time-out done while people are still setting up, or with music loud, is not a time-out.
- Anyone can and should stop the process if something is wrong — "stop the line".
- Handover of counts at a break must be face-to-face with a recount.
- Specimen errors (unlabelled, wrong pot, wrong form) are common and can mean repeat
  surgery — label at the point of removal.
- Local Safety Standards for Invasive Procedures (LocSSIPs) apply outside theatres too.

Uncertainty note: summarised from WHO checklist and common national standards
(e.g. NatSSIPs/AORN); local versions add items.

## Verify
- [ ] Team brief held.
- [ ] Site mark visible post-draping; consent matches list and patient.
- [ ] Time out with all members, antibiotic timing confirmed.
- [ ] Counts done at each required point and recorded.
- [ ] Sign out completed; specimens labelled and read back.
