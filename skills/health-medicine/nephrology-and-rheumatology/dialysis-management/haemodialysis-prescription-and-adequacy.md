# Haemodialysis prescription and adequacy

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** write and adjust a maintenance haemodialysis (HD) prescription that achieves
adequate solute clearance and safe fluid removal, then use monthly data to fix what's
not working — rather than rubber-stamping last month's settings.

Numbers below are typical starting ranges; prescriptions are individualised and unit
protocols govern.

## 1. Prescription elements
| Element | Typical starting point | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Frequency/time | 3×/week, ≥ 4 h | Short sessions drive high UF rates; incremental HD possible with good residual function |
| Blood flow (Qb) | 300–450 mL/min | Limited by access; lower in first fistula uses |
| Dialysate flow (Qd) | 500–800 mL/min | |
| Dialyser | High-flux; size per clearance needs | Online haemodiafiltration where available |
| Dialysate K⁺ | 2 mmol/L common | Avoid 0–1 K⁺ baths (arrhythmia risk); adjust to pre-HD K⁺ |
| Dialysate Ca²⁺ | 1.25–1.5 mmol/L | Per bone/mineral status |
| Dialysate Na⁺ | ~137–140 mmol/L | High Na⁺ worsens thirst and interdialytic weight gain |
| Bicarbonate | ~32–35 mmol/L | Target pre-HD HCO₃⁻ ~22–26 |
| Temperature | 35.5–36.5 °C (cooler) | Improves haemodynamic stability |
| Anticoagulation | Unfractionated heparin bolus ± infusion, or LMWH | Heparin-free with saline flushes or citrate when bleeding risk |
| UF goal | Pre-weight − dry weight + intake during HD | |

## 2. Dry weight and ultrafiltration
- **Dry (target) weight:** lowest post-dialysis weight tolerated without symptoms or
  hypotension, with no oedema and controlled BP. Reassess clinically every visit (lung
  crackles, oedema, JVP, BP trends, cramps, post-HD fatigue); bioimpedance or lung
  ultrasound can help.
- **UF rate** above ~**10–13 mL/kg/h** is associated with higher mortality — extend
  time, add sessions, or counsel on fluid/salt intake rather than pushing UF.
- Interdialytic weight gain target ≲ 2–2.5 kg (or < ~4% body weight).

## 3. Adequacy
- **spKt/V target ≥ 1.4 per session** for 3×/week (minimum delivered 1.2); **URR ≥ 65–70%**.
  URR = (pre-urea − post-urea)/pre-urea. Draw post-dialysis urea by a standardised
  slow-flow method to avoid recirculation errors.
- If Kt/V low: check actual vs prescribed time (missed minutes), Qb achieved, access
  recirculation, dialyser clotting, sample technique; then increase time, Qb, dialyser
  size.
- Adequacy is more than urea: control of BP, volume, phosphate, potassium, acidosis,
  nutrition (albumin, appetite), and symptoms.
- Preserve **residual kidney function**: avoid nephrotoxins and intradialytic hypotension;
  consider incremental HD.

## 4. Intradialytic hypotension
Stop/reduce UF, Trendelenburg, saline bolus. Prevent: reassess dry weight, lower UF rate
(longer/extra session), cool dialysate, hold antihypertensives pre-dialysis, avoid
eating during HD, review cardiac function. Midodrine per nephrologist.

## 5. Vascular access
- Preference: AV fistula > AV graft > tunnelled catheter, but individualised (KDOQI 2019
  "ESKD life-plan").
- Every session: look, listen, feel — thrill (continuous), bruit, arm swelling, prolonged
  bleeding after needle removal (stenosis), aneurysm skin changes (shiny, ulcerated → urgent).
- Catheters: exit-site care, catheter-related bloodstream infection → blood cultures and
  empirical antibiotics, lock solutions per protocol.

## 6. Peritoneal dialysis (brief)
CAPD or automated PD; monitor weekly Kt/V urea ≥ 1.7 (with residual function), fluid
balance, glucose load. **Cloudy effluent ± abdominal pain = peritonitis until proven
otherwise:** effluent cell count (> 100 WBC/µL with > 50% neutrophils after ≥ 2 h dwell),
Gram stain and culture (blood culture bottles), empirical intraperitoneal antibiotics
covering Gram-positive and Gram-negative organisms per ISPD.

## Gotchas
- A good Kt/V with shortened sessions can hide inadequate fluid removal.
- Pre-HD K⁺ high? Ask about diet, missed sessions, constipation, drugs — not just bath.
- Taking BP or cannulating the access arm damages it; label it.
- Monthly bloods taken post- rather than pre-dialysis mislead.

## Verify
- [ ] Every prescription element specified and justified.
- [ ] Dry weight reassessed clinically; UF rate below ~13 mL/kg/h.
- [ ] spKt/V ≥ 1.4 (or URR ≥ 65–70%) with sampling validated.
- [ ] Hypotension episodes reviewed with preventive changes.
- [ ] Access examined and documented each session.
