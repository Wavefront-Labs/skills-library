# Early rheumatoid arthritis: treat-to-target

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** get a patient with new inflammatory arthritis diagnosed and on an effective
DMARD within weeks, then measure disease activity at every visit and escalate until
remission or low disease activity — the "window of opportunity" limits joint damage.

## 1. Recognise inflammatory arthritis
- Joint swelling (synovitis, soft/boggy, not bony), early-morning stiffness > 30–60 min,
  improvement with activity, positive MCP/MTP squeeze test, fatigue.
- Refer urgently (many systems aim for rheumatology review within 3 weeks of symptom
  onset/referral) for persistent synovitis; avoid starting steroids before review if
  possible (masks findings).
- Differential: viral arthritis (parvovirus B19, hepatitis B/C, chikungunya), crystal
  arthritis, psoriatic arthritis (dactylitis, nail changes, DIP involvement, enthesitis),
  reactive arthritis, spondyloarthritis (inflammatory back pain, uveitis, IBD), lupus,
  polymyalgia rheumatica, haemochromatosis (2nd/3rd MCP), sarcoid.

## 2. Workup
RF and **anti-CCP** (more specific; both raise erosion risk), ESR/CRP, FBC, renal and
liver function, urate, ANA if features of CTD, hepatitis B/C and HIV serology (before
DMARDs), X-rays of hands and feet (baseline erosions), ultrasound for subclinical
synovitis if uncertain. Classification (ACR/EULAR 2010, score ≥ 6/10: joints,
serology, acute-phase reactants, duration ≥ 6 weeks) is for research consistency —
**diagnosis and treatment can proceed in clinically clear cases below the threshold**.

## 3. Start treatment
- **Methotrexate** first-line csDMARD: typically start 15 mg weekly (oral or
  subcutaneous), escalate to 20–25 mg/week over ~4–8 weeks as tolerated, with **folic
  acid** (e.g. 5 mg weekly, not the methotrexate day, or per local regimen). **Weekly
  dosing** must be explicit on every prescription — daily dosing errors are fatal.
- Contraindications/cautions: pregnancy plans (stop ≥ 1–3 months before conception per
  current guidance), significant liver disease, alcohol excess, eGFR < 30, active
  infection. Alternatives: leflunomide, sulfasalazine, hydroxychloroquine (as part of
  combination or mild disease).
- **Glucocorticoid bridging** (e.g. short oral taper or IM methylprednisolone) while
  DMARD takes effect; taper off within ~3 months.
- Baseline: chest X-ray (per local practice), vaccination update (pneumococcal,
  influenza, COVID-19, recombinant zoster before JAK inhibitors), contraception advice.

## 4. Measure and escalate (treat-to-target)
- At each visit (every 1–3 months while active): **DAS28-CRP/ESR** (remission < 2.6, low
  ≤ 3.2) or **CDAI** (remission ≤ 2.8, low ≤ 10), with tender and swollen joint counts,
  patient global, CRP/ESR.
- **Expect improvement by 3 months and target by 6 months.** If not reached:
  - Without poor-prognostic factors → change/add another csDMARD.
  - **With poor-prognostic factors** (high activity, RF/anti-CCP high titre, early
    erosions, csDMARD failure) → add a **bDMARD** (TNF inhibitor, IL-6R inhibitor,
    abatacept, rituximab) or **JAK inhibitor**.
  - JAK inhibitors: after ORAL Surveillance, restrict in patients ≥ 65, smokers/ex-smokers,
    or with CV/malignancy/VTE risk unless no alternatives (EMA/FDA warnings).
- Screen for **latent TB** (IGRA ± chest X-ray) and hepatitis B before biologics/JAKi.
- In sustained remission (≥ 6–12 months), consider cautious tapering, glucocorticoids first.

## 5. Safety monitoring (methotrexate example, BSR-style)
FBC, creatinine, ALT/AST, albumin every 2 weeks until dose stable for 6 weeks, then
monthly for 3 months, then every ~3 months. Act on falling WBC/platelets, rising ALT
(> 3× ULN → withhold), new cough/breathlessness (pneumonitis), mouth ulcers. Hold
during significant infections.

## Psoriatic arthritis and axial SpA (brief)
Domain-based treatment (GRAPPA/EULAR): peripheral arthritis — methotrexate then biologics
(TNF, IL-17, IL-23) or JAKi; axial disease and enthesitis — NSAIDs then TNF or IL-17
inhibitors (csDMARDs don't work for axial disease).

## Gotchas
- Normal CRP and negative RF don't exclude RA (seronegative RA is ~20–30%).
- Steroids started in primary care can mask synovitis and delay diagnosis.
- Treating fibromyalgia-driven high tender counts with escalating biologics: check swollen
  joints and ultrasound.
- Co-trimoxazole + methotrexate → marrow suppression; NSAIDs/PPIs interactions at high doses.

## Verify
- [ ] Synovitis confirmed; differential (viral, crystal, PsA, CTD) considered.
- [ ] RF, anti-CCP, CRP/ESR, baseline X-rays, hepatitis/HIV serology done.
- [ ] Methotrexate weekly dose and folic acid clearly prescribed; bridging steroid plan with end date.
- [ ] Disease activity score recorded every visit; escalation at 3/6 months if off-target.
- [ ] TB/hepatitis screening before biologics; monitoring bloods scheduled.
