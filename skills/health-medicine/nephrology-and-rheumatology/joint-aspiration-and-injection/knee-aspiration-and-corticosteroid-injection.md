# Knee aspiration and corticosteroid injection

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** aspirate a knee effusion for diagnosis or relief and, where appropriate,
inject corticosteroid — first pass, aseptically, with the right samples sent and
infection never missed. Requires supervised training before independent practice.

## 1. Indications and contraindications
- **Aspiration:** diagnose an unexplained effusion (**any hot swollen joint — exclude
  sepsis**), crystal confirmation, symptom relief of tense effusion, haemarthrosis
  assessment.
- **Injection:** osteoarthritis flare, inflammatory arthritis synovitis not controlled
  systemically, crystal arthritis where infection excluded.
- **Contraindications to injection:** suspected septic arthritis or overlying cellulitis,
  bacteraemia, prosthetic joint (refer to orthopaedics — don't inject), fracture,
  uncontrolled bleeding disorder. Anticoagulation (therapeutic INR/DOAC) is generally
  not a contraindication for knee aspiration — check local policy. Poorly controlled
  diabetes: warn about glucose rise for several days.

## 2. Consent and preparation
Discuss infection (~1 in 10,000–50,000 for injections — figures vary), post-injection
flare (24–48 h), skin depigmentation/fat atrophy, transient glucose rise, facial
flushing, failure. Prepare: chlorhexidine 2% in alcohol (or povidone-iodine), sterile
gloves (no-touch technique at minimum), 18–21G needle for aspiration (larger for
thick fluid), 23G for local, syringes (20–50 mL), sample tubes (plain sterile for
crystals/culture, EDTA for cell count), blood culture bottles, dressing.

## 3. Approach (landmark)
- Patient supine, knee extended and quadriceps relaxed (rolled towel under the knee
  can help).
- **Superolateral approach:** palpate the superolateral border of the patella; insertion
  ~1 cm superior and lateral, directing the needle under the patella toward the
  intercondylar notch at ~45° toward the midline and slightly downward. Compressing the
  medial suprapatellar pouch pushes fluid laterally.
- Alternative: medial mid-patellar or anterolateral (flexed knee) approaches.
- Mark the site, clean widely and let it dry; don't re-palpate the cleaned site with a
  non-sterile finger.
- Local anaesthetic to skin and capsule (optional for small injections).

## 4. Ultrasound guidance
Improves accuracy, especially for small effusions, obese patients and non-knee joints.
Identify the suprapatellar recess with the probe (linear, high-frequency) transverse
above the patella; in-plane needle approach from lateral. Use a sterile cover and gel.

## 5. Aspirate and send
- Aspirate as much as is easy; note volume, colour, clarity, viscosity.
- **Send:** Gram stain and culture (also inoculate blood culture bottles), **cell count
  with differential**, **crystals** (polarised microscopy — send promptly).
  - Normal < 200 WBC/µL; non-inflammatory (OA) < 2,000; inflammatory 2,000–~50,000;
    **septic often > 50,000 with > 90% neutrophils** — but lower counts don't exclude
    infection.
  - Frank blood → trauma (ligament/fracture with fat globules), anticoagulation,
    PVNS, haemophilia.
- Turbid fluid suspicious for sepsis → **don't inject steroid**; start empirical
  antibiotics per protocol after cultures; urgent orthopaedic review for washout.

## 6. Inject
- Keep the needle in place, swap syringe (hold hub firmly).
- Typical knee doses: triamcinolone acetonide 40 mg or methylprednisolone acetate
  40–80 mg (± lidocaine). Inject smoothly with no resistance (resistance → needle in
  soft tissue/ligament; reposition).
- Limit frequency to ~every 3 months for the same joint (repeated injections may
  accelerate cartilage loss — evidence mixed).

## 7. Aftercare
Plaster, relative rest 24–48 h (benefit modest), ice for flare. Return urgently for
increasing pain, redness, heat or fever after 48 h (infection vs post-injection flare,
which typically settles within 48 h). Diabetic patients monitor glucose. Document: site,
approach, asepsis, needle, volume/appearance aspirated, samples, drug and dose, batch
number, complications.

## Gotchas
- Steroid into an infected joint is catastrophic — when in doubt, aspirate, send and wait.
- Crystals deteriorate and cells lyse with delay; send fresh.
- Dry tap: re-check with ultrasound; fat pad or synovial thickening may block the needle.
- Never inject a prosthetic joint outside orthopaedic care.

## Verify
- [ ] Indication documented; sepsis, cellulitis and prosthesis excluded for injection.
- [ ] Consent covered infection, flare, atrophy and glucose rise.
- [ ] Aseptic no-touch technique with landmark or ultrasound confirmation.
- [ ] Fluid sent for Gram/culture, cell count and crystals; turbid fluid not injected.
- [ ] Drug, dose, batch and aftercare advice recorded.
