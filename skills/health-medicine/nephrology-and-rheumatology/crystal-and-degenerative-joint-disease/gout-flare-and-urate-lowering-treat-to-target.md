# Gout flare and urate-lowering treat-to-target

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** settle each flare quickly, then use urate-lowering therapy (ULT) titrated to
a serum urate target so crystals dissolve and flares stop — the step most often
missed in practice.

## 1. Diagnose
- **Joint aspiration with polarised light microscopy** is the gold standard:
  monosodium urate = needle-shaped, **negatively** birefringent (yellow when parallel to
  the compensator axis). CPPD = rhomboid, weakly **positively** birefringent.
- Always send fluid for **Gram stain and culture** — septic arthritis can coexist with
  crystals; a hot single joint with fever is septic until proven otherwise.
- Clinical diagnosis acceptable when aspiration isn't feasible and the picture is
  classic (first MTP "podagra", rapid onset to maximal pain < 24 h, redness, previous
  similar self-limiting attacks, tophi, hyperuricaemia). Dual-energy CT or ultrasound
  (double-contour sign) can support it.
- **Serum urate can be normal during a flare** — recheck 2–4 weeks after.

## 2. Treat the flare (start early, within 24 h ideally)
Choose by comorbidity:
- **Colchicine** low-dose: US FDA-approved dosing is 1.2 mg then 0.6 mg one hour later (ACR 2020, confirmed); UK-style 1 mg then 0.5 mg (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked); then 0.5 mg 2–3 times daily until settled (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). Reduce/avoid in CKD and with strong CYP3A4/
  P-gp inhibitors (clarithromycin, ciclosporin, some azoles) — interactions are fatal.
- **NSAID** at full dose (+ PPI) if no CKD, heart failure, peptic ulcer or anticoagulation.
- **Glucocorticoid:** oral prednisolone ~30–35 mg/day for 3–5 days (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), or intra-articular
  injection (after excluding infection) — good choice in CKD.
- IL-1 inhibitors for refractory cases (specialist).
Ice, rest; continue ULT if already on it.

## 3. Who needs urate-lowering therapy?
Strong indications (ACR 2020, confirmed): ≥ 1 subcutaneous tophus, radiographic damage attributable to gout, frequent flares
(≥ 2/year). Conditional: after first flare with CKD ≥ stage 3, urate > 9 mg/dL
(535 µmol/L), or urolithiasis. Many clinicians (and UK BSR/NICE 2022 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked)) offer ULT to
everyone after a diagnosis, discussing benefits.

## 4. Start and titrate
- **Allopurinol first-line.** Start low: **≤ 100 mg/day** and lower in CKD (ACR 2020, confirmed; febuxostat ≤ 40 mg/day), then titrate to target by serial urate; the "increase by ~100 mg every 2–5 weeks (50 mg in CKD)" step (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), "many need ≥ 300 mg" (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) and "up to 800–900 mg/day" (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) are common practice rather than ACR text.
- **HLA-B\*58:01 testing** before allopurinol in populations at high risk of severe
  cutaneous reactions (ACR 2020: conditionally recommended for patients of Southeast Asian descent, e.g. Han Chinese, Korean, Thai, and for African American patients).
- **Target serum urate < 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L)** (ACR 2020, strongly recommended); < 5 mg/dL (300 µmol/L) for tophaceous
  disease until tophi resolve (EULAR-style target (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked), not in ACR). Check urate every 2–5 weeks during titration, then every
  6–12 months (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- **Febuxostat** second-line (CV warning from CARES trial (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked) — caution in established CVD);
  uricosurics (probenecid, benzbromarone where available) for under-excretors without
  stones; pegloticase for refractory tophaceous gout.
- **Flare prophylaxis** for at least **3–6 months** when starting ULT (ACR 2020, confirmed): low-dose colchicine
  (0.5–1 mg/day), or low-dose NSAID/prednisolone.
- ACR 2020 conditionally recommends starting ULT during the flare rather than after it resolves (with anti-inflammatory cover).

## 5. Lifestyle and comorbidities (adjuncts, not substitutes)
Limit alcohol (especially beer), sugar-sweetened drinks, high-purine meat/seafood; weight
loss. Review diuretics (thiazides/loops raise urate; losartan mildly lowers it). Screen
CV risk, CKD, diabetes.

## 6. CPPD and osteoarthritis pointers
- **CPPD (pseudogout):** acute flare like gout, often knee/wrist, older adults; check
  calcium, magnesium, phosphate, ferritin, TSH, ALP in younger patients (< 60) — 
  haemochromatosis, hyperparathyroidism, hypomagnesaemia. No crystal-dissolving therapy;
  treat flares as above; colchicine prophylaxis for recurrent attacks.
- **Osteoarthritis:** exercise therapy and weight loss are core; topical NSAIDs for
  knees/hands, oral NSAIDs short-term, intra-articular steroid for flares; avoid
  long-term opioids; paracetamol has small benefit.

## Gotchas
- Stopping allopurinol during a flare causes urate swings and more flares — continue it.
- "Starting dose 300 mg" in CKD raises hypersensitivity risk; start low, go slow.
- Not checking urate after starting ULT = no treat-to-target.
- Azathioprine/mercaptopurine with allopurinol or febuxostat → severe marrow toxicity.

## Verify
- [ ] Diagnosis by crystals or documented clinical criteria; infection excluded.
- [ ] Flare drug chosen against renal function, interactions and comorbidities.
- [ ] ULT indication discussed; allopurinol started low (HLA-B*58:01 where indicated).
- [ ] Urate rechecked and dose titrated to < 6 mg/dL (360 µmol/L).
- [ ] Prophylaxis for 3–6 months; interacting drugs reviewed.
