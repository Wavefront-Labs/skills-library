# Graft dysfunction workup

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** when a kidney transplant recipient's creatinine rises or proteinuria appears,
find the cause quickly — mechanical, haemodynamic, drug, infection or rejection — and
biopsy when the answer isn't clear, because untreated rejection costs graft years.

## 1. Define dysfunction
- Creatinine rise ≥ ~20–25% above the patient's **own stable baseline** (confirm on
  repeat within 24–72 h), new proteinuria (uPCR > ~0.5 g/g), or a rising DSA.
- Ask: hydration, intercurrent illness (vomiting/diarrhoea), adherence (missed doses,
  timing), new drugs, urine output, graft tenderness, fever.

## 2. Think by timeline
| Time post-transplant | Leading causes |
|---|---|
| Days (delayed graft function) | ATN (especially deceased donor), hyperacute/accelerated ABMR, vascular thrombosis (sudden anuria — urgent Doppler), urine leak, obstruction, hypovolaemia |
| Weeks–3 months | Acute T-cell-mediated rejection (TCMR), ABMR, CNI toxicity, obstruction (ureteric stenosis, lymphocele), CMV, pyelonephritis |
| 3–12 months | Rejection (especially if under-immunosuppressed), **BK polyomavirus nephropathy**, CNI toxicity, renal artery stenosis |
| > 1 year | Chronic active ABMR (often de novo DSA from non-adherence), recurrent disease (FSGS, IgA, MPGN), CNI nephrotoxicity, BK, PTLD |

## 3. First-line investigations (same day to 48 h)
- Creatinine, electrolytes, FBC, urinalysis and **uPCR**, urine culture.
- **Tacrolimus trough** (12 h post-dose; confirm timing) — low → rejection risk; high →
  toxicity. Check interacting drugs (azoles, macrolides, diltiazem raise; rifampicin,
  phenytoin, St John's wort lower).
- **Graft ultrasound with Doppler:** hydronephrosis, collections, perfusion, renal artery
  velocities.
- **BK viraemia (plasma PCR)** and **CMV PCR** depending on prophylaxis status and time.
- **Donor-specific antibodies (DSA)** by single-antigen bead assay.
- Donor-derived cell-free DNA (dd-cfDNA) where available — higher levels suggest
  injury/rejection; a supporting test, not a biopsy replacement.

## 4. Correct the reversible
Rehydrate, relieve obstruction (catheter/nephrostomy/stent), stop nephrotoxins (NSAIDs,
contrast where avoidable), adjust tacrolimus to target, treat infection. If creatinine
returns to baseline, monitor closely.

## 5. Biopsy when the cause isn't clear
Indications: unexplained persistent rise, new proteinuria, positive DSA with dysfunction,
delayed graft function beyond ~7–10 days. Report using the **Banff classification**:
- **TCMR:** interstitial inflammation and tubulitis (grades IA–IB), arteritis (IIA–III).
- **Active ABMR:** microvascular inflammation (glomerulitis, peritubular capillaritis),
  evidence of antibody interaction (C4d or molecular), and DSA. Chronic active ABMR adds
  transplant glomerulopathy.
- Borderline changes, BK nephropathy (SV40 staining), CNI arteriolopathy, recurrent or
  de novo glomerular disease.

## 6. Treatment principles (specialist-led)
- **TCMR:** pulse methylprednisolone (e.g. 250–500 mg IV daily × 3); anti-thymocyte globulin
  for steroid-resistant or vascular rejection. Optimise maintenance.
- **Active ABMR:** plasmapheresis + IVIG ± rituximab per centre protocol; evidence is
  weaker; chronic ABMR often focuses on optimising baseline immunosuppression.
- **BK nephropathy:** **reduce immunosuppression** (usually antimetabolite first, then CNI
  target) — no proven antiviral.
- Adherence problems: address directly (pill burden, cost, side effects, adolescence).

## 7. Prevention and surveillance
Scheduled creatinine/uPCR (frequent early, then every 1–3 months), tacrolimus troughs,
BK screening (e.g. monthly to month 9, then 3-monthly to 2 years), DSA surveillance per
centre, CMV prophylaxis by donor/recipient serostatus, cancer screening (skin), CV risk.

## Gotchas
- Mild "pre-renal" rises in transplant patients are rejection until biopsy proves
  otherwise if they don't correct with fluids.
- A trough drawn 2 h post-dose looks toxic; confirm sample timing before changing doses.
- Generic tacrolimus switches change levels — recheck after any formulation change.
- Treating BK with extra steroids (mistaken for rejection) worsens it; check SV40.

## Verify
- [ ] Rise confirmed against personal baseline; history including adherence and drugs.
- [ ] Trough level (timing verified), ultrasound, uPCR, BK/CMV PCR and DSA sent.
- [ ] Reversible causes corrected and creatinine rechecked.
- [ ] Biopsy done when unexplained; Banff diagnosis recorded.
- [ ] Treatment and surveillance plan agreed with the transplant centre.
