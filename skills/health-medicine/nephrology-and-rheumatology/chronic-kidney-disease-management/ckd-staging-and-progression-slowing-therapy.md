# CKD staging and progression-slowing therapy

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** correctly label CKD, estimate the risk of progression and cardiovascular
events, and put every eligible patient on the evidence-based "pillars" that slow
kidney loss — then manage complications and prepare for kidney failure in time.

## 1. Confirm it's chronic
CKD = abnormal kidney structure or function for **> 3 months**: eGFR < 60 mL/min/1.73 m²
and/or kidney damage markers (albuminuria ≥ 30 mg/g [3 mg/mmol], haematuria of renal
origin, abnormal imaging/biopsy, tubular disorders). A single low eGFR during illness is
not CKD — repeat when stable. Rule out AKI on CKD.

## 2. Stage by cause, GFR and albuminuria (C-G-A)
- **eGFR** with the race-free **CKD-EPI 2021 creatinine** equation; add **cystatin C**
  (creatinine–cystatin equation) when muscle mass is atypical (amputees, cachexia,
  bodybuilders) or decisions hinge on the number.
- G1 ≥ 90, G2 60–89, G3a 45–59, G3b 30–44, G4 15–29, G5 < 15.
- **Urine albumin:creatinine ratio (uACR)** on first-morning sample: A1 < 30 mg/g
  (< 3 mg/mmol), A2 30–300 (3–30), A3 > 300 (> 30). Confirm with 2 of 3 samples.
- Place on the **KDIGO heat map** (green → red) to set monitoring frequency (e.g. 1–4
  times/year).
- Estimate kidney-failure risk with the **KFRE** (4-variable: age, sex, eGFR, uACR) for
  G3–G5.

## 3. Find the cause
Diabetes, hypertension, glomerulonephritis, polycystic kidney disease, obstruction,
drugs (NSAIDs, lithium, calcineurin inhibitors), myeloma, family history. Urinalysis
with microscopy (haematuria/casts → glomerular disease), renal ultrasound (size,
asymmetry, cysts, obstruction), and targeted serology/serum free light chains as
indicated.

## 4. Progression-slowing therapy (stack the pillars)
1. **BP control:** target systolic < 120 mmHg where tolerated (KDIGO, standardised
   measurement); individualise for frail/older patients.
2. **RAS blockade** (ACE-I or ARB, max tolerated dose) for albuminuria (especially A2–A3),
   diabetic or not. Expect creatinine to rise; **accept up to ~30%** — larger rise →
   check for renal artery stenosis/volume depletion. Don't combine ACE-I + ARB.
3. **SGLT2 inhibitor** for most patients with eGFR ≥ 20 (with or without diabetes,
   KDIGO 2024); continue until dialysis. Initial eGFR dip is expected.
4. **Finerenone** (non-steroidal MRA) for type 2 diabetes with albuminuria and normal
   potassium despite RAS blockade.
5. **GLP-1 receptor agonist** (e.g. semaglutide — FLOW trial) in type 2 diabetes with CKD.
6. Statin (age ≥ 50 or risk-based), smoking cessation, glycaemic control, weight,
   protein intake ~0.8 g/kg/day (avoid high protein), sodium < 2 g/day.

Monitor K⁺ and creatinine 2–4 weeks after starting/titrating RAS/MRA. Manage
hyperkalaemia (diet, diuretic, potassium binders) rather than stopping RAS blockade
reflexively.

## 5. Complications (typically from G3b onward)
- **Anaemia:** check iron studies; iron first (oral/IV), then ESA/HIF-PHI per
  nephrology targets (Hb not above ~11.5 g/dL with ESA).
- **CKD-MBD:** calcium, phosphate, PTH, alkaline phosphatase, vitamin D; dietary
  phosphate, binders, vitamin D per trends rather than single values.
- **Metabolic acidosis:** consider sodium bicarbonate if HCO₃⁻ persistently < 18–22.
- Drug dosing review at every eGFR change; avoid NSAIDs, gadolinium caution at low eGFR,
  metformin dose limits (stop < 30).
- Vaccines (hepatitis B before dialysis, pneumococcal, influenza).

## 6. Refer and prepare
Refer to nephrology for eGFR < 30, uACR ≥ 300 mg/g (30 mg/mmol), KFRE 5-year risk
≥ 3–5%, rapid decline (sustained eGFR fall ≥ 25% with stage change or > 5/year),
haematuria with casts, resistant hypertension, recurrent hyperkalaemia, hereditary
disease. Discuss kidney replacement options (transplant pre-emptively, home/in-centre
dialysis, conservative care) when KFRE 2-year risk > ~10–40%; plan vascular access early.

## Gotchas
- Creatinine-based eGFR overestimates function in sarcopenic patients.
- Stopping ACE-I/ARB for a small creatinine rise forfeits long-term benefit.
- Trimethoprim and cimetidine raise creatinine without reducing GFR.
- Sick-day rules: pause SGLT2i, ACE-I/ARB, diuretics, metformin during dehydrating illness.

## Verify
- [ ] Chronicity confirmed (> 3 months); stage recorded as C-G-A.
- [ ] uACR measured; KFRE calculated for G3–G5.
- [ ] Cause investigated (urinalysis, ultrasound, targeted tests).
- [ ] Each eligible pillar started or reason documented; K⁺/creatinine rechecked.
- [ ] Complications screened; referral and modality planning triggered by thresholds.
