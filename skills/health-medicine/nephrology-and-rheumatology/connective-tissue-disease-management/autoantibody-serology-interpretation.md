# Autoantibody serology interpretation

> General clinical/professional reference, not a substitute for independent clinical judgement, current guidelines, or verified dosing references.

**Goal:** order autoimmune tests that answer a clinical question, read them in terms of
pre-test probability, and know which result should trigger organ screening — instead
of chasing every positive ANA.

## 1. Order on clinical grounds
Test when features suggest CTD: inflammatory arthritis, photosensitive rash, serositis,
Raynaud's (especially new onset > 30–40 years, digital ulcers, abnormal nailfold
capillaries), proximal weakness, sicca with swelling of glands, cytopenias, active
urine sediment, unexplained ILD, recurrent thrombosis/pregnancy loss. "Tired with
aches" alone has a low pre-test probability — a positive ANA there is usually a false
alarm.

## 2. ANA (screening)
- Gold standard: **indirect immunofluorescence on HEp-2 cells**. Report titre and pattern.
- **~10–15% of healthy people** are positive at 1:80 (more in older adults and women);
  1:160 or higher is more meaningful but still not diagnostic.
- ANA is **> 95% sensitive for SLE** (an entry criterion for 2019 EULAR/ACR at ≥ 1:80) —
  a negative ANA makes lupus very unlikely. Not repeating a negative ANA is usually fine.
- Patterns (ICAP codes): homogeneous (dsDNA, histone), speckled (ENA), **centromere**
  (limited cutaneous SSc), nucleolar (SSc: Scl-70, PM-Scl, Th/To), cytoplasmic (Jo-1,
  ribosomal P, AMA). Patterns guide follow-up tests but are not diagnoses.
- Don't repeat ANA to monitor disease activity.

## 3. Specific antibodies and associations
| Antibody | Main association | Clinical pointer |
|---|---|---|
| Anti-dsDNA | SLE (specific) | Titre may track activity, especially nephritis |
| Anti-Sm | SLE (highly specific, less sensitive) | |
| Anti-Ro/SSA, anti-La/SSB | Sjögren's, SLE, subacute cutaneous LE | **Pregnancy: congenital heart block risk** (fetal echo surveillance) |
| Anti-U1 RNP | Mixed connective tissue disease | Raynaud's, puffy hands |
| Anti-Scl-70 (topoisomerase I) | Diffuse SSc | **ILD risk** → HRCT, PFTs |
| Anticentromere | Limited cutaneous SSc | **Pulmonary arterial hypertension** risk → echo, DLCO |
| Anti-RNA polymerase III | Diffuse SSc | **Scleroderma renal crisis**, cancer association |
| Anti-Jo-1/other synthetases | Antisynthetase syndrome | ILD, mechanic's hands, arthritis |
| Anti-MDA5 | Dermatomyositis | Rapidly progressive ILD |
| Anti-TIF1γ, anti-NXP2 | Dermatomyositis | Cancer screening in adults |
| Anti-HMGCR | Immune-mediated necrotising myopathy | Often statin-associated |
| Antiphospholipid (lupus anticoagulant, anticardiolipin, anti-β2GPI) | APS | Repeat at ≥ 12 weeks to confirm |
| MPO-ANCA / PR3-ANCA | Microscopic polyangiitis / GPA | Urgent renal/lung assessment |
Order myositis panels and ANCA by **ELISA/immunoassay** for specific antigens, not ANA
patterns alone.

## 4. Activity markers
In SLE: **low C3/C4** and rising anti-dsDNA suggest activity (nephritis); combine with
urine sediment, uPCR, FBC, ESR (CRP often normal in lupus flares — a high CRP suggests
infection or serositis). In vasculitis, ANCA titres correlate poorly with relapse —
don't treat a titre alone.

## 5. Organ screening triggered by serology/diagnosis
- SLE: urinalysis + uPCR at every visit; FBC, complement, dsDNA every 3–6 months;
  hydroxychloroquine retinal screening (baseline and annually after 5 years; dose
  ≤ 5 mg/kg/day actual body weight).
- SSc: annual PFTs with DLCO and echo (PAH screening algorithms e.g. DETECT), HRCT at
  baseline, BP monitoring (renal crisis risk, avoid high-dose steroids > ~15 mg prednisolone).
- Myositis: CK, PFTs/HRCT, swallow assessment, age-appropriate cancer screening.

## Gotchas
- Positive ANA in a well person without features → reassure, no treatment.
- Anti-dsDNA assays vary (Crithidia is specific; ELISA more sensitive) — know your lab.
- Drug-induced lupus: antihistone antibodies (hydralazine, procainamide, TNF inhibitors).
- Hep C, infections and malignancy can produce RF, ANA, ANCA and cryoglobulins.
- Lupus anticoagulant testing is unreliable on anticoagulants (especially DOACs).

## Verify
- [ ] Tests ordered for a stated clinical question with pre-test probability considered.
- [ ] ANA reported with titre and pattern; follow-up ENA/dsDNA chosen accordingly.
- [ ] Specific antibody results linked to organ risks and screening ordered.
- [ ] Activity markers interpreted with urine, complement and clinical picture.
- [ ] Confirmatory repeats (e.g. APS at 12 weeks) scheduled.
