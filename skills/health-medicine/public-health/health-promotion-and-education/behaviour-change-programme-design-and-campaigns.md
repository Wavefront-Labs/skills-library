# Behaviour-change programme design and campaigns

> General professional reference. Health claims in any public message must match
> current national guidance and be signed off by the responsible clinical/public
> health lead.

**Goal:** design programmes that change what people actually do — by diagnosing why
they currently behave as they do and shaping environments, not just telling them.

## 1. Define the problem and the behaviour
- Start from the health outcome and its distribution (who is most affected?
  `health-medicine.public-health.community-health-assessment.needs-assessment-indicators-and-priority-setting`).
- Specify the **target behaviour** precisely: who, what, when, where, how often
  ("parents of 1-year-olds book MMR within 4 weeks of invitation", not "improve
  vaccination").
- Consider upstream levers first: price, availability, regulation, built environment.
  Information-only campaigns rarely shift behaviour on their own.

## 2. Diagnose with COM-B
- **Capability** (physical, psychological: knowledge, skills).
- **Opportunity** (physical: time, access, cost; social: norms, support).
- **Motivation** (reflective: beliefs, intentions; automatic: habits, emotions).
Gather evidence: literature, existing data, interviews/focus groups with the target
group, frontline staff insight.

## 3. Choose intervention functions and BCTs
- Map COM-B deficits through the **Behaviour Change Wheel** to functions (education,
  persuasion, incentivisation, coercion, training, restriction, environmental
  restructuring, modelling, enablement) and policy categories.
- Select specific **behaviour change techniques** from the BCT taxonomy (e.g. goal
  setting, prompts/cues, social support, feedback on behaviour, implementation
  intentions). Record the BCT labels so the intervention is replicable and evaluable.
- Apply **APEASE** criteria: affordability, practicability, effectiveness, acceptability,
  side-effects/safety, equity (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 4. Campaign content
- **Audience segmentation** by behaviour stage, barriers and channels, not just
  demographics.
- Messages: one main action, concrete and easy (when/where/how), social norms where
  true ("most parents in X vaccinate"), gain or loss framing tested for the topic.
- **Health literacy**: plain language, active voice, numbers as natural frequencies ("1 in 100"), visual aids;
  translated and culturally adapted, not literally translated.
- **Pre-test** with the audience (comprehension, credibility, unintended reactions);
  avoid fear appeals without an efficacy message.
- Pair with service readiness: a campaign that drives demand to a service that can't
  respond backfires.

## 5. Community engagement and co-production
- Involve communities from problem definition onwards (spectrum: informing →
  consulting → co-producing → community-led). Trusted messengers (faith leaders,
  community health workers) often matter more than channels.
- Settings approach: schools, workplaces, prisons, healthcare — change the setting's
  policies and environment as well as individual behaviour.

## 6. Equity
Universal campaigns can widen inequalities (better-off groups respond more).
**Proportionate universalism**: universal offer, with intensity scaled to need.

## 7. Evaluate
Logic model with process (reach, fidelity, dose), intermediate (COM-B change) and
outcome (behaviour, health) indicators; compare against a counterfactual where
possible (see
`health-medicine.public-health.health-policy-and-programme-evaluation.logic-models-evaluation-design-and-policy-briefs`).

## Gotchas
- "Awareness" as the success metric.
- Victim-blaming tone in messages on obesity, smoking, substance use.
- Copying a successful programme without checking the context's COM-B differs.

## Verify
- [ ] Target behaviour specified (who/what/when/where).
- [ ] COM-B diagnosis evidenced with data from the target group.
- [ ] BCTs labelled and APEASE applied.
- [ ] Materials pre-tested; plain-language and translation checks done.
- [ ] Equity impact considered; evaluation plan with indicators and baseline.
