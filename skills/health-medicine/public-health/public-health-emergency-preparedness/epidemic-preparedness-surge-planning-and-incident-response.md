# Epidemic preparedness, surge planning and incident response

> General professional reference. Legal duties, command structures and terminology
> (e.g. NHS EPRR, US NIMS/ICS) differ by country; follow your
> national framework and local plans. Countermeasure use and dosing follow current
> national guidance.

**Goal:** be ready before the event, scale health capacity fast when it comes, keep
the public informed and trusted, and learn from every activation.

## 1. Risk assessment (all-hazards)
- Use the national/community **risk register** and a hazard vulnerability analysis:
  likelihood × impact for infectious (pandemic influenza, novel respiratory virus,
  VHF, measles), environmental (heat, flood, chemical), and system (cyber, power,
  supply) hazards.
- Identify vulnerable populations (care homes, homeless, dialysis/oxygen-dependent,
  people with disabilities) and critical dependencies.
- National capacity benchmarks: IHR core capacities, **JEE** scores (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).

## 2. Plans, people and exercises
- Generic incident response plan + hazard-specific annexes (pandemic, mass
  casualty, heat, CBRN) + **business continuity** plans for each service.
- Roles pre-assigned and trained; on-call rota; contact lists checked regularly.
- **Exercise cycle**: discussion-based (tabletop) → functional → full-scale; write
  and track improvement actions. Plans that are never exercised fail on first use.

## 3. Incident management structure
- Activate a structured system (ICS/IMS): **incident commander/director**, operations,
  planning/intelligence, logistics, finance/admin; plus public-health technical lead,
  liaison and communications. Strategic/tactical/operational tiers in some systems.
- **Emergency operations centre** with a battle rhythm: situation reports at fixed
  times, decision log (who decided what, why, on what information), action tracker.
- Declare levels/grades and stand-down criteria explicitly.

## 4. Surge capacity — the four S
- **Staff**: redeployment, retired/volunteer registers, training packs, scope-of-
  practice flexibilities, staff welfare and rest.
- **Stuff**: PPE, oxygen, ventilators, drugs, consumables; stockpiles with rotation;
  supply chain alternatives.
- **Space**: escalation beds, cohorting, alternative care sites, mortuary capacity.
- **Systems**: triage protocols, crisis standards of care (ethical framework agreed
  in advance), data flows, mutual aid.
- Use modelled scenarios (reasonable worst case) to size surge, with triggers that
  move between levels.

## 5. Public-health response functions
- Surveillance and epidemic intelligence (see
  `health-medicine.public-health.disease-surveillance.case-definitions-notification-and-signal-detection`),
  case investigation/contact tracing
  (`health-medicine.public-health.outbreak-investigation.stepwise-field-investigation-and-control`),
  lab surge, non-pharmaceutical interventions, and **mass countermeasure
  distribution** (points of dispensing, vaccination centres; eligibility and
  prioritisation frameworks).

## 6. Risk communication (CERC principles)
Be first, be right, be credible; express empathy; promote action; show respect (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked).
- Communicate uncertainty honestly ("what we know, what we don't, what we're doing").
- Consistent spokespeople and coordinated messages across agencies; accessible formats
  and community languages; trusted messengers.
- Monitor and respond to misinformation (infodemic management) without amplifying it.

## 7. Recovery and learning
- Plan recovery from day one: backlog of routine care, staff burnout, mental health.
- **After-action review / debrief** within weeks: what was planned, what happened,
  why, what to change; assign owners and deadlines; re-exercise changed plans.

## Gotchas
- Plans that assume staff are available when schools close or staff fall ill.
- Decision logs neglected — later inquiries depend on them.
- Over-reassuring early messages that destroy trust when the situation worsens.
- Equity: digital-only booking or information excludes vulnerable groups.

## Verify
- [ ] Risk register reviewed and plans mapped to top risks.
- [ ] Exercises held in the last year with actions tracked to closure.
- [ ] Incident structure, activation triggers and stand-down criteria written.
- [ ] Surge levels with triggers for staff, stuff, space and systems.
- [ ] Risk-communication plan with spokespeople and accessible formats.
- [ ] After-action review completed after every activation.
