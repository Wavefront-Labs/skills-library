# Unhooking from thoughts and acting on values

**Goal:** instead of disputing an unhelpful thought, change your *relationship* to it
— see it as a thought, not a fact or an order — make room for uncomfortable feelings,
and act on what matters to you with the thought and feeling still present. These are
self-help adaptations of Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT). Not a substitute
for therapy.

## 1. When to defuse rather than dispute
Disputing (thought records, reframing) works well for specific, testable thoughts.
Defusion fits better when:
- the thought is repetitive and old ("I'm not good enough"), and arguing just feeds it;
- it's unanswerable ("What if something terrible happens?");
- you've already reframed it many times.
The question is not *is it true?* but *is holding onto it helpful right now?*

## 2. Defusion exercises
- **"I'm having the thought that…":** "I'm a failure" → "I'm having the thought that
  I'm a failure" → "I notice I'm having the thought that I'm a failure." Notice the
  distance each step adds.
- **Name the story:** "Ah, the 'not good enough' story again."
- **Leaves on a stream (5 minutes):** imagine sitting by a stream; place each thought
  that arises on a leaf and watch it float by. If you get caught up, notice and start again.
- **Word repetition:** say a loaded word aloud ("failure") rapidly for 30 seconds until
  it becomes just a sound. Research on this word-repetition exercise suggests reduced
  believability and distress, though effects are modest.
- **Silly voice / singing:** say the thought in a cartoon voice or to "Happy Birthday".
  Use with care; it can feel dismissive for painful content.

## 3. Willingness (acceptance)
Acceptance here means **allowing** an uncomfortable feeling to be present without
fighting it, when fighting it isn't working — not liking it or giving up.
1. Notice where the feeling is in the body.
2. Breathe into and around it; let it have space.
3. Describe it like a scientist: size, shape, temperature, movement.
4. Ask: "Am I willing to have this feeling, if it means doing what matters?"

## 4. Clarify values
Values are directions you want to move in, not goals to tick off.
- Pick 3–4 life areas (relationships, work, health, community, growth).
- For each, finish: "In this area, I want to be someone who…" (e.g. "…shows up for
  friends", "…learns and contributes").
- Rate how closely you've lived each value this month, 0–10.

## 5. Committed action
1. Choose one small action that serves a value ("Text Sam to meet up").
2. Predict which thoughts and feelings will show up ("They'll say no"; anxiety).
3. Defuse and allow them — then do the action anyway.
4. Repeat, gradually increasing.
The **choice point**: in a hard moment, ask "Is my next move *toward* what matters, or
*away* from discomfort?"

## Gotchas
- Defusion isn't a trick to make thoughts vanish; if you use it to get rid of them, it
  becomes another form of struggle.
- Acceptance doesn't mean accepting harmful situations (abuse, unsafe work); those call
  for action.
- Values chosen to please others ("should") don't sustain action — check they're yours.
- **Professional guidance:** for persistent intrusive thoughts, trauma memories, OCD,
  psychosis or severe depression, use ACT with a qualified therapist; self-applied
  willingness exercises around trauma can be overwhelming.
- Uncertainty: the exercises are widely published ACT staples; protocols and wording
  vary across ACT authors.
