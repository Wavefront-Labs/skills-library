# Scheduling activity to lift low mood (behavioural activation)

**Goal:** break the loop where low mood → doing less → fewer rewards → lower mood, by
planning and doing activities *before* motivation arrives ("outside-in" rather than
waiting to feel like it). Behavioural activation (BA) is an evidence-based treatment
for depression; this skill covers self-applied BA for everyday slumps and low patches.
**It is not a substitute for treatment of clinical depression.**

## 1. Monitor first (3–7 days)
Hour by hour (or in 2–3 hour blocks), record:
| Time | Activity | Mood 0–10 | Mastery 0–10 | Pleasure 0–10 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9–10 | Scrolling in bed | 3 | 0 | 2 |
| 10–11 | Shower, breakfast | 4 | 4 | 3 |
| 15–16 | Walk with a friend | 6 | 3 | 7 |
- **Mastery** = sense of accomplishment (relative to how hard it was *today*).
- **Pleasure** = enjoyment.
Look for patterns: which activities lift mood, which drag it, when are the low points?

## 2. Map avoidance loops
BA uses **TRAP**: **T**rigger → **R**esponse (feeling) → **A**voidance **P**attern.
E.g. Trigger: email from boss → Response: dread → Avoidance: leave inbox unopened, watch TV.
Avoidance brings short-term relief and long-term cost. Replace with **TRAC**:
Trigger → Response → **A**lternative **C**oping (open the inbox, answer the easiest one).

## 3. Choose activities
Build a list across three kinds:
- **Routine:** sleep times, meals, hygiene, laundry — routines collapse first in low mood.
- **Pleasure:** small enjoyable things (music, sunlight, a hobby, seeing someone).
- **Mastery / necessary:** tasks that give accomplishment (bills, a work chunk, tidying one shelf).
Link at least some to your **values** ("being a good friend" → text one friend).

## 4. Schedule — specifically
- Put activities in a calendar with a time and place: "Tue 5:30pm walk round the park",
  not "exercise more".
- Start small: 2–3 scheduled activities a day.
- Mix mastery and pleasure; spread them through the day, especially at your low points.
- Do it **regardless of mood**; then rate mood, mastery and pleasure afterwards.

## 5. Graded task assignment
Break big avoided tasks into steps so small they're hard to refuse:
"Sort taxes" → (1) find the folder → (2) open last year's return → (3) list missing documents.
Schedule step 1 only. Completing it counts fully.

## 6. Review weekly
- Did scheduled activities happen? What blocked the ones that didn't?
- Which raised mood most? Do more of those.
- Adjust difficulty: too many failures → smaller steps; too easy → add one.

## Gotchas
- Waiting to "feel like it" is the trap BA is designed to break; expect activities to feel
  flat at first, with mood lagging behind action by days or weeks.
- Over-scheduling sets up failure and self-criticism. Fewer, smaller, done.
- Distraction-only activities (endless scrolling) aren't the target; rated mastery and
  pleasure tell you what actually helps.
- **Get professional help** if low mood lasts more than two weeks most of the day, you've
  lost interest in nearly everything, sleep/appetite have changed markedly, or you have
  thoughts of self-harm or suicide — see a GP or mental-health professional, or a crisis
  line / emergency service if you're at risk. BA for clinical depression works best with
  a therapist.

- [ ] You have at least three days of hour-by-hour monitoring with all four ratings.
- [ ] You've written one TRAP and its TRAC alternative.
- [ ] Your activity list covers routine, pleasure and mastery, with some linked to values.
- [ ] This week's calendar has 2–3 specific scheduled activities per day.
- [ ] A weekly review shows which activities lifted mood and what you'll change.
