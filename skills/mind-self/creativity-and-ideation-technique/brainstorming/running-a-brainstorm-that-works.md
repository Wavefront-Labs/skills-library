# Running a brainstorm that works

**Goal:** come out of 30–45 minutes with a large, varied pool of ideas and a short list
worth developing — not a meeting where three people talked and everyone agreed with
the first suggestion.

## The uncomfortable research fact
Classic verbal group brainstorming usually produces **fewer and no better** ideas than
the same people working alone and pooling results ("nominal groups"). The main causes:
**production blocking** (only one person can speak; ideas are forgotten while waiting),
**evaluation apprehension** and **social loafing**. The fix is not to abandon groups but
to add silent, individual generation.

## 1. Write a sharp prompt
Phrase it as a question starting "How might we…?" with a concrete scope:
"How might we cut first-week drop-off for new gym members?" beats "Ideas for retention?"
Too broad → generic ideas; too narrow → the answer is already implied.

## 2. Osborn's rules (state them out loud)
1. Defer judgement — no critique during generation, including "yes, but".
2. Go for quantity — aim for a number (e.g. 50).
3. Welcome wild ideas — they are easier to tame than dull ones are to excite.
4. Combine and build on others' ideas.

## 3. Generate: pick a format
- **Solo first, then share (recommended default):** 5–8 minutes silent writing, one idea
  per sticky note or line, then round-robin sharing.
- **Brainwriting 6-3-5:** 6 people each write 3 ideas in 5 minutes on a sheet, pass it
  on; the next person builds on or adds to them. Six rounds gives up to 108 ideas in
  ~30 minutes. Works remotely with a shared doc per person.
- **Round-robin:** each person offers one idea in turn; "pass" allowed. Stops dominant
  voices.
- **Reverse brainstorming:** "How might we make drop-off *worse*?" Then invert each
  answer. Good when the group is stuck or cynical.
- **Solo brainstorm:** set a timer, a number target, and write without stopping; when you
  stall, switch to a stimulus (see lateral-thinking random entry, or a SCAMPER pass).

## 4. Converge (separately, after a break if possible)
1. **Cluster** similar ideas (affinity mapping) and name each cluster.
2. **Dot-vote:** each person gets ~3–5 dots, votes silently, then reveal.
3. Pick 2–3 to develop, using explicit criteria (impact, effort, fit).

## Facilitation tips
- The boss speaks last, or not at all, during generation.
- Expect a slump after the first ~10–15 ideas; the obvious ones come first. Push through.
- Capture everything visibly; nothing is "off the board".
- Remote: use a shared board with anonymous notes for the silent phase.

## Gotchas
- **"Brainstorm" that is really a discussion** — if someone is arguing, you're converging.
- **Too many people:** above ~8, split into groups.
- **Vague prompt** produces vague ideas; spend the first five minutes on the question.
- **No follow-up:** ideas without an owner and next step die. Assign them before leaving.
- Exact productivity gaps between verbal and nominal groups vary by study; the direction
  is consistent.

## Verify
- [ ] The prompt is a specific "How might we…" question.
- [ ] Generation and evaluation happened in separate phases.
- [ ] There was silent individual generation before sharing.
- [ ] You reached a quantity target and pushed past the first slump.
- [ ] Shortlisted ideas each have an owner and a next action.
