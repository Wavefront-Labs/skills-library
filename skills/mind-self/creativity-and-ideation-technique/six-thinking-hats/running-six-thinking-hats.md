# Running Six Thinking Hats

**Goal:** have everyone (or just you) think in the *same mode at the same time* —
facts, then ideas, then risks — instead of one person attacking while another defends.
De Bono called this "parallel thinking".

## The six hats
| Hat | Mode | Typical questions |
|---|---|---|
| **Blue** | Process / control | What are we thinking about? What sequence? What did we conclude? |
| **White** | Information | What do we know? What's missing? How would we get it? |
| **Red** | Feelings, gut, intuition | What's my gut reaction? (no justification required) |
| **Black** | Caution, risks | What could go wrong? Why might this not fit? |
| **Yellow** | Benefits, optimism | Why could this work? What's the value, even if unlikely? |
| **Green** | Creativity, alternatives | What else? What new options or modifications? |

## The rules that make it work
- **Everyone wears the same hat at once.** During yellow, even the sceptic lists benefits.
- **The hat is a mode, not a person.** Never "you're a black-hat type". Labels end the method.
- **Red is short and unjustified.** 30 seconds each, no explanations — it surfaces feelings
  that otherwise leak into "objective" arguments.
- **Time-box each hat** — typically 2–5 minutes; red often 1 minute total.
- **Blue opens and closes.** The facilitator (or you, solo) holds the blue hat throughout.

## Sample sequences (adapt to the task)
- **Evaluating a proposal:** Blue → White → Green (improve it) → Yellow → Black → Red → Blue.
- **Generating solutions:** Blue → White → Green → Yellow → Black → Green (fix the
  black-hat problems) → Blue.
- **Quick decision (15 min):** Blue → Red → Yellow → Black → Red → Blue. The second red
  shows whether views changed.
- Put **yellow before black** for new ideas: fragile ideas otherwise get killed before
  anyone articulates their value.
- Use **green straight after black** to turn objections into design requirements.

## Running it in a meeting
1. **Blue:** state the focus as a question; show the planned sequence and timings.
2. Write each hat's output on a separate visible column.
3. Facilitator redirects off-mode comments: "Good point — hold it for black."
4. **Closing blue:** summarise, decide, assign next steps. If no decision, name what
   information (white) is missing.

## Solo use
Write six headings and fill them in order on paper. Especially useful when you notice
you are only listing risks (stuck in black) or only defending an idea you love (stuck in
yellow). The forced red-hat line often reveals the real reason you're hesitating.

## Gotchas
- **Black-hat dominance:** critique feels like rigour, so groups drift into it. Keep it
  time-boxed and do yellow first.
- **Fake yellow:** "benefits" written sarcastically. The facilitator should ask for
  specific value.
- **Hat-as-costume theatre** — physical hats or long explanations waste time; colour
  labels on the agenda are enough.
- **No closing blue:** the session ends with lots of output and no decision.
- Strong claims about productivity gains come largely from the method's own promoters;
  independent evidence is limited. It is most clearly useful as a meeting-discipline device.

## Verify
- [ ] The session opened with a blue-hat question and a sequence.
- [ ] Everyone was in the same mode during each segment.
- [ ] Yellow came before black for any new idea.
- [ ] Red was brief and unjustified.
- [ ] The closing blue produced a decision or a named information gap plus owners.
