# Resolving contradictions with TRIZ

**Goal:** stop trading one thing off against another ("stronger but heavier", "faster but
less accurate") and look for a solution that removes the conflict, using patterns Genrich
Altshuller distilled from large numbers of patents. This is the self-applied thinking
method; engineering use is covered in
engineering-technology.invention-prototyping.concept-development.concept-generation-and-screening.

## 1. State the contradiction precisely
- **Technical contradiction:** improving parameter A worsens parameter B.
  "Making the backpack bigger (capacity ↑) makes it heavier and bulkier on the commute."
- **Physical contradiction:** one element must have opposite properties.
  "The backpack must be *large* (to carry gym kit) and *small* (for the train)."
Turning a technical contradiction into a physical one usually sharpens the problem.

## 2. Describe the Ideal Final Result (IFR)
Write: "The system *itself* delivers [the function] with no added cost, complexity or harm."
"The bag is exactly as large as its contents, by itself." Ideality is a direction, not a
target you'll hit — it stops you settling for a compromise too early.

## 3. Separate the opposites (for physical contradictions)
- **In time:** large when needed, small otherwise → expandable zip gusset.
- **In space:** large in one place, small in another → slim main body, external clip-on
  pouch for gym kit.
- **On condition:** behaves differently under different conditions → compression straps
  that tighten when empty.
- **Between the whole and its parts:** the whole is large, each part small → a modular
  system of small bags.

## 4. Use the inventive principles (for technical contradictions)
Altshuller's 40 principles are recurring solution patterns. The classic **contradiction
matrix** (39 engineering parameters × 39) suggests which principles most often resolved
a given pair. For non-engineering problems, many people skip the matrix and simply run
through the principles as prompts. A useful starter subset:
- **1 Segmentation** — divide into independent parts.
- **2 Taking out** — remove the troublesome part or isolate the needed one.
- **10 Preliminary action** — do it (partly) in advance.
- **13 The other way round** — invert the action or fix the moving part.
- **15 Dynamics** — make it adjustable or changeable.
- **24 Intermediary** — use an in-between object or process.
- **25 Self-service** — the system serves or repairs itself.
- **35 Parameter changes** — change state, concentration, flexibility, temperature.
Numbering above follows the commonly published list; check against a reference before
citing numbers.

## 5. Inventory resources
List what is already available at zero cost: substances, fields (heat, gravity, motion),
space, time, information, people's existing behaviour, by-products. Ideal solutions
usually reuse something already there ("use the commuter's own jacket pockets").

## Worked non-engineering example
Contradiction: *meetings need to be thorough but short.* Separate in time — written
pre-read (preliminary action) before, decision-only meeting during. Segmentation —
split into a 10-minute decision meeting and optional deep-dive.

## Gotchas
- **Vague contradictions** produce vague principles. Name the two parameters concretely.
- **Reaching for compromise** ("medium-sized bag") is exactly what TRIZ is meant to avoid.
- **Matrix worship:** the 39×39 matrix is dated and built for physical engineering; treat
  its suggestions as prompts, not answers.
- Full TRIZ (ARIZ, substance-field analysis, trends of evolution) is much larger and
  needs dedicated training; this covers the most transferable core.

## Verify
- [ ] The problem is written as an explicit contradiction (and ideally a physical one).
- [ ] An Ideal Final Result statement exists.
- [ ] All four separation strategies were tried.
- [ ] At least five inventive principles were applied as prompts with an idea each.
- [ ] A free-resource list was made and used.
- [ ] The chosen idea removes, rather than balances, the trade-off.
