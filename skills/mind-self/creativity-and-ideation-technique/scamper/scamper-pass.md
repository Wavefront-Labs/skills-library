# A SCAMPER pass — improve something by asking the same seven questions

**Goal:** generate improvement or variation ideas for an *existing* thing (a product,
service, process, recipe, lesson, story) by forcing yourself through a fixed checklist,
so you don't stop at the two ideas that come naturally.

SCAMPER is Bob Eberle's mnemonic arrangement of Alex Osborn's earlier idea-spurring
questions.

## 1. Define the subject and list its attributes
Write the thing at the top, then list its parts and attributes: components, materials,
steps, users, shape, size, timing, place, price, colour. Example — *a weekly team
meeting*: attendees, agenda, length, room, time slot, chair, notes, decisions, frequency.
Attributes give each question something specific to bite on.

## 2. Ask each prompt of each attribute
- **S — Substitute:** what component, material, person, place or rule could be swapped?
  *Swap the chair each week; swap the meeting for a written update.*
- **C — Combine:** merge with another thing, purpose, or step? *Combine with the
  retrospective; combine notes and agenda in one live doc.*
- **A — Adapt:** what else is like this, and what could we borrow from it? *Adapt a
  hospital shift handover format; adapt a stand-up.*
- **M — Modify / Magnify / Minify:** change size, frequency, shape, emphasis — more or
  less? *15 minutes instead of 60; monthly deep-dive plus weekly short.*
- **P — Put to other use:** could it serve another purpose or audience? *Use the slot
  for cross-team demos once a month.*
- **E — Eliminate:** remove a part, step or feature — what happens? *Drop status updates
  entirely; no slides.*
- **R — Reverse / Rearrange:** flip order, roles, direction or timing? *Decisions first,
  discussion after; juniors run it.*

## 3. Log everything, judge later
One line per idea, tagged with its letter. Don't evaluate during the pass; silly answers
often lead to sensible ones (eliminate the meeting → what would we lose? → that's the
only part worth keeping).

## 4. Screen
Mark each idea: keep / maybe / drop against your actual goal (cheaper? faster? better
experience?). Combine compatible keepers. Pick one or two to trial.

## Variants and extensions
- **Osborn's fuller checklist** adds questions such as "rearrange?" and "magnify vs minify"
  as separate items — use whichever list you will actually run.
- **Attribute modification:** change one attribute at a time to every alternative value
  (round, square, triangular…) — a lightweight form of morphological-analysis.
- **Reversal as a standalone technique:** list the main assumptions about the subject and
  reverse each one.

## Practical tips
- 20–30 minutes is enough for one subject; set a target (e.g. 3 ideas per letter).
- In a group, assign letters to pairs and rotate.
- Works best on something concrete. For blank-page problems use brainstorming or
  lateral-thinking random entry instead.

## Gotchas
- **Skipping letters** that feel irrelevant — "Put to other use" and "Eliminate" are
  often the most productive precisely because they feel odd.
- **Staying abstract:** "make it better" is not a Modify idea; "cut it to 15 minutes" is.
- **Treating SCAMPER as a decision tool** — it only generates; evaluate separately.
- Letter expansions vary slightly between sources (e.g. M = Modify/Magnify/Minify, R =
  Reverse/Rearrange); the coverage, not the exact words, matters.

## Verify
- [ ] The subject's attributes were listed before asking the questions.
- [ ] All seven letters produced at least one idea.
- [ ] Ideas are concrete enough to try next week.
- [ ] Evaluation happened after the pass, against a stated goal.
