# Stacking a new habit onto an anchor

**Goal:** make a new behaviour automatic by attaching it to something you already do
every day, so the existing action becomes the reminder.

## The recipe
> **After I** [existing anchor], **I will** [new tiny behaviour].

Examples:
- After I pour my morning coffee, I will write one line in my journal.
- After I sit down on the train, I will open my language app.
- After I brush my teeth at night, I will floss one tooth.
- After I close my laptop for the day, I will write tomorrow's top task on a sticky note.

## 1. Choose the anchor
List things you do **every day, at a consistent time and place, without thinking**:
waking, the first coffee, showering, brushing teeth, parking the car, lunch, arriving
home, plugging in your phone. A good anchor:
- happens **reliably** (not "when I go to the gym");
- has a **clear end point** ("after I *put down* the toothbrush" is crisper than "while
  brushing");
- sits in the **right place** for the new habit (floss by the sink, not in a drawer
  upstairs);
- matches the **frequency** you want (daily anchor for a daily habit).

## 2. Size the new behaviour tiny
Fogg's rule: make the starter version take under about 30 seconds and need almost no
motivation. One push-up, one line, one tooth, open the book. You are building the
*cue → action* link first; you can do more once started, but the commitment is only
the tiny version. On bad days, the tiny version still counts.

## 3. Prepare the environment
Put whatever the habit needs right at the anchor: the journal next to the coffee
machine, the vitamins by the kettle, the running shoes by the bed.

## 4. Mark the win
Fogg recommends an immediate small "celebration" (a fist pump, "nice") right after the
behaviour to make it feel rewarding. The evidence base for this specific element is
mostly his own practice-based work — treat it as plausible, not proven. A checkmark on a
tracker serves a similar purpose (see
`mind-self.habit-and-self-management.self-monitoring`).

## 5. Grow and chain
- Once the tiny version is automatic (you do it without the note reminding you), expand
  it gradually: one line → three lines.
- Add a second stack link only when the first is solid: "After I write one line, I will
  drink a glass of water." Chains longer than 3–4 links get fragile; if one link breaks,
  the rest go too.

## How long it takes
Lally et al. (2010) followed people adding a daily habit: time to reach near-maximum
automaticity ranged from **18 to 254 days**, median about **66**. "21 days" is a myth.
Missing a single day did not materially derail the process.

## Gotchas
- **Vague anchors fail:** "after breakfast" when breakfast moves around the morning.
- **Too big too early:** "after coffee I'll run 5 km" collapses within a week.
- **Weekend and travel days** break anchors; have a fallback anchor for them.
- Writing the recipe down and putting it where you'll see it for the first couple of
  weeks helps (an implementation-intention effect — see
  `mind-self.habit-and-self-management.implementation-intentions`).

## Verify
- [ ] Your recipe is written as "After I …, I will …" with a concrete anchor.
- [ ] The anchor happens daily at a consistent time and place.
- [ ] The starter behaviour takes under a minute.
- [ ] Everything needed sits where the anchor happens.
- [ ] After several weeks you notice yourself doing it without the reminder.
