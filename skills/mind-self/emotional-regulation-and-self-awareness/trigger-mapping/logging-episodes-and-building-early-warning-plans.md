# Logging episodes and building early-warning plans

**Goal:** stop being surprised by your own reactions. Record episodes, find the
pattern behind them, and decide in advance what you'll do when the early signs
appear.

"Trigger" here means an everyday emotional trigger — the thing that reliably
sets off a strong reaction (snapping at a partner, shutting down in meetings,
spiralling worry). For trauma-related triggers that bring flashbacks or
overwhelming distress, mapping is best done with a trauma-informed therapist.

## 1. Log each episode (5 minutes, soon after)
Use a notebook, a notes file or a spreadsheet with these columns:

| Field | Example |
|---|---|
| Date / time | Tue 18:40 |
| Situation (facts only) | Partner asked "did you pay the bill?" |
| State beforehand | Hungry, long day, behind on work |
| First body signal | Heat in face, jaw clenched |
| Thought / interpretation | "They think I'm useless" |
| Emotion + intensity 0–10 | Defensive anger 7, shame 4 |
| What I did | Snapped, left the room |
| Outcome / cost | Tense evening, apologised later |
| What I wish I'd done | Said "not yet, I'll do it after dinner" |

Write the situation as a camera would record it; keep interpretations in their
own column. That separation is where most insight comes from.

## 2. Find the pattern (weekly, after 8–15 entries)
Read the log in one sitting and look for:
- **Recurring situations**: criticism, feeling ignored, being rushed, unfairness,
  uncertainty, mess, particular people or places.
- **Vulnerability factors**: the "HALT" check — Hungry, Angry (already), Lonely,
  Tired — plus poor sleep, alcohol, pain, hormonal cycle, illness. Often the
  trigger is the same every day but only "lands" when you're depleted.
- **Recurring interpretations**: a theme like "I'm not respected" or "I'll be
  abandoned" behind very different situations.
- **Time of day** clusters.
Write your top three triggers as one-liners: *"Questions about tasks when I'm
tired and behind → I hear criticism → defensive anger."*

## 3. Identify early-warning signs
For each top trigger, what comes *before* the reaction? Usually a body signal
(heat, tight chest, fists), a thought ("here we go"), or an urge (to leave, to
raise your voice). The earlier the sign you can catch, the more choice you have.
A rough scale helps: green (calm), amber (signs present, still thinking
clearly), red (acting on autopilot). Plans work in amber; in red, the only plan
is a pause.

## 4. Write if-then plans
Implementation intentions — "If X, then I will Y" — have good evidence for
turning intentions into action. For each trigger:
- **If** I notice my jaw clench when asked about tasks, **then** I'll take one
  slow breath and say "give me a second".
- **If** I'm in red, **then** I'll say "I need ten minutes" and leave the room,
  and come back.
- **Prevention**: **if** it's after 6pm and I haven't eaten, **then** I'll eat
  before discussing anything admin.
Keep plans short, concrete and rehearsed — imagine the scene and run through
it a few times.

## 5. Review and adjust
After a few weeks, compare: frequency, intensity, how often you caught amber.
Revise plans that you never used — usually they were too vague or the sign too
late.

## Gotchas
- **Logging only the big blow-ups** gives a biased picture. Log near-misses too.
- **"They triggered me"** can slide into blame. The map is about *your* pattern;
  you can still address others' behaviour separately and directly.
- **Avoiding every trigger** shrinks life. Some are worth reducing (skip the
  argument while hungry); others need building tolerance.
- **Safety:** if episodes involve physical aggression, harm to yourself, or
  feeling out of control, seek professional help (GP, counsellor, anger
  management programme) rather than relying on self-help alone.

## Verify
- [ ] You have at least 8 logged episodes with situation and interpretation separated.
- [ ] Your top three triggers are written as one-liners including vulnerability factors.
- [ ] Each has a named early-warning sign.
- [ ] Each has a rehearsed if-then plan, including a red-zone pause.
- [ ] You've reviewed frequency and intensity after a few weeks.
