# Designing accountability that actually bites

**Goal:** when your own intentions aren't enough, set up outside pressure — a
person, a cadence, a stake — strong enough to change behaviour on the day it
counts, without turning it into shame.

## Why it works
Present-you discounts future rewards heavily. Commitment devices make skipping
costly *now*: someone will ask, money will go, you'll have to report a miss.
Behavioural-economics studies show people will voluntarily bind themselves and
that binding can work (e.g. a commitment savings account for quitting smoking).
*(Effects are real but uneven; many people never sign up or set stakes too low.)*

## 1. Choose the mechanism
| Mechanism | Best for | Strength |
|---|---|---|
| Accountability partner (1:1) | Most personal goals | Medium–high |
| Small group / mastermind | Work and creative projects | Medium–high |
| Body doubling / coworking session | Getting started on dreaded tasks | High for the session |
| Public pledge | Reputation-sensitive people | Variable — see gotchas |
| Commitment contract with money | Clear binary behaviours | High |
| Paid coach/trainer | When you'll show up for an appointment | High |

## 2. Accountability partner set-up
- **Pick someone reliable, not your harshest critic** and not someone who'll let
  everything slide. A peer with their own goal (mutual accountability) works well.
- **Agree specifics in writing**: the goal, the exact behaviour to report (process
  goals work best — "3 writing sessions of 45 min"), the cadence and the format.
- **Cadence**: weekly check-in is a good default; daily text for fragile new
  habits ("done ✓" or "missed — reason"); monthly for long projects.
- **Script the check-in** (10 minutes): What did I commit to? What did I do? What
  got in the way? What's my commitment for next week?
- **Agree what happens on a miss** — a question, not a lecture: "What will you
  change?"

## 3. Commitment contracts with stakes
1. Define a **binary, verifiable** behaviour ("gym 3×/week, checked by app log"),
   not a fuzzy outcome ("be healthier").
2. Set a **stake that stings but doesn't harm**: an amount you'd genuinely hate to
   lose but can afford.
3. Choose where the money goes: a friend, a charity, or (stronger for some) an
   "anti-charity" you dislike.
4. Name a **referee** who confirms each period.
5. Set the term: 4–12 weeks, then review.
Services exist that handle this (for example stickK or Beeminder); a friend with
a spreadsheet works too.

## 4. Public pledges — use carefully
Announcing a goal works for some, but there's a hypothesis (Gollwitzer and
colleagues) that announcing an *identity* goal ("I'm going to be a writer") and
getting praise can give a premature sense of completion. *(Research is limited and
mixed.)* Safer: announce the **behaviour and the check-in date** ("I'll post my
weekly word count every Friday"), not just the dream.

## 5. Body doubling
Working alongside someone — in person or on a video call, cameras on, each on
their own task — makes starting and staying much easier for many people,
including many with ADHD. Format: 2 minutes to state tasks, 50 minutes silent
work, 5 minutes report back. *(Mostly anecdotal and practitioner evidence.)*

## Gotchas
- **Shame spirals**: if check-ins feel humiliating, you'll start avoiding them —
  and the goal. Keep the tone curious.
- **Stakes too low** become a fee for skipping. Raise them, or switch mechanism.
- **Gaming**: vague definitions invite loopholes. Tighten wording after the first miss.
- **Partner fade**: most arrangements drift by week 4–6. Put check-ins in both
  calendars and schedule a reset conversation in advance.
- **Money stakes are not suitable** if finances are tight or gambling is a
  problem; use social or time-based stakes instead.
- If the goal is recovery from addiction or an eating disorder, use professional
  and mutual-aid support rather than penalty systems.

## Verify
- [ ] You've chosen a mechanism matched to the goal.
- [ ] The behaviour reported is specific and verifiable.
- [ ] A check-in cadence and script are agreed and in the calendar.
- [ ] Any stake stings but is affordable, with a referee named.
- [ ] Misses lead to a "what will you change?" conversation, not shame.
