# Running a WOOP session

**Goal:** in 5–10 minutes, test whether a wish is worth pursuing and, if it is,
leave with a concrete plan for the obstacle most likely to stop you.

## Why not just think positive?
Oettingen's research found that indulging in positive fantasies about a desired
future can *lower* effort and achievement — the mind partly experiences the
success in advance. Contrasting the fantasy with the obstacle in present reality
creates energy where the wish is feasible, and lets you let go where it isn't.
Combined with if-then planning (Gollwitzer's implementation intentions), this is
called **MCII**; **WOOP** is the user-friendly version. *(Well studied across
health, study and relationship goals; effect sizes vary and are typically
moderate.)*

## The four steps
Find a quiet few minutes. Close your eyes for the imagining steps. Take them in
this order — the order matters.

**1. Wish** — something important to you, challenging but feasible, within a
chosen time frame (today, this week, this month). Sum it up in 3–6 words.
- *"Finish the grant application draft this week."*

**2. Outcome** — what's the single best result of fulfilling it? How would it
feel? Name it in a few words, then **really imagine it** for a minute or two —
the details, the feeling.
- *"Relief, and an evening free of guilt."*

**3. Obstacle** — what is it **in you** that stands in the way? Not "my
colleagues interrupt" but your inner response: an emotion, a habit, a belief, an
urge. Name it, then imagine it vividly too.
- *"When I open the document I feel overwhelmed and switch to email."*
Tip: if you can't find an inner obstacle, ask "what's the moment I usually give
up?"

**4. Plan** — one if-then statement: *If [obstacle], then I will [action].*
- *"If I feel overwhelmed opening the document, then I'll write just the next
  heading and one bad sentence."*
Say it to yourself once or twice, and imagine executing it.

## Writing it down
A one-line template you can keep in a notes app:
`W: … | O: … | O: … | P: If … then I will …`

## Reading the result
- **Energised and the plan feels workable** → commit; put the plan where you'll
  see it.
- **The obstacle seems insurmountable or the outcome turned out not to matter**
  → that's useful too. Downsize the wish, choose a different time frame, or let
  it go without guilt.

## Good if-then plans
- The "if" is a specific, recognisable cue (a feeling, a time, a place, a
  thought).
- The "then" is a small, immediate action, not a resolution ("I'll try harder").
- One obstacle per WOOP. For a second obstacle, run another WOOP.
- For outer obstacles you genuinely can't control, plan your response: "If a
  colleague interrupts, then I'll say I'll reply at 3."

## Practice tips
- Daily WOOP in the morning for the day's main wish is a common routine.
- Revisit your WOOP after a slip: was the obstacle right? Adjust the plan.
- Apps and free materials exist (the official WOOP site and app).

## Gotchas
- **Skipping the outcome imagery** weakens the contrast; give it real time.
- **External obstacles** feel easier to name but give you less to work with.
- **Wishes that are too easy** don't benefit; WOOP shines on things you care about
  and keep putting off.
- **Mood**: if thinking about obstacles leaves you hopeless rather than
  energised across many wishes, low mood may be the bigger issue — consider
  talking to a GP or counsellor.

## Verify
- [ ] Your wish is important, challenging and feasible within a time frame.
- [ ] You imagined the outcome before the obstacle, not the reverse.
- [ ] The obstacle is an inner one (feeling, habit, urge, belief).
- [ ] The plan is a single "if…then I will…" with a specific cue and small action.
- [ ] You've decided to commit, adjust or drop the wish based on the contrast.
