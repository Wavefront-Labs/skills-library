# Everyday breathing patterns for stress

**Goal:** have a few simple breathing patterns you can use anywhere — before a
difficult call, stuck in traffic, lying awake — to bring your stress level down a
notch or two.

These are calming, slow-breathing techniques. They're **not** the intense,
fast "breathwork" used to induce altered states, which carries different risks.

## Why slow breathing helps
Slowing the breath, especially lengthening the exhale, tends to shift the nervous
system towards its calming (parasympathetic) mode: heart rate eases, and heart
rate variability rises. Reviews of slow breathing report reduced self-rated
anxiety and arousal. *(Effects are generally modest and short-term; studies vary in
quality. Good as one tool among several.)*

## 0. Foundation: breathe low and through the nose
- Sit or stand tall, shoulders relaxed. One hand on the chest, one on the belly.
- Breathe in through the nose so the **belly hand moves** more than the chest hand.
- Breathe out gently, through the nose or pursed lips.
- Practise 2–3 minutes a day until low breathing feels natural.

## 1. Extended exhale (the all-rounder)
- In through the nose for about **4**, out slowly for about **6–8**.
- 5–10 rounds. Counts are guides; adjust so it's comfortable, never strained.
- Useful anywhere, invisible to others.

## 2. Box breathing (for focus under pressure)
- In **4** → hold **4** → out **4** → hold **4**. Repeat 4–8 rounds.
- Picture tracing the sides of a square.
- Popular with military and first responders for staying steady under pressure.
- If holds feel uncomfortable or raise anxiety, shorten them to 2 or drop them.

## 3. Paced / resonance breathing (for a daily practice)
- Breathe at roughly **5–6 breaths per minute**: e.g. in for 5 seconds, out for 5
  (or in 4, out 6).
- 10–20 minutes daily, with a breathing app or a visual pacer to keep the
  rhythm.
- This is the rate used in HRV biofeedback, which has evidence for reducing
  stress and anxiety symptoms over weeks of practice. The exact best rate varies
  slightly per person.

## 4. 4-7-8 (for winding down)
- In through the nose for **4**, hold **7**, out through the mouth with a soft
  whoosh for **8**. Four rounds to start.
- Often used at bedtime. *(Popular, but specific research on this exact pattern is
  thin.)*

## 5. Physiological sigh (for a fast reset)
Two inhales through the nose (a full breath plus a top-up sip), then one long,
slow exhale through the mouth. 1–3 times. See
mind-self.emotional-regulation-and-self-awareness.self-soothing-technique.

## Pre-stressor routine
Before something you know will be stressful (a presentation, a medical
appointment, a hard conversation):
1. 2 minutes before: 6–10 extended-exhale breaths.
2. Relax shoulders and jaw on each exhale.
3. One physiological sigh right before you start.
4. Afterwards, 1 minute of paced breathing to come down.

## Gotchas
- **Over-breathing**: breathing too deeply or fast causes light-headedness,
  tingling in the fingers or lips. Slow down and make breaths smaller. If you feel
  dizzy, stop and breathe normally.
- **Breath holds** aren't for everyone. Skip holds if you're pregnant, have
  uncontrolled high blood pressure, heart or lung conditions — or check with a
  doctor first.
- **Focusing on breath can raise anxiety** for some people, especially with panic
  disorder. Try counting objects or grounding instead, or keep the eyes open.
- **Practise when calm**: you'll reach for it under stress only if it's familiar.
- **Not a treatment** for anxiety disorders, panic attacks or breathing problems
  like asthma. If stress or panic is frequent, or you have breathlessness
  without a clear reason, see a doctor.
