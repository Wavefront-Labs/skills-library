# Running and tuning timed focus blocks

**Goal:** turn "I'll work on it this afternoon" into a series of short, protected
blocks with a defined start, a defined end and a break you actually take — then
adjust the block length from evidence rather than habit.

## 1. The basic block (pomodoro form)
1. **Pick one task** for the block and write it down. If it's bigger than a few
   blocks, write the first concrete piece ("draft section 2 intro", not "report").
2. **Set a visible timer** — classic length is 25 minutes. A physical kitchen
   timer or a standalone timer app beats a phone, which invites checking.
3. **Work only on that task until it rings.** No email, no "quick look".
4. **Mark it done** (a tick on paper is enough) and **take a 5-minute break**
   away from the screen.
5. After **four blocks, take a longer break** of 15–30 minutes.

The rule that makes it work: the block is indivisible. If you abandon it for a
distraction, it doesn't count — start a fresh one rather than pausing the timer.

## 2. Handling interruptions mid-block
- **Internal** (a thought, an urge to check something): write it on an
  *interruption list* beside you and carry on. You'll deal with it in a break.
  Tally how many you had — the count is useful data.
- **External** (someone asks something): use *inform, negotiate, schedule,
  call back* — "I'm in the middle of something; can I come to you in 15
  minutes?" Then actually do it.
- **Genuine emergency:** stop, void the block, deal with it. Don't feel bad;
  just don't let "emergency" become a cover for anything that pings.

## 3. Tuning the interval length
25/5 is a starting point, not a law. Adjust to the task and to you:

| Situation | Try |
|---|---|
| Starting is the hard part, task feels aversive | 10–15 min blocks; momentum matters more than length |
| Creative or deep technical work with long warm-up | 45–90 min blocks, 10–20 min breaks |
| Lots of small admin items | Batch several into one 25-min block |
| You keep finishing mid-thought when the bell rings | Lengthen by 10 min, or use a "flowtime" variant: work until a natural stopping point, then break in proportion (roughly 1 min per 5 worked) |

The popular "52 minutes on / 17 off" figure comes from one company's analysis of
app-usage data, not controlled research — treat it as one more option to test,
not a proven optimum. *(Uncertain: no strong evidence favours any single ratio.)*

## 4. Design breaks that restore
A break that is spent scrolling a feed often leaves attention more fragmented
than before. Better: stand up, walk, look at something far away, get water,
stretch. Keep breaks timed too — an untimed "5 minutes" drifts into 25. See
mind-self.attention-and-focus-training.attention-restoration for break
content.

## 5. Weekly review
Look at the tallies: blocks completed per day, interruptions per block, which
tasks overran their estimate. Typical findings: you complete far fewer blocks
than you planned (plan for that number next time), and interruptions cluster at
a particular time or from a particular source (fix that source — see
distraction management).

## Gotchas
- **Rigidity backfires.** If the bell rings when you're deeply absorbed, it's fine
  to finish the thought; the technique serves focus, not the reverse.
- **Collaborative work** doesn't fit tidy blocks; use them for solo work and
  protect them with calendar holds.
- **Timer apps on the phone** pull you towards notifications. Use a separate device
  or put the phone face-down out of reach.
- **Counting blocks as productivity** can reward busy-work. Pair the count with
  "what moved forward?"
- If you can rarely hold even 10 minutes on anything for weeks, despite trying,
  that may be worth raising with a doctor — sustained attention problems can have
  medical causes (sleep, mood, ADHD) that technique alone won't fix.

## Verify
- [ ] Each block started with one named, concrete task.
- [ ] Interruptions went on a list, not into action, unless truly urgent.
- [ ] Breaks were timed and spent away from the screen.
- [ ] You changed block length at least once based on how the last week went.
- [ ] You know your realistic number of completed blocks per day.
