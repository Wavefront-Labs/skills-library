# Engineering devices and workspace against distraction

**Goal:** stop relying on willpower in the moment. Change the environment once so
that the distracting option is slower, quieter or absent when you sit down to work.

## Principle: friction and cues
Behaviour follows the path of least resistance. Every notification is a cue; every
one-tap app is zero friction. You win by **removing cues** and **adding
friction** to what you want less of, and doing the opposite for what you want more.

## 1. Notification audit (do this first, ~30 minutes)
Go through every app on your phone and computer:
- **Keep on**: people who need you urgently (calls/messages from a short list of
  favourites), calendar alerts, anything safety-related.
- **Badge only or off**: email, social media, news, shopping, games, most work
  chat channels.
- **Off entirely**: promotional notifications of any kind.
Where: iOS Settings › Notifications; Android Settings › Notifications (long-press
a notification to change that app); Windows Settings › System › Notifications;
macOS System Settings › Notifications. *(Menu names move between OS versions; the
locations are approximate.)*

In chat tools (Slack, Teams), mute busy channels and allow only direct mentions.

## 2. Focus modes
Use the built-in mode rather than an extra app where possible: **Focus** (iOS/macOS),
**Focus mode / Do Not Disturb / Digital Wellbeing** (Android), **Focus / Do not
disturb** (Windows). Create a "Work" mode that:
- allows calls/messages from a few named people (and repeated calls, so true
  emergencies get through);
- hides distracting apps or home-screen pages;
- turns on automatically on a schedule or when you open your work app.

## 3. Add friction to tempting apps
Ranked roughly from light to heavy:
1. Move the app off the home screen into a folder on the last page.
2. Log out after each use so opening it needs a password.
3. Set a daily time limit (Screen Time / Digital Wellbeing).
4. Switch the phone to **grayscale** during work hours — colour makes feeds more
   compelling for many people (evidence is mixed; try it for a week).
5. Delete the app and use the website only, or block the site during work hours
   with a blocker (browser extension or system-level). Pick blockers that are
   hard to switch off mid-session.

## 4. Physical workspace
- Phone **in another room or a drawer**, not face-down on the desk. Some studies
  suggest even a visible phone draws on attention *(findings have been mixed in
  replication; out of sight is still the safe default).*
- Clear the desk of everything except the current task's materials.
- Noise: noise-cancelling headphones or steady background sound if the space is
  busy; a "headphones on = please don't interrupt" agreement with housemates or
  colleagues.
- If you can, keep one place for focused work and do leisure scrolling elsewhere,
  so the place itself becomes a cue.

## 5. Scheduled communication windows
Decide when you check email and messages (e.g. three set times a day), put it in
your calendar, and tell people your typical response time. An autoresponder or
status message ("checking messages at 11 and 3") sets expectations and reduces
the guilt that drives checking.

## Gotchas
- **Blocking without replacement**: if you block one feed, the urge migrates to
  another. Notice the urge itself and capture it rather than chasing a new
  outlet.
- **Too-strict rules collapse.** Hard blocks all day often get abandoned within a
  week. Start with work hours only.
- **Don't silence what you're responsible for**: on-call alerts, a child's school,
  a carer's phone. Whitelist them explicitly.
- **Smartwatches** re-create every notification on your wrist — audit them too.
- Re-audit every few months; new apps arrive with notifications on by default.

## Verify
- [ ] No app can make a sound during work hours unless it's on your short list.
- [ ] A work focus mode exists and turns on without you remembering.
- [ ] Your top two time-sink apps need at least two extra steps to open.
- [ ] The phone is out of sight while you work.
- [ ] Communication windows are in your calendar and others know them.
