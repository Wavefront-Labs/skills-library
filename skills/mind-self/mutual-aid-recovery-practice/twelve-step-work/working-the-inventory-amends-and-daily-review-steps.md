# Working the inventory, amends and daily-review steps

**Goal:** a plain reference for the written and spoken work in the middle of the 12 steps,
as members commonly describe it — so a newer member knows what's being asked. It is no
substitute for a sponsor, a home group, or the fellowship's own literature, and it isn't
clinical treatment. Fellowships differ (AA, NA, CA, GA, Al-Anon…); follow your own.

## Where these steps sit
Paraphrasing the arc: steps 1–3 concern admitting the problem and becoming willing to
rely on a power beyond yourself (many members understand this non-religiously — the group,
the programme). **Steps 4–9 are the action steps** covered here. Steps 10–12 are the
maintenance steps: continued inventory, prayer or meditation, and carrying the message.

## Step 4 — the written inventory
Most sponsors use the **resentment inventory** laid out in the Big Book as four columns:
| 1. Who/what I resent | 2. What happened | 3. What it affected in me | 4. My part |
|---|---|---|---|
Column 3 traditionally covers self-esteem, security, relationships, finances and
ambitions. Work **down each column** for all entries before moving to the next, rather
than across rows — it keeps you from justifying as you go. Column 4 asks where you were
selfish, dishonest, self-seeking or afraid; it's about your patterns, not blame. Many
inventories add lists for **fears** and **sexual conduct/relationship harms**.
Practical points: write by hand or type, but write it, don't just think it; aim for
honesty over completeness; most people take weeks, not days. Keep it somewhere private.

## Step 5 — sharing it
Read the inventory to one trusted person — usually your sponsor, sometimes a member of
the clergy or a counsellor. The listener's job is to hear it and point out patterns, not
to absolve or judge. Choose someone who will keep confidence; inventories can contain
legally or personally sensitive material.

## Steps 6–7 — readiness
Identify the recurring defects your inventory exposed (fear, dishonesty, resentment) and
become willing to let them go; many members write the opposite practice beside each
(fear → acting despite fear).

## Step 8 — the amends list
List everyone harmed; your inventory is the main source. Many sponsors sort into: **now**,
**later**, **maybe**, and **never directly** (where contact would cause harm).

## Step 9 — making amends
- **Direct amends:** acknowledge specifically what you did, without excuses or "but you…",
  ask what you can do to set it right, and do it (repay money, repair damage).
- **The key limit:** not when doing so would injure that person or others — e.g. revealing
  an affair to someone's partner, contacting someone who has asked for no contact, or
  breaching a court order. Talk every risky amend through with your sponsor first.
- **Legal exposure:** amends involving crimes or significant money should be discussed
  with a lawyer before you confess anything. *(Draft: general caution, not legal advice.)*
- **Indirect or living amends** where direct ones aren't possible: changed behaviour,
  restitution through charity, letters you don't send for the deceased.
- Amends aren't apologies for forgiveness; the other person owes you nothing.

## Step 10 — the nightly review
A few minutes each night (or a spot-check during the day): Was I resentful, selfish,
dishonest or afraid today? Do I owe an apology? What did I do well? Promptly admit and
correct wrongs. It's the inventory in miniature, so problems don't pile up again.

## When to get professional help
Trauma, abuse or violence surfacing in an inventory; thoughts of self-harm; or physical
withdrawal symptoms — these need a doctor or therapist alongside the programme. Stopping
alcohol, benzodiazepines or opioids abruptly can be medically dangerous; seek medical
detox advice. Many fellowships explicitly support members using professional treatment.

## Verify
- [ ] Inventory is written, worked column by column, and includes "my part".
- [ ] Shared with one trusted listener who keeps confidence.
- [ ] Every risky amend was discussed with your sponsor (and a lawyer where needed).
- [ ] No amend was made that would harm the other person or a third party.
- [ ] A daily review is happening.
