# Using SMART Recovery and LifeRing tools

**Goal:** know what the main non-12-step mutual-aid programmes ask of you as a participant
and how to use their core worksheets. These programmes are self-help and peer support;
they work alongside, not instead of, medical and psychological treatment — SMART and
LifeRing both welcome members using medication or therapy. Check each programme's current
materials, as tool names and wording get revised.

## SMART Recovery
Science-informed, drawing on cognitive-behavioural therapy, rational emotive behaviour
therapy (REBT) and motivational interviewing. It covers any addictive behaviour, uses
no "powerlessness" or labels, and meetings include facilitated discussion (crosstalk is
allowed, unlike most 12-step meetings).

**The 4-Point Program:** (1) building and maintaining motivation, (2) coping with urges,
(3) managing thoughts, feelings and behaviours, (4) living a balanced life.

**Cost-Benefit Analysis (CBA)** — a four-box grid:
| | Benefits | Costs |
|---|---|---|
| **Using/doing it** | short- and long-term | short- and long-term |
| **Not using/stopping** | | |
Be honest in the "benefits of using" box — understanding what the behaviour gives you is
the point. Mark items short- or long-term, then rate importance. Keep it and re-read it
when motivation dips.

**Hierarchy of Values:** list what matters most (family, health, work); compare with
where your time and money actually go.

**Urge log:** date/time, situation, intensity (1–10), thoughts, what you did, outcome.
Patterns tell you which situations to plan for.

**DEADS** — urge-coping menu: *Deny/Delay*, *Escape*, *Avoid*, *Distract*, *Substitute*.
**DISARM** — label the urge's inner voice ("the craving talking"), name it, argue back
and refuse.

**ABC tool** (from REBT): *A* activating event → *B* beliefs about it → *C* emotional or
behavioural consequence. Then *D* dispute the belief ("Is it true I *can't* stand this?")
and *E* write an effective new belief. Used for cravings, anger, shame.

**Change Plan Worksheet:** the changes I want, why, steps, who can help, how I'll know it's
working, what could interfere. Typically done once motivation is clear.

## LifeRing Secular Recovery
Built on **sobriety, secularity and self-help** ("the 3-S"). Specific to abstinence from
alcohol and other drugs. Key practices:
- **Sobriety priority:** sobriety comes first, ahead of other choices, as a decision you
  remake rather than an identity.
- **The sober self vs the addict self:** meetings aim to strengthen the sober self through
  positive, present-focused talk; typical meetings ask "how was your week?" and what's
  coming up.
- **Personal recovery plan:** the *Recovery by Choice* workbook walks through domains
  (body, home, people, work, feelings, time) and lets you design your own programme.
No sponsors in the 12-step sense; members support each other in and between meetings.

## Other programmes
Women for Sobriety (thirteen positive acceptance statements), SOS (Secular Organizations
for Sobriety), Recovery Dharma (Buddhist-informed, non-theistic). Many people combine
meetings from more than one programme.

## Gotchas
- **Worksheets done once and filed** don't help; revisit CBA and urge logs weekly in
  early change.
- **Moderation goals:** SMART supports people deciding their own goal, but for some
  (physical dependence, liver disease, pregnancy, past failed attempts) abstinence is
  medically indicated — ask a doctor.
- **Withdrawal:** stopping heavy alcohol or benzodiazepine use suddenly can be dangerous;
  get medical help first.

## Verify
- [ ] A completed CBA with honest benefits of using, re-read recently.
- [ ] An urge log with at least a week of entries and a DEADS plan for top situations.
- [ ] At least one ABC worked through on a real situation.
- [ ] A written change or recovery plan with a support person named.
- [ ] Medical advice sought where withdrawal or health risks apply.
