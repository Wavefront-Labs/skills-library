# Walking meditation: formal and natural pace

**Goal:** keep attention on the experience of walking — mainly the feet and legs — so
that movement becomes the meditation object. Useful when sitting is too sleepy, too
restless or physically uncomfortable, and as a bridge to mindfulness in daily life.
Educational guidance.

## 1. Formal walking: set up
- **Lane:** a straight, flat, uncluttered path of about 10–20 paces. Indoors is fine
  (a hallway); a garden path or quiet room works well.
- **Posture:** upright, eyes open with gaze lowered a couple of metres ahead (not at
  the feet). Hands clasped in front, behind, or relaxed at your sides.
- **Footwear:** bare feet or thin socks indoors give clearer sensation; shoes outdoors.
- **Length:** 10–30 minutes. On retreats, walking sessions often alternate with sitting.

## 2. The step cycle — choose a resolution
Attend to the sensations, not the image of your feet.
| Pace | Phases noted | Use when |
|---|---|---|
| Very slow | lifting – moving – placing (– shifting weight) | Building precision; mind is busy |
| Slow | lifting – placing | General practice |
| Medium | left – right | Restless or drowsy |
| Natural | whole-body sense of walking | Bridge to daily life |
Optionally note the phases silently (see
mind-self.mindfulness-and-contemplative-practice.noting-practice.labelling-experience-with-noting).

## 3. The turn
At the end of the lane, **stop**. Feel standing for a breath or two: weight in the
soles, the body upright. Note "intending to turn", turn slowly in a few steps, pause
again, then start back. The turn is where minds most often wander — it's a built-in
checkpoint.

## 4. When the mind wanders
Same as sitting: notice, return attention to the next step. If you've wandered a lot,
slow down for a few lengths. If you're drowsy, speed up. Stopping completely for a
moment to re-establish attention is fine.

## 5. Natural-pace mindful walking (outdoors, everyday)
- Walk at normal speed; attention on the rhythm of steps, the swing of arms, contact
  with the ground, air on the skin.
- Widen periodically to sights and sounds, then come back to the feet.
- Choose a stretch of a regular route (the walk from the car park to the office) as
  your daily practice segment.
- Leave headphones out for this stretch.

## Gotchas
- **Safety first:** at very slow pace, balance is wobbly — use a wall or rail nearby,
  and stay aware of traffic, kerbs and other people outdoors. Never do eyes-closed
  walking.
- Very slow walking in public draws attention; do formal slow walking in private spaces
  and natural pace in public.
- Looking at your feet strains the neck and narrows attention to visual images.
- Trying to make each step "perfect" creates tension; it's attention, not choreography.
- People with balance problems, recent injuries or dizziness should keep to natural pace
  with support, or adapt to standing or seated movement practice; check with a clinician
  if unsure.
- Uncertainty: phase names and lane lengths vary by tradition (e.g. Zen *kinhin* uses a
  specific half-step-per-breath rhythm not covered in detail here).

## Verify
- [ ] You can name which step phases you were tracking and keep them for a whole session.
- [ ] You paused and turned deliberately at each end of the lane.
- [ ] You adjusted pace in response to drowsiness or restlessness.
- [ ] You have one everyday stretch of route where you walk mindfully at natural pace.
- [ ] You stayed safe: eyes open, aware of surroundings, support nearby when slow.
