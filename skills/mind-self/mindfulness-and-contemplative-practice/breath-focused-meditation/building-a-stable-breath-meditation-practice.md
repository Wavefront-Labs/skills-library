# Building a stable breath meditation practice

**Goal:** train attention to rest on the breath and come back when it wanders,
reliably enough that a daily sit becomes calmer and more stable over weeks and months.
This is educational guidance in the style of a good meditation primer, not a treatment.

## 1. Posture (2 minutes to set up, then forget it)
- **Seat:** chair, cushion or kneeling bench — all fine. Hips slightly higher than
  knees takes strain off the lower back. On a chair, feet flat, back away from the
  backrest if you can hold it comfortably.
- **Spine upright but not rigid**; imagine the crown of the head lifted gently.
  Shoulders drop, chin slightly tucked, hands resting on thighs or in the lap.
- **Eyes:** closed, or open with a soft downward gaze ~1 m ahead (helps if you get drowsy).
- Lying down is acceptable if sitting hurts, but expect more sleepiness.
Discomfort is allowed to be fixed: shift mindfully rather than enduring sharp pain.

## 2. Pick one anchor and keep it for the session
Common anchor points:
- **Nostrils / upper lip** — fine, subtle sensation of air; good for sharpening.
- **Chest** — rise and fall; middling.
- **Belly** — broad, slow movement; grounding, easier for beginners and anxious minds.
- **Whole-body breath** — the general sense of breathing everywhere.
Try each for a few sessions, then **commit to one**; switching mid-sit is a form of
wandering. Do not control the breath — let it run at its own pace and simply feel it.

## 3. The core loop (this is the whole practice)
1. Rest attention on the anchor sensation.
2. At some point you notice you're thinking about something else.
3. **That moment of noticing is the success**, not the failure. Note "wandering" lightly.
4. Return to the breath gently, without commentary.
5. Repeat — hundreds of times per sit is normal, especially early on.
The usual beginner trap is self-criticism ("I'm bad at this"), which is just more wandering.

**Optional aid — counting:** count exhalations 1 to 10, then start again; if you lose
count or reach 15, start from 1. Drop counting once it becomes mechanical; it's a crutch
for scattered days, not the goal.

## 4. Session length and progression
| Stage | Length | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Weeks 1–2 | 5–10 min daily | Consistency beats length. Same time, same place. |
| Month 1–3 | 15–20 min | Add a minute or two when sits feel settled. |
| Established | 20–45 min | Longer sits show deeper stability and subtler wandering. |
Use a timer with a gentle bell so you're not checking the clock. A brief tally after each
sit (length, rough steadiness 1–5) shows progress better than memory does.

## 5. Common hindrances and responses
- **Drowsiness:** eyes open, sit straighter, sit earlier in the day, take a few deliberate deeper breaths, then let them go.
- **Restlessness:** switch to the belly anchor, lengthen attention on the exhale, count.
- **Breath feels forced** once you watch it: normal; it eases. Shift attention to where
  the breath is felt passively (belly), or briefly to sounds, then return.
- **Strong emotion arising:** you can widen attention to include it, or open your eyes and
  ground in the feet and room. You are always free to stop.

## Gotchas
- Expecting a "blank mind" — not the aim; the aim is noticing and returning.
- Breath focus is not universally calming. For some people (e.g. with panic, asthma or
  trauma histories) watching the breath raises anxiety; use the feet, hands or sounds as
  the anchor instead. If meditation repeatedly brings up distressing memories,
  dissociation or panic, pause and seek a trauma-informed teacher or clinician.
- Uncertainty: the stage lengths above reflect common teaching conventions, not a
  research-fixed schedule.

## Verify
- [ ] You can name your chosen anchor and sat with it the whole session.
- [ ] You returned each time without scolding, and can count roughly how many returns.
- [ ] You've practised on at least 5 of the last 7 days at a fixed time.
- [ ] Your log shows session length or steadiness trending up over a month.
- [ ] You have a fallback anchor (feet, sounds) if breath focus feels uncomfortable.
