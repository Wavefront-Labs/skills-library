# Self-hypnosis — a session structure and suggestion wording that works

**Goal:** run a 15–20 minute self-hypnosis session reliably, with suggestions worded so
the absorbed mind takes them up, and come out of it clear-headed. This is for everyday,
non-clinical goals (calm before a talk, a sleep routine, practice motivation, small
habits). It is not a treatment for anything.

## When not to use it alone
See a qualified clinician (and mention any hypnosis you do) if you are dealing with
trauma or PTSD, psychosis or dissociative symptoms, epilepsy, severe depression, an
eating disorder, or physical pain that hasn't been diagnosed — hypnotic analgesia can
mask a symptom that needs a doctor. Never practise while driving or operating anything.

## The five parts
1. **Prepare (1–2 min).** Sit upright in a supported chair (lying down tends to become
   sleep). Phone silent. Decide on **one** goal and write its suggestion before you start
   (see below) — composing in trance is where sessions drift.
2. **Induce (3–5 min).** Two dependable self-inductions:
   - *Eye fixation:* pick a spot slightly above eye level. Stare at it, breathe slowly,
     and tell yourself the lids are getting heavy. Let them close when they want to.
   - *Progressive relaxation:* move attention from scalp to feet, releasing each area
     on an out-breath.
   Pick one and keep it; the same induction every time becomes a conditioned shortcut.
3. **Deepen (2–5 min).** A countdown from 10 to 1, each number "twice as relaxed", or
   imagine descending a staircase of ten steps. Depth beyond light-to-medium is rarely
   needed for self-suggestion — don't chase "going under".
4. **Suggest (5–8 min).** Repeat your prepared suggestion slowly 3–5 times, pairing it
   with a vivid image of yourself doing the thing. Add one **post-hypnotic cue**: "Whenever
   I press thumb and forefinger together, I feel this same calm." Practise the cue once
   while still in trance.
5. **Emerge (1 min).** Count up 1 to 5: "on five, eyes open, alert, refreshed". Stretch,
   look around, wait a minute before standing. Always emerge deliberately, even if
   interrupted — it prevents the groggy, spacey hangover.

## Wording suggestions
- **Positive, not negative:** "I stay calm and steady when I stand up to speak", not
  "I won't be nervous" — the second keeps "nervous" in focus.
- **Present or near-future tense, first person,** specific to a situation.
- **Believable steps:** "each day it gets a little easier" beats "I am fearless".
- **Sensory and emotional detail:** what you'll see, hear and feel when it's working.
- **Short:** one or two sentences you can repeat without reading.
- One goal per session; rotate goals weekly at most.

## Building the practice
Daily for two to three weeks, same time and place, is the usual guidance. Expect the
first sessions to feel like plain relaxation — that's normal; most people experience
hypnosis as focused absorption, not unconsciousness. Recording your own script and
playing it back is a reasonable aid; keep the voice slow and the emergence count on
the recording.

## Gotchas
- **Falling asleep** is the commonest failure: sit up, practise earlier in the day,
  shorten the deepener.
- **Trying hard blocks it.** Absorption is allowed, not forced; "noticing" works better
  than "making".
- **Evidence is mixed by goal.** Hypnosis has reasonable research support as an adjunct
  for some pain, anxiety and IBS uses under clinicians; self-hypnosis for habits like
  smoking shows modest, inconsistent results. Treat it as a support, not a fix. *(Draft:

- **Distress during a session** (an unexpected memory or strong emotion): open your eyes,
  count yourself up, ground yourself (feet on the floor, name five things you can see).
  If it recurs, stop and talk to a professional.

## Verify
- [ ] The suggestion was written down, positive and specific before the session.
- [ ] You used the same induction and deepener as last time.
- [ ] You emerged with a deliberate count and felt alert within a minute or two.
- [ ] The post-hypnotic cue produces a noticeable shift when used outside trance.
- [ ] None of the "see a clinician" conditions apply to your goal.
