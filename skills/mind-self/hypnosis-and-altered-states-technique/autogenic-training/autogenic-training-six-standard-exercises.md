# Autogenic training — the six standard exercises

**Goal:** learn the classic self-directed relaxation method (J. H. Schultz, 1930s) so you
can bring on a calm, heavy-warm state in a few minutes by repeating short formulas with
passive attention.

## Posture and session shape
Three options: lying on your back; reclining in an armchair; or the "coachman" seat
(sitting forward, forearms on thighs, head hanging loosely). Sessions are short — **2–3
practices a day, 3–5 minutes each** while learning. Each formula is repeated silently
3–6 times, slowly, with the attention resting on the body part — noticing, not trying.
This "passive concentration" is the whole skill: effort makes it fail.

Open every session with the rest formula: *"I am completely calm."*

## The six standard exercises (add one at a time)
1. **Heaviness** — *"My right arm is heavy."* (Left-handers start with the left.) Then
   left arm, both arms, legs, whole body.
2. **Warmth** — *"My right arm is warm"*, spreading as above. Heaviness and warmth
   together reflect muscle relaxation and peripheral blood-flow change.
3. **Heart** — *"My heartbeat is calm and regular."*
4. **Breathing** — *"My breathing is calm"*, or the traditional *"It breathes me."*
   Don't control the breath; let the formula describe it.
5. **Solar plexus** — *"My solar plexus is warm"* (warmth in the upper abdomen).
6. **Forehead** — *"My forehead is pleasantly cool."* (Cool, never cold.)

Typical progression: about **one to two weeks per exercise**, adding the new formula
after the earlier ones are "taking" within a few repetitions; the full sequence takes
two to three months. A compressed practised session eventually runs: calm → heavy →
warm → heart → breath → solar plexus → forehead → calm, in a few minutes.

## Cancellation (every time)
End with a deliberate return, in this order: **"Arms firm"** (flex the arms strongly a
few times), **"breathe deeply"**, **"eyes open."** Skipping it leaves heaviness, dizziness
or grogginess. The exception: sessions used to fall asleep at night.

## Keep a log
After each session, jot: time, posture, which formulas took, side effects. Patterns show
up quickly — e.g. practice right after meals or coffee working poorly.

## Autogenic discharges
Twitches, tingling, brief emotions, images or a sense of floating are common and
traditionally called "autogenic discharges". Note them and carry on; if one is
unpleasant, cancel and resume next session.

## Medical cautions
- **Heart formula:** people with heart conditions should skip or reword it ("my chest is
  calm") and check with their doctor first.
- **Diabetes, blood-pressure or thyroid medication:** deep relaxation can shift
  requirements (e.g. blood glucose, blood pressure). Tell your clinician and monitor.

- **Breathing formula** with asthma: keep it descriptive, never directive.
- **Solar plexus warmth** is sometimes avoided with ulcers or digestive bleeding.
- Not a substitute for treatment of anxiety disorders or depression; it's often used
  alongside it. Serious psychiatric conditions call for supervised practice.

## Gotchas
- Trying to *make* the arm heavy produces tension. Repeat the phrase and wait.
- Practising for 20+ minutes early on doesn't help; short and frequent wins.
- Cold hands at first are normal; warmth usually follows heaviness by a week or two.

## Verify
- [ ] Each session opens with the calm formula and ends with full cancellation.
- [ ] You add a new exercise only once the previous ones take within a few repeats.
- [ ] Log shows 2–3 short sessions a day.
- [ ] Heart and other formulas are adjusted for any relevant health condition.
