# Lucid dreaming — journal, reality checks, and MILD + wake-back-to-bed

**Goal:** become aware you're dreaming while still in the dream, using the combination
with the best general track record — good recall, daytime reality checks, and MILD done
after a short wake-up late in the night — without wrecking your sleep.

## 1. Dream journal (weeks 1–2, then ongoing)
Recall comes first: you can't use lucidity you don't remember.
- On waking, **don't move or open your eyes** for a moment; replay the dream backwards.
- Write or voice-record immediately, even fragments ("blue corridor, late for something").
- Date each entry, give it a title.
- After 10–14 days, read back and list your **dream signs**: recurring places, people,
  themes, and impossibilities (lights not switching on, text changing, teeth falling out,
  being back at school).
Most people go from vague to several dreams a night within a couple of weeks.

## 2. Reality checks (daytime habit)
Several times a day — and especially whenever a dream sign shows up in waking life —
genuinely ask "am I dreaming?" and test:
- **Nose pinch:** pinch your nose shut and try to breathe through it. In dreams, you often can.
- **Read twice:** read text, look away, read again. Dream text tends to change.
- **Hands/fingers:** look closely; dream hands often look odd. Try pushing a finger
  through your palm.
Do two checks, not one, and actually expect the answer could be "yes". Mechanical checks
done without curiosity don't carry into dreams.

## 3. MILD with wake-back-to-bed
1. Set an alarm for about **5 hours** after sleep onset (REM periods lengthen toward morning).
2. When it goes off, get up briefly — note any dream you just had. Stay awake only a
   few minutes (studies used short wake periods; long ones cost sleep).
3. Back in bed, recall the dream you woke from. Repeat the intention: *"Next time I'm
   dreaming, I will remember I'm dreaming."*
4. Imagine re-entering that dream, spotting a dream sign, realising you're dreaming, and
   what you'll do.
5. Fall asleep holding the intention. If other thoughts intrude, return to the phrase.

## 4. Staying lucid
Excitement wakes most beginners. When you become lucid:
- Stay calm; **rub your hands together**, touch surfaces, or spin slowly — engaging dream
  senses tends to stabilise the scene.
- Remind yourself "I'm dreaming" every so often; lucidity can fade into ordinary dreaming.
- Have a simple plan ready (fly, find a mirror, ask a dream character a question).

## Protect your sleep
- WBTB at most a few nights a week; if you feel tired or irritable, stop. Sleep comes first.
- **Sleep paralysis** (awake, unable to move, sometimes with frightening presence or
  sounds) can occur, more often with WILD (wake-initiated) attempts and disrupted sleep.
  It's harmless and passes; focus on moving a finger or slowing your breath.
- **False awakenings** — dreaming you woke up — are a good moment for a reality check.
- If you have insomnia, a sleep disorder, or a history of dissociation or psychosis, talk
  to a clinician before deliberately disrupting sleep.

## Gotchas
- Results vary widely; many people take weeks or months to their first lucid dream.
- Supplements marketed for lucid dreaming (e.g. galantamine) have side effects and
  interactions — don't take them without medical advice.

## Verify
- [ ] Journal has 10+ entries and a written dream-sign list.
- [ ] Reality checks happen ~10 times a day with real questioning.
- [ ] WBTB alarm set ~5 h after sleep onset, wake period kept short.
- [ ] Daytime alertness isn't suffering.
