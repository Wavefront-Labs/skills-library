# Using rhyme, shape and alphabet pegs

**Goal:** remember a numbered list — ten tasks, the steps of a procedure, a top-10 — and
be able to say "what was number 7?" instantly, without running through items 1–6.
Pegs give **random access**, which the link method does not.

## 1. Learn one peg list first (one-time)
**Number-rhyme pegs** (easiest to learn — common English set; variants exist):
1 bun · 2 shoe · 3 tree · 4 door · 5 hive · 6 sticks · 7 heaven · 8 gate · 9 wine · 10 hen

**Number-shape pegs** (the digit's shape suggests the image):
1 candle/pencil · 2 swan · 3 handcuffs (or a bird's wings) · 4 sailboat · 5 hook ·
6 elephant's trunk/golf club · 7 cliff edge/boomerang · 8 snowman · 9 balloon on a string ·
10 bat and ball

**Alphabet pegs** (26 pegs; pick a sound-alike or concrete word per letter):
A ape · B bee · C sea · D dog (or deed) · E eel… choose words that are concrete and
personally vivid, and write the full list down.

Drill the list forwards, backwards and at random until each number gives its peg
instantly. Ten minutes for the rhyme pegs; longer for alphabet.

## 2. Hook each item to its peg
Make an **interacting, exaggerated image** of peg + item:
- Item 1 "call the plumber": a giant **bun** spraying water like a burst pipe; a plumber
  is stuck in it.
- Item 4 "buy printer ink": a **door** dripping black ink, smearing your hands as you
  open it.
- Item 7 "renew passport": your passport floating up to **heaven** on angel wings.
Hold each image for ~5 seconds and *see* it.

## 3. Recall
Go through the numbers: "1 — bun — oh, the plumber." For a specific number, jump
directly to its peg.

## 4. Going beyond ten
- Use the alphabet pegs for 11–36, or a second "layer" (e.g. 11 = a *bun* made of
  *candles*, mixing rhyme and shape).
- For lists of 20–100 items, the Major System 00–99 words (see major-system) are a
  ready-made, scalable peg list.
- For very long ordered material, a memory palace scales better.

## 5. Reusing pegs
Pegs get reused constantly, so old images interfere ("ghosting").
- Keep separate peg sets for different purposes (rhyme for daily to-dos, shape for
  something else).
- For long-term storage of a list, use a set you don't reuse, or review often.
- A day or two between uses is usually enough for a short-term list to fade (rule of
  thumb; test it yourself).

## When to use pegs vs other methods
- **Pegs:** short numbered lists needing random access (to-do lists, ranked items).
- **Link/story:** order matters but random access doesn't (see link-and-story-method).
- **Memory palace:** long ordered lists, speeches.

## Gotchas
- **Weak peg list** — if you hesitate on "6 is… sticks?", items will be lost. Over-learn it.
- **Passive pairings** ("a bun next to a plumber") don't stick; make them collide.
- **Mixing two peg systems mid-list** causes confusion; pick one per list.
- **Abstract items**: convert to a concrete symbol first (passport, not "bureaucracy").

## Verify
- [ ] You can name all pegs in any order without hesitation.
- [ ] A 10-item list is recalled fully after 10 minutes.
- [ ] Asked "what was number 6?", you answer without counting from 1.
- [ ] The list survives a recall test the next day (if you need it to).
