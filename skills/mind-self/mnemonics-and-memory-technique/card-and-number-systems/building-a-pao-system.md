# Building a PAO system for cards and numbers

**Goal:** memorise a shuffled deck or a long digit string fast, by giving every card (or
every two-digit number) a fixed **Person, Action and Object**, then compressing three
cards or six digits into a single image placed in a memory palace. Competition rules and
records are covered in
sports-recreation.mind-sports-and-games.memory-sports.memory-palaces-pao-and-competition-disciplines;
this skill is the construction and training method.

## How PAO compresses
Each item has three parts. To encode three items **A, B, C**, take the **person of A**,
the **action of B** and the **object of C**:
- 7♥ = Einstein / writing on a blackboard / chalk
- 2♣ = a chef / flipping / frying pan
- K♠ = a knight / jousting / lance
Sequence 7♥ 2♣ K♠ → *Einstein flipping a lance*. One image, three cards. A deck becomes
17 images plus one leftover card: about 18 palace loci.

## 1. Choose the mapping
- **Cards:** derive each card's letters from rank and suit (e.g. suit → a letter, rank →
  a letter or Major System sound), then pick a person whose initials or name match.
  Many systems exist; any consistent one works.
- **Digits (00–99):** start from your Major System words (see major-system) and pick a
  person whose name fits the sounds, or use initials (1=A, 2=B…: 23 → "BC" → a person
  with those initials).
- Person: someone you can picture instantly — friends, family, celebrities, characters.
- **Action and object must be unique to that person** and visually distinct from all
  others (avoid three people who all "throw" something).

## 2. Write the table
A spreadsheet with columns: item, person, action, object, notes. Fill 52 rows (cards) or
100 rows (digits). Expect several sessions; revise weak rows as you test.

## 3. Learn the table (staged)
1. Item → person, until instant.
2. Item → action, item → object.
3. Mixed: shown an item and a role (P, A or O), give the answer.
Flashcard software with spaced repetition (e.g. Anki) suits this; check current features.

## 4. Training progression (rule-of-thumb targets)
1. **Conversion drill:** flip through a deck naming the three-card image, no memorising;
   time it. Aim to halve the time over a few weeks.
2. **Half deck in a palace**, untimed; recall and check.
3. **Full deck**, timed; record memorisation time and errors.
4. Then digits: 24, 48, 96 digits in a palace.
Log every session (date, time, errors). Error-free beats fast; speed comes from
conversion fluency, so drill that most.

## 5. Common errors and fixes
- **Swapped order within a triple** → keep P-A-O order strict and the image scene
  coherent.
- **Two images merging** at one locus → vary the palace or make loci more distinct.
- **Blank locus** → usually a weak person; rebuild that row.
- **Ghosting** from yesterday's deck → rotate palaces.

## Variants
- **PA / PO systems** use two parts (two cards per image): fewer associations to learn,
  more loci needed.
- **Larger systems** (e.g. 1,000 digit-images or two-card systems with 2,704 images) are
  used by top competitors; huge up-front cost.

## Gotchas
- **Building the system too fast** with weak, generic actions ("holding", "standing").
- **Changing the table mid-training** — every change costs re-learning; settle it early.
- **Skipping the palace** — PAO compresses images but doesn't store order.
- Time figures for "good" performance vary widely; compare with your own log, not
  others' records.

## Verify
- [ ] Every item (52 or 100) has a unique person, action and object written down.
- [ ] Any item → P, A or O in under ~1 second.
- [ ] Three random cards convert into one coherent image.
- [ ] A full deck is recalled error-free in a palace (untimed first).
- [ ] Sessions are logged with time and errors.
