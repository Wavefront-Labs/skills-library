# Building and using a memory palace (method of loci)

**Goal:** remember an ordered list — a speech's points, a shopping list, the steps of a
procedure, 20 vocabulary words — by placing images along a mental route you already know.
For competition-level use see
sports-recreation.mind-sports-and-games.memory-sports.memory-palaces-pao-and-competition-disciplines.

## 1. Choose the palace
A place you know so well you can walk it in your mind without effort: your home, the
route to work, a school, a relative's house. Familiarity is everything — the locations
must come back on their own.

## 2. Fix the route and loci
- Decide a **fixed direction** (e.g. clockwise through each room) and never vary it.
- Pick distinct **loci** (stations) along it: front door, doormat, coat hook, radiator,
  sofa, TV… Start with **10–20**.
- Make loci **different from each other** (not five similar cupboards) and spaced a few
  steps apart.
- Walk the route mentally twice, then list the loci on paper with numbers. Mark every 5th
  or 10th locus (e.g. "locus 10 is the bath") so you can jump in.

## 3. Turn each item into an image
Convert the item into something concrete and vivid:
- Concrete items: use them directly but exaggerate (a *giant* loaf).
- Abstract items: use a sound-alike or symbol — "democracy" → a ballot box; "Ottawa" →
  an otter.
- Make it **active, exaggerated, unusual, multisensory** — smell, noise, texture, movement.
  Humour and absurdity help.

## 4. Place the image interacting with the locus
Don't just set the image *near* the locus — make them **interact**: the giant loaf is
jammed in the front door, crumbs everywhere, and you have to shove through it. Spend
~5–10 seconds per placement, actually "seeing" it.

## 5. Recall by walking
Close your eyes, start at the door, walk the route, and "look" at each locus. The image
should be waiting there. If a locus is empty, move on and return later — don't stall.

## 6. Review on a schedule
For lasting memory, walk the palace again roughly after 10 minutes, 1 hour, 1 day,
3 days and a week (the exact intervals are a rule of thumb; expanding gaps are what
matter). For a one-off shopping list, one review is enough.

## 7. Reuse, clearing and multiple palaces
- **Short-term use:** reuse a palace after a few days; old images fade ("ghosts").
  Some people imagine clearing each locus before reuse.
- **Long-term knowledge:** dedicate a palace and don't reuse it.
- Build several palaces; rotating between them prevents ghost interference.

## Speeches
Put one *key idea* per locus, not every sentence. The palace gives order and cues;
you speak naturally from each cue.

## Gotchas
- **Unfamiliar route** — if you have to think "what comes after the kitchen?", the palace
  is not ready.
- **Passive images** ("a loaf sitting there") fade; make them do something.
- **Similar loci** cause swaps and skipped items.
- **Too much per locus** — one image (or one tight scene) each, at least at first.
- **Ghosting** when reusing too soon: wait longer or use a fresh palace.
- Accounts differ on review intervals; adjust to what your own recall tests show.

## Verify
- [ ] You can list all loci in order, forwards and backwards, without hesitation.
- [ ] Each item has a vivid, active image interacting with its locus.
- [ ] Recall the full list in order after 10 minutes with no notes.
- [ ] Recall again after one day; repair any empty loci with stronger images.
- [ ] You can jump to locus 10 (or 20) directly.
