# Chaining items with links and stories

**Goal:** remember a sequence — packing list, recipe steps, the order of a presentation —
with no preparation (no peg list or palace needed), by making each item trigger the next.

## Two variants
- **Link method (pairwise):** item 1 interacts with item 2; then forget 1 and make 2
  interact with 3; and so on. Each link is a separate little scene.
- **Story method:** weave all items into one continuous narrative with a plot. Easier to
  hold together, and the plot itself gives extra cues; a bit slower to build.
Try both; many people use links for 5–15 items and stories for anything they need to
retell.

## 1. Make each item imageable
Convert to a concrete picture: "passport" is fine; "insurance" → an umbrella; "call Mum"
→ a phone with a mum-shaped face. Decide the picture *before* linking.

## 2. Build each link (the craft)
Example list: **umbrella, eggs, passport, charger, sunscreen.**
- *Umbrella → eggs:* you open the umbrella and hundreds of eggs fall out and smash on
  your head.
- *Eggs → passport:* a yolk splatters your passport; the photo now has an egg face.
- *Passport → charger:* the passport is plugged into the wall with a charger, its pages
  glowing as it charges.
- *Charger → sunscreen:* the charger cable squirts sunscreen instead of electricity.
Rules that make links hold:
- **Interaction**, not adjacency — one thing *acts on* the other.
- **Exaggeration** — size, number, speed (hundreds of eggs, not one).
- **Absurdity or emotion** — funny, disgusting, painful scenes stick better.
- **Senses** — the crunch, the smell, the sticky feel.
- ~5 seconds of genuinely visualising each link.

## 3. Story version of the same list
"I open my **umbrella** in the airport and a flood of **eggs** pours out; one smashes on
my **passport**, so I dry it by plugging it into a **charger**, which starts squirting
**sunscreen** over the queue." One scene, five items.

## 4. Recall
Start with the first item (anchor it to something you'll certainly remember — the
destination, the task's name, the front door) and follow the chain.

## 5. Repairing a broken chain
The weakness of chaining: lose one link and everything after it goes.
- **Skip forward from the end:** many people remember the last item well (recency); work
  backwards if your links were vivid in both directions.
- **Re-anchor every 5 items:** tie item 5, 10, 15 to the start image or a mini-title
  so you can restart the chain mid-way.
- **Strengthen the weak link** immediately when you find it, then re-run the chain.
- For long lists, switch to a memory palace, where a lost item doesn't break the rest.

## Gotchas
- **Linking two items that are naturally related** (cup and saucer) feels easy but is
  easily confused with other ordinary memories; make it strange anyway.
- **Too-long chains** (30+) are fragile — split into sub-chains or use a palace.
- **Ordering within a link** — make the link point forward (umbrella *causes* eggs)
  so direction is clear.
- **Reusing the same images** across lists causes mix-ups.

## Verify
- [ ] Each item has a concrete image chosen before linking.
- [ ] Every link is an interaction, exaggerated, with at least one sensory detail.
- [ ] You recall the whole list forwards after 10 minutes without notes.
- [ ] You can also recall it backwards (a check that each link is strong).
- [ ] Weak links found on recall were rebuilt.
