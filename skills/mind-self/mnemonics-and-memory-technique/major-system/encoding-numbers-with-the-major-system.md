# Encoding numbers with the Major System

**Goal:** turn numbers — which are abstract and forgettable — into words and images,
so you can remember phone numbers, PINs, dates, prices and long digit strings.

## 1. The code (by sound, not spelling)
| Digit | Sound(s) | Memory hook |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | s, z, soft c | *z*ero starts with z |
| 1 | t, d, th | t has one downstroke |
| 2 | n | n has two downstrokes |
| 3 | m | m has three downstrokes |
| 4 | r | fou*r* ends in r |
| 5 | l | Roman L = 50 |
| 6 | j, sh, ch (as in *cheese*), soft g | a written J looks like a reversed 6 |
| 7 | k, hard c, hard g, q | K can be drawn from two 7s |
| 8 | f, v | script f has two loops like 8 |
| 9 | p, b | p is a mirrored 9 |

**Vowels and w, h, y are free** — they carry no value, so you insert them to make words.
Double letters count once (*butter* = b-t-r = 914, not 9114). Silent letters count
nothing (*knee* = n = 2). It is the **sound** that counts: *phone* = f-n = 82.

Learn the table first; drill it until each digit→sound is instant (a day or two of short
sessions).

## 2. Build a 00–99 word list (one-time investment)
Pick a concrete, imageable noun for every two-digit number:
00 sauce, 01 suit, 02 sun, 07 sock, 10 toes, 12 tin, 21 net, 33 mummy, 41 rat,
52 lion, 64 chair, 71 cat, 85 file, 92 bun, 99 pipe.
Rules for choosing: concrete over abstract, distinct from each other, personally vivid.
Write the full list, then review it daily until you can name any number's word in under
two seconds. Expect this to take one to a few weeks of short practice (an estimate; it
varies a lot by person).

## 3. Encoding a number
Split into pairs and convert, then link the images:
- Phone ending **4152 0712**: 41 rat, 52 lion, 07 sock, 12 tin → a *rat* rides a *lion*
  who wears one *sock* stuffed with *tin* cans.
- For longer strings, place one or two images per locus in a memory palace
  (see memory-palace).
- Odd number of digits: use a single-digit word for the last one (1 tie, 4 ray, 9 bee).

## 4. Dates
- Year: drop or encode the century as context (1066 → 10 *toes* + 66 *judge*:
  a judge stepping on toes at the Battle of Hastings).
- Day/month: encode as numbers, or attach the image to the event's location.

## 5. Practising
- Read car registrations, prices and page numbers and convert them silently.
- Start with 8-digit strings; build to 20, 40, 80.
- Time yourself on the 00–99 list.

## Gotchas
- **Spelling vs sound** — *cent* is s-n-t (021), not c-n-t. Say it aloud if unsure.
- **Abstract words** in your list ("tune" is fine; "noun" is hard to picture) slow you down.
- **Accents differ** — decide whether *r* in non-rhotic speech counts, and be consistent.
- **Similar images** (cat, cot, kite) collide; vary the categories.
- Many published lists differ; yours only needs to be consistent with itself.

## Verify
- [ ] You can give the sound for any digit instantly, and vice versa.
- [ ] Your 00–99 list is written down and each number recalls its word in under ~2 s.
- [ ] You can encode and recall a 10-digit phone number after 5 minutes without notes.
- [ ] You can decode a word back to digits correctly (check double and silent letters).
