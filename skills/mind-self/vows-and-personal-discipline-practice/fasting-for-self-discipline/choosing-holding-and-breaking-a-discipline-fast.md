# Choosing, holding and breaking a discipline fast

**Goal:** use deliberate abstinence from food (or another appetite) to practise restraint —
noticing urges and letting them pass — safely. Fasting as religious observance belongs
with its tradition; fasting for weight loss or metabolic health is a health topic.
Here the point is the practice of not acting on an appetite.

## Who should not fast (or only with a doctor's guidance)
- Anyone with a current or past **eating disorder** — a "discipline" frame can feed it.
- **Diabetes** (especially on insulin or sulfonylureas — hypoglycaemia risk), low blood
  pressure, heart, kidney or liver disease, gout.
- **Pregnant or breastfeeding**, under 18, older adults who are frail, underweight people.
- Anyone on medicines that must be taken with food or affect blood sugar or blood pressure.
If unsure, ask a doctor or pharmacist before starting anything longer than skipping a meal.

## Pick a form — start small
| Form | What it is | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| Skipped meal | one meal, water/tea allowed | first practice |
| Sunrise-to-sunset | no food in daylight, eat in the evening | a single day of discipline |
| 24-hour | dinner to dinner | after several easier ones go smoothly |
| Partial / appetite fast | no sugar, no snacking, no caffeine, no alcohol for a set period | daily-life restraint, lower medical risk |
| Non-food fast | no shopping, no news, no complaining for a day | when food fasting isn't suitable |
Fasts **beyond 24–36 hours** are outside a self-discipline practice: they carry real risks
(including **refeeding syndrome** after prolonged fasting) and need medical supervision.
Water should almost always be allowed; dry fasting is risky for dehydration.

## Before
Decide the form, start and end time, and what's allowed (water, black tea, coffee) in
writing. Choose a day without heavy physical work, driving long distances, or important
exams. Tell someone you live with.

## During: working with hunger
- Hunger comes in **waves** that peak and ease within roughly 15–30 minutes, often at
  habitual meal times. Notice the wave, name it ("hunger, rising"), breathe, and watch it
  pass — this is the discipline itself (**urge surfing**).
- Keep a short log: time, urge strength 1–10, what you did. Patterns teach more than the
  fast does.
- Keep busy but gentle: walks, reading, a task. Avoid food shopping and cooking for others
  where possible on early attempts.
- Expect irritability and low focus; plan around them rather than test yourself.

## Stop rules — end the fast at once if
Dizziness or faintness, palpitations, confusion, severe headache, shakiness or sweating
(possible low blood sugar), vomiting, or simply feeling unwell. Ending a fast for safety is
not a failure of discipline; ignoring a warning sign is.

## Breaking it well
Break with something small and easy: water, then a modest meal (soup, fruit, yoghurt,
some bread). Avoid a huge or very rich meal and alcohol. After short fasts, ordinary food
within an hour is fine. Bingeing after a fast is a sign the fast was too long or too strict.

## Carryover
Afterwards, write one sentence about what you noticed — how urges rose and passed, what
cues triggered them. The value is applying that: pausing before a snack, a purchase, an
angry reply.

## Gotchas
- **Frequency creep:** fasting more and more often, feeling guilty when eating, or hiding
  it are warning signs of disordered eating — stop and talk to a professional.
- **Moralising food** ("I was good today") turns discipline into shame; keep the frame on
  attention, not virtue.
- **Caffeine withdrawal** headaches on a no-caffeine fast are common; taper first.

## Verify
- [ ] Contraindications checked; doctor consulted if any apply.
- [ ] Form, duration and allowances written down beforehand; ≤ 24 h unless supervised.
- [ ] Stop rules known; fast ended if one occurred.
- [ ] Broken gently; urge log reviewed and one lesson written.
