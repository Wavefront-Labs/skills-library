# Drafting, living and reviewing a personal rule of life

**Goal:** turn what you value into a short written set of commitments that shapes your
days, weeks and seasons — a "rule" in the old monastic sense of a measure or trellis,
not a list of prohibitions — and keep it alive by reviewing it on a schedule. Religious
rules taken within a tradition belong with that tradition; this is the secular craft.

## 1. Start from values, not habits
Write 3–5 things you want your life to be *for* (e.g. health, close relationships, craft,
learning, service). For each, ask: "what's the smallest regular practice that would make
this real?" Commitments that don't trace to a value tend to be the first ones dropped.

## 2. Write commitments as floors
A rule fails when it describes your best week. Write the **floor** — what you'll do on a
bad week — and treat anything above as a bonus.
- Too high: "Exercise for an hour every day."
- Floor: "Move for 20 minutes on five days a week; walk on the other two."
Make each one **observable** (you can say yes/no at day's end) and give it a **when/where**
cue: "after coffee, at the kitchen table, 10 minutes of reading."

## 3. Layer by rhythm
| Layer | Examples | Count |
|---|---|---|
| Daily | wake time, a quiet 10 minutes, no phone at meals | 3–5 max |
| Weekly | one full rest day, call a family member, review the week | 2–4 |
| Monthly/seasonal | a day alone in nature, a decluttering pass, a retreat day | 1–3 |
| Standing principles | "I don't buy on the same day I want it", "I answer letters within a week" | a few |
Ten to fifteen total commitments is a practical upper limit; most good first rules have fewer.

## 4. One page, visible
Fit it on one page under value headings. Put it where you'll see it (inside a notebook
cover, a pinned note). A rule you have to look up isn't shaping the day.

## 5. Living it
- **Track lightly** for the first month: a tick per commitment per day. Patterns of misses
  are information, not failure.
- **Expect seasons.** Illness, a new baby, exam weeks: declare a temporary "travel rule"
  (a reduced core of 2–3 floors) rather than abandoning the whole thing.
- **Accountability:** sharing it with one trusted person helps; a group can help more but
  can also turn it into performance.

## 6. Review cycle
- **Weekly (5 min):** what held, what didn't, any obstacle to solve.
- **Quarterly (30–60 min):** revise wording; drop commitments that have become automatic
  (they're habits now) or that no longer serve a value; promote one new one.
- **Annually:** reread your values and rewrite the rule from scratch, then compare.
Change the rule in review, not in the moment. Mid-week edits are usually rationalisation.

## Gotchas
- **Perfectionism:** a rule kept 80% of the time is working. Treat a miss as a reason to
  return, not to restart from zero.
- **Too many prohibitions** make it brittle; frame mostly as practices to do.
- **Copying someone else's rule** wholesale skips the values step and rarely sticks.
- **Rigidity harming others:** if the rule is crowding out relationships or rest, the
  rule is wrong, not you. Compulsive rigidity that causes distress is worth discussing
  with a professional.

## Verify
- [ ] Every commitment traces to a written value.
- [ ] Each is a floor, observable, and has a when/where cue.
- [ ] Fits on one page and is somewhere you see daily.
- [ ] Weekly and quarterly reviews are in your calendar.
- [ ] A reduced "travel rule" exists for hard seasons.
