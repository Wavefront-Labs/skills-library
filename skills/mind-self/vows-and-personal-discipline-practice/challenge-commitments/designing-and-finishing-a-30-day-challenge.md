# Designing and finishing a 30-day challenge

**Goal:** use a time-boxed challenge — 30 days of something, a dry month, a no-spend
month — as a discipline tool, finish it, and keep the useful part afterwards.

## 1. Pick one challenge
One at a time. Good candidates are **small enough to do on your worst day** and
meaningful enough that the end result matters: 10 minutes of drawing daily, no alcohol
for a month, 8,000 steps, no takeaway food. Avoid stacking ("no sugar, run daily and
wake at 5") — the failure of one tends to take the rest down.

## 2. Define success tightly (write it)
- **The daily rule:** exactly what counts. "Write 300 words" or "write for 15 minutes" —
  not "write".
- **Dates:** start and finish dates on the calendar. Starting on the 1st of a month is a
  convenient fresh-start cue.
- **Edge cases:** travel days, illness, social events. Decide now — e.g. "on travel days,
  the floor is 5 minutes"; "a sip of a toast counts as a break".
- **Miss policy:** pick one before starting. Strict (any miss ends it), or the more
  forgiving **never miss twice** (a single miss is allowed; two in a row ends it). The
  forgiving rule is usually better for building habits.

## 3. Set up for the days
- **When/where cue:** "after I brush my teeth at night, I stretch for 10 minutes on the
  bedroom floor."
- **Remove friction / add friction:** lay out the gear; or clear the alcohol from the house.
- **Tracker:** a paper calendar with an X per day ("don't break the chain") placed where
  you see it.
- **A partner or public pledge** helps many people; a group doing the same challenge helps
  more.

## 4. Survive the middle
Week one runs on novelty; **days ~10–20 are where most challenges die** — novelty gone,
benefits not obvious. Plan for it:
- Pre-schedule a small reward or check-in at day 15.
- Shrink to the floor on bad days rather than skip.
- Re-read your written reason.
- Look at the row of Xs — the streak is genuinely motivating.

## 5. Harvest at the end
On the final day, review in writing:
1. Did I meet the rule? How many days?
2. What changed — energy, sleep, money, skill, mood?
3. What was hardest, and what made it easier?
4. **What will I keep?** Turn the challenge into an ongoing, smaller standard: "drink
   only at weekends", "draw three times a week". Write it as a rule with a cue.
Don't simply stop and revert — or immediately start a harder challenge. Give the
harvested habit a few weeks first. Habits typically take longer than 30 days to become
automatic (one study found a wide range, median around two months), so the end of the
challenge is the start of the habit, not proof of it.

## Gotchas
- **Rebound:** finishing a dry or no-spend month with a binge undoes much of it; plan the
  first day after.
- **All-or-nothing collapse** after a miss — that's what the miss policy is for.
- **Health-related challenges:** a "no alcohol" month is safe for most people, but heavy
  daily drinkers can get dangerous withdrawal (shaking, sweating, seizures) on stopping
  suddenly — see a doctor first. Intense exercise or diet challenges also warrant medical
  advice if you have health conditions.

## Verify
- [ ] One challenge; written daily rule, dates, edge cases and miss policy.
- [ ] A when/where cue and a visible tracker.
- [ ] A plan for days 10–20.
- [ ] A written end-of-challenge review with one kept habit.
