# Designing a digital detox and a re-entry policy

**Goal:** step away from chosen devices or platforms for a set period, and come back
with a written usage policy — so the detox changes behaviour instead of being a
holiday followed by a rebound.

## 1. Baseline (3–7 days before)
Read your real numbers: iPhone *Settings › Screen Time › See All App & Website Activity*;
Android *Settings › Digital Wellbeing & parental controls* (names vary by manufacturer).
Note daily total, pickups, top 5 apps, and when use spikes (bed, waking, queues, work
breaks). Write down what each top app is *for* to you: contact, news, boredom relief.

## 2. Define the fast
Choose scope and length deliberately:
| Format | Example |
|---|---|
| Digital sabbath | one day a week, phone off sunset to sunset |
| Platform fast | no social media/short video for 30 days |
| Evening fast | all screens off 21:00–07:00 |
| Full detox | phone on calls/texts only for a week, no laptop outside work |
Write: what's off, what's still allowed (maps, banking, messaging with family, work
tools), and the end date. Thirty days is a common length for a platform fast — long enough
for the urge to check to fade. *(Draft: commonly cited, not an evidence threshold.)*

## 3. Logistics
- **Tell people** who will notice (family, group chats) and set an auto-reply if needed.
- **Plan the essentials** you'd otherwise lose: alarm clock, offline maps or a paper map,
  music player, 2FA codes (keep a backup method — don't lock yourself out of accounts).
- **Remove, don't rely on willpower:** log out, delete apps from the phone, move them off
  the home screen; set Screen Time/Digital Wellbeing limits with a passcode someone else
  holds. Grayscale mode makes feeds markedly less appealing.
- **Change the environment:** charge the phone outside the bedroom; keep a book where the
  phone used to sit.

## 4. Substitutes
The detox fails in the empty moments the phone used to fill. Write a menu: for queues
(a pocket book, just looking around), for evenings (a hobby, a walk, a call), for
boredom (accept it — boredom is part of what you're retraining). Schedule at least one
offline activity you look forward to each week.

## 5. During
Note urges in a small notebook: time, trigger, what you did. Lapses happen — usually
on autopilot. Close the app and carry on; don't scrap the detox.

## 6. Re-entry: rebuild deliberately
At the end, don't reinstall everything. For each app, decide if it earns a place and write
a rule:
- **Purpose:** "Instagram to post my work, not to scroll."
- **When/where:** "desktop only", "after 18:00", "15 minutes, timer on".
- **How:** notifications off except people; no apps in bed; one news check a day.
Re-add one app at a time, a few days apart, and compare screen time to your baseline after
two and four weeks.

## Gotchas
- **Work or care obligations:** if someone may need to reach you urgently, keep calls
  on — a detox shouldn't create a safety gap.
- **Swapping one feed for another** (from Instagram to news sites) is common; your
  baseline numbers will show it.
- **"Dopamine fasting"** claims about resetting brain chemistry are pop-science; the
  practical value is breaking cue-response habits.
- If use is seriously affecting sleep, work, relationships or mood and you can't cut it,
  that may warrant talking to a GP or therapist.

## Verify
- [ ] Baseline numbers recorded before starting.
- [ ] Fast scope, allowed exceptions and end date written down.
- [ ] Essentials (alarm, maps, 2FA) covered without the banned apps.
- [ ] Substitute menu exists; urges logged.
- [ ] Each returning app has a written purpose/when/how rule; usage rechecked at 2 and 4 weeks.
