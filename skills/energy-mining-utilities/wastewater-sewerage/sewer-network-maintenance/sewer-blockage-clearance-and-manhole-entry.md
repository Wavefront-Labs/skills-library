# Sewer blockage clearance and manhole entry

**Goal:** get sewage flowing again quickly, from the surface where possible, and if anyone
must enter a manhole, do it so everyone comes back out.

## 1. Locate the blockage
1. Lift manhole covers along the line (lifting keys, good posture, two people on heavy
   covers). **Surcharged** (full) chamber with a **dry or low** chamber downstream: the
   blockage is between them.
2. Note what's coming out — fat, wipes, roots, collapsed pipe debris — it decides the tool.
3. Protect the public around open chambers (barriers, cones, no one leaning in).

## 2. Clear from the surface first
- **Work from the downstream (dry) chamber upstream** where possible, so released
  sewage drains away from you and doesn't flood the chamber you're working at.
- **Rods:** screw-joint rods, always turn **clockwise** while working — turning
  anticlockwise unscrews them and leaves rods in the pipe. Plunger or corkscrew for soft
  blockages.
- **Jetting:** usually faster for fat and silt; see the jetting skill
  (`energy-mining-utilities.wastewater-sewerage.jetting-vacuum-units.jetting-nozzle-selection-and-vacuum-tanker-operation`) for nozzle choice.
- **Roots:** root cutter on jetter or mechanical cutter, then CCTV — roots return through a
  defective joint, which needs repair.
- Once flowing, flush and, if repeated, **CCTV** the line to find the cause.

## 3. Is entry really needed?
Most blockages never need entry. Enter only if the job cannot be done from the surface
(e.g. freeing a stuck tool, manual work on benching, valve operation). Entry needs a
**permit / safe system of work** and trained, equipped people.

## 4. Confined-space entry essentials
1. **Atmosphere testing before entry and continuously during:** calibrated multi-gas
   monitor (O2, LEL, H2S, CO), bump-tested that day. Test at top, middle and bottom —
   H2S is heavier than air and collects low; methane is lighter.
   Typical limits: O2 19.5–23.5%; LEL < 10%; H2S below workplace exposure limits (very low
   ppm). Above limits = no entry.
2. **Ventilate** with forced air (blower ducted to the bottom), not just by leaving the
   cover off.
3. **Isolate** flows where possible (bungs/over-pumping) and know upstream pump station
   schedules and the weather — rain can fill a sewer in minutes.
4. **Top person** stays at the surface for the whole entry with comms, the entrant on a
   harness to a tripod and winch. Rescue plan in place before entry; would-be rescuers who
   enter without equipment are a leading cause of confined-space deaths.
5. Escape set (self-rescue breathing apparatus) worn by entrants per your procedures.
6. Exit immediately on any alarm, change in weather, or feeling unwell.

## 5. Hygiene
Gloves, eye protection, no eating/smoking until washed. Leptospirosis (Weil's disease) and
gastro infections are real risks — report flu-like illness after sewer work.

## Gotchas
- A clear chamber at the top can be lethal at the bottom; testing only at the cover is not testing.
- Disturbing sludge releases H2S suddenly — keep monitoring during work.
- H2S at high concentration deadens the sense of smell; "it doesn't smell now" is a warning.

## Verify
- [ ] Blockage position confirmed from chamber levels before clearing.
- [ ] Rods turned only clockwise; all rods recovered.
- [ ] Entry only with permit, tested/ventilated atmosphere, top person, rescue kit.
- [ ] Cause found (CCTV) for any repeat blockage; record updated.

*Uncertain:* gas thresholds, permit requirements and training standards differ by
jurisdiction — use your employer's confined-space procedure.
