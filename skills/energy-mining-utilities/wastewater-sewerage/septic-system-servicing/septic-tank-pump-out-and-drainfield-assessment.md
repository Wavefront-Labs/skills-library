# Septic tank pump-out and drainfield assessment

**Goal:** remove accumulated solids before they reach the drainfield, and tell the owner
honestly whether the system is working, needs repair, or is failing.

## 1. Before pumping
1. Locate tank and lids (as-built drawing, probe, follow the outlet pipe from the house).
   Many tanks have two compartments — both need pumping.
2. **Measure layers** before pumping: a sludge judge or measuring stick through the outlet
   side. Typical guidance: pump when the scum bottom is within ~7–8 cm (3 in) of the outlet
   baffle bottom, or sludge top within ~30 cm (12 in) of it, or combined solids exceed
   ~25–33% of tank depth.
3. Note the **liquid level**: above the outlet invert = outlet or drainfield restriction;
   well below = leaking tank.

## 2. Pumping
- Pump through the **main access (manway)**, not just the inspection port over a baffle,
  so you can break up scum and remove all of it.
- Break the scum mat and mix sludge with the hose/back-flushing so solids come out, not
  just liquid. Leave a few centimetres of residual — don't wash or "start" the tank with
  additives; bacteria repopulate from use.
- Don't pump out a tank in high groundwater without assessing flotation risk (plastic and
  fibreglass tanks can pop out of the ground when empty).
- Record gallons/litres removed and dispose of septage only at an authorised site.

## 3. Inspect while open
- **Inlet baffle/tee:** present, intact, not blocked. Missing inlet baffle → scum blocks
  the house sewer.
- **Outlet baffle/tee and effluent filter:** missing outlet baffle lets solids flow into the
  drainfield — the most damaging defect. Pull and hose the effluent filter (back into the
  tank inlet side), refit.
- Tank walls and lids for cracks, corrosion (concrete above liquid line attacked by H2S),
  roots, unsafe lids (a collapsed or unsecured lid is a fatal fall risk — secure it).
- Backflow from the outlet pipe when the tank is pumped = drainfield saturated.

## 4. Assess the drainfield
- Walk the field: ponding, soggy ground, lush strips of grass over trenches, odour,
  effluent surfacing.
- Distribution box: level, even flow to all lines, no solids.
- Inspection ports/observation tubes: standing liquid depth in trenches. Permanent ponding
  near the top = biomat clogging or hydraulic overload.
- Ask about water use: leaking toilets, new occupants, water softener backwash and
  sump pumps routed to the septic all overload it.

Classify findings: **functioning**, **functioning with maintenance needs** (missing baffle,
filter), **failing** (surfacing effluent, sewage backups not caused by a blockage).
Surface effluent is a public health issue — many authorities require it to be reported.

## Gotchas
- Never enter a septic tank; the atmosphere can kill in breaths. All work is from above.
- Pumping doesn't fix a failing drainfield; it buys days.
- Driving the truck over the drainfield crushes pipes and compacts soil.

## Verify
- [ ] Layers measured and recorded before pumping; all compartments pumped.
- [ ] Baffles present; effluent filter cleaned; lids secured.
- [ ] Liquid level relationship to outlet noted.
- [ ] Drainfield signs checked and classified; owner given written findings.
- [ ] Septage disposed of at an authorised facility with records.

*Uncertain:* pump-out thresholds and reporting rules vary by state/country; follow your
local onsite wastewater regulations.
