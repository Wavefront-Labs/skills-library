# Jetting nozzle selection and vacuum tanker operation

**Goal:** clean a sewer or drain efficiently with water jetting, recover the debris with the
vacuum side, and get rid of the load legally — without injuring yourself or blowing
sewage into someone's house.

## 1. Pressure vs flow
Pressure cuts; **flow carries**. Sewer jetting on combination units typically runs high
flow at moderate pressure (e.g. hundreds of L/min at ~100–200 bar); drain jetters lower
flow. To move debris down a large pipe you need flow; to cut fat or roots you need
pressure concentrated in the right jets.

## 2. Choosing the nozzle
- **Penetrating (forward jet) nozzle:** for a complete blockage — opens a path so water can
  flow. Don't use as a cleaning nozzle; it doesn't scour walls.
- **Standard cleaning nozzle (rear jets ~ 15–30°):** propels the hose and scours debris
  back towards the manhole. Shallower jet angle = more thrust and pulling power.
- **Rotating/spinning nozzle:** for fat, grease and scale — even coverage of the pipe wall.
- **Heavy "bomb"/grenade nozzle:** large pipes and heavy silt.
- **Root cutter / chain flail:** roots and hard deposits; follow with CCTV.
Match nozzle orifice size to the pump's flow at your working pressure (the nozzle chart),
otherwise you'll get either low pressure or the relief valve dumping flow.

## 3. Jetting technique
1. Set up at the **downstream** manhole and jet **upstream**, pulling debris back towards
   you to be vacuumed out. Exception: penetrating a blockage may need an upstream approach.
2. Fit a **hose guide/tiger tail** at the pipe entry so the hose doesn't cut the pipe
   or chamber.
3. Advance the nozzle **before** turning pressure up; never have pressure on while the
   nozzle is out of the pipe — a nozzle can reverse back out of the pipe and whip
   (mark the hose ~ 1–2 m from the nozzle so you know when it's close).
4. Work in passes: send up, retrieve slowly to pull debris, vacuum, repeat until clean.
5. Watch for **back-pressure** in connected properties — jetting a blocked lateral can push
   sewage up through toilets and sinks; warn residents, check WCs with low water seals.

## 4. Vacuum loading
- Position the boom so it doesn't contact overhead lines (clearance rules are strict).
- Keep the intake just above debris, not buried; too much air breaks vacuum transport,
  buried intake chokes.
- Watch tank fill level and the float/shut-off; overfill sends liquid into the vacuum pump.
- Combination units with water recycling need screens checked — grit wrecks the jetting pump.

## 5. Discharge
Discharge only at an authorised point (treatment works reception, licensed waste site) with
the right waste transfer documentation. Never decant into a road gully or watercourse.
Grit/rag loads often go to skip, not sewer.

## 6. Safety
- High-pressure water penetrates skin: a jetting injection injury is a medical emergency
  even if it looks small — tell the hospital it's a high-pressure injection injury.
- PPE per jetting code: face shield, waterproofs, gloves, safety boots; dead-man control
  where fitted. Never point a lance or nozzle at anyone.
- Frost: drain the system after use or it splits pumps and hoses.

## Verify
- [ ] Nozzle matches job and pump flow chart.
- [ ] Working upstream from downstream manhole with hose guide fitted.
- [ ] Pressure off whenever nozzle is near or outside the pipe end.
- [ ] Residents warned about back-pressure where laterals connect.
- [ ] Load discharged at an authorised point with paperwork.

*Uncertain:* pressure/flow ranges vary widely by unit; follow your machine and your national
jetting code of practice.
