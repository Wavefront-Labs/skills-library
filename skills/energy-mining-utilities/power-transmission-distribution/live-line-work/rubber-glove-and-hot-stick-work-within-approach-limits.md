# Rubber-glove and hot-stick work within approach limits

**Goal:** do energised work on distribution lines with two independent layers of
protection at every moment, so one mistake is never fatal. This is a specialist
authorisation: nothing here replaces your employer's live-work procedures, your
competency sign-off or the job-specific plan. De-energised methods are in
`energy-mining-utilities.power-transmission-distribution.overhead-line-work.conductor-repair-and-insulator-replacement-on-isolated-lines`.

## 1. Pick the method
- **Hot stick:** you stay outside the minimum approach distance (MAD) and work through
  insulated sticks. Slower, but your body never enters the danger zone.
- **Rubber glove:** you work in contact with live parts wearing rated gloves and
  sleeves, from an insulated aerial device, with everything else in reach covered.
  Typically used up to the middle distribution voltages (practice varies by utility).
- **Barehand:** the worker is bonded to the conductor and at line potential, isolated
  from earth by an insulated boom. Transmission-level; separate training. Not covered here.

## 2. Minimum approach distances
MAD is set by voltage and is the distance any uninsulated part of you or conductive
tool must stay from a live part you're not insulated from. US values from OSHA 29 CFR 1910.269 Table R-6 (alternative distances for 72.5 kV and less, AC, 
worksite at or below 900 m / 3,000 ft), phase-to-ground / phase-to-phase: 
0.301-0.750 kV 0.33 m / 0.33 m; 0.751-5.0 kV 0.63 m / 0.63 m; 5.1-15.0 kV 0.65 m / 0.68 m; 
15.1-36.0 kV 0.77 m / 0.89 m; 36.1-46.0 kV 0.84 m / 0.98 m; 46.1-72.5 kV 1.00 m / 1.20 m. Above 900 m the distances 
are multiplied by an altitude correction factor, and employers may instead calculate distances 
from the standard's formula tables. **Other jurisdictions and later amendments differ: confirm the 
table your employer's procedure adopts, for your voltage, altitude and exposure.** The OSHA values already include an inadvertent-movement component
(M = 0.31 m or 0.61 m in the formula), so do not add it twice, but do not plan to work at the minimum either: arms swing, booms bounce.

## 3. Rubber goods
Glove classes (ASTM D120 classes as tabled in 29 CFR 1910.137, max use voltage AC rms): **00 = 500 V, 0 = 1 kV, 1 = 7.5 kV,
2 = 17 kV, 3 = 26.5 kV, 4 = 36 kV.** Use a class rated at or above the
phase-to-phase voltage your procedure specifies.
- **Air test before every use:** roll the cuff to trap air, squeeze, listen and feel
  for leaks, inspect inside and out for cuts, ozone cracking, embedded wire.
  Turn them inside out to look at the palm.
- Always wear **leather protectors** over the rubber (and never wear protectors alone).
- Under 29 CFR 1910.137, electrical retest is before first issue and every 6 months for gloves,
  every 12 months for sleeves and blankets; check the date stamp. Out of date = out of service.
- Store flat or cuff-down, out of sunlight, never folded or with tools inside.

## 4. Cover-up discipline
1. From the bucket, before you move in, identify every live and earthed part within reach.
2. **Cover in order: closest first**, working outward (line hose on conductors,
   blankets and hoods on hardware, covers on earthed crossarm braces and neutrals too —
   phase-to-ground contact kills as easily as phase-to-phase).
3. Uncover in **exact reverse order**.
4. Only expose one phase at a time where possible; never reach between two
   different potentials.

## 5. System protection
- Ask control to **block auto-reclose** (non-reclose / hot-line tag) on the feeding
  breaker or recloser before work starts. If something faults, it trips once and stays
  out instead of re-energising onto you.
- Keep radio contact with the controller; agree who can lift the block.

## 6. Hot-stick care
- Wipe with a silicone cloth before use; inspect for cracks, delamination,
  contamination. A wet or dirty stick is a conductor.
- Keep in a dry bag. Under 29 CFR 1910.269(j) live-line tools used for primary protection come out of
  service every 2 years, and whenever required after damage, for examination, cleaning, repair and testing.

## 7. Stop conditions
Stop and make safe if: rain, fog or high humidity sets in, thunder is heard, wind
makes the boom unstable, the rubber or a stick gets damaged, the crew loses
communication, or anyone feels rushed or unsure. Live work is never an emergency.

## Verify
- [ ] Method chosen and MAD taken from the adopted table for this voltage and altitude, with a working margin above it.
- [ ] Gloves are the right class, in test date, and air-tested today; protectors on.
- [ ] Reclose blocked and confirmed by control before touching.
- [ ] Cover-up closest-first, including earthed parts; removed in reverse.
- [ ] Sticks clean, dry and inspected; stop conditions briefed.
