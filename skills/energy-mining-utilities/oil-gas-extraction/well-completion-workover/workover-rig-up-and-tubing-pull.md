# Workover rig-up and tubing pull

**Goal:** safely open a producing well, pull and replace its completion components, and
return it to production without losing control or damaging the formation.

> Status: draft. General well-servicing practice; barrier requirements, pressure test
> values and procedures come from the operator's well programme, the rig contractor and
> local regulations.

## 1. Planning the job
- Read the well file: current completion diagram (tubing size, packer type and depth,
  nipples, rods/pump), last pressures, fluids (H₂S?), and the reason for the workover.
- Agree the **barrier plan**: most operators require **two independent, tested
  barriers** between the reservoir and surface at all times (e.g. kill fluid column +
  BOP, or a plug + tree).

## 2. Kill the well
- Pump **kill fluid** (brine or mud) of a density giving a hydrostatic above reservoir
  pressure with the required margin. Methods: bullhead down the tubing into the
  formation, or circulate via a sliding sleeve/ports.
- Watch for losses; a well that won't stay full may need a fluid-loss pill. Monitor
  for flow before removing the tree.
- Use clean, filtered, compatible fluid — dirty or incompatible brine damages the
  formation (skin) and costs production long after the rig leaves.

## 3. Swap tree for BOP
- With barriers confirmed (well dead and flow checked, plus back-pressure valve or
  plug in the hanger where used), **nipple down** the Christmas tree and **nipple up** the
  BOP stack.
- **Pressure test** the BOP and connections (low then high) to the programme values and
  record the charts. No test, no operations.

## 4. Pull the completion
- Unseat the packer per its type: straight pull, rotation, or cut/mill for permanent
  packers. Know the shear values before pulling; don't exceed tubing or rig limits.
- Expect swabbing when pulling packers and large tools — pull slowly through the first
  joints and keep the hole full.
- **Tubing tally:** measure and number every joint; inspect for corrosion, pitting,
  scale, holes and wear (rod wear in pumped wells). Rejects are laid down and
  replaced. Collect scale or debris samples for chemistry.
- Rod-pumped wells: pull rods first, then tubing; inspect rod couplings and pump.

## 5. Rerun and return to production
- Run the new completion to the tally, drifting tubing where specified; set packer and
  pressure test the annulus.
- Nipple down BOP, nipple up tree, test tree, then unload the kill fluid (swab, gas lift
  or nitrogen) to bring the well on.

## 6. Perforating and stimulation support (safety points)
- Perforating guns contain explosives: radio silence/radio-frequency controls as the
  service company requires, only authorised people at the wellhead, and gun arming
  away from personnel. Treat a gun that didn't fire as live.
- Frac and acid jobs: stay out of the high-pressure line zone, verify iron is tested
  and restrained, and watch the pressure limits on the wellhead and casing.

## Gotchas
- H₂S wells: monitors, escape packs and buddy system; kill fluid can still release
  gas when the packer unseats.
- A stuck packer pulled too hard can part the tubing — then you're fishing.
- Paperwork matters: an inaccurate completion diagram is the root cause of many
  workover surprises.

## Verify
- [ ] Two tested barriers in place before removing the tree.
- [ ] BOP pressure tested and recorded.
- [ ] Tubing tallied and inspected; rejects documented.
- [ ] New completion run to tally and annulus tested.
- [ ] Well returned to production and the completion diagram updated.
