# Tripping pipe and managing the hole

**Goal:** pull out of and run into the hole without taking a kick, without damaging
the hole, and without hurting anyone on the floor.

> Status: draft. General practice; trip speeds, fill rules and floor procedures are set
> by the drilling programme, the contractor's procedures and well control standards
> (e.g. IWCF/IADC). Related: energy-mining-utilities.oil-gas-extraction.well-control.detecting-a-kick-and-shutting-in.

## 1. Roles
- **Driller:** controls drawworks, pumps and rotary; watches the instruments and trip tank.
- **Derrickhand:** racks stands in the fingerboard from the monkeyboard (or the
  pipe-handling system does), and often looks after the mud.
- **Floorhands/roughnecks:** set slips, run tongs or the iron roughneck, make up and
  break out connections.
- **Toolpusher/rig manager:** supervises, approves the trip plan.

## 2. Before the trip
- Circulate the hole clean (bottoms-up or more) so cuttings aren't left to pack off.
- Do a **flow check** with pumps off: watch the well for the set time; no flow before
  you start pulling.
- Pump a **slug** (heavier mud) if pulling dry pipe is planned, so the pipe comes out
  empty and the floor stays clean.
- Fill out the **trip sheet**: expected displacement per stand for the pipe and BHA.

## 3. Pulling out of hole (POOH)
- The hole loses volume equal to the steel removed; it must be **filled** from the trip
  tank to keep hydrostatic pressure. Compare actual fill against calculated after
  every few stands (or continuously with a circulating trip tank).
- **Hole taking less fill than calculated** = formation fluid may be entering (a kick)
  → stop, flow check, follow well control procedure.
- **Swabbing:** pulling too fast reduces bottom-hole pressure and can suck fluid in.
  Respect the planned trip speed, especially through tight spots and with balled bits
  or large BHAs.
- Tight spots / overpull beyond the agreed margin: don't just pull harder. Work the pipe
  per the plan; back-ream if required.

## 4. Running in hole (RIH)
- **Surge:** running too fast pushes pressure into the formation and can cause losses.
  Watch the trip tank for **returns greater than** the pipe displacement (gain) or less
  (losses).
- Slow down at casing shoes, liner tops, BHA changes and known ledges.
- Fill pipe periodically if running with a float.

## 5. Floor work
- Slips set only when the pipe is stationary; never ride the elevators; hands off
  pipe when it's moving. Keep out of tong swing paths and the line of fire of
  hanging loads.
- Make up connections to the specified torque for the connection type; dope threads per
  procedure. Incorrect torque causes washouts and twist-offs.
- Drop objects: secure tools in the derrick, use lanyards, and clear the red zone when
  anything is working overhead.

## 6. Casing and cement support
Running casing follows the same fill/displacement logic with different numbers; fill
the casing as specified, watch for losses, and pre-plan the cement job hand-offs.

## Gotchas
- Trip tank discrepancies are the earliest kick indicator you'll get. Act on them.
- Connection time is risk time — most floor injuries happen during repetitive tasks.
- Pulling wet pipe floods the floor and hides slip hazards; slug or use a mud bucket.

## Verify
- [ ] Hole circulated clean and flow-checked before pulling.
- [ ] Trip sheet filled, actual vs calculated fill compared regularly.
- [ ] Trip speeds held within swab/surge limits.
- [ ] Connections made up to specified torque.
- [ ] Floor red zones observed; dropped-object controls in place.
