# Operating an LHD and a mine haul truck

**Goal:** move broken rock efficiently with mobile plant while avoiding the main killers:
collisions, runaways, falls of ground at drawpoints, tyre events and machine fires.

> Controls, speeds and limits are machine- and site-specific; your OEM
> manual, site traffic management plan and operator assessment govern.

## 1. Pre-start (every shift)
- Walkaround: tyres (cuts, bulges, pressure if the site measures it), leaks, hoses
  rubbing, articulation pin and steering, bucket/tray damage, lights, beacon, horn,
  reversing alarm, mirrors/cameras.
- Fire suppression system: gauge in range, actuators accessible, nozzles uncovered.
  Clean off fuel-soaked dust around turbos and exhausts — the usual ignition point.
- Test brakes (service, park, emergency) on a safe grade per procedure.
- Record defects; a machine with a brake or steering defect is tagged out, not "driven
  carefully".

## 2. LHD bogging technique
- Approach the muckpile square, bucket flat and just above the floor. Drive in with
  steady power, crowd the bucket back as it fills, lift slightly to break out. Avoid
  spinning tyres — it is the main source of tyre wear underground.
- Keep the floor clean and level; a rough floor damages tyres and makes the machine
  pitch.
- **Drawpoints and open stopes:** the brow is the hazard. Operators stay behind the
  **remote-bogging line** (usually marked by a sign, chain or line on the wall) and use
  **line-of-sight remote or tele-remote** to reach into unsupported ground or the stope.
  Know the remote system's e-stop and fail-safe behaviour.
- Hang-ups in a drawpoint are cleared by the specified method (water cannon, secondary
  blasting from a safe position) — **never** by going under the hang-up.

## 3. Driving underground trucks and LHDs
- Loaded descending a decline: low gear, use the retarder/engine brake, don't ride the
  service brakes (they fade and can catch fire).
- Right of way, passing bays and radio calls per site rules; light vehicles give way to
  heavy. Dip lights at oncoming traffic.
- Collision-avoidance or proximity detection systems warn of people and vehicles —
  treat an alert as real until you've confirmed otherwise.

## 4. Surface haul trucks
- Huge blind spots: walk around or check cameras before moving off; use horn signals
  (codes are site-specific; learn yours).
- Loaded on the ramp: select gear and retarder speed before the descent; most trucks
  have automatic retarder control — know its setting and its limits. Never coast.
- At the dump: approach square to the windrow, stop with the rear wheels short of it,
  apply the dump brake, raise the body, ease forward as it empties if required.
  Don't dump on soft or cracked edges (US surface metal and nonmetal, 30 CFR 56.9304(b): where the ground may not support the equipment, dump a safe distance back from the edge of the unstable area).
- Tyres: if a tyre is overheated or the truck has contacted power lines or been
  involved in a fire, **clear the exclusion zone** as set by your site tyre-event procedure and the tyre maker; tyres can explode after overheating or fire. Follow the site tyre-event procedure.

## 5. Machine fire
Stop in a safe place away from fuel and the face, shut down, actuate the suppression
system, get out, report, and use a hand extinguisher only if it's safe. Underground,
smoke will travel with the ventilation — warn people downstream.

## Gotchas
- Fatigue: microsleeps on long hauls cause most run-off-road incidents.
- Fresh rock on a bench floor slashes tyres — call for a cleanup.
- Remote-controlled machines can move unexpectedly if the transmitter is dropped —
  carry it on a harness.

## Verify
- [ ] Pre-start and suppression check done; defects reported.
- [ ] Remote-bogging line respected; no one under unsupported ground.
- [ ] Gear and retarder set before descents.
- [ ] Dump approached square, rear wheels short of the windrow.
- [ ] Tyre and fire exclusion procedures known.
