# Charging a blast and handling misfires

**Goal:** get a designed pattern from drilled holes to a fired, cleared and accounted-for
blast with nobody hurt, and treat any misfire as the hazard it is. This is the
shotfirer's side of the job; the pattern itself is designed upstream (see
engineering-technology.mining-geological-engineering.drilling-blasting.blast-pattern-design).

> Written from general industry practice (US surface metal and nonmetal references: 30 CFR 56.6306 and 56.6311). Exact quantities, wait times,
> licensing and paperwork are set by your jurisdiction's explosives regulations, the
> manufacturer's product data and your site's blasting management plan — those win.

## 1. Before any explosive arrives
- **Dip every hole** with a weighted tape: depth, water, collapse, and whether it has
  broken through into a void or an old hole. Record against the hole number on the
  plan. Short holes get redrilled or recharged to the shortened depth, not "close enough".
- **Check the face and burden** on front rows. A thin burden where the previous blast
  back-broke is the classic flyrock source; reduce the charge or re-design, don't charge
  to plan.
- **Wet holes** need water-resistant product (emulsion or packaged), or dewatering and
  a liner. Bulk ANFO in standing water dissolves, under-performs and gives orange
  NOx fume.
- **Set up the area:** signage, barricades, only authorised people, no smoking or
  ignition sources, and keep radio/phone rules if any electric initiators are in use.

## 2. Priming and loading
1. Make primers at the hole, one at a time: detonator fully inserted in the booster
   well, lead secured, never forced. Keep detonators separate from bulk product
   until used.
2. Lower the primer to the hole bottom (or the design position) on its lead, then load
   the column: bulk truck hose or poured product to the **design collar height**. Watch
   for product disappearing into cracks or voids — a hole that takes far more than its
   calculated load is telling you something.
3. **Stem** with angular crushed aggregate to the design length. Drill cuttings stem poorly and invite ejection
   and air overpressure.
4. Underground, holes are typically charged up-hole with pneumatic ANFO loaders or
   emulsion pumps; static build-up in plastic hoses is a known hazard with electric
   detonators — use semi-conductive hose and earthing as specified.

## 3. Tie-in and firing
- Tie in last, after everyone and all equipment not needed have left the pattern. (US, 30 CFR 56.6306(e): before connecting to a power source or attaching an initiating device, all persons leave the blast area except those in a shelter or other protected location.)
- Follow the timing plan exactly; check surface delays against the hole-by-hole plan.
  With electronic detonators, log and test every detonator with the logger; with
  electric, check circuit resistance with an approved blasting ohmmeter.
- **Guard every access route** to the exclusion zone with someone briefed and in radio
  contact. Count people out; sweep the zone.
- Sound the warning signals the site uses (often: pre-blast warning, firing, all-clear),
  get positive confirmation from every guard, then fire.

## 4. After the blast
- Wait the site-specified re-entry time for fume to clear (and longer underground until
  ventilation confirms gas readings). Re-enter with a gas monitor underground.
- (US, 30 CFR 56.6306(g): work does not resume in the blast area until a qualified person has examined it for blast-related hazards.) Inspect the muckpile and face for un-detonated product, cut-offs, unfired holes and
  hanging ground before giving the all-clear.

## 5. Misfires — the non-negotiable rules
- **Assume it is live.** Do not approach before the waiting period set by your regulation, the explosives manufacturer and your blasting plan. US surface metal and nonmetal rule 30 CFR 56.6311 sets no fixed waiting period: faces and muck piles are examined for misfires after each blast, only work needed to remove the misfire and protect the people doing so is allowed in the area, a misfire that cannot be safely disposed of gets warning signs at every approach and management is told immediately, and misfires are reported to mine management by the end of the shift.
- Keep the exclusion zone guarded; report to the supervisor and record it.
- Re-firing or removal is done only by a competent person under the mine's written misfire procedure and the explosives manufacturer's instructions; do not improvise.
- **Never** drill into, pull out, or dig at a misfired charge or a socket ("bootleg")
  that may hold explosive. Mark sockets and wash them before any drilling nearby.
- Account for every detonator and every kilogram: issue minus used minus returned must
  equal zero.

## Gotchas
- Back-break and over-charged front rows cause most flyrock incidents.
- Lightning: stop charging and clear the area when a storm approaches.
- Electronic and shock-tube systems from different makers are not interchangeable.

## Verify
- [ ] Every hole dipped and recorded; anomalies acted on before loading.
- [ ] Loaded quantities and stemming lengths match the plan within tolerance.
- [ ] Timing checked; circuit or logger test passed.
- [ ] All guards confirmed, area cleared, signals given.
- [ ] Post-blast inspection done; misfires reported, marked, handled per rules.
- [ ] Explosives reconciliation balances to zero.
