# Self-escape and mine rescue team response

**Goal:** get yourself out (or into a refuge) when the alarm goes, and understand how a
trained rescue team works so you can support it — or train to be on it.

> Mine rescue is learned through accredited training and regular drills,
> not from a page. Equipment models, durations and procedures here are generic;
> your site's emergency response plan and the manufacturer's instructions apply.

## 1. The alarm
Underground warning methods are set out in each mine's escape and evacuation plan (US metal and nonmetal, 30 CFR 57.11053, requires procedures for notifying miners of an emergency, an escape plan for each working area and posted maps of escape routes and refuge chambers); typical devices include radio or personal emergency device messages and strobes. On warning: stop
work, shut down equipment safely, and follow the plan — usually make your way to the
escapeway or nearest refuge, not toward the problem.

## 2. Self-rescuers
- **Filter self-rescuer (FSR):** converts CO to CO₂ using a catalyst. Gives **no
  oxygen**; useless in oxygen-deficient air. Gets hot in use — that is normal.
- **Self-contained self-rescuer (SCSR):** chemical or compressed oxygen; works in
  any atmosphere for its rated time (US underground coal, 30 CFR 75.1714: approved self-rescue devices must give at least 1 hour of protection; actual duration depends heavily on exertion).
- Donning (SCSR, generic): open the case, start the oxygen (activation/pull tab),
  mouthpiece in, **nose clip on**, goggles on, breathe through the mouth only. Do not
  remove the mouthpiece to talk. Practise on a training unit; first-time donning
  under stress is where people fail.
- Know where caches of spare units are along your escape route; swap before one runs out.

## 3. Self-escape
- Follow the designated escapeway (lifelines, signage or reflectors as your mine's escape plan specifies). In smoke, keep low, stay in contact with the wall
  or lifeline, and move as a group.
- If escape is cut off: go to a **refuge chamber** or fresh-air base, seal it per the
  instructions, start its air or oxygen supply and scrubbers, and **communicate** (phone,
  radio, tapping on pipes per the code). Mark your location outside if possible.
- Barricading (building a sealed area with brattice) is a last resort where no refuge exists.

## 4. The rescue team
- US teams (30 CFR 49.2) have five members plus one alternate, all qualified, trained and equipped; a captain leads, wearing **closed-circuit breathing apparatus**, supported by a backup team at the
  **fresh-air base (FAB)** and a briefing officer on surface.
- Before going under oxygen: briefing (map, last known positions, gas readings,
  ventilation status, objective), **BA pre-use test** (positive/negative pressure,
  alarms), equipment check, time out logged.
- In the hazardous area: advance at the pace of the slowest member; the captain
  **gas tests** (CH₄, CO, O₂, H₂S as relevant) and reports to the FAB at set intervals;
  mark the route ("captain's chalk": date, time, team, readings); watch ground and
  ventilation. Turn-back is set by the **lowest cylinder pressure** in the team, with
  reserve for the return trip.
- Live casualties take priority over recovery and over firefighting. Don't alter
  ventilation (open doors, remove stoppings) without surface command authorising it —
  it can push fire gases onto people or create an explosive mixture.

## 5. If you are not on the team
Report to your muster point, get on the tag board/tracking system so you are
accounted for, give command exact information about where people were working, and
don't self-deploy into the mine.

## Gotchas
- Sweaty goggles and hyperventilation are the commonest SCSR problems; slow breathing.
- An FSR in a fire with low oxygen gives false confidence.
- "Fresh air" at the base must be confirmed by gas tests, not assumed.

## Verify
- [ ] You know your self-rescuer type, its limits and can don it in practice.
- [ ] You know the escapeway, caches and nearest refuge from your workplace.
- [ ] Team BA pre-use tests and briefing completed before entry.
- [ ] Gas readings and positions reported at intervals; turn-back pressure respected.
- [ ] Everyone accounted for on the tag board.
