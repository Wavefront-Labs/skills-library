# Grading scrap metal by test, and weighbridge practice

**Goal:** pay (or get paid) the right price for every load by identifying what the
metal really is, keeping grades clean, and weighing honestly — while keeping
dangerous and illegal material out of the yard. Grade names below follow the common
North American ISRI/ReMA vocabulary; your buyers' specs and local naming rule.

## 1. First split: ferrous or non-ferrous
- **Magnet test:** a strong magnet sticks firmly to carbon steel and cast iron
  (ferrous). It doesn't stick to aluminium, copper, brass, lead, zinc, and **most
  austenitic stainless (304/316)** — though cold-worked stainless can be slightly
  magnetic. Non-ferrous is worth far more, so this split is the most important one.
- **Weight in the hand:** aluminium is light; lead very heavy; copper and brass heavy.

## 2. Non-ferrous identification
- **Colour on a fresh scratch or file cut:** copper is salmon-pink; brass yellow;
  bronze darker gold-brown; aluminium silvery-white and soft; zinc bluish-grey;
  lead grey and very soft (scratches with a fingernail).
- **Copper grades** (prices differ a lot): bright, clean, uncoated, unalloyed wire
  (e.g. "bare bright") pays most; tarnished or lightly coated wire next; then pipe and
  mixed copper with solder/fittings; insulated wire is priced on recovery.
- **Aluminium:** clean extrusions (window frames without glass, rubber or steel),
  sheet, cast (brittle, grainy fracture), cans, and mixed shredded (often "zorba").
  Attachments (steel screws, plastic) drop the grade.
- **Stainless:** 316 contains molybdenum and pays more than 304; you can't tell them
  apart reliably by eye — you need an analyser.

## 3. Analysers
- **Handheld XRF** reads alloy chemistry in seconds. Clean the spot (paint, plating
  and oxide distort readings), hold steady flat against the metal, and check the
  grade library match. XRF struggles with light elements (it can't see carbon;
  magnesium and aluminium are weak), so **LIBS** analysers are used where those
  matter. Calibration-check with a reference coupon each shift.
- Radiation safety: XRF guns emit X-rays; never point one at a person or hold samples
  in your hand during a shot; follow the licence conditions for the device.
- **Spark test** on a grinder (ferrous only): carbon steel gives bright bushy
  bursts; stainless gives fewer, duller orange sparks; cast iron short red sparks.
  Useful for sorting steels, not a precise test.

## 4. Weighbridge practice
- Weighbridge must be **in calibration/verification date**, the deck clear of debris,
  and the vehicle entirely on the deck (no wheels on the approach).
- **Gross weight** loaded, **tare** empty after tipping (stored tares only if your
  rules allow and the vehicle hasn't changed). Driver out of the vehicle or
  consistently in it — be consistent.
- Make **deductions** (dirt, water, attachments, non-metallics) visibly and record the
  reason; photograph the load. Disputes are won with photos.
- Record seller ID, vehicle registration and payment method where the law requires
  it — many countries require ID and ban or limit cash for scrap to deter metal theft.

## 5. Reject and report
- **Radiation alarm** at the gate portal: stop the vehicle, keep people back, follow
  the procedure and call the radiation protection adviser. Never cut into an
  unknown source.
- **Sealed or pressurised containers** (gas cylinders, closed drums, fuel tanks,
  airbags, shock absorbers): explosion risk in shears and furnaces — reject or
  depollute properly.
- **Likely stolen goods:** manhole covers, rail parts, new cable cut to lengths,
  bronze plaques, utility-branded metal. Refuse and report per local law.

## Verify
- [ ] Every load magnet-tested; non-ferrous identified by colour, weight or analyser.
- [ ] Analyser calibration checked this shift; spots cleaned before shots.
- [ ] Weighbridge in date, vehicle fully on deck, gross and tare recorded.
- [ ] Deductions photographed and reasoned; seller ID recorded where required.
- [ ] Radiation alarms, sealed vessels and suspect items handled by procedure.
