# Working a collection round with bin lifters and contamination checks

**Goal:** finish the round on time without anyone hit by the truck, hurt by the
lifter, or a load catching fire — and keep recycling loads clean enough to sell.
Refuse collection consistently ranks among the more dangerous jobs, mainly because of
vehicles moving among people. Follow your depot's safe system of work and the
vehicle manufacturer's instructions; this is the practice beneath them.

## 1. Start of shift
- **Vehicle walkround** with the driver: tyres, lights, mirrors and cameras, reversing
  alarm, beacons, lifter controls and emergency stops (test them), hydraulic leaks,
  body and tailgate secure, fire extinguisher present.
- Hi-vis, gloves (cut and puncture resistant), safety boots, eye protection if your
  task needs it.
- Know the round's known hazards: narrow streets, schools at drop-off times, steep
  driveways, dogs, overhead cables near where bins are lifted.

## 2. Around the moving vehicle
- **Never ride on the outside** of the vehicle (steps or footboards) unless your
  vehicle and procedure explicitly allow it; ride in the cab and walk between stops.
- **Reversing** is where most serious injuries happen: avoid it in the route design,
  and when unavoidable, use a trained banksman/reversing assistant standing where the
  driver can see them in the mirror. Lost sight of the banksman = driver stops.
- Stay out of the zone behind the truck; don't walk between the truck and a fixed
  object.

## 3. Bin-lifter technique
- Present the bin square to the lifter with the comb/lugs fully engaged; check the
  lid will fall clear.
- **Keep hands, feet and your body out of the lift arc** while it cycles; never reach
  into the hopper. Use the two-handed or hold-to-run controls as designed — don't
  defeat them.
- Watch for bins that flip out (overloaded or wrongly engaged); stop the cycle.
- **Overweight bins** (soil, rubble): don't fight them. Leave a tag explaining why.
- Commercial containers on trunnion lifts: check wheels, lids and brakes; push,
  don't pull, on slopes.

## 4. Look before you tip
- **Hot loads:** smoke, heat, or the smell of burning from a bin — don't tip it into
  the body. Common causes are hot ash and lithium batteries. If something ignites in
  the hopper, stop compaction, move the vehicle away from people and buildings, call
  the fire service and follow your hot-load procedure (often a controlled tip in a
  safe open space).
- **Sharps and hazardous items** (needles, gas bottles, chemicals): don't handle with
  bare hands; follow the report procedure.
- **Recycling contamination:** look in the top of the bin. Food waste, nappies,
  bagged recycling (where not accepted), textiles and garden waste commonly reject a
  load at the MRF. Tag the bin with the reason and leave it (or take it, per local
  policy) so the household learns what went wrong.

## 5. Records
Log missed bins and why (blocked access, not out, contaminated, overweight), damaged
bins, hazards found and near misses. Accurate records protect the crew against
complaints and help fix the route.

## 6. Never enter the body
The compaction body is a confined space with a crushing mechanism. Clearing a jam
needs isolation (key out, hydraulics off, lock off) and your depot's procedure.

## Verify
- [ ] Walkround done; lifter emergency stops tested.
- [ ] Every reverse done with a banksman in view, or avoided.
- [ ] No one rode outside the cab or entered the lifter arc.
- [ ] Hot loads and hazardous items handled under procedure, not tipped.
- [ ] Contaminated bins tagged; misses and hazards logged.
