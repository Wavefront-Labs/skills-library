# Tipping face, compaction, cover and gas-well balancing

**Goal:** turn incoming waste into dense, covered, stable fill that uses the least
airspace (airspace is the landfill's product), while keeping odour, litter, leachate
and gas under control. Your permit conditions override everything here; numbers
below are typical, not universal.

## 1. Working face layout
- **Keep the active face as small as traffic allows.** A small face means less
  cover soil needed, less litter, less odour, fewer birds and less rainwater entering
  waste (= less leachate).
- Separate the **tipping area for trucks** from the **compactor working area**;
  a spotter or banksman directs vehicles to tip at the toe of the face.
- Check loads as they tip: banned or hazardous items, hot loads, asbestos,
  liquids. Have a procedure (and an area) for quarantining a suspect load.
- Wind: work so the face is sheltered from the prevailing wind; litter fences
  downwind; pick litter daily.

## 2. Compaction
- Spread waste in **thin layers** (commonly about 0.5 m or less) and compact
  **up the slope**, typically on a slope around 1 in 3 to 1 in 4 (operators vary).
- Several passes (often 3–5) of a steel-wheeled compactor; more passes add little.
- Track achieved density from survey volumes and weighbridge tonnages
  (in-place densities around 0.8–1.0 t/m³ are often quoted for good compaction of
  municipal waste — **site-specific; treat as indicative**). A falling density trend
  means passes or layer thickness have slipped.
- Avoid compacting directly over or near gas wells, leachate risers and liner
  edges; mark them so drivers can see them.

## 3. Cover
- **Daily cover** at the end of each working day: commonly ~150 mm of soil, or an
  approved alternative (tarps, foam, certain processed materials) where the permit
  allows. Cover controls vectors, odour, litter and fire.
- **Intermediate cover** (thicker) on areas that won't receive waste for months; seed it.
- Strip or scrape back daily cover before placing the next lift where practical: soil
  layers left in the fill trap leachate and block gas paths.

## 4. Leachate
- Keep leachate pumps running and record levels in collection sumps. Many permits
  limit the head of leachate on the liner (US rules use 30 cm).
- Divert clean surface water away from the active cell. Rain into the face is the
  biggest leachate source you control.

## 5. Gas-well balancing
Use a calibrated portable gas analyser at each wellhead. Record methane (CH₄),
carbon dioxide (CO₂), oxygen (O₂), balance gas (mostly nitrogen), temperature,
flow and vacuum.
- **Healthy well:** CH₄ roughly 45–60%, CO₂ most of the rest, O₂ near 0%.
- **Over-pulling:** O₂ and balance gas rising, CH₄ falling → air is being drawn in
  through cover. Close the valve a little.
- **Under-pulling:** high CH₄, low flow, odour or surface emissions nearby → open a
  little.
- Adjust in **small increments**, then leave it (gas fields respond over hours to
  days). Big swings chase their tails.
- **Rising temperature with oxygen present, or CO appearing,** can indicate a
  subsurface fire. Escalate immediately; don't keep pulling harder on it.
- Check wellhead integrity (cracked casing, loose fittings, flooded wells — high
  liquid levels block gas; they need pumping).

## Verify
- [ ] Face kept compact; tipping and compacting zones separated; loads checked.
- [ ] Thin layers, several passes, up-slope; density trended.
- [ ] Cover placed daily to permit; intermediate cover on idle areas.
- [ ] Leachate levels within permit; surface water diverted.
- [ ] Every well read and adjusted in small steps; O₂ intrusion and hot spots escalated.
