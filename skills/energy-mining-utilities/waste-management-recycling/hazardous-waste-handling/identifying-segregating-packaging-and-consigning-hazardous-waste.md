# Identifying, segregating, packaging and consigning hazardous waste

**Goal:** get hazardous waste from where it's made to a licensed facility without a
reaction in the drum, a leak in transit or a paperwork breach. Rules are
jurisdiction-specific (US RCRA/DOT; EU List of Wastes and ADR; others); the method
below is common to all. The legal responsibility usually stays with the
producer until the waste reaches its final destination ("cradle to grave",
"duty of care").

## 1. Identify and classify
- Start with the **safety data sheet** for the original product and what the process
  did to it (spent solvents pick up contaminants; mixtures change classification).
- **US:** is it a *listed* waste (F, K, P, U lists) or does it show a *characteristic*
  — ignitable (D001), corrosive (D002), reactive (D003), toxic (D004–D043 by
  leaching test)? **EU/UK:** assign the six-digit List of Wastes (EWC) code; for
  "mirror entries" you must assess the hazardous properties (HP codes) of the actual
  waste, often needing analysis.
- Unknown material: treat as hazardous, isolate it, and get it sampled and analysed.
  Never sniff-test or mix to "see what happens".

## 2. Segregate incompatibles
Keep apart (separate containers and, ideally, separate bunded areas):
- **Acids vs bases** (heat, spattering).
- **Acids vs cyanides or sulphides** (toxic gas).
- **Oxidisers** (nitrates, peroxides, hypochlorite) **vs organics and flammables**
  (fire).
- **Water-reactives** away from aqueous wastes and sprinklers.
- Halogenated vs non-halogenated solvents (disposal route and cost differ).
Check SDS section 10 (stability and reactivity) when unsure. Clinical waste is
segregated by colour-coded streams that differ by country — follow your local code.

## 3. Package
- Use containers **compatible with the contents** (no metal for corrosives, no
  certain plastics for some solvents) and **UN-approved** for transport where needed.
- Leave headspace (often about 5–10%) for expansion; liquids need secondary
  containment in storage.
- **Keep containers closed** except when adding waste. Funnels left in drums are a
  common inspection finding.
- **Sharps** go only into rigid sharps containers, never filled past the line;
  never re-sheath needles.

## 4. Label
At minimum: the words "hazardous waste" (or your jurisdiction's equivalent), contents,
hazards (pictograms/hazard classes), accumulation start date, producer/site. Transport
adds UN number, proper shipping name and class marks per ADR/DOT.

## 5. Store and consign
- Store in a designated, secure, ventilated, bunded area; observe accumulation time and
  quantity limits for your producer category.
- Use **licensed carriers and permitted receiving facilities** only; check their
  licences.
- Complete the **manifest / consignment note** accurately (codes, quantities,
  container types, hazards). In the US the uniform hazardous waste manifest (now
  largely e-Manifest); in England a consignment note. Keep copies for the required
  period and **chase the returned copy confirming receipt** — if it doesn't come
  back, the law often requires you to investigate and report.

## 6. Spill response (small, known spills)
1. Warn others, keep people away, eliminate ignition sources.
2. Put on PPE appropriate to the SDS.
3. **Stop the source** if safe (upright the container), **contain** with booms/socks,
   protect drains.
4. **Absorb** with a compatible absorbent (never sawdust or other combustibles on
   oxidisers); neutralise only if trained and the kit is designed for it.
5. Bag, label and dispose of clean-up material as hazardous waste. Report per
   procedure. Large or unknown spills: evacuate and call the emergency service.

## Verify
- [ ] Each waste classified with code and reasoning recorded.
- [ ] Incompatibles physically separated; SDS reactivity checked.
- [ ] Compatible, closed, UN-approved containers with headspace and labels.
- [ ] Carrier and facility licences checked; manifest complete; receipt copy returned.
- [ ] Spill kit stocked and compatible with the wastes on site.
