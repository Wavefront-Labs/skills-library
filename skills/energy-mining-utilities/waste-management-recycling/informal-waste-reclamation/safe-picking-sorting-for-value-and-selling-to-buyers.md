# Safe picking, sorting for value, and selling to buyers

**Goal:** get the most money from the material you collect while avoiding the
injuries and illnesses that end many waste pickers' working lives. Millions of
people do this work worldwide; conditions, laws and prices vary greatly by city.
Where a cooperative, union or municipal scheme exists, it is usually the best route
to safer work and better prices.

## 1. The big dangers and how to avoid them
- **Vehicles on dumps:** most deaths and serious injuries at dump sites involve
  trucks and bulldozers. Never pick behind a reversing truck or in front of a
  compactor; stay where drivers can see you; don't sleep or rest on or near the
  working area.
- **Waste slides and collapses:** steep, freshly dumped piles and undercut faces can
  collapse, especially after rain. Keep away from the toe of high piles.
- **Fires and smoke:** dumps burn and smoulder underground; ground can give way into
  burning voids. Avoid smoking areas; smoke is toxic.
- **Sharps and medical waste:** needles, blades and glass cause infections and
  bloodborne disease. Use a hook or tongs, not bare hands; sturdy gloves and closed
  shoes help a lot. Leave obvious medical waste alone.
- **Health basics:** get a **tetanus vaccination** (and hepatitis B where
  available); wash hands before eating; clean and cover cuts at once; drink clean
  water; seek care for wounds that go red and hot.
- **Don't burn cable** to get copper out. The smoke is poisonous, it's often illegal,
  and burnt copper usually sells for *less* than clean stripped copper. Strip with a
  knife or a simple stripping tool.

## 2. Know what pays
Prices change weekly and differ by buyer; ask several buyers and keep a small notebook
of prices per kilo. Typical value order (high to low, roughly):
1. **Metals:** copper (clean, stripped) and brass highest; aluminium (cans,
   profiles); then steel/iron (heavy but cheap per kilo). A magnet tells steel from
   aluminium and copper.
2. **Plastics:** clear **PET bottles** and **HDPE** (milk, detergent bottles) usually sell;
   film and mixed plastics often sell poorly or not at all locally.
3. **Paper and cardboard:** clean, dry cardboard and white paper; wet or food-soiled
   paper is often refused.
4. **Glass** sells in some cities only, and is heavy for its price.

## 3. Prepare material for better prices
- **Sort by type and colour:** mixed loads get the lowest price for the whole bag.
- **Clean and dry:** remove food and liquid; cardboard kept dry; bottles emptied.
- **Separate parts:** remove caps and labels if your buyer pays more; take steel
  screws out of aluminium; strip insulation from copper.
- **Compact:** flatten cans, bottles and boxes so carts carry more and buyers can
  check quality quickly.

## 4. Selling well
- **Check the scale:** carry a known weight (for example a sealed 1 kg packet or a
  1-litre bottle of water ≈ 1 kg) and weigh it on the buyer's scale. Watch the
  reading yourself.
- Ask for the price before you unload; ask what deductions (for dirt or water) will
  be made.
- **Group selling:** pooling material with others, or through a cooperative, often
  gets a buyer further up the chain (closer to the factory) and a better price per kilo.
- Keep a simple record: date, material, kilos, price. It shows which material and
  which buyer is really worth your time.

## 5. Route and street work
- Agree with shops, offices and households to collect their separated recyclables on
  fixed days; regular sources are worth more than random searching.
- Take care with carts in traffic: lights or reflectors at night, stay to the side.
- Respect bans on certain sites; ask the municipality whether registration as a
  recycler is possible, as it can bring access and protection.

## Verify
- [ ] You stay out of truck and compactor working zones and away from steep piles.
- [ ] Gloves, closed shoes and tools for sharps; tetanus vaccination up to date.
- [ ] Cable stripped, never burned.
- [ ] Material sorted, clean, dry and compacted; prices compared between buyers.
- [ ] Scale checked with a known weight before every sale.
