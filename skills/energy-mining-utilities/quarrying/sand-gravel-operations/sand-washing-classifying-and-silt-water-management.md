# Sand washing, classifying and silt water management

**Goal:** turn as-dug sand and gravel into clean, in-grading products — concrete sand,
building sand, washed gravel — while keeping enough clean water circulating and the
silt out of watercourses. The two halves are linked: dirty recycled water makes dirty
sand.

## 1. The washing train (typical)
1. **Feed hopper and grizzly** — scalp oversize and keep feed rate steady.
2. **Rinsing screen** with spray bars — separates gravel sizes and washes off fines;
   sand and water pass through to the sand plant.
3. **Scrubbing** for clay-bound feed — log washer or attrition scrubber breaks up
   clay balls that rinsing alone won't remove.
4. **Sand classifying** — **hydrocyclones** (fast, compact, cut at a set particle size)
   or **sand screws / bucket wheels** (simpler, coarser cut). The target is to remove
   silt and clay below about 63–75 µm while keeping the fine sand the grading needs.
5. **Dewatering screen** — drains sand to a stockpile-ready moisture (often ~10–15 %).

## 2. Getting the cut right
- **Cut point** too fine → silt stays in the sand, fails fines content and
  cleanliness tests (sand equivalent, methylene blue), makes concrete sticky.
- Cut point too coarse → you throw saleable fine sand into the lagoon and the sand
  goes "harsh" and out of grading at the fine end.
- Cyclone levers: feed pressure (keep constant — a varying sump level is the usual
  culprit), **apex/spigot** and **vortex finder** sizes, feed density. A "roping"
  (rope-like, dry, narrow) underflow means the cyclone is overloaded; a wide spray
  means it's underloaded or the apex is too large.
- Check by sieve: sample the sand product and the cyclone overflow. Overflow should
  contain very little above the cut size.

## 3. Water and silt management
- Washing uses a lot of water per tonne; most plants recycle 80–90 % or more.
- **Silt lagoons/settling ponds:** dirty water flows through a series of ponds; silt
  settles, clear water returns to the plant. Ponds fill — plan dig-out or new cells
  well ahead. Lagoon walls are engineered structures; inspect for seepage and
  overtopping risk, and follow tip/lagoon regulations where they apply.
- **Thickener + flocculant:** a compact alternative. Flocculant (usually a polymer)
  makes fine particles clump and settle fast. Dose to jar-test results; overdosing
  wastes money and can leave residual polymer in the water.
- **Filter press / belt press** produces a handleable cake from the thickener
  underflow where lagoon space is short.
- **Discharge:** any water leaving site must meet the discharge consent/permit
  (suspended solids, pH). Monitor and keep records.

## 4. Dry digging and dredging notes
- Dry pits: keep the working floor above the water table or pumped; dig in benches.
- Wet pits/dredging: dredger or dragline; the deposit's fines content arrives at
  the plant all at once — expect to manage feed rate to the washing capacity.

## Gotchas
- Cyclone wear (apex, liners) slowly shifts the cut point; inspect on schedule.
- Freezing: spray bars, pipework and dewatering screens need winter measures.
- Lagoon edges are drowning hazards: fencing, signs, rescue equipment and no lone work
  near them.
- Uncertain: cut sizes, moisture and recycling rates depend on deposit and product
  specs; numbers here are typical, not design values.

## Verify
- [ ] Sand sample in grading and passing fines/cleanliness tests.
- [ ] Cyclone underflow shape normal; sump level and feed pressure steady.
- [ ] Cyclone overflow sieve shows little saleable sand being lost.
- [ ] Lagoon capacity and wall condition checked; dig-out planned.
- [ ] Discharge water within permit limits and recorded.
