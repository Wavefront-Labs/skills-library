# Charging, firing and post-blast inspection of a bench blast

**Goal:** turn an approved blast design into a safe, compliant shot: the right charge in
the right holes, flyrock and vibration controlled, everyone clear, every hole fired,
and the face handed back only after inspection. This is work for a **licensed /
certified shotfirer** under the quarry's explosives rules and blast specification.
Pattern design itself is covered in
engineering-technology.mining-geological-engineering.drilling-blasting.blast-pattern-design;
this skill is execution on the bench.

## 1. Check the design against the ground
- **Face profile:** a laser profile of the face and **borehole deviation survey**
  show the real **front-row burden** — the rock between each front hole and the free
  face. Too little burden anywhere is the main cause of **flyrock**. Where the
  profile shows thin burden (back-break, an overhang, a hollow), adjust the charge or
  stemming at that hole per the design rules — or stop and get the design revised.
- Measure each hole depth and check for water, blockages and voids (cavities or open
  joints swallow explosive and over-charge). Record as you go.
- Confirm the **exclusion zone**, sentry positions, warning signals and timing with
  site management; nearby roads, footpaths and neighbours are in the plan.

## 2. Charging
- Only the shotfirer and authorised assistants on the shot; no unnecessary people or
  vehicles. No smoking, phones per site rule.
- Prime and charge each hole to the specified charge and column height — measure
  the rise with a tape as you load; don't estimate. Unexpectedly fast loading = a void.
- **Stemming:** use the specified length and material (crushed angular stone of the
  specified size, not drill cuttings). Short or poor stemming causes cratering, flyrock
  and air overpressure.
- Keep detonator leads/tubes protected; tie in to the initiation sequence exactly as
  designed. With electronic detonators, log and test each one with the system's
  tester.

## 3. Clearing, guarding and firing
- Sweep the exclusion zone; close roads per plan; sentries in place with radios. (US, 30 CFR 56.6306(f): ample warning before firing, a clear exit route for the person firing, and all access routes guarded or barricaded.)
- Sound the agreed warnings at the agreed intervals.
- Final circuit test; confirm sentries all-clear; fire from the firing point outside
  the danger area.
- Vibration and air overpressure monitored at agreed locations (seismographs);
  results kept against planning or permit limits (often expressed as peak particle
  velocity, PPV, in mm/s).

## 4. Post-blast inspection
- Wait the specified time after firing before approaching (longer with any doubt).
- The shotfirer inspects the blast area: face, muckpile surface, for **misfires**
  (unfired holes, cut-offs, exposed explosive or detonators), and for unstable
  ground or overhangs.
- **Misfire:** keep the exclusion zone in place, follow the site misfire procedure
  exactly, report it, and don't let loading start until the misfire is dealt with by
  a competent person.
- US rules: 30 CFR 56.6311 requires faces and muck piles to be examined for misfires after each blast, allows only work needed to remove the misfire in the area, requires warning signs at every approach and immediate notice to management for a misfire that cannot be safely disposed of, and requires misfires to be reported by the end of the shift; 30 CFR 56.6306(g) bars resumption of work until a qualified person has examined the blast area.
- Only then declare all-clear and stand down sentries.

## 5. Record
Blast record: hole depths, burdens, charges per hole, stemming, initiation sequence,
weather, monitoring results, misfires and any flyrock. Trend vibration results to keep
compliance ahead of complaints.

## Gotchas
- Flyrock events nearly always trace back to burden or stemming anomalies that were
  visible on the profile or during loading.
- Lightning: stop charging and clear the area when storms approach; follow the site
  rule.
- Uncertain: licensing, warning signals, wait times and vibration limits are
  jurisdiction- and site-specific — follow the quarry's explosives rules.

## Verify
- [ ] Face profile and borehole survey reviewed; thin-burden holes adjusted per design.
- [ ] Every hole measured, charged and stemmed to spec, with records.
- [ ] Exclusion zone cleared and guarded; warnings sounded; all-clear received before firing.
- [ ] Post-blast inspection done; any misfire handled by procedure before handover.
- [ ] Monitoring results and blast record filed.
