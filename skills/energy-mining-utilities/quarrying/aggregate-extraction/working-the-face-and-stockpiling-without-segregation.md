# Working the face and stockpiling without segregation

**Goal:** deliver even, clean feed from the blasted face to the primary crusher, and
turn the plant's products into stockpiles that still meet their grading when they
reach the customer. Most "the plant is underperforming" and "the product failed its
sieve test" problems start at the face or the stockpile, not inside the crusher.

## 1. Before loading the muckpile
- Check the post-blast inspection is signed off (misfires cleared, face examined;
  see energy-mining-utilities.quarrying.quarry-blasting.charging-firing-and-post-blast-inspection-of-a-bench-blast).
- Inspect the **highwall** from a safe distance for loose rock and overhangs; keep
  the loading position out from under it. Scaling is done before work, not during.
- Know the face's material zones: overburden and weathered rock on top, clay seams,
  any change in rock type that affects product quality.

## 2. Loading for even feed
- Dig the muckpile across its full width in passes rather than mining one hole into
  it — this mixes coarse and fine fractions and keeps the primary fed evenly.
- **Oversize:** boulders bigger than the primary's gape bridge the crusher.
  Set them aside for a rock breaker or secondary breakage; never feed them hoping
  they'll go through.
- **Clay and overburden:** keep out of the feed. Load dirty material to a separate
  stockpile or a scalping route. Clay blinds screens and fails cleanliness tests
  (e.g. fines/sand-equivalent, methylene blue).
- Talk to the plant operator: if the primary is starved or flooded, adjust truck
  cycle or loading rate, not just the crusher.
- **Blending** different face zones to keep feed consistent is often cheaper than
  fixing variable products downstream.

## 3. Stockpiling without segregation
Segregation = large particles roll to the toe of a pile, fines stay at the centre and top.
The result: loads taken from different parts of the pile have different gradings.
- **Single-size products** (e.g. 10 mm, 20 mm) segregate little — conical piles are fine.
- **Graded products** (e.g. 0–32 mm subbase, 0–4 mm sand with a range) segregate badly.
  Better methods:
  - **Radial stacker** moving in arcs to build the pile in layers.
  - **Layered (tiered) stockpiles** built by trucks tipping in a pattern and a dozer
    or loader spreading in thin layers — not pushing over the edge.
  - Keep conveyor drop height low (telescopic chutes, rock ladders).
- Loading out: take full-height faces of the pile, not just the toe or the top.
- Separate products clearly with **walls or gaps** — cross-contamination ruins both.
- Keep pile floors clean and drained; don't load the bottom layer of mud into product.

## 4. Quality checks
- Sample stockpiles and loads per the testing plan (frequency set by the product
  standard and factory production control). Sample from multiple points; one scoop
  from the toe proves nothing.
- Sieve analysis against the grading envelope; trend results so drift is visible
  before a failure.

## Gotchas
- Haul roads and edges: berms or guardrails at least mid-axle height of the largest self-propelled mobile equipment that usually travels the roadway (US MSHA, 30 CFR 56.9300(b)).
- Wet weather changes everything: sticky feed, blocked chutes, slurry on stockpile
  floors.
- Test methods and sampling frequencies vary by country and product standard; confirm them in the standard that applies to your product.

## Verify
- [ ] Post-blast sign-off received and highwall inspected before loading.
- [ ] Oversize and clay kept out of primary feed.
- [ ] Graded products stockpiled in layers with low drop heights.
- [ ] Products separated; load-out from full pile height.
- [ ] Sampling done to plan and results trended.
