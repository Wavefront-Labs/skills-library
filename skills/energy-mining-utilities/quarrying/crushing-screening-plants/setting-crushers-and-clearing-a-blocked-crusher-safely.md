# Setting crushers and clearing a blocked crusher safely

**Goal:** get the right product sizes out of a crushing and screening train (fixed or
tracked mobile) at good throughput, and handle the most dangerous routine job in a
quarry plant — a blocked or bridged crusher — without anyone being crushed, caught
or struck.

## 1. Know your settings
- **Jaw crusher (primary):** the **closed side setting (CSS)** — narrowest gap between
  jaws at the bottom of the stroke — controls top size of the product. Measure with a
  lead/foam ball or gauge as the maker describes; don't trust an un-recalibrated
  display after liner wear.
- **Cone crusher (secondary/tertiary):** CSS set hydraulically on most modern cones;
  liners wear, so the setting must be re-checked and adjusted to hold product size.
  Run cones **choke-fed** (feed chamber full, covering the mantle head) — this gives
  better shape, even liner wear and steadier load. A starved cone makes flaky product
  and wears unevenly.
- **Impact crushers (HSI/VSI):** rotor speed and apron/curtain gap set product size and
  shape; good for cubical product, but wear on blow bars is high in abrasive rock.
- Reduction ratio per stage is limited by the crusher type (check the maker's data); asking one stage to do too much overloads it.

## 2. Screens
- **Blinding** (fines and clay coating the mesh) → wet or sticky feed; consider
  washing, different media, or ball-tray cleaners.
- **Pegging** (particles stuck in apertures) → near-size material; rubber/polyurethane
  or self-cleaning media.
- Overloaded deck → material carried over, "oversize" in product. Check feed rate and
  deck angle/stroke.
- Inspect mesh for holes daily: one hole contaminates a whole product stockpile.

## 3. Feed control
- Keep feed steady (feeders interlocked to crusher level/load). Surges cause
  blockages and trips.
- **Tramp metal** (bucket teeth, drill bits) — magnets and metal detectors upstream
  of cones; a trip is cheaper than a broken mainshaft.
- Oversize at the primary: stop feed and break it with the rock breaker; don't let it
  bridge.

## 4. Clearing a blockage — the safe way
US rule 30 CFR 56.14105: repairs or maintenance of machinery are performed only after the power is off and the machinery is blocked against hazardous motion; machinery may be moved only for adjustments or testing that cannot be done otherwise, with people effectively protected.
1. **Stop the feed** and stop the crusher.
2. Use **remote means first:** fixed rock breaker, hydraulic release (cone
   clearing/tramp-release circuits), jaw reversing if fitted. Most blockages can be
   cleared without anyone entering.
3. If anyone must go near or into the crusher, the chamber or under the feed:
   **isolate and lock out** every energy source — electrical, hydraulic (relieve
   accumulators), pneumatic, gravity (material above that can fall), flywheel
   rotation (wait until it has stopped and is restrained). Each person fits their own
   lock; test for zero energy by trying to start.
4. Never stand on or in the material in a jaw or feed hopper — it can drop suddenly
   when the blockage clears. Work from a platform with fall protection.
5. Remove locks only when everyone is clear and accounted for.

## Gotchas
- "It'll only take a second with a bar" while the crusher is running is how people
  die. Stop and isolate.
- A flywheel keeps turning after power is off; wait until it has stopped.
- Hot work on crusher liners needs a procedure approved by the liner maker.
- Uncertain: settings, ratios and clearing devices are machine-specific; follow the
  OEM manual and site safe system of work.

## Verify
- [ ] CSS measured and recorded; adjusted for liner wear.
- [ ] Cone choke-fed; screens free of blinding, pegging and holes.
- [ ] Tramp metal protection working.
- [ ] Blockages cleared by remote means where possible.
- [ ] Any entry done under full isolation with personal locks and a zero-energy test.
