# Haul truck and loader operation on benches and haul roads

**Goal:** move rock from face to plant and from stockpile to customer without
vehicles going over edges, running over people or smaller vehicles, or losing brakes
on a ramp. Collisions, overturning and falls over edges are the vehicle hazards this skill targets, so the routine rules are the job.

## 1. Pre-start (every shift)
- **Walkaround:** tyres (cuts, pressure, wheel nuts), leaks, lights, mirrors and
  cameras clean, steps and handrails, fire extinguisher, seatbelt, horn and reversing
  alarm working.
- **Brake test** per site procedure — service and park brakes, often on a test ramp
  or with a stall test. A truck that fails stays parked.
- Check fluid levels and the defect book; report defects before driving.
- Radio check with the site channel; know the call signs and the day's road layout.

## 2. Haul roads
- **Windrows (bunds/berms)** on any edge where a vehicle could go over: US MSHA, 30 CFR 56.9300(b), requires berms or guardrails at least mid-axle height of the largest self-propelled mobile equipment that usually travels the roadway. A windrow is a warning and a stop, not a
  barrier you can lean on.
- **Gradients:** keep to the site's maximum;
  descend loaded ramps in the correct low gear with retarder engaged, never coasting.
- Road width: wide enough for the widest vehicle plus clearance on two-way roads; single-lane sections need passing
  bays and radio calls.
- **Right of way** per site rules (often loaded trucks have priority; light vehicles
  give way and stay well clear). Light vehicles use flags/beacons and radio before
  entering haul roads.
- Water roads for dust but not to the point of making them slippery; watch for
  potholes and spillage.

## 3. Loading at the face or stockpile
- The loader/excavator operator controls the loading area. Trucks wait outside it
  until signalled in (horn/light/radio code per site).
- Spot the truck so the bucket doesn't pass over the cab; truck driver stays in the
  cab with seatbelt on.
- Load evenly and to rated payload — overload damages tyres and brakes and spills on
  the road. Payload systems help; check them.
- Loader: keep the floor level, bucket low when travelling, no working under an
  unscaled face.

## 4. Tipping
- Tip at the plant hopper or a designed tip with a stop block/bund at the edge.
- On tip edges (overburden, fill): tip **short** of the edge and push over with a
  dozer, especially on soft or freshly placed ground, where edges can collapse under rear wheels (30 CFR 56.9304(b): where the ground may not support the equipment, dump a safe distance back from the edge).
- Tip on level ground; tipping on a cross-slope or with a stuck load can roll a truck
  or ADT. Lower the body before moving off.

## 5. Blind spots and people
- Large trucks have blind spots big enough to hide a pickup. Before moving: look,
  check cameras/proximity system, sound the horn per rule (codes are site-specific).
- Pedestrians on foot near mobile plant only with radio contact and operator
  acknowledgement.

## Gotchas
- Brake fade on long descents comes from wrong gear selection, not bad luck.
- A light vehicle parked close behind a truck is invisible — park well clear and in
  sight.
- Fatigue on long monotonous hauls; take breaks as rostered.
- Gradients and horn codes are site-specific — follow your quarry's traffic management plan.

## Verify
- [ ] Walkaround and brake test passed; defects reported.
- [ ] Windrows intact on all open edges used today.
- [ ] Ramps descended in gear with retarder, within the gradient limit.
- [ ] Loading by signal; bucket never over the cab; payload in limit.
- [ ] Tipping on level ground and short of soft edges; blind spots checked before every move.
