# Firewood seasoning, moisture testing and cord measurement

**Goal:** deliver firewood that actually burns well (dry, right size) in the volume the
customer paid for.

## 1. Cut and split
- **Buck** to a length that fits customers' stoves — commonly 25 cm (10 in) for small
  stoves, up to 40–45 cm (16–18 in) for larger stoves/open fires. Ask or offer options.
- **Split** to roughly 7–15 cm across. Splitting exposes wood to drying; unsplit rounds
  dry very slowly because bark holds moisture.
- Chainsaw and splitter work: chaps/trousers, helmet with visor and ear defenders, gloves,
  boots; hands away from wedge and ram, one operator per splitter.

## 2. Season
- Stack **off the ground** (pallets/rails), single rows or with gaps, **bark up** on top
  layers, in sun and wind, top covered but sides open.
- Typical times: many hardwoods 1–2 years air-drying (oak often 2+), softwoods and birch
  less. Wood cut and split in late winter/spring dries fastest over summer.
- Kiln drying gets to < 20% in days; store kiln-dried logs covered so they don't re-wet.

## 3. Test moisture properly
- Target: **below 20% moisture content** (wet basis, as used by schemes like Ready to
  Burn); 15–20% burns best.
- Take a log from the middle of the stack, **split it**, and immediately push the
  pin-meter probes into the **freshly split face**, along the grain, mid-length. Surface
  readings on the outside lie — the outer layer dries first.
- Test several logs per batch; record the highest typical reading.
- Set the meter for wood species if it allows; very cold wood reads low.
- Signs of dry wood: radial cracks in end grain, lighter weight, loose bark, a hollow
  "clack" when two logs knock together. These support the meter, they don't replace it.

## 4. Measure what you sell
- **Full cord (US/Canada):** stacked volume 4 ft × 4 ft × 8 ft = 128 ft³ (~3.62 m³)
  tightly stacked.
- **Face cord / rick:** 4 ft × 8 ft × one log length (e.g. 16 in) — about 1/3 cord at
  16-inch logs. It is ambiguous; state dimensions on invoices — many jurisdictions require
  firewood to be sold by the cord or fraction, not "truckload".
- **Metric:** stere = 1 m³ stacked; loose (thrown) volume is roughly 1.4–1.7× the stacked
  volume for the same wood (depends on log size). Say whether you sell "loose" or "stacked".
- Measure your bags/crates/trailer once and label them with stacked/loose volume.

## 5. Delivery
Deliver dry wood in rain covered; stack for the customer where agreed (charge for it).
Give burning advice: small hot fires, air open at start, don't load wet wood on a smouldering
fire (tar/creosote and smoke).

## Gotchas
- Wood stored under plastic tight to the stack sweats and goes mouldy — cover the top only.
- "Seasoned" bought from a supplier is not proof — meter it yourself.
- Moving wood across regions can spread forest pests; many places restrict this — check.

## Verify
- [ ] Logs cut to agreed length and split to sensible size.
- [ ] Moisture < 20% measured on a freshly split face from several logs.
- [ ] Volume stated in a legal unit (cord, fraction, m³ stacked/loose).
- [ ] Load matches invoice volume.

*Uncertain:* legal sale units and moisture schemes differ by country/state; follow local rules.
