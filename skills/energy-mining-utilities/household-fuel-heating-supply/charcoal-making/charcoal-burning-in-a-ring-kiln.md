# Charcoal burning in a ring kiln

**Goal:** convert a kiln-load of wood into good lump charcoal — black, ringing, not
under-burnt "brands" and not burnt to ash — by controlling air.

## 1. The principle
Charcoal is wood heated with **restricted air**: some wood burns to drive the process, the
rest drives off water then volatiles and becomes carbon. Too much air → ash and low
yield. Too little/sealed too early → brown, unconverted brands. Yields by weight are
typically ~ 15–25% of the dry wood in a well-run ring kiln.

## 2. Wood
- **Dry wood** (seasoned, ideally under ~20–25% moisture) — wet wood spends hours just
  steaming and lowers yield.
- Similar diameters together (e.g. 5–15 cm); split larger rounds. Mixed sizes leave thick
  pieces under-done when thin ones are finished.
- Hardwoods (oak, beech, ash, hornbeam, hazel) make dense restaurant/BBQ charcoal;
  softwoods make lighter, faster-burning charcoal.

## 3. Loading a steel ring kiln
Kiln: steel ring (often 2–2.5 m diameter) on a level base, with air inlet channels (ports)
at the base and chimneys that can swap between ports.
1. Lay stringers/smaller wood over the inlet channels so air reaches the centre.
2. Load larger pieces at the bottom and centre, packing tight vertically/horizontally to
   leave few voids; finish with smaller wood and kindling at the top.
3. Leave a lighting space in the centre or light from the top/ports, per your method.

## 4. Burn and read the smoke
1. Light with lid off or vented. Once burning well (fire established through the top),
   **place the lid** and seal the lid rim with sand/soil. Set chimneys on alternate ports.
2. **Thick white, damp smoke** — water being driven off. This is the long phase; leave air
   ports open.
3. **Yellowish, pungent smoke** — volatiles (tars). Carbonisation under way.
4. Watch for uneven burning: one side's chimney smoke thinning early → move chimneys to
   other ports to pull the fire there.
5. **Thin blue/clear smoke** from a chimney → that section is done. When all ports show
   blue, **seal**: remove chimneys, block every port and seal all joints with soil/sand.
Total burn is often 12–24 h for a ring kiln, depending on wood and weather.

## 5. Cool and unload
Leave sealed to cool — often 24 h or more. Opening hot charcoal lets air in and it
re-ignites. Check by hand on the steel (cool) before opening; have water handy. Unload
with gloves and dust mask.

## 6. Grade and bag
Riddle (screen) to remove fines (fines can be sold as biochar/soil amendment). Pick out
brands (brown, smoky) for the next burn. Good charcoal is black throughout, light,
rings when knocked together. Bag by weight; label with species and origin.

## Earth kiln/clamp variant
Stack wood in a mound, cover with leaves/straw then soil leaving vents; same air logic but
harder to control — tend it continuously, plug holes where it burns through.

## Gotchas
- Sealing too early is the most common error (brands); too late gives ash.
- Wind fans one side — shelter or rotate chimneys accordingly.
- Never sleep in or leave a vehicle near the kiln: CO accumulates; smoke can affect
  neighbours — check local rules on smoke and burning.

## Verify
- [ ] Wood dry and sized consistently.
- [ ] All ports showed thin blue smoke before sealing.
- [ ] Kiln fully sealed and cool before opening.
- [ ] Charcoal black throughout; brands recycled; yield recorded.

*Uncertain:* burn times and yields vary greatly with kiln design, wood and weather.
