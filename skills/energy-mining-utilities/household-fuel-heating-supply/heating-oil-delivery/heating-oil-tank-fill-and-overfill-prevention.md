# Heating oil tank fill and overfill prevention

**Goal:** put the ordered fuel into the right tank, metered correctly, with zero spill —
most domestic oil pollution incidents happen at delivery or from failed tanks.

## 1. Before you pull the hose
1. **Right address, right tank, right product.** Kerosene (28-second/home heating oil in
   UK/Ireland) vs gas oil (35-second/red diesel) vs heating oil #2 (US): wrong product can
   damage boilers. Confirm against the order and the tank label.
2. **Inspect the tank** (walk to it, don't assume):
   - Bund (secondary containment) present where required, not full of rainwater or oil.
   - Tank body: cracks, bulges, rust, weeping seams; plastic tanks discoloured, swollen or
     split — UV-aged plastic tanks fail suddenly.
   - Fill point capped and accessible; vent clear (wasps' nests and blocked vents cause
     pressurisation and blow-back).
   - Gauge working.
   - **Ghost fills:** abandoned fill pipes (old tank removed, pipe still on the wall) have
     put thousands of litres into basements. If a fill pipe doesn't lead to a visible tank
     you can verify, **don't fill**.
3. Estimate space: tank capacity (label) minus current contents (gauge/dip) — compare to
   the order. If the order won't fit, deliver what fits and note it.

## 2. During the fill
- Wheels chocked, handbrake on, PTO pump per vehicle procedure; spill kit and drip
  tray out.
- Lay hose without trip hazards or kinks; protect the customer's paths.
- **Stay with the nozzle the whole time** — never latch an auto-nozzle and walk away.
- Listen for the **vent whistle/alarm**; when it stops (or the overfill alarm sounds), stop
  immediately. Watch the gauge. Slow the flow near full.
- In US practice, a fill with no whistle (vent alarm missing or not working) is a red
  flag — confirm level another way or don't fill.

## 3. After
- Drain the hose back properly, recap the fill, wipe any drips.
- Record meter ticket litres/gallons (temperature-compensated where required), leave a
  ticket, note tank condition issues in writing for the customer.

## 4. If oil is spilled
1. Stop the flow; contain with spill kit pads/booms; block drains and keep it out of
   watercourses.
2. Don't wash it away with water or detergent.
3. Report immediately to your office and, where required, the environmental regulator/
   emergency line. Spills reaching drains, watercourses or soil usually need specialist
   clean-up and insurance notification.
4. Record photos, volumes and times.

## Gotchas
- Gauges on old tanks stick; a gauge reading "half" on a full tank causes overfills — dip
  where possible.
- Tanks inside buildings/basements are higher risk; their vents must terminate outside.
- Fuel sitting on the customer's sight-tube valve (left open) can leak — close sight tubes.
- Cold weather: kerosene waxing isn't usual, but gas oil can wax/gel; additives per supplier.

## Verify
- [ ] Product, tank and address verified; tank inspected including bund and vent.
- [ ] Fill pipe confirmed to connect to a real, visible tank.
- [ ] Driver attended the nozzle throughout; stopped at whistle/alarm.
- [ ] Meter ticket matches order; tank issues reported in writing.
- [ ] Any spill contained, reported and recorded.

*Uncertain:* product names, bunding rules and reporting thresholds differ by country.
