# Fire lighting by ferro rod and bow drill

**Goal:** get a sustainable fire from a spark or a friction ember, reliably, in less than
ideal conditions. Most failures are not the ignition — they're poor tinder and not enough
prepared fuel.

## 1. Prepare everything before making a spark
Gather three piles, each **more than you think you need**:
1. **Tinder** — fine, dry, fluffy: birch bark (burns even damp due to oils), dry grass,
   cattail/thistle down, fatwood shavings, cotton wool with petroleum jelly, char cloth.
2. **Kindling** — pencil-lead thickness up to thumb thickness, dead and snapping crisp
   (dead wood still on the tree or off the ground is drier than ground litter).
3. **Fuel** — wrist thickness and up.
**Feather sticks:** shave long curls down a dry stick without detaching them; split wet
wood to reach the dry core.

## 2. Ferro rod
- Scrape the black coating off a new rod first.
- Hold the striker still on the rod close to the tinder and **pull the rod back** rather
  than pushing the striker forward — sparks land in the tinder and you don't scatter it.
- Firm pressure, steep angle, slow controlled stroke throws hot, large sparks.
- Tinder that is fluffed and shielded from wind catches; flat, compacted tinder doesn't.

## 3. Bow drill
Kit: **hearth board** and **spindle** from the same dry, soft-medium, non-resinous wood
(willow, lime/basswood, cedar, poplar, ivy; hazel works when dry), a **bearing block** (hard
wood, stone with a hollow, or a lubricated socket), and a bow with cord.
1. Spindle ~ thumb-thick, ~ 20–25 cm, straight; blunt point at bottom, sharper at top
   (less friction in the bearing).
2. Carve a starting depression near the edge of the hearth, burn it in, then cut a
   **V-notch** to the centre of the burned hole (about 1/8 of the circle). The notch
   collects hot dust.
3. Kneel with your foot on the hearth beside the hole, wrist locked against your shin,
   spindle vertical. Long, full strokes of the bow; start steady, then add speed and
   downward pressure once it smokes.
4. Keep going 10–20 seconds after heavy smoke. Dark brown/black dust filling the notch
   and smoking on its own = ember.
5. Let it consolidate, then tip it into the tinder bundle, fold the bundle around it, and
   **blow gently and steadily** until flame. Then place it in the prepared fire lay.
Grey/light dust = not hot enough (faster, more pressure); squealing and glazing = add
grit or re-cut; spindle flipping out = bearing too rough or not vertical.

## 4. Fire lay
- **Teepee** for quick catch: tinder in centre, kindling leaned around, leave a gap on
  the windward side to light and feed oxygen.
- **Log cabin / platform** on wet or snowy ground: a base of sticks keeps the fire off the
  damp.
- Add kindling progressively; don't smother it. Thumb-thick sticks catching = add fuel.

## 5. Safety and impact
Clear ground of leaves to mineral soil, check local fire bans and land permission, keep it
small, keep water/soil ready, and drown-stir-feel until cold before leaving.

## Gotchas
- Green or damp kindling is the usual failure, not the spark.
- Breathing hard on a small ember blows it apart — gentle long breaths.
- Plastic-rich "survival" tinder is fine; avoid treated or painted wood.

## Verify
- [ ] All three fuel piles ready before ignition.
- [ ] Ferro rod pulled back into tinder, not tinder scattered.
- [ ] Bow drill produces dark dust that keeps smoking on its own.
- [ ] Fire reaches thumb-thick fuel without restarting.
- [ ] Fire fully extinguished (cold to the touch) before leaving.
