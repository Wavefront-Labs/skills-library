# LPG cylinder exchange and bulk tank delivery

**Goal:** get propane or butane into the customer's system without a leak, overfill or
fire — and recognise the installations you should not fill.

## 1. LPG basics that drive the rules
- Stored as a liquid under pressure; vapour is **heavier than air** — it pools in drains,
  cellars and low ground. Ignition sources at ground level matter.
- Liquid expands a lot with temperature, hence fill limits leaving vapour space (ullage).
- Odorised (ethyl mercaptan) so leaks can be smelt — but odour can fade in new or rusty
  tanks ("odour fade"); use a detector, not just your nose.
- Cold burns: liquid LPG on skin causes frostbite — gloves and eye protection.

## 2. Cylinder exchange at a customer
1. Customer appliances off; cylinder valve closed on the empty before disconnecting.
2. Check the cylinder location: outdoors or ventilated, upright, on firm level ground, not
   below ground level or near drains/cellar openings, away from ignition sources (distances
   per local code). Butane/propane indoors only where codes and appliance allow.
3. Inspect the full cylinder you bring: in-date test/requalification mark, valve guard,
   no dents/corrosion/fire damage, seal/cap in place.
4. Check regulator and pigtail/hose: correct gas (butane vs propane regulators differ),
   washer/O-ring present and undamaged, hose not cracked or out of date. Replace if doubtful.
5. Connect (left-hand threads on many propane connections — check), open valve slowly,
   **leak test** with leak-detection solution or detector at all joints. Never a flame.
6. Secure cylinder (chain/strap) where required. Show customer how to shut off.

## 3. Bulk tank delivery
1. Park per company procedure: engine off when transferring (unless PTO pump), wheels
   chocked, earthing/bonding if required, hazard zone clear of ignition sources, extinguisher out.
2. **Inspect the tank before filling**: corrosion, damage, tank stable on supports, valves
   and relief valve caps intact, signage, no combustibles or vegetation build-up, distances
   to buildings and boundaries still compliant (new sheds/extensions creep closer).
3. Read the float gauge, then fill using the **fixed liquid level gauge (bleeder)** —
   open it; when it spits white liquid mist instead of vapour, the tank is at its maximum
   (commonly ~80–85% depending on code/tank). Stop. Float gauges alone are not reliable for
   the final fill.
4. Close valves, disconnect hose (small liquid release — face away), check for leaks at the
   fill valve.
5. If the tank was **empty (out of gas)**, many codes require a leak/pressure check of the
   customer's system and relighting of pilots by a qualified person before supply — don't
   just fill and leave.

## 4. Refuse or tag out when
Smell of gas with no explanation, damaged tank or cylinder, overdue requalification,
encroached safety distances, unsafe access, or an appliance/system you suspect is
leaking. Report per your company process.

## Gotchas
- A tank "full" by float gauge may still be overfilled — always use the bleeder.
- Mixing butane regulators on propane cylinders over-pressures appliances.
- Hot day + full tank = relief valve venting; respect the fill limit.

## Verify
- [ ] Cylinder/tank inspected and in test date before supply.
- [ ] Location meets distance and drainage rules.
- [ ] Bulk fill stopped at fixed liquid level gauge.
- [ ] All joints leak-tested with solution/detector.
- [ ] Out-of-gas systems checked before re-supply; customer briefed.

*Uncertain:* fill limits, distances and out-of-gas procedures vary by code (NFPA 58, UKLPG,
national rules) — follow your company's procedures.
