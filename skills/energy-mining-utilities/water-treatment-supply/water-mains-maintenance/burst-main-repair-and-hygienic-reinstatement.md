# Burst main repair and hygienic reinstatement

**Goal:** stop the leak, fix the pipe so it lasts, and put it back into supply without
letting contamination into drinking water.

## 1. Make safe and isolate
1. Protect the public: traffic management, barriers, keep people off undermined surfaces.
2. Identify the main (size, material, age) from records; locate other services (gas, power,
   telecom) with records **and** a cable locator before digging.
3. Isolate with the fewest valves that do the job; record each valve and turns. Warn
   sensitive customers. If possible keep slight positive pressure while exposing the break
   so dirty trench water can't be sucked into the main — then isolate fully to fit.
4. Pump the trench out continuously; water in the trench must stay **below** the pipe
   when it is open.

## 2. Excavate
Hand-dig near the main and other services. Shore or batter the trench per the depth rule
your jurisdiction uses (commonly >1.2–1.5 m needs support). Spoil back from the edge.

## 3. Choose the repair
- **Circumferential crack, small hole, pinhole:** stainless full-circle repair clamp sized
  to the pipe's *measured* OD (measure — cast iron and PVC ODs differ for the same nominal).
- **Longitudinal split, crushed or very corroded pipe:** cut out a section and insert a new
  piece with two couplings/flange adaptors. Restrain joints near bends if thrust is a risk.
- Clean the pipe surface to sound metal where the clamp gasket seats. Centre the clamp
  over the defect and tighten bolts evenly, alternating, to the maker's torque.

## 4. Hygiene during the job
- Fittings and new pipe stay capped/bagged and off the ground until fitted.
- Before fitting, swab or spray the inside of new pipe and fittings, and the exposed cut
  ends, with a chlorine solution (hygiene codes typically specify ~1% / 10,000 mg/L for
  spray/swab of fittings). Clean tools and gloves the same way.
- Any foreign matter seen inside the main = flush longer and sample before supply.

## 5. Recharge, flush, sample
1. Open the upstream valve **slowly** while venting air at a hydrant/washout downstream;
   trapped air causes surges and milky water complaints.
2. Flush until clear water and normal chlorine residual — at least several pipe volumes
   of the affected length.
3. Take bacteriological samples per policy (often one downstream of the repair). Where
   the main was fully depressurised with contamination likely, many utilities require a
   satisfactory result or a full disinfection before return to supply.
4. Open all valves fully, check for leaks at the repair under full pressure before
   backfilling.

## 6. Reinstate and hand off
Backfill in compacted layers with suitable material, surround pipe with sand/fine
material, mark with warning tape. Hand surface reinstatement to the roads team with
measurements and photos. Update the asset record: cause, material, repair type, GPS.

## Gotchas
- Old cast iron cracks again near the first break when you excavate or tighten unevenly.
- Closing too many valves creates low-pressure zones elsewhere and backflow risk.
- A repair clamp on the wrong OD range seals at first then weeps.

## Verify
- [ ] Valves recorded and all returned open; no zone left with low pressure.
- [ ] Trench kept dewatered below pipe while open.
- [ ] Fittings swabbed/sprayed with chlorine before fitting.
- [ ] Main flushed with normal chlorine residual; samples taken per policy.
- [ ] No leak at full pressure before backfill; asset record updated.

*Uncertain:* chlorine strengths and the sample-before-supply rule vary by utility and
country — follow your hygiene code.
