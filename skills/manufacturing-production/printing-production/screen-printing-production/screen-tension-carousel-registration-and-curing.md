# Production screen printing: tension, registration and curing

**Goal:** print multi-colour jobs on an automatic press at production speed with
consistent registration, and prove every piece is fully cured. For hand/studio
technique see handcrafts.hand-printing.screen-printing.photo-emulsion-screens-registration-and-curing;
this skill is the production version.

## 1. Screens: consistency is everything
- **Mesh count** by job: lower counts (e.g. ~110–160 threads/inch) for underbases and
  heavy plastisol on garments; higher (~200–305) for fine detail and halftones;
  graphics and industrial work often finer still. Match ink supplier recommendations.
- **Tension:** measure with a tension meter (N/cm) at several points; retensionable or
  static frames per shop standard. Typical production targets are roughly in the
  **20–35 N/cm** range depending on mesh and frame — the point is that **all screens in
  a job are similar** so they behave alike. Low tension → poor print release,
  smearing, registration that wanders.
- **Emulsion and exposure:** coat consistently (same coater, same number of coats
  print/squeegee side), dry fully in the dark, find exposure time with an exposure
  calculator/step test per mesh-emulsion combination. Underexposed stencils break down
  mid-run.
- Use pin registration (film and screen on a pre-registration system) so screens
  arrive at the press nearly in register.

## 2. Press setup (automatic carousel)
1. Load screens in print order (e.g. underbase → flash → highlight white → colours).
2. **Off-contact:** a small gap (often ~1–3 mm) between screen and substrate so the
   mesh peels away behind the squeegee. Too much → distortion, misregistration; too
   little → smearing.
3. **Squeegee:** durometer (e.g. 70 for general, 80 or triple-ply for detail),
   sharp edge, angle (~10–15° from vertical), pressure just enough to clear the mesh.
   Over-pressure drives ink through, spreads the print and wears stencils.
4. **Flood stroke** fills the stencil before the print stroke; set flood bar so
   it doesn't press ink through.
5. **Registration:** print a test on a pallet sheet or test shirt, correct each head's
   micro-adjusters (X, Y, rotation) against the key colour, re-print until marks align.

## 3. Flash and curing
- **Flash cure** between colours (especially after a white underbase) just gels the
  surface so the next screen doesn't pick it up. Over-flashing makes the next layer
  adhere poorly; watch pallet heat build-up as the press warms (reduce flash time
  as it does).
- **Final cure** in the conveyor dryer: the **ink film** must reach the supplier's
  cure temperature for the full film thickness — plastisol is commonly specified
  around **150–165 °C (300–330 °F)**; water-based and discharge inks need the water
  driven off too, which takes longer dwell.
- Measure: thermolabel/temperature strips or a dryer data logger (donut probe) run
  through on a piece. Air temperature in the dryer is not ink temperature.

## 4. Proving cure
- **Stretch test:** stretch a printed area; under-cured plastisol cracks and doesn't
  recover.
- **Wash test** on a sample per run or per shift (and after any change of ink, garment
  or dryer setting): wash per care label, check cracking, fading, peeling.
- For industrial parts, use the specified adhesion test (e.g. cross-hatch tape test)
  and solvent rub where specified.

## 5. Running and reconciling
- Check every Nth piece against the approved strike-off for registration and colour.
- Count in, count good, count spoiled — blanks are often customer-supplied and must
  be accounted for.

## Gotchas
- Dye migration on polyester: red/black dyes bleed into white ink when overheated —
  use low-cure, bleed-blocker inks and keep cure temperature controlled.
- Pallet adhesive build-up lifts garments mid-print; renew it.
- A screen that "fixed itself" mid-run usually had stencil breakdown — check it.

## Verify
- [ ] All screens in the job within a narrow tension band; exposure from a step test.
- [ ] Off-contact, squeegee angle/pressure set to the minimum that prints clean.
- [ ] Registration aligned to the key colour on a test piece.
- [ ] Ink film temperature measured, not just dryer setpoint.
- [ ] Stretch/wash (or adhesion) test passed and recorded.
