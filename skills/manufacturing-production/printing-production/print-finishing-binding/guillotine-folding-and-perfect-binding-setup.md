# Finishing: guillotine, folding and perfect binding setup

**Goal:** turn printed sheets into accurately trimmed, folded and bound products that
pass inspection, without damaging work or people. Postpress is where a good print job
is most often spoiled.

## 1. Guillotine cutting
- **Safety first:** two-hand controls and light curtains must work — never defeat them;
  knife changes only with the manufacturer's knife-change tools and procedure, two
  people where required. A guillotine knife is lethal.
- **Program the cuts** (Polar and similar program memories): first cut a straight
  reference edge (gripper edge and side lay edge are the printed references), then
  cut to marks. Save programs per job for repeats.
- **Lift height:** smaller lifts cut more accurately; tall lifts of coated/slippery
  stock "draw" (bottom sheets cut shorter than the top). Check top and bottom sheets.
- **Clamp pressure:** enough to stop movement; too much marks soft or coated stock and
  leaves clamp marks on laminated work. Use a clamp cover/false clamp plate on
  sensitive work.
- **Cutting stick:** rotate/turn when the knife cut line gets deep; a worn stick
  causes unclean bottom sheets.
- Dull knife symptoms: ragged edges, increased draw, dust. Track cuts per knife.

## 2. Folding (buckle and knife folders)
- **Grain direction:** fold parallel to the grain where possible. Folding across
  grain on heavier stock (roughly above ~150–170 gsm, depending on stock) cracks —
  especially through heavy ink or coating.
- **Score/crease** before folding heavy or coated sheets: score on the folder with
  scoring wheels or offline with a creaser; fold so the score bead is on the inside
  of the fold.
- **Buckle plates:** set stops to fold length, sheet guides square; adjust fold-roll
  gap to stock caliper (too tight crushes/marks, too loose skews). Test feed several
  sheets, check fold position against the dummy, then run.
- Static and humidity: sheets that stick or double feed — condition stock, adjust air.

## 3. Saddle-stitching
- Signatures collated in order; stitch heads set to wire gauge and spine thickness;
  clinchers form flat legs on the inside.
- Check **creep** compensation from imposition (inner pages trim narrower); confirm
  margins on a trimmed copy.
- Check stitch position relative to the spine and head/foot (typically 2 stitches
  roughly a quarter in from each end for A4 — follow job spec).

## 4. Perfect binding
1. **Gathered book block** (check collation marks along spine — a stepped line shows
   order).
2. **Spine milling** removes the folds so every leaf is glued; then **notching or
   roughening** gives glue penetration. Mill depth enough to reach every leaf
   (typically ~2–3 mm).
3. **Adhesive:**
   - **EVA hotmelt** — fast, cheap; weaker on coated papers and in cold/hot
     conditions.
   - **PUR** — much stronger, flexible, better for coated stock and lay-flat; needs
     moisture-cure time (often ~24 h to full strength) before final trimming/shipping
     per supplier, and strict temperature and nozzle/tank management.
4. Cover scoring at the hinges and spine; cover aligned and nipped.
5. **Three-knife trim** after the glue has set enough per adhesive type.

## 5. Testing bindings
- **Page pull test:** pull-test a page with a pull tester (N/cm of page length), from
  several positions (front, middle, back). Compare to the shop/customer minimum; PUR
  tested only after cure.
- Flex test: open the book flat several times; check for pages loosening or cracking.

## Gotchas
- Cutting before ink is dry → set-off and smearing under clamp pressure.
- PUR left in the pot over a weekend can cure solid; follow purge/shutdown procedures.
- Wrong grain on the book block → wavy pages and cover warp.

## Verify
- [ ] Guillotine safeties checked at start of shift; reference edges cut first.
- [ ] Top and bottom sheets of a lift measured for draw.
- [ ] Folding with/along grain or scored; fold position checked to dummy.
- [ ] Creep compensation confirmed on trimmed saddle-stitched copies.
- [ ] Pull test results recorded after appropriate cure time.

*Draft note:* gsm thresholds, mill depth and cure times are typical; machine and
adhesive suppliers' data govern.
