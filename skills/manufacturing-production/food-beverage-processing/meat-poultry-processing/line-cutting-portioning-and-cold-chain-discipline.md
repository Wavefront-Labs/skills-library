# Line cutting, portioning and cold-chain discipline

**Goal:** cut to specification at line speed without injuring yourself, while keeping
product cold and clean. Yield, safety and microbiology all live in the same hands here.

## 1. Knife safety and the edge
- **PPE:** cut-resistant/mesh glove on the non-knife hand (and mesh apron/arm guard for
  boning toward the body), steel-toe non-slip boots. Chainmail must fit — loose gloves get caught.
- **Steel often, sharpen rarely**: a few light strokes on the steel every few minutes keeps
  the edge aligned; a blunt knife needs force and slips. Many plants have sharpening done
  centrally; know your swap routine.
- Cut **away from yourself** where possible; don't reach across a neighbour's station.
- Knives in a scabbard or sterilizer when not cutting; never on the belt.
- Knife **sterilisers** (≥82 °C water, common rule in many regulations; check local) —
  dip between carcasses or after contamination.

## 2. Cutting to spec for yield
- Learn the **spec sheet**: trim level (fat cover in mm), cuts included, bone-in/out, weight range.
- Over-trimming gives away saleable meat; under-trimming fails customer spec. Supervisors
  usually measure yield per operator or per line — accurate cutting is money.
- Trimmings are graded by **chemical lean** (CL, e.g. 85CL, 65CL) — mixing grades hurts value.
- Check for bone fragments, cartilage, blood clots, abscesses and foreign bodies;
  remove and report anything unusual (abscess → contaminated knife → sterilise).

## 3. Ergonomics at pace
- Stand square at the right working height; adjust platform if fitted.
- Rotate stations as scheduled; repetitive strain (carpal tunnel, tendinitis) is the
  dominant injury in boning rooms. Report early tingling/pain — early is treatable.
- Let a sharp knife do the work; grip pressure is the main risk factor.

## 4. Portioners and injectors
- **Portioners** (waterjet, blade; e.g. Marel I-Cut, JBT DSI) scan each piece and cut to
  target weight/shape. Check sample weights against target each hour (or per spec); scan
  accuracy drops if belts are wet or product is too warm and soft.
- **Brine injectors**: needle count, pressure and belt speed set **pump %** (weight gain).
  Weigh before/after on a sample tray. Blocked or bent needles cause streaky, uneven
  injection — inspect and clean.
- **Tumblers/massagers**: vacuum level, time and rpm per recipe.
- Lock-out before clearing any jam. Portioner waterjets and blades cause amputations.

## 5. Cold chain
- **Carcass chilling**: target deep muscle temperature before cutting — EU red meat rule
  is ≤7 °C (with derogations for hot-boning or transport conditions); poultry ≤4 °C.
- Boning/cutting rooms typically kept ≤12 °C (EU requirement for cutting rooms or
  equivalent controls).
- Product in the room moves **first in, first out**; no stacked crates waiting by the line.
- Probe product temperature at the frequency on the HACCP plan; out-of-limit product is
  held, not hurried into the chiller.
- Packed product into chillers/blast freezers within the set time; no doors propped open.

## 6. Hygiene
- Wash/sanitise hands, gloves and aprons at every break and between tasks; boot wash at entry.
- Don't let product touch the floor; dropped product is dealt with per SOP (often
  condemned or trimmed under supervision).
- Pathogens to keep in mind: Salmonella and Campylobacter (poultry), E. coli O157 (beef),
  Listeria (ready-to-eat and cold, wet environments).

## Gotchas
- Using a dull knife "for one more carcass".
- Glove with a broken ring — replace, don't tape.
- Leaving trays of cut meat waiting at room temperature when the packing line stops.
- Carrying a knife off the line or wearing mesh gloves near rotating machinery (entanglement).

## Verify
- [ ] PPE fitted and intact; knife sharp and steeled; sterilisers at temperature.
- [ ] Cuts meet spec on supervisor yield/trim checks.
- [ ] Portion/injection sample weights within tolerance.
- [ ] Product temperatures logged and in limit; no waiting crates.
- [ ] No unreported pain or near-misses.
