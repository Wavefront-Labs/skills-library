# Aseptic technique and cleanroom behaviour

**Goal:** you never become the contamination source. In aseptic processing the
product is not sterilised after filling, so operator behaviour is a critical control.
The governing text in Europe is EU GMP Annex 1 (revised 2022, applicable from 25 August 2023 and fully applicable since 25 August 2024);
the FDA aseptic processing guidance (October 2004) covers similar ground in the US.

## 1. Grades in one line each
- **Grade A**: the critical zone — open product, stoppers,
  filling needles. Unidirectional (laminar) air; take the ISO class and airflow velocity from Annex 1 (gated: unconfirmed, source is paywalled or blocked). The Annex 1 PDF could not be read as text.
- **Grade B**: the background room for Grade A in a RABS/open line; full sterile gowning.
- **Grade C/D**: preparation areas with less stringent gowning.
An isolator's interior is Grade A; its background can be Grade C or D.

## 2. Gowning (Grade B)
- Follow the posted sequence and the step-over bench rules exactly — your gowning
  qualification (initial and periodic, with glove/gown contact plates) proves you can.
- Sterile hood, mask, goggles, one-piece suit, boots, two pairs of sterile gloves are
  typical; no skin exposed; mirror check at the end.
- Sanitise gloves with the sterile agent your SOP names, frequently and after touching anything non-sterile.
- If a gown tears or a glove is breached: leave the area and re-gown. No improvising.

## 3. Movement and first air
- **First air** = air arriving at the critical site before touching anything. Never put
  your hand, arm or a tool between the HEPA filter and open product/components.
- Move **slowly and deliberately**; fast motion disturbs airflow and sheds particles.
- Work from the side; never lean over open vials or the stopper bowl.
- Minimise people and talking in the room; stay out of Grade A unless intervening.
- Don't touch your face, mask or goggles; don't rest against walls or equipment.

## 4. Isolators and RABS
- **Glove integrity** checks (visual every use, leak test per schedule — often per batch
  or campaign; site-specific). A failed glove after a batch makes that batch an investigation.
- Use sterile tools for anything inside; gloves are not tools for touching product-contact parts.
- RABS doors stay closed during filling; an opened door is a documented intervention and
  usually requires additional disinfection and line clearance per procedure.
- Know the isolator's decontamination cycle (typically vaporised hydrogen peroxide) and
  don't open transfer ports outside the validated sequence.

## 5. Interventions
- **Inherent** (planned, e.g. setup, weight checks) vs **corrective** (fixing a fallen
  vial, a jam). Only interventions in the written list and qualified in the media fill
  are allowed; everything else is a stop-and-ask.
- Record each intervention: time, type, who, duration. Annex 1 expects review of
  interventions as part of batch release.
- Fallen vials in Grade A are generally removed with sterile tongs and the affected units
  rejected per SOP — never set them back upright onto the line.

## 6. Media fills and environmental monitoring
- **Media fill / APS:** a run filling growth medium instead of product, including the worst-case
  interventions and the maximum number of people. You must participate to stay qualified
  (at the frequency in your site's qualification procedure).
- **EM:** settle plates in place throughout filling (don't move or cover them), contact
  plates on surfaces, **glove prints at exit from Grade A interventions**, plus particle
  counters.
- Handle plates with sterile technique and label time/location; a dropped plate is reported, not quietly replaced.

## Gotchas
- Spraying IPA directly onto product-contact parts or towards open vials.
- Hurrying an intervention because the line is stopped — that's when contamination happens.
- Forgetting your own glove print after an intervention.
- Sneezing/coughing inside: turn away from Grade A, then re-sanitise and report.

## Verify
- [ ] Gowning and aseptic qualifications current, media fill participation in date.
- [ ] No body part crossed first air during the shift.
- [ ] Every intervention was on the approved list and is recorded.
- [ ] Gloves (isolator/RABS) checked and any breach reported.
- [ ] EM samples taken at the required points and handed over correctly labelled.
