# Rotary screen registration and colour kitchen

**Goal:** a printed run that matches the approved strike-off — every colour in
register, shades consistent from first metre to last, fixed and washed so it passes
fastness. Rotary screen machines (Stork/SPGPrints, Zimmer, Reggiani) print many colours
at speed, so set-up errors make a lot of seconds very fast.

## 1. Before the machine: the colour kitchen
- Print paste = colourant (pigment or dye) + **thickener** + auxiliaries (binder for
  pigment; urea, alkali, etc. for reactive). Weigh by recipe on calibrated scales or
  an automatic dispensing system.
- **Viscosity** is the paste's key control: measure (Brookfield-type viscometer) and
  record. Too thin → flushing/bleeding and spread lines; too thick → poor penetration,
  screen blocking.
- Match each colour to the approved standard on the **same fabric** (drawdown or a
  sample print), and check against the strike-off under the specified illuminant.
- Label every bucket with design, colourway, screen position and batch. Wrong bucket
  in the right position is a classic fault.
- Track leftover paste for reuse in close shades — saves cost, but only with records.

## 2. Screens and blanket
- Check each screen for damage (pinholes, dents), correct mesh for the design (fine
  detail vs heavy blotch), end rings secure.
- Screens mounted in **design order** per the job sheet (light to dark is common, but
  follow the separation plan).
- **Blanket** (printing belt): clean, adhesive layer good — fabric must stick flat and
  not move, or registration drifts. Re-glue when adhesion weakens.
- Fabric prepared to print: width stable, pre-treated (for reactive/digital), no creases.

## 3. Registration
1. Set the first screen as the reference.
2. Align each subsequent screen to it using register marks/crosses on the screen
   design: **longitudinal** (in repeat, along the run) and **lateral** (across).
3. Fine-tune at slow speed with short fabric lengths; check by eye with magnifier at
   register marks and at design fits (outline to fill).
4. Re-check after reaching production speed — registration often shifts with speed
   (fabric stretch, blanket speed).
- Registration problems that come and go → fabric slipping on blanket, uneven
  tension, or a screen drive issue.

## 4. Squeegee and pressure
Blade squeegee or **magnetic rod**: pressure (magnet strength level), rod diameter and
speed decide paste amount. Heavier pressure → more paste, deeper penetration, risk of
flushing. Adjust per colour position to match the strike-off.

## 5. Strike-off and approval
Print a **strike-off** length, dry/fix it as bulk would be, compare against the approved
standard for shade, registration and handle. Customer or QC approval before running.

## 6. Fixation and wash-off
- **Pigment**: curing (dry heat, time/temperature per binder supplier) — under-cure
  fails **rubbing fastness**.
- **Reactive/acid/disperse**: steaming (saturated or superheated) then washing-off to
  remove thickener and unfixed dye — poor washing gives staining and harsh handle.
- Test fastness (rubbing, washing) on a sample from each lot.

## 7. Digital printing note
Inkjet textile printers rely on **pre-treatment** of fabric, colour management (RIP,
ICC profile for that fabric/ink) and head maintenance (nozzle checks). Shade variation
between machines is handled through profiles, not paste.

## Defects
| Defect | Cause |
|---|---|
| Out of register | Screen setup, fabric moving on blanket, tension/speed |
| Flushing/bleeding | Paste too thin, too much pressure, wet fabric |
| Streaks along run | Squeegee/rod damage, blocked screen area |
| Spots | Paste contamination, dried paste lumps |
| Shade drift | Paste viscosity change, reuse of different paste |

## Verify
- [ ] Every bucket labelled and viscosity recorded.
- [ ] Registration checked at production speed.
- [ ] Strike-off approved before bulk.
- [ ] Fixation conditions logged; rub/wash fastness pass on lot sample.
