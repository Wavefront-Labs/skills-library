# Running a waste walk and a kaizen event

**Goal:** turn "we should be more lean" into measured, sustained improvement on one
process. This skill covers the shop-floor end: seeing waste at the gemba and running a
focused kaizen event. For mapping the whole value stream first, see
engineering-technology.industrial-manufacturing-systems-engineering.lean-six-sigma.value-stream-mapping-and-flow-design.

## 1. The seven wastes (and the eighth)
Remember them as **TIMWOODS**:
- **T**ransport — moving material further than needed (forklift shuttles, distant stores).
- **I**nventory — WIP piles between stations, raw material for weeks.
- **M**otion — operator reaching, bending, walking, searching.
- **W**aiting — operator watching a machine, machine waiting on material or approval.
- **O**verproduction — making ahead of demand; the worst waste because it hides the others.
- **O**verprocessing — tighter tolerance, extra polishing, double inspection nobody needs.
- **D**efects — scrap, rework, sorting.
- **S**kills — unused ideas and abilities of the people doing the work (the
  modern "eighth" waste).

## 2. The waste walk
- Go to the place, at normal production, with a clipboard — not a meeting room.
- Pick **one** process or product flow and follow it; stand in one spot for 20–30 min
  (Ohno's "chalk circle") and watch.
- For each observation note: where, which waste, rough size (seconds, metres, pieces),
  and a photo. Count WIP between stations; time a cycle; pace out walking distances.
- **Ask the operators** what slows them down. They know; they are rarely asked.
- Don't fix or criticise during the walk — you are collecting facts.
Rank findings by impact × ease. The top one, if it's bigger than a quick fix, becomes
a kaizen event.

## 3. Scoping the kaizen event
Write a one-page charter (A3 format works):
- **Scope:** one cell/line/process, with clear start and end points.
- **Problem and baseline:** e.g. "Cell 4 makes 38 units/hr vs 45 target; WIP 140 pcs;
  lead time 2.5 days". Collect the baseline *before* the event.
- **Target:** measurable, e.g. 45/hr, WIP <30.
- **Team:** 5–8 people — operators from the area (at least half), maintenance, a
  quality person, an outsider with fresh eyes, a facilitator.
- **Sponsor** who can approve changes and spend on the spot, and **maintenance
  support booked** for the week.

## 4. The event (typical 3–5 days)
1. **Day 1 — learn and observe:** short training on waste, then everyone goes to the
   gemba, times cycles, draws a spaghetti diagram and a current-state layout.
2. **Day 2 — analyse:** root causes (5 whys, fishbone), brainstorm countermeasures,
   pick the ones to try. Prefer simple, cheap, reversible.
3. **Day 3–4 — try it for real:** move benches, re-sequence work, set up a one-piece
   flow cell or a kanban loop, build cardboard mock-ups before steel. Run production and
   measure. Iterate (PDCA: plan, do, check, act).
4. **Day 5 — standardise and report:** new standard work and layout, 5S, visual
   controls; report out to management on the floor with before/after numbers; list of
   open actions with owners and dates.

## 5. Sustain
- **30-day action list** reviewed weekly; most kaizen gains are lost when the "to be
  finished" items never are.
- Re-measure at 30 and 90 days against baseline. Put the KPI on the area's board.
- Leaders keep doing gemba walks to see the new standard is being followed.

## Gotchas
- **Don't cut heads as a result.** If a kaizen frees people and they are made
  redundant, nobody will join the next one. Redeploy freed capacity.
- One-piece flow exposes every problem previously hidden by WIP — expect more stops in
  week one, and fix them rather than putting the WIP back.
- Events without baseline data end in opinion, not results.

## Verify
- [ ] Waste walk sheet has quantified observations by waste type.
- [ ] Charter has baseline, target, scope and sponsor before day 1.
- [ ] Changes tested in live production with measured results.
- [ ] New standard work posted; 30/90-day re-measure booked.
