# Pick-to-light kitting, count accuracy and protective packing

**Goal:** every kit or carton contains exactly the right items, in the right quantity,
undamaged on arrival, with the right label — at the station's pace. A mispick costs far
more downstream (return, reship, line stoppage at the customer) than it saves.

## 1. Pick-to-light and put-to-light
- **Pick-to-light:** a light on the bin shows *what* to pick and a display *how many*.
  Pick the quantity, then **press the confirm button** on that bin. Confirming before
  picking, or confirming a different bin "to clear it", breaks the system's check.
- **Put-to-light:** light shows which order/kit position to put the item into.
- Work the lights in the order they light; if a light is on and the bin is empty or the
  part looks wrong, press the **short/exception** function or call the team leader — don't
  substitute.
- Wrong pick? Use the system's correction, so stock records stay right.

## 2. Paper or screen pick lists
- Check the **part number and revision**, not just the description or picture —
  look-alike parts are where errors happen.
- Tick each line as you pick; don't pick from memory.
- Where the station has a scanner, **scan each item** (or the bin label) before it goes
  in the kit; the system then catches wrong parts.

## 3. Getting the count right
- Small counts: count into your hand, then into the kit, in fixed groups (e.g. fives).
- **Weigh counting** for many small parts (screws, washers): sample a known count (at
  least 10–25 pieces; more for light or variable parts) to set the piece weight, tare
  the container, then count by weight. Re-sample on each new lot — piece weight
  varies between lots and suppliers. Small parts lighter than the scale's resolution
  can't be weigh-counted reliably.
- **Kit verification:** check weight of the finished kit against expected weight, or a
  shadow tray / cut-out insert where every item has a place so a gap is obvious.

## 4. Protective packing
- **Carton size:** the smallest box that fits product plus the cushioning it needs.
  Too big = crushing and wasted void fill; too small = no protection at the edges.
- **Cushioning/void fill:** paper, air pillows, foam, moulded pulp, corrugated
  inserts. Fill voids so contents don't move when the box is shaken. Fragile items: at
  least a few cm of cushioning on all sides and away from corners (exact amounts depend
  on product fragility; follow the pack specification).
- Heavy items at the bottom; separate items that can damage each other.
- Liquids: closures tight, upright, bagged with absorbent if required.
- Seal with tape across all seams per spec (commonly "H" taping for heavier cartons).
- The pack spec may be validated by **drop testing** (e.g. ISTA procedures) — don't
  change materials or carton without approval.

## 5. Labelling the carton
- Print the label for **this** order from the system; check the order number on the
  label matches the packing slip and the items in the box.
- Place the shipping label on the largest flat face, not over a seam or edge; keep
  barcodes flat and unwrinkled; remove or cover old labels on reused boxes.
- Hazard labels (batteries, flammables) where required.
- Scan the label at pack-out where the system asks; that "closes" the order.

## 6. Pace without errors
- Keep the station 5S: one order on the bench at a time, finished cartons off the
  bench immediately. Two open orders = swapped contents.
- If you lose count, **start that line again** — don't guess.

## Verify
- [ ] Every pick confirmed after picking (or scanned).
- [ ] Weigh-count sample reset for each lot; kit check weight or shadow tray passed.
- [ ] Carton right-sized; contents don't move when shaken.
- [ ] Label order number = packing slip = contents; label flat on top face.
- [ ] Only one order open on the bench.
