# Surface prep, bead application and open time at a bonding station

**Goal:** a bond or seal that reaches full strength and doesn't leak — every unit. Most
adhesive failures in production are not the adhesive: they are a dirty surface, a broken
bead, a bad mix or a part assembled after the adhesive had skinned over. The adhesive's
**technical data sheet (TDS)** and the work instruction set the numbers.

## 1. Before you start
- Check the adhesive is the specified product, **within its shelf life**, and has been
  stored as required (some are refrigerated and must reach room temperature *before
  opening* to avoid condensation). Record the lot number for traceability.
- Read the safety data sheet: gloves (nitrile for most; check), ventilation for
  solvents and isocyanates, eye protection. Cyanoacrylates bond skin quickly.
- Know the three times from the TDS:
  - **Open time** — after applying, how long before you must close the joint.
  - **Pot life** (2K, mixed) — how long mixed material stays usable.
  - **Fixture/handling time** and **full cure** — when it can be moved, and when it
    reaches full strength.

## 2. Surface preparation
- **Clean:** wipe with the specified solvent (often IPA) on a lint-free cloth, *one
  direction*, with a clean face of the cloth each pass, then let it flash off. Wiping
  back and forth spreads contamination. Don't touch the cleaned surface: skin oil
  ruins bonds.
- **Abrade** if specified (scuff pad, grit blast), then clean again to remove dust.
- **Primer / activator:** apply the specified thin coat, respect its flash-off and its
  *maximum* time before bonding.
- **Low surface-energy plastics** (PP, PE, some powder coats) bond poorly without
  special adhesives or treatment (plasma, flame, primer). Dyne pens/test inks check
  surface energy where the process calls for it.
- Release agents from moulding are a common hidden contaminant.

## 3. Dispensing
- **1K cartridges/sausages:** manual, pneumatic or battery guns. Cut the nozzle to give
  the specified bead size, usually at an angle.
- **2K cartridges with a static mixer:** before use, **balance** the cartridge (extrude
  until both components flow evenly), fit the mixer, then **purge** the first length of
  bead to waste (the TDS or work instruction gives how much, often a few cm or a mixer
  volume). If you stop longer than the pot life, replace the mixer — it will cure inside.
- **Meter-mix machines / robots:** check ratio and flow at shift start (e.g. by weighing a
  shot) per the station procedure.
- Discard material with wrong colour (mix indicator) or streaks.

## 4. Laying the bead
- Gun at a consistent angle (roughly 45°) and speed, **push** the bead where the
  instruction allows (it's forced into the surface better than dragging).
- **Continuous** bead with no gaps; at the start/end of a closed loop (a gasket), overlap
  the ends as the instruction says — the join is the common leak path.
- **Bead size** matters: too small = starved joint/leak; too big = squeeze-out and
  interference. Check with a bead gauge or a template, or weigh the part before and
  after on sample units.
- Keep the bead on the path: place it where the drawing shows (often inboard of bolt
  holes for FIPG).

## 5. Assembly and cure
- Close the joint **within open time**; if the bead has skinned over or open time is
  exceeded, remove and re-apply — don't press harder.
- Apply the specified pressure/clamp/fixture and don't disturb it until fixture time.
- Humidity-curing products (silicones, MS polymers, 1K PU) cure slower in dry or cold
  conditions; heat-cure products need the specified oven profile.
- Clean squeeze-out with the specified method while wet if required.

## 6. Checks
- Visual bead continuity and squeeze-out on 100% where visible.
- Destructive tests on sample parts at the set frequency (peel, lap-shear or pull test);
  look for **cohesive failure** (adhesive tears within itself) — **adhesive failure**
  (clean separation from a surface) points to surface prep.
- Leak test for seals where specified.

## Verify
- [ ] Adhesive lot recorded, in date, conditioned.
- [ ] Surfaces cleaned/primed per instruction and not touched after.
- [ ] 2K balanced and purged; ratio check done.
- [ ] Bead continuous, right size, on path; joint closed within open time.
- [ ] Sample tests show cohesive failure.

*Uncertain: all times, bead sizes and purge amounts are product-specific — use the TDS.*
